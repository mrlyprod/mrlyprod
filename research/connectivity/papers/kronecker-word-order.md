## Introduction

![Two pictures of 64 by 64 cells, top left and bottom right, each the Kronecker word of length 6 read off the Thue-Morse sequence 0, 1, 1, 0, 1, 0 over the gasket design 7 and the domino design 3, the top left sending 0 to the gasket, the bottom right sending 0 to the domino; both hold 216 filled cells, the top left in 14 connected pieces and the bottom right in 45, pieces coloured blue and orange in turn.](paper-kronecker-word-order)

Take a two-by-two square and fill some of its four cells. That is a design, and there are fifteen non-empty ones. Substitute a shrunken copy of a second design into every filled cell of the first, a third into every filled cell of that, and so on. After `level` steps the picture is a `2^level x 2^level` array of filled and empty cells, the Kronecker product `A_(c_1) (x) ... (x) A_(c_level)` of the designs chosen, outermost factor first. The list `w = (c_1, ..., c_level)` is a word, and the question is what the picture remembers of the order of its letters.

Half of the answer is classical and decides the shape of the rest. Two orders of the same multiset of letters differ by a perfect shuffle, one permutation applied to the rows and to the columns alike, so the side, the number of filled cells, the rank, the characteristic polynomial, the determinant and the trace cannot see the order. A shuffle does not preserve which cells share an edge. So the geometry is free to move, and the first part of this paper says which parts move and how early. The component count splits at length two, on the two-cell pair `{3, 6}` with four pieces against two (Theorem 4.2), and the two-cell designs are classified: dominoes commute with dominoes, diagonal pairs with diagonal pairs, and a domino against a diagonal pair never commutes (Theorem 4.3); any two designs of three or more cells give one piece in either order (Theorem 4.4). The boundary cell count holds at length two by an exact pairing of the four cells that can be interior (Theorem 4.1) and splits at every length from three on, on one explicit family (Theorem 4.5). A census over all 105 reorderable pairs and all 210 reorderable triples of the ten designs of two or three cells counts, observable by observable, how many multisets split (Fact 4.7). Behind the counts sits one algebraic fact: the component count is a rational series of Hankel rank four, six integer matrices in the frame of components, horizontal runs, vertical runs and fill (Theorem 5.7), although the count itself reaches `2 * 4^(level - 1)` (Theorem 5.9) and the geometric bookkeeping one would naively carry grows like `2^(level - 1)` as well (Proposition 5.10).

Since the order matters at every fixed length, the second part asks about the rate. Fix two letters and an infinite word over them, and ask whether `(1/level) log comp` converges and whether the limit can see anything the letter frequencies cannot. The hope, stated so that its refutation is visible, was that an aperiodic word with balanced frequencies, Thue-Morse, would return a number different from a periodic word of the same frequencies, a non-stationary effect in a construction whose dimension is already known to be a frequency average. Every one of the `C(15, 2) = 105` two-letter alphabets has an exact closed form for the count, in five regimes from six geometric lemmas (Theorem 6.1); two thirds of the alphabets fall to one lemma, the zero-contact cut. From the forms, at every interior frequency the rate exists, depends on the frequency vector alone and is therefore order-blind (Theorem 7.3); on 89 of the 105 alphabets it is the growth rate of the fill, which was order-blind to begin with, and the one class of alphabets whose rate sits strictly between the constant-word values and that ceiling is a domino against the full tile (Theorem 7.4). The one linear rule exact on constant words, `Phi(f) = (f_6 + f_9) log 2`, is refuted at every interior frequency on 78 of the 105, and no continuous rule of the frequencies alone can replace it there (Theorem 7.5). Along Thue-Morse over a gasket and a domino the rate is exactly `(1/2) log 6`, with the certificate `|log comp - (level/2) log 6| < 4.885` at every length from four (Theorem 7.6). The interior hypothesis is load-bearing: at a boundary frequency three words with the same frequencies have rates `0`, `log 2` and none at all (Theorem 7.9). No norm theorem could have supplied the rate, since the matrix-norm exponent along a constant word is the logarithm of its fill while the component exponent is `0` on thirteen of the fifteen letters (Theorem 8.1).

The figure is the two halves in one picture: the same six letters, three gaskets and three dominoes, read off the Thue-Morse sequence in its two readings, 14 pieces one way and 45 the other at length six, and the same rate `(1/2) log 6` in the limit either way.

What this paper adds is the alphabet and the experiment, not the construction. A word of designs is a non-autonomous iterated function system restricted to one finite parity alphabet, where the contractions change with the step and Moran constructions are the affine case ([Rempe-Gillen and Urbanski 2016](../refs.md), [Gu and Miao 2023](../refs.md)); the plane pictures are generalised Sierpinski carpets, whose connectedness has published necessary and sufficient conditions ([Cristea and Steinsky 2010](../refs.md)), which decide whether the count is `1` and not what it is; the topology of the limit sets of such systems, fractal squares included, is a developed subject ([Xiao 2019](../refs.md), [Nakajima and Watanabe 2025](../refs.md)), and a level-`level` component count is not a statement about any limit, the two disagreeing in both directions; several distinct ratios inside one level, which this construction cannot express, is the multiscale substitution programme ([Smilansky and Solomon 2021](../refs.md)); a word is an S-adic directive sequence in symbolic dynamics ([Berthe and Delecroix 2014](../refs.md)); and Kronecker graphs ([Leskovec and coauthors 2010](../refs.md)) build networks by the same product under a different adjacency, two vertices of a tensor product of graphs being adjacent when both coordinates are, where here two cells are adjacent when they share a grid edge, so neither theory transfers. What is specific is narrower: the alphabet is finite, enumerated and classified by symmetry; the composition law is the Kronecker product, which makes contact multiplicativity one line; and order is run as a controlled experiment, side, fill, density and dimension held fixed while only the letter order moves. Nothing here reads the word off the picture, and it could not in general: which Kronecker factorisations a binary matrix admits is the subject of [Voet and De Novellis 2025](../refs.md), who enumerate them, and it is why the object of study is the alphabet and the word, not the tile. No prior art for these counts and rates was found in the literature searched, which is a report on a search and not a claim of novelty.

Section 2 fixes the words. Section 3 proves the classical half. Section 4 is what the order changes at fixed length, Section 5 the rank-four series, Section 6 the closed forms, Section 7 the exponent, Section 8 what no norm can see. Section 9 states what is open and Section 10 names every generator. Every count in this paper is printed by one study of the tree, `lab/rs/magic-words`, except where the frame of Section 5 is concerned, which `lab/py/jsr-schedules` prints, and the crate functions `mrly::math::bang::magic` and `mrly::math::bang::word::components` draw and count every picture a second way.

## The setting

Base `2` and `dim = 2` throughout; `log` is the natural logarithm unless a unit is named.

**Definition 2.1 (designs and codes).** A design is a subset `F_c` of the four cells of the `2 x 2` grid, encoded by its code `c` in `{1, ..., 15}`: bit `i` of `c` is the cell in row `floor(i/2)` and column `i mod 2`, rows downward and columns rightward from `0`. So `3` is the top row, `12` the bottom row, `5` the left column, `10` the right column, `9` the main diagonal `{(0, 0), (1, 1)}`, `6` the anti-diagonal `{(0, 1), (1, 0)}`, `7` the L missing the bottom-right cell, and `15` the full tile. `A_c` is the `2 x 2` binary array of `F_c` and `fill(c) = |F_c|`.

**Definition 2.2 (words and pictures).** A word `w = (c_1, ..., c_level)` gives the `2^level x 2^level` binary array `A_w = A_(c_1) (x) ... (x) A_(c_level)`, first letter outermost, with `A_e` the one filled cell for the empty word: the cell with row digits `(r_1, ..., r_level)` and column digits `(s_1, ..., s_level)` in base `2`, most significant first, is filled exactly when `(r_i, s_i)` lies in `F_(c_i)` for every `i`. Its fill is `fill(A_w) = prod_i fill(c_i)`. `w_(1..i)` is the prefix of length `i`, and `w c` the word with the letter `c` appended, so that `A_(w c) = A_w (x) A_c` replaces every filled cell of `A_w` by a copy of `A_c`.

**Definition 2.3 (observables).** For a binary array `X` of side `n`: `comp(X)` is the number of connected components of the filled cells under edge adjacency, two cells being adjacent when they share an edge; a filled cell is interior when its four edge neighbours exist and are filled, and the boundary cell count is `bd(X) = fill(X) - interior(X)`; the exposed-edge count is `4 fill(X) - 2 E(X)` with `E` the number of adjacent filled pairs; `holes(X)` is the number of components of the empty cells under edge adjacency that do not meet the border; the Euler characteristic is `N - A + Q` over filled cells, adjacent filled pairs and fully filled `2 x 2` blocks; the main-diagonal count is the number of filled cells `(d, d)`, the anti-diagonal profile the vector whose entry `s` counts the filled cells `(x, y)` with `x + y = s`; `H(X)` and `V(X)` are the numbers of maximal horizontal and vertical runs of filled cells; and the contact counts are `h(X)`, the number of rows whose first and last cells are both filled, and `v(X)`, the number of columns whose top and bottom cells are both filled. Two side-by-side copies of `X` touch exactly when `h(X) > 0`, two stacked copies exactly when `v(X) > 0`.

**Definition 2.4 (order-blind and order-sensitive).** Two words are multiset-equivalent when one is a permutation of the other. An observable is order-blind at length `level` on a set of codes when it agrees on every pair of multiset-equivalent words of that length over those codes, and order-sensitive otherwise. A multiset counts in a census only when it has two or more distinct orderings; a constant word cannot show order sensitivity.

**Definition 2.5 (the six classes).** The contact-free designs, those with `h = v = 0`, are the four units `1, 2, 4, 8` and the two diagonals `6, 9`. The heavy designs, connected with `h >= 1` and `v >= 1`, are the four gaskets `7, 11, 13, 14` and the full tile `15`. The remaining four are the dominoes, `3, 12` the row dominoes with `h = 1`, `v = 0` and `5, 10` the column dominoes with `h = 0`, `v = 1`; two dominoes are like when they share an orientation. Units, row dominoes, column dominoes, diagonals, gaskets and the full tile are the six classes of every statement below, and the `C(15, 2) = 105` two-letter alphabets fall into `20` non-empty class pairs, `15` of distinct classes and `5` inside one class, the full tile being alone in its class.

**Definition 2.6 (frequency and exponent).** An infinite word `w` over `{a, b}` has letter frequencies `f = (f_a, f_b)` when the number of occurrences of each letter in `w_(1..level)`, divided by `level`, converges; the frequency is interior when `f_a > 0` and `f_b > 0`. The component exponent is `chi(w) = lim (1/level) log comp(A_(w_(1..level)))` when the limit exists; the fill exponent is `f_a log fill(a) + f_b log fill(b)`, which exists whenever `f` does and never sees the order. The constant-word functional is `Phi(f) = (f_6 + f_9) log 2`; by Lemma 5.6 it is the unique linear functional on the frequency simplex that agrees with the rate of every constant word.

## What the shuffle cannot see

**Proposition 3.1 (the perfect shuffle).** Let `A` be `m x m` and `B` be `n x n`. There is a permutation matrix `S` of size `mn`, depending only on `m` and `n`, with `B (x) A = S (A (x) B) S^T`. Consequently, if `phi` is any function of a square array with `phi(P X P^T) = phi(X)` for every permutation matrix `P`, then `phi(A_w)` is order-blind at every length.

**Proof.** Index the rows of `A (x) B` by pairs `(i_A, i_B)` through `i = n i_A + i_B` and the rows of `B (x) A` by the same pairs through `i' = m i_B + i_A`. By definition `(A (x) B)[(i_A, i_B)][(j_A, j_B)] = A[i_A][j_A] B[i_B][j_B] = (B (x) A)[(i_B, i_A)][(j_B, j_A)]`. Let `S` be the permutation matrix of `(i_A, i_B) -> (i_B, i_A)` on the `mn` index pairs; the identity says that `B (x) A` is `A (x) B` with `S` applied to the rows and the same `S` to the columns. For the consequence, let `w'` be a permutation of `w`. A permutation is a product of adjacent transpositions, and by associativity an adjacent transposition of two letters of `w` is the shuffle of the two corresponding factors conjugated by the identity on the factors outside them, itself a simultaneous row and column permutation of `A_w`. Composing, `A_(w') = P A_w P^T` for a permutation matrix `P`, so `phi(A_(w')) = phi(A_w)`. □

**Corollary 3.2.** Side, fill, density, rank, characteristic polynomial, determinant and trace are order-blind at every length. The trace is the main-diagonal count, and it is the product `prod_i |{d in {0, 1} : (d, d) in F_(c_i)}|`.

**Proof.** Each is unchanged by `X -> P X P^T`. For the product, the cell `(x, x)` is filled exactly when `(x_i, x_i)` lies in `F_(c_i)` for every digit position `i`, and the digits `x_1, ..., x_level` range independently over `{0, 1}` as `x` ranges over `{0, ..., 2^level - 1}`. □

Proposition 3.1 is classical and is proved here only so that the paper stands alone; it is stated first because it fences off the whole algebraic side. Everything below is about observables that are not invariant under simultaneous row and column permutation, and the dividing line is one sentence: a shuffle does not preserve which cells share an edge. One more observable is order-blind for a geometric reason rather than an algebraic one.

**Lemma 3.3 (contacts multiply).** `h(A_w) = prod_i h(A_(c_i))` and `v(A_w) = prod_i v(A_(c_i))`. In particular whether two adjacent copies of `A_w` touch is order-blind and is decided letter by letter.

**Proof.** It suffices to treat `A (x) B` with `A` of side `m` and `B` of side `n`. A row of `A (x) B` is indexed by a pair `(r, r')` with `r < m` and `r' < n`, and its first and last cells are the cells `(r', 0)` and `(r', n - 1)` of the copies of `B` sitting in the block columns `0` and `m - 1` of block row `r`. So the row `(r, r')` has both end cells filled exactly when row `r` of `A` and row `r'` of `B` both do, and counting pairs gives `h(A (x) B) = h(A) h(B)`. The column statement is the transpose, and induction on the length finishes. A product of per-letter quantities does not see the order, and it is positive exactly when every factor is. □

## What the order changes

The mechanism behind every statement of this section is one sentence. In `A_a (x) A_b` the filled cells are the copies of `A_b` placed in the blocks `q` of `F_a`; two copies can merge only when their blocks share an edge, and then exactly through that edge, side by side when `h(A_b) > 0` and stacked when `v(A_b) > 0`, because a cell of one copy and a cell of the other are adjacent only across the shared block edge and only at a row or column whose end cells are both filled. Copies in blocks that share no edge never merge, two cells of such blocks differing by at least two in some coordinate.

**Theorem 4.1 (boundary cells are order-blind at length two).** For all codes `a, b` in `{0, ..., 15}`, the empty design allowed, `bd(A_a (x) A_b) = bd(A_b (x) A_a)`. Writing `S(q) = {0, 1}^2 - {q}` and `bar p = (1 - p_1, 1 - p_2)`,

```
interior(A_a (x) A_b) = sum over p in {0, 1}^2 of [S(p) subset F_a] [S(bar p) subset F_b] ,
```

which is symmetric in `a` and `b` because `p -> bar p` is an involution.

**Proof.** A cell of the `4 x 4` grid in row `0` or `3` or column `0` or `3` lacks a neighbour, so only the four central cells can be interior; index them by `p` in `{0, 1}^2` through `z(p) = (2 - p_1, 2 - p_2)`. A cell `(x, y)` decomposes into its outer index `(floor(x/2), floor(y/2))`, which must lie in `F_a`, and its inner index `(x mod 2, y mod 2)`, which must lie in `F_b`. Since `floor((2 - p_i)/2) = 1 - p_i` and `(2 - p_i) mod 2 = p_i`, the cell `z(p)` has outer index `bar p` and inner index `p`. Its vertical neighbours sit in rows `1 - p_1` and `3 - p_1`, with outer rows `0` and `1` and inner row `1 - p_1 = bar p_1`, in the column of `z(p)`; both are filled exactly when `(bar p_1, p_2)` lies in `F_b` and both cells of column `bar p_2` of `A_a` lie in `F_a`. Its horizontal neighbours sit in columns `1 - p_2` and `3 - p_2`, with outer columns `0` and `1` and inner column `bar p_2`, in the row of `z(p)`; both are filled exactly when `(p_1, bar p_2)` lies in `F_b` and both cells of row `bar p_1` of `A_a` lie in `F_a`. Collecting, the condition on `A_a` is that `bar p`, all of column `bar p_2` and all of row `bar p_1` lie in `F_a`, and the union of these is every cell with row `bar p_1` or column `bar p_2`, that is every cell but `p`, so `S(p) subset F_a`; the condition on `A_b` is that `p`, `(bar p_1, p_2)` and `(p_1, bar p_2)` lie in `F_b`, the three cells other than `bar p`, so `S(bar p) subset F_b`. Summing the indicator over the four candidates gives the display. Swapping `a` and `b` replaces the term at `p` by the term at `bar p`, a fixed-point-free involution of `{0, 1}^2`, so the sum is unchanged; and `bd = fill - interior` with fill order-blind by Corollary 3.2. □

**Theorem 4.2 (the smallest witness).** `comp(A_3 (x) A_6) = 4` and `comp(A_6 (x) A_3) = 2`. Among the six two-cell designs `{3, 5, 6, 9, 10, 12}` the pair `{3, 6}` is the lexicographically first that fails to commute.

**Proof.** `F_3 = {(0, 0), (0, 1)}` and `F_6 = {(0, 1), (1, 0)}`. In `A_3 (x) A_6` the copies of `A_6` sit in the blocks `(0, 0)` and `(0, 1)`, so the filled cells are `(0, 1), (1, 0), (0, 3), (1, 2)`; no two differ by one in one coordinate and zero in the other, so all four are isolated. In `A_6 (x) A_3` the copies of `A_3` sit in the blocks `(0, 1)` and `(1, 0)`, giving the horizontal dominoes `{(0, 2), (0, 3)}` and `{(2, 0), (2, 1)}` in blocks that share no edge, so `comp = 2`. The only earlier pair in lexicographic order, `{3, 5}`, commutes by Theorem 4.3. □

**Theorem 4.3 (the two-cell dichotomy).** For the component count at length two: two dominoes always commute, giving `1` when they are like and `2` when they are not; two diagonals always commute, giving `4`; and a domino against a diagonal never commutes, `comp(A_D (x) A_G) = 4` and `comp(A_G (x) A_D) = 2` for every one of the eight ordered pairs of a domino `D` and a diagonal `G`.

**Proof.** A row domino has `h = 1`, `v = 0`, a column domino `h = 0`, `v = 1`, and a diagonal `h = v = 0`, read off Definition 2.3. Domino against diagonal: in `A_D (x) A_G` the two blocks of `F_D` share an edge but `A_G` has no contact, so the copies do not merge, and each copy is two isolated cells, total `4`; in `A_G (x) A_D` the two blocks of `F_G` share no edge and each copy of the connected `A_D` is one piece, total `2`. Two dominoes `A` and `B`: the two blocks of `F_A` are side by side when `A` is a row domino and stacked when it is a column domino, each copy of `B` is one piece, and the copies merge exactly when `B` has the contact in that direction, `h(B) > 0` for a row domino `B` and `v(B) > 0` for a column domino `B`; so the count is `1` exactly when the orientations agree, a condition symmetric in `A` and `B`. Two diagonals: the blocks share no edge and each copy is two isolated cells, total `4` in either order. □

**Theorem 4.4 (three cells or more).** If `fill(a) >= 3` and `fill(b) >= 3` then `comp(A_a (x) A_b) = comp(A_b (x) A_a) = 1`. The designs of three or more cells are exactly the heavy designs `7, 11, 13, 14, 15`.

**Proof.** A design of fill at least `3` omits at most one cell of `{0, 1}^2`, so at most one of its two rows and at most one of its two columns meets the omitted cell: a full row and a full column survive, giving `h >= 1` and `v >= 1`; and it is connected, the full tile plainly and three cells as an L whose corner shares an edge with both other cells. Conversely a design of fill at most `2` is a unit, a domino or a diagonal, none heavy. Now let `fill(a), fill(b) >= 3`. Every copy of `A_b` in `A_a (x) A_b` is one piece; two copies at blocks sharing an edge merge, since `A_b` has the contact in that direction; and `A_a` is connected, so every two copies are joined by a chain of blocks sharing edges. Hence `comp = 1`, and the hypothesis is symmetric. □

**Theorem 4.5 (boundary cells split at every length from three).** For every `k >= 1` the words `(7, 3^k, 13)` and `(3^k, 7, 13)`, of length `k + 2`, carry the same multiset and

```
bd(A_(7, 3^k, 13)) = 9 * 2^k ,    bd(A_(3^k, 7, 13)) = 2^(k+3) + 1 .
```

So the boundary cell count is order-sensitive at every length `level >= 3`, already on the three codes `{3, 7, 13}`, each of fill `2` or `3`.

**Proof.** Both pictures have `fill = 3 * 2^k * 3 = 9 * 2^k`. `A_(3^k)` is the top row of its `2^k x 2^k` grid, since each top-row letter forces one more row digit to `0` and leaves the column digit free. In `A_(3^k) (x) A_13` every one of those `2^k` cells becomes a copy of `A_13 = {(0, 0), (1, 0), (1, 1)}` in rows `0` and `1` of the `2^(k+1)` grid, a strip of height `2` with every other row empty; `A_7 (x) (A_(3^k) (x) A_13)` places three such blocks, at `(0, 0)`, `(0, 1)` and `(1, 0)`, and since `2^(k+1) >= 4` every filled cell has the exterior or an empty row directly above it or directly below it. The interior is `0` and `bd = 9 * 2^k`. In `A_(3^k) (x) A_(7, 13)` the top row of `A_(3^k)` becomes `2^k` copies of the `4 x 4` picture `X = A_7 (x) A_13` side by side in rows `0` to `3`. `X` places `A_13` in the blocks `(0, 0)`, `(0, 1)`, `(1, 0)`, so its four rows hold the columns `{0, 2}`, `{0, 1, 2, 3}`, `{0}`, `{0, 1}`. Row `0` of the strip is the border and row `3` has the empty row `4` below it, so interior cells lie in rows `1` and `2`. In row `2` the only filled cell of a copy is its column `0`, whose right neighbour is empty. In row `1` the cells at columns `1`, `2`, `3` of a copy have an empty cell above them, at `(0, 1)`, below them at `(2, 2)`, and above them at `(0, 3)` respectively; the cell at column `0` has `(0, 0)`, `(2, 0)` and `(1, 1)` filled, and its left neighbour is the cell `(1, 3)` of the copy to its left, filled, when there is such a copy, and the border otherwise. So the interior is `2^k - 1` and `bd = 9 * 2^k - 2^k + 1`. The two counts differ by `2^k - 1 >= 1`. □

At `k = 1` the counts are `18` and `17`, and `{3, 7, 13}` is one of the `36` sensitive triples of Fact 4.7. Theorems 4.1 and 4.5 together put the threshold exactly at length three.

**Corollary 4.6 (block reduction).** For designs `c_1, ..., c_p` and every `level >= 1`, associativity of the Kronecker product gives `(A_(c_1) (x) ... (x) A_(c_p))^((x) level) = A_(u^level)` with `u = (c_1, ..., c_p)`, cell for cell. So every periodic word is the self-similar picture of its one-period composite tile, a design at base `2^p` with fill `prod_i fill(c_i)`, every observable of a periodic word is governed by the one-tile theory, and genuinely non-stationary behaviour needs an aperiodic word.

**Proof.** Both sides are the `p * level`-fold product `A_(c_1) (x) ... (x) A_(c_p) (x) A_(c_1) (x) ...`, bracketed differently. □

**Fact 4.7 (the landscape).** Over the fifteen non-empty codes at length two there are `225` words, `120` multisets and `105` with two or more orderings; over the ten-code library `{3, 5, 6, 7, 9, 10, 11, 12, 13, 14}` of every code of fill `2` or `3` at length three there are `1000` words, `220` multisets and `210` with two or more orderings. The number of those multisets on which each observable takes two or more values:

| observable | length 2, of 105 | length 3, of 210 |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| fill, side, density | 0 | 0 |
| main-diagonal count | 0 | 0 |
| boundary cells | 0 | 36 |
| exposed edges | 78 | 188 |
| components | 74 | 188 |
| Euler characteristic | 78 | 188 |
| holes | 10 | 100 |
| anti-diagonal profile | 99 | 204 |

The anti-diagonal profile splits first on the one-cell pair `{1, 2}`, whose two orders put the one filled cell at coordinate sums `1` and `2`. The ten-code library is the only one of the `3003` ten-code subsets of the fifteen whose length-three row is the one printed. Domain: the `225` words of length two and, for the uniqueness of the library, all `3375` words of length three over the fifteen codes, the `1000` of the library among them. Generator: `lab/rs/magic-words`, which draws every word twice, inside the study and through `mrly::math::bang::magic`, and gets the same cells both ways.

Two readings in the table invert the answer if taken the other way. The main-diagonal count is order-blind at every length by Corollary 3.2 and the anti-diagonal profile is order-sensitive on `99` of `105` already at length two: take the whole profile, never one coefficient, and take the count, never the profile. The boundary cell count of Theorem 4.1 counts cells with an empty or exterior neighbour; the exposed-edge count `4 fill - 2 E` is a second reading of the word boundary, and that one is order-sensitive at length two on `78` of `105`. The rows of the table for fill, the main-diagonal count and boundary cells at length two are checks of Corollary 3.2 and Theorem 4.1; the other rows are counts.

## Six lemmas and a rational series of rank four

Six geometric lemmas carry the rest of the paper. The first three say what one appended letter does to the component count, by class; the fourth says what it does to the run counts; the fifth and sixth are the two closed forms that the first three do not reach.

**Lemma 5.1 (the zero-contact cut).** (a) If a suffix `u = w_(p..level)` has `h(A_u) = v(A_u) = 0` then `comp(A_w) = fill(A_(w_(1..p-1))) comp(A_u)`. (b) If `z` is contact-free then `comp(A_(z u)) = fill(z) comp(A_u)` for every word `u`, and in particular `comp(A_z) = fill(z)`. (c) If `c` is contact-free then `comp(A_(w c)) = fill(c) fill(A_w)` for every word `w`.

**Proof.** (a) Write `A_w = A_(w_(1..p-1)) (x) A_u`. Each filled cell of the prefix carries one copy of `A_u` in its own block, and two cells in different blocks are adjacent only when the blocks share an edge, across that edge, at a row of `A_u` with both end cells filled for side-by-side blocks or a column with both end cells filled for stacked blocks; both counts are zero, so distinct copies never merge and the count is the number of copies times the count of one copy. (b) A contact-free design is a unit or a diagonal, whose filled cells are pairwise non-adjacent, so the `fill(z)` copies of `A_u` in `A_z (x) A_u` sit in blocks sharing no edge and never touch, whatever the contacts of `A_u`. (c) is (a) with the one-letter suffix `u = c`, together with `comp(A_c) = fill(c)` from (b). □

**Lemma 5.2 (a heavy suffix letter does nothing).** If `c` is heavy then `comp(A_(w c)) = comp(A_w)` for every word `w`.

**Proof.** In `A_w (x) A_c` each filled cell of `A_w` becomes a block congruent to `A_c`, which is connected. Two blocks at horizontally adjacent cells meet exactly when `h(A_c) >= 1` and two at vertically adjacent cells exactly when `v(A_c) >= 1`, both of which hold; two blocks at non-adjacent cells are disjoint and never touch. So the graph on blocks with an edge where two blocks meet is isomorphic to the graph on the filled cells of `A_w` with an edge where two cells are adjacent, and components correspond. □

**Lemma 5.3 (a domino suffix letter counts runs).** For a row domino `p`, `comp(A_(w p)) = H(A_w)`; for a column domino, `comp(A_(w p)) = V(A_w)`.

**Proof.** A row domino has one full row and one empty row. In `A_w (x) A_p` every filled cell of `A_w` becomes a block whose one filled row is the same row of the block for every block and whose other row is empty. The rows of the picture therefore alternate between a copy of a row of `A_w` with every cell doubled and an empty row, so no two filled rows are vertically adjacent, and doubling each cell merges exactly the cells that were already horizontally adjacent. Components are in bijection with the maximal horizontal runs of `A_w`. The column case is the transpose. □

**Lemma 5.4 (the run law).** Let `r(c)` and `s(c)` be the numbers of non-empty rows and columns of `A_c`. Then `H(A_e) = V(A_e) = 1` and, for every word `w` and letter `c`,

```
H(A_(w c)) = h(c) H(A_w) + (r(c) - h(c)) fill(A_w) ,    V(A_(w c)) = v(c) V(A_w) + (s(c) - v(c)) fill(A_w) .
```

**Proof.** Row `2i + t` of `A_w (x) A_c`, for `t` in `{0, 1}`, holds one copy of row `t` of `A_c` for every filled cell of row `i` of `A_w`. A non-empty row of a `2 x 2` design is one run and an empty row is none; two copies coming from horizontally adjacent filled cells of `A_w` fuse into one run exactly when row `t` of `A_c` has both end cells filled. So row `2i + t` carries `[row t non-empty] fill_i - [row t full] (fill_i - H_i)` runs, with `fill_i` and `H_i` the filled cells and the runs of row `i` of `A_w` and `fill_i - H_i` its number of adjacent filled pairs. Summing over `t` gives `r(c) fill_i - h(c) (fill_i - H_i)`, and summing over `i` gives the law. The column law is the transpose, and the one filled cell has one run each way. □

**Lemma 5.5 (the row-block law).** Over an alphabet of one row domino and the full tile, `comp(A_w) = 2^(n - j)` with `n` the number of full letters of `w` and `j` the length of its terminal run of full letters. The transposed statement holds for a column domino.

**Proof.** A row domino forces the row digit at its place to one value `d` and leaves the column digit free; the full tile forces neither. So the filled set is a product `R x {0, ..., 2^level - 1}` with `R` the set of rows whose digit equals `d` at every domino place, `|R| = 2^n`. Every row of `R` is a full horizontal line, two of them lie in one component exactly when they are vertically adjacent, and the count is the number of maximal blocks of consecutive integers in `R`. Index the row digits so that place `i` contributes `2^(level - i)`. The last `j` places are full, hence free, and place `level - j` is a domino, hence forced, whenever `j < level`. So `R` is a union of blocks of `2^j` consecutive integers, one for each choice of the free digits above place `level - j`, and the integer just above such a block differs from its top element by flipping the forced digit at place `level - j` away from `d`, so it is not in `R` and the blocks are pairwise non-adjacent. There are `2^(n - j)` of them. If `j = level` then `R` is everything and the count is `1 = 2^(n - j)`; if `n = 0` then `j = 0`, `R` is one row and the count is `1`. □

**Lemma 5.6 (constant words).** `comp(A_(c^level)) = comp(A_c)^level` for every code `c`, and `comp(A_c) = 2` exactly for the diagonals `6, 9` and `1` for the other thirteen.

**Proof.** A unit word is one cell. A domino word of one orientation is a full line, by Lemma 5.5 at `n = 0` or directly. A heavy letter gives `1` at every length by Lemma 5.2. A diagonal is contact-free with fill `2`, so Lemma 5.1(c) applies at every suffix and the count is `2^level`. Finally `comp(A_c)` is `1` for the nine designs of fill `1`, `3`, `4` and the four dominoes, and `2` for the two diagonals, whose two cells share no edge. □

**Theorem 5.7 (the component count is a rational series of rank four).** Let `s(w) = (comp, H, V, fill)(A_w)` as a row vector. Then `s(e) = (1, 1, 1, 1)` and `s(w c) = s(w) T_c`, where `T_c` depends only on the class of `c` and sends `(comp, H, V, fill)` to

```
unit          (fill, fill, fill, fill)          diagonal      (2 fill, 2 fill, 2 fill, 2 fill)
row domino    (H, H, 2 fill, 2 fill)            column domino (V, 2 fill, V, 2 fill)
gasket        (comp, H + fill, V + fill, 3 fill)  full tile    (comp, 2 H, 2 V, 4 fill)
```

Hence `comp(A_w) = (1, 1, 1, 1) T_(c_1) ... T_(c_level) e_1^T` for every word, the empty word included: six integer `4 x 4` matrices, one per class, reproduce the component count of every word as one matrix product, and the same product reads `H`, `V` and the fill off its other three coordinates.

**Proof.** The fill coordinate is `fill(A_(w c)) = fill(c) fill(A_w)`, and the fills of the six classes are `1, 2, 2, 2, 3, 4`. The run coordinates are Lemma 5.4 with `(r, h)` equal to `(1, 0)`, `(2, 0)`, `(1, 1)`, `(2, 0)`, `(2, 1)`, `(2, 2)` and `(s, v)` equal to `(1, 0)`, `(2, 0)`, `(2, 0)`, `(1, 1)`, `(2, 1)`, `(2, 2)` on the six classes in the order listed. The component coordinate is Lemma 5.1(c) for a unit and a diagonal, Lemma 5.3 for the two domino classes and Lemma 5.2 for the gasket and the full tile. Induction from `s(e) = (1, 1, 1, 1)`, the one filled cell, gives the product. □

The six matrices are lower triangular in this frame, with diagonals `(0, 0, 0, 1)`, `(0, 0, 0, 2)`, `(0, 1, 0, 2)`, `(0, 0, 1, 2)`, `(1, 1, 1, 3)` and `(1, 2, 2, 4)`; Section 8 reads the spectral consequences. Theorem 5.7 bounds the Hankel rank of the series `w -> comp(A_w)` by four, since the Hankel matrix `(u, v) -> comp(A_(u v))` factors as the product of the `(words x 4)` matrix of rows `(1, 1, 1, 1) T_u` and the `(4 x words)` matrix of columns `T_v e_1^T`.

**Fact 5.8 (Hankel ranks).** Index a `241 x 241` integer matrix by the words of length at most two over the fifteen codes, with entry `(u, v)` the observable of `A_(u v)`. Its rank is `4` for the component count, `4` for the Euler characteristic, `8` for the boundary cell count and `11` for the hole count, and each of the four observables is reproduced by a linear representation of that dimension, built by Hankel-basis elimination in exact rational arithmetic with basis words `e, 3, 5, 15` for the first two, `e, 3, 5, 7, 11, 13, 14, (7, 14)` for the boundary and `e, 3, 5, 7, 11, 13, 14, 15, (7, 6), (7, 7), (11, 11)` for the holes, exact on all `54240` words of length one to four and on `120` seeded words of length five to seven, zero mismatches. Domain: those `54360` words. Generator: `lab/rs/magic-words`. With Theorem 5.7 the Hankel rank of the component series is exactly `4`, so the frame of Theorem 5.7 is a minimal representation; the ranks of the truncation are lower bounds for the other three series, for which this paper has no proof of the representation.

**Theorem 5.9 (the ceiling).** For every word of length `level >= 1`, `comp(A_w) <= 2 * 4^(level - 1)`, and `(15^(level - 1), 6)` attains it: `comp(A_(15^(level - 1), 6)) = 2 * 4^(level - 1)` for every `level >= 1`; so does `(15^(level - 1), 9)`, the other checkerboard.

**Proof.** One cell from each component is an independent set of the `2^level x 2^level` grid under edge adjacency. The grid has a perfect matching, each cell of a row paired with its right-hand neighbour, rows having even length, and an independent set holds at most one cell of each matched pair, so no subset of the grid has more than `4^level / 2` components. In `A_15^((x) (level - 1)) (x) A_6` the outer full tiles leave every coordinate free and move a cell by even offsets, and the innermost letter fills exactly the two cells `(0, 1)` and `(1, 0)` of odd coordinate sum of each `2 x 2` block; so the picture is the checkerboard of all cells with `x + y` odd, no two of which share an edge, and its `2 * 4^(level - 1)` cells are as many components; with `9` in place of `6` the innermost letter fills the two cells of even coordinate sum and the picture is the other checkerboard. □

The exhaustive maxima over all `15^level` words read `8, 32, 128` at lengths two to four, and through the representation the bound is attained again at length five (`lab/rs/magic-words`); at length one the maximum is `2`, by Lemma 5.6. So both the count and the obvious way of computing it grow without bound.

**Proposition 5.10 (the naive geometric state is unbounded).** Let `kappa(X)` be the number of components of `X` that meet the border of its grid, the components whose fate is undecided when `X` is placed inside a larger picture, which bounds from above the number that can merge with anything outside. Then `kappa(A_(15^(level - 1), 3)) = 2^(level - 1)`.

**Proof.** The full tiles leave every coordinate free and the top-row domino forces the last row digit to `0`, so the picture fills the cell `(x, y)` exactly when `x` is even: `2^(level - 1)` full-width rows separated by empty rows, each one component and each meeting the left and right border. □

So the count reaches `2 * 4^(level - 1)` and the bookkeeping `2^(level - 1)`, while four numbers carry both: Hankel rank is the dimension of a span of functions, not the size of any geometric state, and the two have no reason to agree. The algebraic source of order sensitivity is the noncommutation of the six matrices, and it is a necessary condition only.

**Proposition 5.11 (noncommutation is necessary, not sufficient).** Of the `15` pairs of distinct classes, `14` have `T_a T_b != T_b T_a`; the one commuting pair is the units against the diagonals, with `T_diagonal = 2 T_unit`. If `comp` takes different values on `(a, b)` and `(b, a)` then `T_a T_b != T_b T_a`. The converse is false: `T_3 T_5 != T_5 T_3` while `comp(A_3 (x) A_5) = comp(A_5 (x) A_3) = 2`, and `T_7 T_15 != T_15 T_7` while `comp(A_7 (x) A_15) = comp(A_15 (x) A_7) = 1`.

**Proof.** By Theorem 5.7, `comp(A_a (x) A_b) - comp(A_b (x) A_a) = (1, 1, 1, 1) (T_a T_b - T_b T_a) e_1^T`, so different values force a nonzero commutator. The identity `T_diagonal = 2 T_unit` is read off the table of Theorem 5.7, and two matrices one of which is a scalar multiple of the other commute. For the two counterexamples, compose the maps of Theorem 5.7: a row domino then a column domino sends `(comp, H, V, fill)` to `(2 fill, 4 fill, 2 fill, 4 fill)`, and a column domino then a row domino sends it to `(2 fill, 2 fill, 4 fill, 4 fill)`, so `T_3 T_5 - T_5 T_3` has the single nonzero row `(0, 2, -2, 0)`, the fill coordinate contributing `2 fill` to the second coordinate and `-2 fill` to the third; a gasket then the full tile sends `(comp, H, V, fill)` to `(comp, 2 H + 2 fill, 2 V + 2 fill, 12 fill)`, and the full tile then a gasket to `(comp, 2 H + 4 fill, 2 V + 4 fill, 12 fill)`, so `T_7 T_15 - T_15 T_7` has the single nonzero row `(0, -2, -2, 0)`. Both commutators have a zero first column and so annihilate `e_1^T`, which is why the two counts agree, by Theorem 4.3 for `{3, 5}` and Theorem 4.4 for `{7, 15}`; neither commutator vanishes. Order sensitivity needs `(1, 1, 1, 1) (T_a T_b - T_b T_a) e_1^T != 0`, which is strictly stronger than `T_a T_b != T_b T_a`. The count of `14` commuting failures out of `15` is a finite computation on the six matrices, printed by `lab/rs/magic-words`. □

## The closed forms on all 105 alphabets

**Theorem 6.1 (five regimes).** Let `{a, b}` be one of the `105` two-letter alphabets and `w` a word over it of length `level`. Then `comp(A_w)` is given exactly by the table, whose five regimes are mutually exclusive and exhaust the alphabets; the first is every alphabet with a contact-free letter, and the other four assume neither letter is contact-free.

| regime | `comp(A_w)` | alphabets |
| --- | --- | ---: |
| one letter contact-free | `fill(A_(w_(1..p)))`, `p` the last contact-free place, and `1` if there is none | 69 |
| two heavy letters, or two like dominoes | `1` | 12 |
| two unlike dominoes | `2^(level - r)`, `r` the terminal run | 4 |
| a domino and the full tile | `2^(n - j)`, `n` the number of full letters, `j` their terminal run | 4 |
| a domino and a gasket | `1 + sum fill(A_(w_(1..i-1)))` over the gasket places `i <= m`, `m` the last domino place, and `1` if there is no domino | 16 |

**Proof.** Counting first. The contact-free codes are `1, 2, 4, 8, 6, 9`, so the alphabets of the first regime number `C(6, 2) + 6 * 9 = 69`. The remaining `C(9, 2) = 36` lie among the four dominoes, the four gaskets and the full tile: `C(5, 2) = 10` pairs of heavy letters and `2` pairs of like dominoes make `12`, `4` pairs of unlike dominoes, `4` pairs of a domino with the full tile and `16` pairs of a domino with a gasket; `69 + 12 + 4 + 4 + 16 = 105`.

First regime. If both letters are contact-free then `p = level`, the one-letter suffix `w_level` has zero contact, and Lemma 5.1(a) then (b) give `comp(A_w) = fill(A_(w_(1..level-1))) comp(A_(w_level)) = fill(A_(w_(1..level-1))) fill(w_level) = fill(A_(w_(1..p)))`. Otherwise exactly one letter `z` is contact-free; call the other `c`. If `w` carries no `z` then `w = c^level` with `c` a domino, a gasket or the full tile and `comp(A_w) = 1` by Lemma 5.6. Otherwise let `p` be the last place carrying `z`; every later letter is `c`, so the suffix `u = z c^(level - p)` has `h(A_u) = h(z) h(c)^(level - p) = 0` and likewise `v(A_u) = 0` by Lemma 3.3. Lemma 5.1(a) gives `comp(A_w) = fill(A_(w_(1..p-1))) comp(A_u)` and Lemma 5.1(b) gives `comp(A_u) = fill(z) comp(A_(c^(level - p))) = fill(z)` by Lemma 5.6; multiplying, `comp(A_w) = fill(A_(w_(1..p)))`.

Second regime. Two heavy letters: `comp(A_(c_1)) = 1` since `A_(c_1)` is connected, and Lemma 5.2 keeps it `1` at every appended letter. Two row dominoes: every letter forces the row digit at its place and leaves the column digit free, so the picture is one full row; two column dominoes are the transpose.

Third regime. Let `r` be the terminal run and suppose `r < level`, so that place `level - r` carries the other orientation. The suffix `u = w_(level - r..level)` has `h(A_u) = h(w_(level - r)) h(w_level)^r` and `v(A_u) = v(w_(level - r)) v(w_level)^r`; one orientation has `h = 0` and the other `v = 0`, so both products vanish, and Lemma 5.1(a) gives `comp(A_w) = fill(A_(w_(1..level - r - 1))) comp(A_u) = 2^(level - r - 1) comp(A_u)`. And `comp(A_u) = 2`: up to transposition `A_u` is a column domino carrying in each of its two cells a copy of `A_(w_level^r)`, a full horizontal line at one row of its `2^r x 2^r` block, and the two copies sit one above the other, their lines `2^r >= 2` rows apart. If `r = level` the word is constant and `comp(A_w) = 1 = 2^(level - r)`.

Fourth regime. Lemma 5.5.

Fifth regime. Let `q` be the gasket and `p` the domino, a row domino without loss of generality. If `w` carries no domino then `w = q^level` and `comp(A_w) = 1` by Lemma 5.2. Otherwise let `m` be the last domino place; every later letter is the heavy `q`, so `comp(A_w) = comp(A_(w_(1..m)))` by Lemma 5.2, and the last letter of `w_(1..m)` is the row domino, so `comp(A_(w_(1..m))) = H(A_(w_(1..m-1)))` by Lemma 5.3. Lemma 5.4 telescopes: from `H(A_e) = 1`, a row domino has `(r, h) = (1, 1)` and leaves `H` unchanged, a gasket has `(r, h) = (2, 1)` and adds `fill(A_(w_(1..i-1)))` at its place `i`. So `H(A_(w_(1..m-1))) = 1 + sum fill(A_(w_(1..i-1)))` over the gasket places `i <= m - 1`, which is the sum over the gasket places `i <= m` since `m` is a domino place. □

Two thirds of the alphabets fall to Lemma 5.1 alone, and Lemma 5.5 is a convenience rather than a need: over a row domino and the full tile, Lemma 5.2 strips the terminal full run, Lemma 5.3 turns the count into `H` of the prefix before the last domino place, and by Lemma 5.4 a full letter doubles `H` while a row domino keeps it, so that `H = 2^(n - j)` again. Every form of the table is in this way an evaluation of the product of Theorem 5.7, whose four transfer laws are the one recursion behind all `105` alphabets. The table regroups, without changing a value, the forms the tree proves family by family: `2^(k - m)` for a unit against a domino with `k` the last unit place and `m` the unit count, `2^d` for a unit against a diagonal with `d` the diagonal count, `2^k` for a domino against a diagonal with `k` the last diagonal place, `3^(g - j)` for a gasket against a unit with `g` the gasket count and `j` its terminal run, `2^d 3^(g - j)` for a gasket against a diagonal, `4^(F - j)` and `2^d 4^(F - j)` for the full tile against a unit and a diagonal, `1` inside every class except `2^level` inside the diagonal class, each being `fill(A_(w_(1..p)))` read on that alphabet.

**Fact 6.2 (the forms against the drawing).** Every form of Theorem 6.1 is checked two ways by `lab/rs/magic-words`, against the matrix product of Theorem 5.7 and against the picture drawn cell by cell from Definition 2.2 and counted by union-find over row runs. On each of the `59` alphabets of the seven named families, all `32766` words of length at most `14` against the product and all `254` words of length at most `7` against the drawing; on the `46` alphabets of a gasket against a unit, a diagonal or a domino and of the full tile against a unit or a diagonal, `753572` words of length at most `13` against the product and `11684` of length at most `7` against the drawing; zero mismatches. The zero-contact cut of Lemma 5.1(a) is also checked where its hypothesis holds and counted where it does not: `49420` of all `54240` words of length at most four admit a suffix with both contacts zero, zero mismatches, and the other `4820` carry nonzero contact in one direction at every suffix, every word over a domino and the full tile among them, so the fourth regime is never reached by the cut and comes from Lemma 5.5 or from the run law. Domain: those words. Generator: `lab/rs/magic-words`.

**Corollary 6.3 (every count off the fifth regime is 3-smooth).** On each of the `89` alphabets outside the fifth regime every count `comp(A_w)` has the form `2^alpha 3^beta`. A gasket against a domino is the only family whose counts need a prime above `3`.

**Proof.** Read Theorem 6.1 regime by regime: a product of fills drawn from `{1, 2, 3, 4}`, the constant `1`, `2^(level - r)` and `2^(n - j)`. Only the fifth regime is a sum. □

**Fact 6.4 (a stationary control, and the one rough family).** `comp(A_((7, 3)^k)) = (6^k + 4)/5`, reading `2, 8, 44, 260, 1556, 9332` at `k = 1` to `6`, checked to `k = 8` against Theorem 6.1 and the product of Theorem 5.7; its per-letter rate is `(1/2) log 6`, from a periodic and therefore stationary word. The largest count at length `8` over the alphabet `(3, 7)` is `1094 = 2 x 547`, at the word `7, 7, 7, 7, 7, 7, 7, 3`, over all `256` words of that length. Domain: those words. Generator: `lab/rs/magic-words`.

## The exponent at an interior frequency

**Lemma 7.1 (terminal runs vanish at interior frequency).** Let `w` be an infinite word over `{a, b}` whose letter frequencies exist and are interior, and let `r(level)` be the length of the terminal run of `w_(1..level)`. Then `r(level) = o(level)`.

**Proof.** Suppose not: there are `eps > 0` and lengths `level_1 < level_2 < ...` with `r(level_i) >= eps level_i`. Passing to a subsequence, the letter `x` absent from the terminal run is the same throughout; let `N_x(level)` count its occurrences in `w_(1..level)`. Then `N_x(level_i) = N_x(M_i)` with `M_i = level_i - r(level_i) <= (1 - eps) level_i`. If `M_i` is bounded then `N_x(level_i)` is bounded and `f_x = 0`. Otherwise `N_x(M_i)/M_i -> f_x`, so `N_x(level_i)/level_i = (N_x(M_i)/M_i)(M_i/level_i) <= (f_x + o(1))(1 - eps)` while the left side tends to `f_x`; hence `f_x <= (1 - eps) f_x` and `f_x = 0`, against interiority. □

**Lemma 7.2 (the sandwich).** Over a gasket-and-domino alphabet suppose `w` carries a domino, let `m` be its last domino place, suppose a gasket occurs before `m`, and let `i*` be the last gasket place before `m` and `T = fill(A_(w_(1..i*-1)))`. Then `T < comp(A_w) <= 1 + (3/2) T`. Moreover `fill(A_w)/T = 3 * 2^alpha 3^beta` with `alpha = m - i* >= 1` and `beta = level - m >= 0`; when no three consecutive letters of `w` are equal, `fill(A_w)/T` lies in `[6, 108]` and `comp(A_w)/fill(A_w)` in `(1/108, 5/12]`.

**Proof.** By Theorem 6.1, `comp(A_w) = 1 + sum fill(A_(w_(1..i-1)))` over the gasket places `i <= m`. The largest term is the one at `i*`, namely `T`, so `comp(A_w) > T`. If `i < i'` are consecutive gasket places at or before `m` then `fill(A_(w_(1..i'-1)))/fill(A_(w_(1..i-1))) = fill(A_(w_(i..i'-1))) >= 3`, since `w_i` is a gasket and every letter has fill at least `2`; read backwards from `T` the terms shrink by factors of at least `3`, so their sum is at most `T (1 + 1/3 + 1/9 + ...) = (3/2) T`. For the second claim, the suffix `w_(i*..level)` has the shape `q p^alpha q^beta`, because `i*` is the last gasket place at or before `m` and `m` is the last domino place, so `fill(A_w)/T = 3 * 2^alpha 3^beta`; no three equal consecutive letters gives `alpha <= 2` and `beta <= 2`, whence `fill(A_w)/T` lies in `[3 * 2, 3 * 4 * 9] = [6, 108]`. Finally `1 + (3/2) T <= (5/2) T` since `T >= 1`, so `comp/fill <= (5/2) T / (6 T) = 5/12` and `comp/fill > T / (108 T) = 1/108`. □

**Theorem 7.3 (existence, order-blindness, frequency only).** Let `w` be an infinite word over one of the `105` alphabets whose letter frequencies exist and are interior. Then `chi(w)` exists, depends only on `f`, and equals `f_a eps_a + f_b eps_b` with the per-letter weights of the regime of Theorem 6.1: `eps_c = log fill(c)` for both letters in the first regime; `eps_a = eps_b = 0` in the second; `eps_a = eps_b = log 2` in the third; `eps_domino = 0` and `eps_full = log 2` in the fourth; `eps_domino = log 2` and `eps_gasket = log 3` in the fifth. In particular `chi` is the same along Thue-Morse, along every periodic word with those frequencies, along every strictly ergodic word in which both letters occur, and at almost every Bernoulli word with `0 < p < 1`.

**Proof.** Write `N_c(level)` for the count of the letter `c` in `w_(1..level)`, so `N_c(level)/level -> f_c`. First regime: `log comp(A_(w_(1..level))) = log fill(A_(w_(1..p(level))))` with `p(level)` the last contact-free place. If both letters are contact-free then `p(level) = level` and the right side is `sum_(i <= level) log fill(w_i)`, whose average tends to `f_a log fill(a) + f_b log fill(b)`. Otherwise `level - p(level)` is the terminal run of the other letter, `o(level)` by Lemma 7.1, and `log fill(A_(w_(1..p(level)))) = sum_(i <= level) log fill(w_i) - (level - p(level)) log fill(c) = sum_(i <= level) log fill(w_i) + o(level)`, the same limit. Second regime: `comp = 1` and `chi = 0`. Third: `log comp = (level - r(level)) log 2 = level log 2 + o(level)`. Fourth: `log comp = (n(level) - j(level)) log 2` with `n(level)/level -> f_full` and `j(level)` a terminal run, `o(level)`; the limit is `f_full log 2`. Fifth: let `m(level)` be the last domino place and `i*(level)` the last gasket place at or before it, both existing for large `level` since both frequencies are positive. Then `level - m(level)` is a terminal run of gaskets and `m(level) - i*(level)` is the terminal run of `w_(1..m(level))`, both `o(level)` by Lemma 7.1, since `m(level) -> infinity`. With `T(level) = fill(A_(w_(1..i*(level)-1)))`, Lemma 7.2 gives `log comp = log T(level) + O(1)`, and `log T(level) = sum_(i < i*(level)) log fill(w_i) = sum_(i <= level) log fill(w_i) - O(level - i*(level)) = level (f_p log 2 + f_q log 3) + o(level)`, each `log fill(w_i)` being at most `log 3`. Dividing by `level` gives the claim in all five cases, and in each the limit is a function of `f` alone. The final sentence: Thue-Morse has both frequencies `1/2`; a periodic word and a strictly ergodic word carrying both letters have frequencies, interior by minimality in the second case; a Bernoulli word has frequencies `(p, 1 - p)` almost surely by the strong law. □

**Theorem 7.4 (saturation).** At every interior frequency the component exponent equals the fill exponent on `89` of the `105` alphabets and falls strictly below it on the other `16`, the `12` of the second regime and the `4` of the fourth. Among the `16`, exactly the `4` of the fourth regime, a domino against the full tile, have `Phi(f) < chi < fill exponent`; at equal frequencies that exponent is `(1/2) log 2` against `Phi = 0` and a fill exponent of `(3/2) log 2`. Up to transposition this is the one class pair with that property.

**Theorem 7.5 (the constant-word functional is refuted).** At every interior frequency `Phi` is exact on `27` of the `105` alphabets, the `15` whose two letters are units or diagonals and the `12` of the second regime, and refuted on the other `78`. On each of the `78` the obstruction sits at a vertex rather than in the interior: the interior formula is itself linear, so some linear functional matches `chi` there, but it extends continuously to the closed simplex and at some vertex takes a positive value, while the constant word at that vertex has count `1` and rate `0`. Hence on those alphabets no continuous functional of `f` alone gives the rate of every word whose letter frequencies and rate both exist.

**Proof of Theorems 7.4 and 7.5.** Write `phi_c = log 2` for a diagonal and `0` otherwise, so that `Phi(f) = f_a phi_a + f_b phi_b`, and `eps_c` for the weight of Theorem 7.3. Since one frequency is free on the open simplex, `chi` equals the fill exponent at every interior `f` exactly when `eps_c = log fill(c)` for both letters, and `Phi` is exact at every interior `f` exactly when `eps_c = phi_c` for both. First regime, `69` alphabets: `eps_c = log fill(c)`, so all saturate; `eps_c = phi_c` needs `log fill(c) = phi_c`, true for a unit and a diagonal and false for a domino, a gasket and the full tile, so `Phi` is exact on the `C(6, 2) = 15` alphabets of units and diagonals and refuted on `54`. Second regime, `12`: `eps = 0` while every fill is at least `2`, so none saturates; no letter is a diagonal, so `Phi` is exact on all `12`. Third, `4`: `eps = log 2 = log fill`, all saturate, `phi = 0`, all refuted. Fourth, `4`: `eps_domino = 0 != log 2`, none saturates; `eps_full = log 2 != 0`, all refuted. Fifth, `16`: `eps = log fill`, all saturate; `phi = 0 != eps`, all refuted. Adding: saturation on `69 + 4 + 16 = 89`, failure on `12 + 4 = 16`; `Phi` exact on `15 + 12 = 27`, refuted on `54 + 4 + 4 + 16 = 78`. An alphabet has `Phi < chi < fill exponent` at interior `f` exactly when saturation fails and `Phi` is refuted, since otherwise one inequality is an equality; saturation fails only in the second and fourth regimes and `Phi` is exact throughout the second, so only the fourth survives, the `4` alphabets of a domino against the full tile, one class pair up to transposition; at `f = (1/2, 1/2)` its exponent is `(1/2) log 2` against `Phi = 0` and `(1/2) log 2 + (1/2) log 4 = (3/2) log 2`. For the wrong shape, pick on each of the `78` a vertex at which the interior formula is positive: in the first regime with a refuting letter `c`, the fill exponent reads `log fill(c) > 0` at the vertex of `c`; in the third it reads `log 2` at both vertices; in the fourth `log 2` at the full-tile vertex; in the fifth `log 2` or `log 3`. The constant word at that vertex has rate `0` by Lemma 5.6, its letter being no diagonal in any case listed. A continuous functional agreeing with `chi` on the open simplex agrees with the linear interior formula there and so, by continuity, at the vertex, where it is positive and misses the constant word. □

By Theorem 7.3, at an interior frequency `chi` is a function of `f` alone on every one of the `105` alphabets. So Thue-Morse returns exactly what a periodic word of the same frequencies returns and what almost every Bernoulli word with those frequencies returns, and every one of those stationary words misses `Phi` by the same amount. What a gap against `Phi` refutes is the frequency functional, not stationarity; no aperiodic word witnesses anything here, and this retires the conjecture that the component exponent is where a level-varying construction stops being a frequency average. The caveat travels with the result: on `89` of the `105` the rate carries nothing the order-blind fill law did not already give, and the whole content there is in the finite-length count, not the rate.

Let `t_n` be the Thue-Morse sequence, the parity of the binary digit sum of `n` ([OEIS A010060](https://oeis.org/A010060)), and read a word over a gasket-and-domino alphabet off it by sending `t_(i-1) = 0` to one letter and `t_(i-1) = 1` to the other at place `i`. There are two readings, and they give different words; the figure draws both at length six.

**Theorem 7.6 (the Thue-Morse value).** Over each of the `16` gasket-and-domino alphabets, and under either reading, the Thue-Morse word has `chi = (1/2) log 6`, with the two-sided certificate

```
| log comp(A_(w_(1..level))) - (level/2) log 6 | <= log 108 + (1/2) log (3/2) < 4.885    for every level >= 4 .
```

In nats the value is `0.8958797346140275`, which the study prints truncated as `0.895879734614027`, and in units of `log 2` it is `1.292481250360578`, floats naming an exact constant.

**Proof.** Two properties of `t` do all the work. No three consecutive terms are equal: among three consecutive indices there is an even index `2n` whose successor `2n + 1` is among them, and `t_(2n) = t_n` while `t_(2n+1) = 1 - t_n`. The letter counts are balanced: the prefix of length `level` reads the indices `0` to `level - 1`, and pairing them as `(2k, 2k + 1)` each pair contributes one of each letter, so `|N_q(level) - level/2| <= 1/2` for the gasket count `N_q`. Fix an alphabet, a reading and `level >= 4`, which is what it takes for a gasket place at or before a domino place to occur in both readings, so that `m` and `i*` of Lemma 7.2 are defined; no lower bound on `T` is needed, and none is available, the prefix `q p p q` having `i* = 1` and `T = 1`. By Lemma 7.2 and the first property, `comp/fill` lies in `(1/108, 5/12]`, so `|log comp - log fill| <= log 108`. By the second property, with `N_p = level - N_q` the domino count, `|log fill - (level/2) log 6| = |(N_p - level/2) log 2 + (N_q - level/2) log 3| = |N_q - level/2| log (3/2) <= (1/2) log (3/2)`. Adding the two gives the certificate, and `log 108 + (1/2) log (3/2) = 4.884863...`. Dividing by `level` gives the limit. Nothing in the argument names the alphabet beyond the fills `3` and `2`, so it covers all `16` alphabets and both readings. □

**Fact 7.7 (the certificate, measured).** Along Thue-Morse under both readings on all `16` alphabets at every `4 <= level <= 2^14`, the largest deviation `|log comp - (level/2) log 6|` is `4.273459` nats against the certificate constant `4.884864`; the prefix rate in units of `log 2` reads `1.291967463826` at `level = 4096` and `1.292352803727` at `level = 2^14` under one reading, `1.291291597694` and `1.292183837194` under the other, against the limit `1.292481250360578`, all floats. The run structure behind the proof is read to `level = 2^20`: the longest terminal run is `2`, the letter counts are exactly equal at every even length, the terminal run of one letter takes the value `0` on `524288` prefixes, `1` on `349526` and `2` on `174762`, and the run-boundary word `t_n xor t_(n+1)` is the period-doubling word on all `1048575` terms. Domain: those lengths. Generator: `lab/rs/magic-words`.

**Fact 7.8 (the saturation ratio over one finite sweep).** This is the result easiest to overstate: the exponents agree with the fill exponent, the counts do not. Along Thue-Morse over `(3, 7)` and over `1 <= level <= 2^14`, the ratio `comp/fill` takes its smallest value `0.0113766545`, above the floor `1/108 = 0.009259...` of Lemma 7.2, and its largest value at `level >= 5` is the exact rational `43397/186624`, at `level = 16`, under one reading and `151/648`, at `level = 8`, under the other. The cutoff is not cosmetic: at `level = 4` one reading gives `1/4`, and under that reading the sweep holds one length above `5/12`, below `4`, which the certificate excludes. At `level = 4096` the ratio is `0.2325367033` under one reading and `0.0341299634` under the other, neither the largest nor the smallest value of the sweep. Nothing here is a claim about the limit; Conjecture 9.2 is what the paper says about that. Domain: those lengths. Generator: `lab/rs/magic-words`.

**Theorem 7.9 (the interior hypothesis is sharp).** Fix the alphabet `{3, 6}` and the frequency vector `f = (1, 0)`. The constant word `3^infinity` has `chi = 0`; the word carrying the letter `6` exactly at the square places has `chi = log 2`; the word carrying `6` exactly at the powers of `2` has upper rate `log 2`, lower rate `(1/2) log 2` and no rate, its prefix rates accumulating on the whole interval `[(1/2) log 2, log 2]`. The same three statements hold over `{3, 7}`, which carries no diagonal letter, with `7` in place of `6`, so the pathology is not a property of the diagonal class. The orbit closure of each of the three words is countable and uniquely ergodic, and for the two non-constant words it is not minimal; no minimal word is implicated. Hence no statement of Theorem 7.3 survives dropping the interior hypothesis.

**Proof.** Over `{3, 6}` the letter `6` is contact-free and `3` is not, so the first regime of Theorem 6.1 gives `comp(A_(w_(1..level))) = fill(A_(w_(1..p(level)))) = 2^(p(level))` with `p(level)` the last place of the prefix carrying `6`, both letters having fill `2`; in each of the three words the letter `6` occurs on a set of density `0`, so `f = (1, 0)`. For the constant word `p = 0` and `comp = 1`. For the squares word `p(level) = floor(sqrt(level))^2`, and for `level` in `[n^2, (n + 1)^2)` the ratio `p/level` is at least `n^2/((n + 1)^2 - 1)`, which tends to `1`, so `chi = log 2`. For the powers-of-`2` word `p(level) = 2^(floor(log_2 level))`, so the prefix rate in units of `log 2` is `2^k/level` for `level` in `[2^k, 2^(k+1))`, equal to `1` at `level = 2^k` and decreasing to just above `1/2` at `level = 2^(k+1) - 1` in steps of size at most `1/level`; every point of `[1/2, 1]` is within `1/level` of a value taken in the `k`-th block, so the accumulation set of the prefix rates is exactly `[1/2, 1]` in those units, the upper rate is `log 2`, the lower rate `(1/2) log 2`, and the limit does not exist. Over `{3, 7}` the fifth regime applies. For the word carrying `7` exactly at the powers of `2` and `level` in `(2^k, 2^(k+1)]`, the last domino place `m` is `level`, or `level - 1` when `level` is a power of `2`, and the last gasket place at or before `m` is `i* = 2^k`; so `T = fill(A_(w_(1..2^k - 1))) = 2^(2^k - 1 - k) 3^k`, Lemma 7.2 gives `log_2 comp = 2^k + O(k)` throughout the block, the prefix rate is `2^k/level + O(k/level)`, and the argument above applies. The constant word `3^infinity` carries no gasket and has `comp = 1`; the squares word has `log_2 comp = floor(sqrt(level))^2 + O(sqrt(level))` by the same sandwich, hence rate `log 2`. For the orbit closures: in each of the two non-constant words the gaps between consecutive non-constant letters tend to infinity, so every limit point of the shift orbit carries at most one non-constant letter, and the closure is the orbit together with those countably many limit words and the constant word, hence countable. An invariant probability measure on a countable set is atomic, the forward image of an atom of largest mass is an atom of at least that mass, so the forward orbit of such an atom is finite and is a periodic orbit, and the only periodic point in the closure is the constant word, a fixed point; so the point mass there is the only invariant measure and each closure is uniquely ergodic. For the two non-constant words the closure properly contains that fixed point and is not minimal; the constant word is a fixed point and is minimal. □

**Fact 7.10 (the boundary rates, measured).** For the powers-of-`2` word over `(3, 7)` the prefix rate in units of `log 2` reads `0.502367981` at `level = 2048`, `1.002164269` at `level = 2049` and `0.500219405` at `level = 32768`, and over the single block `4097 <= level <= 8192` it decreases from `1.00123` to `0.50073`, which is the interval of Theorem 7.9 visible in one sweep. For the same word over `(3, 6)` the prefix rate is exactly `2^k/(2^(k+1) - 1)` at `level = 2^(k+1) - 1`, printing `4/7, 8/15, ..., 8192/16383`, and exactly `1` at every `level = 2^k`; the squares word has prefix rate `1` at every `level = n^2` and `n/(n + 2)` at `level = (n + 1)^2 - 1`. Domain: those lengths. Generator: `lab/rs/magic-words`.

A headline that does not exist, said so that it is not written later: the component exponent does not fail to exist along Thue-Morse. It converges, exactly, by Theorem 7.6. The genuine non-existence witness is the third word of Theorem 7.9, which sits at a boundary frequency and whose orbit closure is not minimal.

## What no norm can see

**Theorem 8.1 (the matrix-norm exponent is a different number).** (i) In the frame of Theorem 5.7 every `T_c` is lower triangular with diagonal `([c heavy], h(c), v(c), fill(c))`, so its spectral radius is `fill(c)`; along the constant word `c^infinity` the matrix-norm exponent `lim (1/level) log ||T_c^level||` is `log fill(c)` in every matrix norm, while `chi(c^infinity) = log comp(A_c)` is `0` for the thirteen codes outside the diagonal class. (ii) For every non-empty subfamily `F` of the letter matrices the joint spectral radius is `max_(c in F) fill(c)` and the lower spectral radius is `min_(c in F) fill(c)`, both attained by one-letter words. (iii) Hence on the `78` alphabets whose two fills differ, `log JSR` exceeds `chi` at every interior frequency; on the `27` alphabets of one fill, `log JSR = log fill` equals `chi` on the `19` that saturate and exceeds `chi = 0` on the `2` pairs of like dominoes and the `6` pairs of gaskets. So an argument returning a top Lyapunov exponent ([Furstenberg and Kesten 1960](../refs.md)) or a joint spectral radius ([Rota and Strang 1960](../refs.md), [Jungers 2009](../refs.md)) returns `log fill` or `log max fill`, not `chi`: it answers a different question.

**Proof.** (i) In the table of Theorem 5.7 the new component coordinate uses the old components only for a heavy letter, the new `H` uses only `H` and the fill, the new `V` only `V` and the fill, and the new fill only the fill, so in the order `(comp, H, V, fill)` each `T_c` is lower triangular, and its diagonal entries are the coefficient of `comp` in the new `comp`, which is `1` for a heavy letter and `0` otherwise, `h(c)`, `v(c)` and `fill(c)`. Since `fill(c) >= max(1, h(c), v(c))`, a row with both end cells filled having two cells, the spectral radius is `fill(c)`, and Gelfand's formula gives the norm exponent `log fill(c)` in any norm. `chi(c^infinity) = log comp(A_c)` by Lemma 5.6, which is `0` unless `c` is a diagonal. Explicitly, a row domino sends `(comp, H, V, fill)` to `(H, H, 2 fill, 2 fill)`, so `(1, 1, 1, 1) T_3^level = (1, 1, 2^level, 2^level)`: one full row of `2^level` cells, one component, `2^level` vertical runs, and a matrix whose powers grow like `2^level`. (ii) For any submultiplicative norm, `max_c rho(T_c) <= JSR(F) <= max_c ||T_c||`. Every `T_c` has non-negative entries and its column sums are `(comp(A_c), r(c), s(c), fill(c))`, each at most `fill(c)`, so in the norm that takes the largest column sum `||T_c|| = fill(c)`, and with `rho(T_c) = fill(c)` the bracket closes: `JSR(F) = max fill`, attained by the constant word at a fullest letter. For the lower spectral radius, `T_w` is lower triangular with last diagonal entry `prod_t fill(c_t) >= (min fill)^level`, so `||T_w|| >= rho(T_w) >= (min fill)^level` in any norm, and the constant word at an emptiest letter attains it. (iii) By Theorem 7.3, `chi` is at most the fill exponent `f_a log fill(a) + f_b log fill(b)`, which is strictly below `log max fill` at every interior `f` when the fills differ. When they agree the fill exponent is `log fill`, which `chi` reaches exactly on the saturating alphabets of Theorem 7.4; the one-fill alphabets are the `6` pairs of units, the `15` pairs among dominoes and diagonals and the `6` pairs of gaskets, and of these only the `2` like-domino pairs and the `6` gasket pairs lie in the second regime. □

**Fact 8.2 (the same obstruction in the Hankel basis).** In the representation built on the basis words `e, 3, 5, 15`, with `lambda = (1, 0, 0, 0)` and `gamma = (1, 1, 1, 1)^T`, the largest entry of `M_3^level` is `2^(level + 2) - 2`, reading `6, 14, 62, 1022, 262142, 17179869182` at `level = 1, 2, 4, 8, 16, 32`, while `comp(A_(3^level)) = 1` at every `level` to `32`; neither `M_3` nor `M_7` is a non-negative matrix, `M_3` carrying `2` negative entries and `M_7` four, and none of the `8190` products of `M_3` and `M_7` of length one to twelve is entrywise positive, so entrywise positivity in that basis is the wrong test for the invariant cone that the frame of Theorem 5.7 exhibits. Domain: those powers and products. Generator: `lab/rs/magic-words`.

**Fact 8.3 (the bracket closes at length one).** On the alphabets `{3, 6}` and `{3, 7}` an exhaustive scan of all `2^level` words to `level = 16` finds the largest spectral radius of a product to be exactly `2^level` and `3^level`, so the scan's own bracket on the joint spectral radius reads `[2.000000000, 2.000000000]` and `[3.000000000, 3.000000000]` at every one of the sixteen lengths; on `{3, 6}` every word ties, its spectral radius being `2^level`, since both matrices preserve the line through `(1, 2)^T` in the leading `2 x 2` block and act on it by `2`. The Kronecker lifting of Blondel and Nesterov reads exactly `(fill_a^k + fill_b^k)^(1/k)` above, by triangularity of Kronecker powers, `2.828427125, 2.378414231, 2.244924097` on `{3, 6}` and `3.605551276, 3.138288993, 3.042371177` on `{3, 7}` at `k = 2, 4, 6`, strictly above the answer at every finite `k`. Domain: those two alphabets to `level = 16` and `k <= 6`. Generator: `lab/py/jsr-schedules`, with the lifting bound at source in [Blondel and Nesterov 2005](../refs.md).

The route by which these rates were expected to come was ergodic: a common invariant cone, a contraction along a uniquely ergodic driving word, the limit read off the contraction. The cone exists, in the chart normalised by the common right eigenvector `(1, 2, 2, 4)^T` of the fill functional in the Hankel basis `e, 3, 5, 15` of Fact 8.2, which is `e_4` in the frame of Theorem 5.7, and none of it is used above, because the observable `comp` is a forward orbit `(1, 1, 1, 1) T_w e_1^T`, not the nested family a projective contraction compresses; `log comp` is neither subadditive nor a norm, and by Theorem 8.1 a norm theorem returns `log fill` where the answer is `0`. The hypothesis in force throughout Section 7 is that both letter frequencies exist and are positive, which linear recurrence and strict ergodicity with both letters present imply ([Berthe and Delecroix 2014](../refs.md)), and which neither bounded gaps nor unique ergodicity alone implies: bounded gaps between the letters do not force the frequencies to exist, and the words of Theorem 7.9 are uniquely ergodic with a zero frequency.

## Open problems

The paper stops in four places, and each is a real gap. Everything used to state them is defined above.

**Conjecture 9.1 (words with no letter frequencies).** Positive lower density of both letters is not enough for `chi` to exist. Over `(3, 7)` the tripling word `W_(k+1) = W_k 7^|W_k| 3^|W_k|` with seed `W_1 = (3, 7)` keeps both letters at lower density at least `1/4`, and its prefix rate ranges over `[0.4792, 1.4379]` in units of `log 2` on `1024 <= level <= 4096` with no narrowing (`lab/rs/magic-words`, floats). The closed form is exact there; the non-existence of the limit is measurement, not proof, and which words without letter frequencies carry a component exponent is open. The right hypothesis is unknown: Theorem 7.9 kills frequency alone, this word kills positive lower density, and minimality over `{3, 6}` restores the limit, since bounded gaps between diagonal letters force `p(level)/level -> 1`.

**Conjecture 9.2 (the accumulation set of the saturation).** Whether `comp/fill` converges at all along Thue-Morse over a gasket-and-domino alphabet is open; Fact 7.8 is that it does not settle over `1 <= level <= 2^14`, its values spreading over more than a factor of twenty while Lemma 7.2 traps every value at `level >= 4` in `(1/108, 5/12]`. The accumulation set is unidentified and is plausibly the attractor of the two affine maps that the two letters induce on the chart `(b, c) = ((fill - H)/fill, (fill - V)/fill)`, in which `comp/fill = 1 - b - c` on these alphabets, `(b, c) -> ((1 + b)/3, (1 + c)/3)` for the gasket and `(b, c) -> ((1 + b)/2, 0)` for a row domino, read along the word; this is a question about an attractor, not about rates.

**Conjecture 9.3 (where a non-stationary effect could still live).** Nothing here speaks about alphabets of three or more letters, about the exponents of the Euler characteristic, the boundary and the hole counts, whose representations have the larger truncated ranks `4`, `8` and `11` of Fact 5.8, or about bases above `2`, where the alphabet is `2^(base^2)` designs and the two contact counts become contact profiles, so that neither the zero-contact cut nor the run law has been tried. Whether any of these makes the exponent depend on more than the letter frequencies, which is what a genuinely non-stationary result would need, is open; at an interior frequency on two letters it provably does not, on any of the `105` alphabets, and a periodic word carries no new theory at any alphabet by Corollary 4.6.

**Conjecture 9.4 (the other three series).** The four transfer laws behind Theorem 5.7 reproduce every form of Theorem 6.1, so the component table does collapse to one recursion. Whether the Euler, boundary and hole series of Fact 5.8 answer to a transfer law of the same kind, which would prove their representations as Theorem 5.7 proves the component one and would explain the ranks `8` and `11`, is open; the obstruction is that a larger tile's copies can merge along a cycle rather than through one contact, and then the new count need not be linear in the old frame.

## Reproducibility

Every count in this paper is printed by one of two studies of the tree, each run from the repository root, each printing only and writing nothing, each exiting nonzero if a structural law, a closed form, a bound or a cross-check fails. `cargo run --release --manifest-path research/connectivity/lab/rs/magic-words/Cargo.toml` runs in about five seconds and holds one `1024 x 1024` raster at a time; it prints Fact 4.7 with its denominators and the uniqueness of the ten-code library among the `3003` ten-code subsets, the sweeps behind Corollary 3.2, Lemma 3.3 and Theorem 4.1 on all `3375` words of length three and all `256` code pairs, Theorems 4.2 to 4.4 on their finite domains, the six periodic cases of Corollary 4.6 cell for cell against `mrly::core::Tensor::fractal` of the composite tile, the Hankel elimination and the four ranks of Fact 5.8 with the representation checked on `54360` words, the growth families and the exhaustive maxima of Theorem 5.9 and Proposition 5.10 to `level = 10`, the `14` of `15` commuting failures of Proposition 5.11, every form of Theorem 6.1 against the product and against the drawing on the domains of Fact 6.2, the zero-contact cut on `49420` of `54240` words, the ledger of Theorems 7.4 and 7.5 re-derived pair by pair from the forms, the Thue-Morse run structure, certificate and prefix rates of Fact 7.7, the saturation sweep of Fact 7.8, the boundary words of Fact 7.10, the stationary control and the count `1094` of Fact 6.4, the tripling word of Conjecture 9.1, the matrix powers and products of Fact 8.2, and the sandwich `comp/T` in `[1.0014, 2.0000]` on six named words. It draws every word twice, inside the study and through `mrly::math::bang::magic`, and the two agree cell for cell on all `3375` words of length three. `uv run python research/connectivity/lab/py/jsr-schedules/jsr.py` runs in about nine seconds; it rebuilds the representation by the same elimination, reads the frame of Theorem 5.7 off the basis words, asserts every frame matrix lower triangular with the diagonals and column sums of Theorem 8.1, checks the transfer laws against drawn cells on `3856` words, and prints the scan and the lifting of Fact 8.3. The crate `mrly`, module `math::bang::word`, counts components, fills and runs of any word (`components`, `fills`, `prefixes`) and spells Thue-Morse readings (`spell`, `thue_morse`), and `math::bang::magic` draws the picture; the figure `figures/paper-kronecker-word-order.ts` builds the two readings of length six over `(7, 3)` with those functions, labels the pieces by its own flood fill, and asserts `216` cells and `14` and `45` pieces against `mrly::math::bang::word::components`, in a few milliseconds a theme. The claims behind every Theorem and Fact are the rows of [order sensitivity](../claims/order-sensitivity.md), [the component exponent](../claims/component-exponent.md) and [the cocycle's joint spectral radius](../claims/cocycle-joint-spectral-radius.md) in this topic and [Kronecker word order](../../magic/claims/kronecker-word-order.md) in the magic topic; the notes [order in the mixed product](../notes/order-in-the-mixed-product.md) and [word order, closed and bounded](../notes/word-order-closure-and-spectral-radius.md) carry the same results in the order they were found, and [magic words](../../magic/notes/magic.md) the grammar of the words.
