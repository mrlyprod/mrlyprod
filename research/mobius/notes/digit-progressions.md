## The digit progressions

The half interval and the even digits are two cells of one family: a digit progression `P = {u, u + r, .., u + (L-1) r}` inside `{0..base-1}`, of length `L >= 2` and step `r >= 1`, an interval at `r = 1`. The absolute transform of a progression forgets its start and reads its step only through a dilation, so every member of length `L` shares the transfer operator of one interval; the step decides which denominators keep digit-side decay, and the start decides whether `0` is a digit. This section proves the Mobius bound on every progression that carries a certificate below `1/5`, gives every interval a certificate in closed form, sharpens it at the half lengths of both parities, and names the denominator class each step loses with what pays for it. Every number below is printed by [lab/py/digit-progressions](../lab/py/digit-progressions/).

- **The family and its transform. Proved.** `hat P(t) = e(u t) hat I_L(r t)` with `I_L = {0..L-1}` and `|hat I_L(t)| = |D_L(t)| = |sin(pi L t)/sin(pi t)|`, so `|hat P_i(t)| = |hat I_(L,i)(r t)|` at every level, `I_(L,i)` the `i`-digit strings of `I_L`. Write `r = r_1 r_0` with `r_1` dividing a power of `base` and `gcd(r_0, base) = 1`. With `y = base^i` and `g_i = gcd(r, y)`, the map `a -> r a mod y` covers the multiples of `g_i`, each `g_i` times, so `sum_(a < y) |hat P_i(s + a/y)| = g_i sum_(c < y/g_i) |hat I_(L,i)(r s + c g_i/y)| <= r_1 Sigma^L_i(r s)`, `Sigma^L_i` the grid sum of `I_L`, with equality and no factor at `r_1 = 1`. So a certificate `(C, alpha_1)` of `I_L` is a certificate `(r_1 C, alpha_1)` of every progression of length `L` and step `r`, whatever its start: its exponent is a function of `(L, base)` alone, its constant carries `r_1`. The half interval is `I_fill`; the even digits at odd base are `r = 2`, `L = fill`, the transfer bullet of the section above; the odd digits `O = {1, 3, .., base-2}` at odd base are `u = 1`, `r = 2`, `L = fill - 1 = (base-1)/2`; at even base the half interval `H = {0..base/2 - 1}` is `I_(base/2)`, and the even and odd digits are `u = 0` and `u = 1` with `r = r_1 = 2` and `L = base/2`, certificate `(2C, alpha_1)`.
- **The denominators a step loses. Proved.** At a residue with data `a/y = l/d + eta`, write `d = d_1 d_0` with `d_1` dividing a power of `base` and `gcd(d_0, base) = 1`. Then `r a/y = r l/d + r eta`, and `r l/d` in lowest terms has denominator `d' = d/gcd(d, r)`, whose part prime to `base` is `d_0/gcd(d_0, r_0)`. If `d_0` does not divide `r_0`, Lemma A' of [coprime](../../coprime/notes/coprime.md) for the interval `I_L`, which keeps the pair `{0, 1}`, read at `l'/d' + r eta`, gives `|hat P_k(a/y)| <= L^k c'^(floor(2k/(3 m_(d'))))` with `c' = c'(base, L)` whenever `|r eta| < y^(-2/3)/(4 base (base - 1))`, and `m_(d') <= m_d`. If `d_0` divides `r_0`, Lemma A' gives nothing: at the pure `d = g > 1` dividing `r_0` the number `r l/g` is an integer and `|hat P_k(l/g + eta)| = |hat I_(L,k)(r eta)|`, the full mass `L^k` at the point itself at every level, while on the mixed class `d = g e` the transform may keep mass or vanish outright, as at base `10`, `P = {0, 3, 6, 9}` and `d = 6`, where `r l/6 = 1/2 mod 1` at every `l` prime to `6` and `D_4(1/2) = 0`. So the class a step can lose is `d = g e` with `g > 1` dividing `r_0` and `e` dividing a power of `base`: the even digits' `d = 2e` at `r_0 = 2`, `d = 3e` at a step `3` prime to `base`, nothing when `r` divides a power of `base`, and no other class at any step; a dilation by a unit mod `base` can lose only the denominators whose part prime to `base` divides it, and the region below pays that whole class with no decay asked of it. What pays is the arithmetic at `base r_0`. Put those `d < Z` with `h < Z` into region C2'': partial summation of `e(n eta)` along the block gives `|S_P(a/y)| <= 16 Z max_(u <= x, r') |M(u; d, r')|` as at `d = 2e` above, whether or not `d` divides `y`, and there are at most `tau(r_0) N_Z` such `d`. The proof of (M) in region C2 reads `base` only through the finite set of its primes: run with `rad(base r_0)` in place of `rad(base)`, it gives (M) at every `d` dividing a power of `base r_0` with `d <= exp(sqrt(log x)/2)`, any exceptional zero sitting on a real primitive character of conductor dividing `8 rad(base r_0)`, a finite family fixed by `base` and `r`, so Siegel's theorem is never invoked. At `r_0 = 2` the arithmetic bullet of the section above gives the same (M) by reduction to `base`.
- **The theorem on a progression. Proved.** Let `P` be a progression of length `L >= 2` and step `r` whose interval `I_L` carries a shifted-grid certificate below `1/5`. Then there are `C, c > 0` computable from `base` and `P` with `|M_P(x)| <= C A_P(x) exp(-c sqrt(log x))` at every `x >= 2`. The dissection runs as on the even digits with the re-cut above: regions A and B read the certificate `(r_1 C, alpha_1)` of the family bullet and `|g_P'| <= pi base (base - 1)`, which holds at every digit set; C1' is Lemma A' through the dilation, its hypothesis holding once `4 r base^2 Z <= y^(1/3)`, with `c'(base, L)` carried into the assembly; C2'' is the bullet above, and costs nothing the assembly does not absorb. The digit `0` is the one other input a progression may lack: at `u >= 1` the design is not nested by level, `D_k` is not `S_P` below `base^k`, and the blocks bullet of the dissection already takes the shorter blocks `D_l` with the mass floor from the strings one digit shorter than `x`; no other step reads `0`. When moreover `r_0 = 1`, as at every interval, region C1 decays at every denominator it holds, and the prime count of [coprime](../../coprime/notes/coprime.md), whose only use of the pair is Lemma A' in region C1, runs on `P` with `kappa_P = (base/phi(base)) #{f in P : gcd(f, base) = 1}/L`.
- **One certificate for every interval. Proved.** At every base and every length `2 <= L <= base - 1`, `G_L(t) = sum_(r < base) |D_L((t + r)/base)|` is at most `Lambda(L, base) = 2L + (2 base/pi) H_(floor(base/2)) + (1 - 2/pi)(base^2 + 1)/(2 base)` at every `t`, `H_n` the harmonic number, so `Sigma^L_i(s) <= Lambda(L, base)^i` at every `i` and every real `s`, the transfer operator read at `phi = 1`: a certificate with `C = 1` and `base^(alpha_1) = Lambda(L, base)/L`. Proof. The points `(t + r)/base` sit at distance `delta` from `Z` with `base delta` in `{t, 1 + t, ..}` and `{1 - t, 2 - t, ..}`, each at most `base/2`. The two with `base delta < 1` weigh at most `L` each. Every other weighs at most `csc(pi delta) <= 1/(pi delta) + 2(1 - 2/pi) delta`, the chord of the convex `csc z - 1/z` on `(0, pi/2]`: the low ones `t + r`, `1 <= r <= floor(base/2)`, give `1/(pi delta) <= base/(pi r)`, the high ones `r' - t`, `2 <= r' <= floor(base/2) + 1`, give `base/(pi (r' - 1))`, and the sum of every `delta` is at most `(base^2 + 1)/(4 base)`. □ The numerator is read only as `1`, so the bound is uniform in `L` and blind to the start. At `L >= c base` it gives `base^(alpha_1) <= 2 + K(base)/c` with `K(base) = (2/pi)(log(base/2) + gamma + 1/base) + (1 - 2/pi)(base^2 + 1)/(2 base^2)`, by `H_n <= log n + gamma + 1/(2n)`. So the theorem above holds on every interval of length at least `c base` and every progression of that length at every base from `B(c)`, the least base from which `2 + K(base)/c < base^(1/5)`: `B(9/10) = 121872`, `B(3/4) = 377657`, `B(1/2) = 4980971`, `B(1/3) = 66917470`, `B(1/4) = 417306678`, `B(1/5) = 1705120674`, `B(1/10) = 125105365918`, each certified at 120 bits at `B(c)`, the smoothed bound failing at `B(c) - 1`, a half line since the gap increases from `(10/(pi c))^5` on (verb `wall`); `B(c)` is the wall of the smoothed `K` and not of `Lambda` itself, which can clear at isolated bases below it; the prime count with `kappa_P` holds there on every interval and every progression whose step divides a power of `base`. The numerator is where the bound is loose: at base `10007` the one-step reading `max G_L/L` is `9.4837` at `L = 5004` and `13.1938` at `L = 3336`, against `Lambda/L = 13.9424` and `19.9137`, and the half interval's certificate reads `8.4640` at `L = 5004` and the odd digits' bullet below `8.4654` at `L = 5003` (verb `census`).

- **The primes on a progression. Conjecture.** When `r_0 > 1` the lost class carries a main term for `Lambda`, as `d = 2` does on the even digits, and the prime count of [coprime](../../coprime/notes/coprime.md) does not run until that term is written. The conjectured count is `sum_(n in S_P, n <= x) Lambda(n) = sum_(n in S_P, n <= x) kappa_P(len(n)) + O(A_P(x) exp(-c sqrt(log x)))` with `kappa_P(len) = kappa_P prod_(p | r_0) (p/(p - 1)) 1_(p does not divide u R_len)`, which on the odd digits at odd base is `2 base/(base - 1)` on the members of odd length and `0` on the others.
