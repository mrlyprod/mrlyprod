Fix a base `b >= 2` and a digit set `F` inside `{0, .., b - 1}` with `0 in F`, and write `fill = card F`. The design `D` is the set of integers whose base-`b` digits all lie in `F`, and its level `L`, written `D_L`, holds the `fill^L` integers below `b^L` with all `L` digits in `F`, leading zeros included; since `0 in F`, `D_L` sits inside `D_(L+1)` and `D` is the union of its levels. A three-term progression is a solution of `x + z = 2y` with `x != z`, and `D` is progression-free when it holds none. The question is which designs are progression-free and how large `fill` gets at a given base; the familiar answer keeps every digit below `b/2`, so that adding two digits never carries, and takes `F` progression-free inside `{0, .., ceil(b/2) - 1}`. This page proves that the whole question is a question about carries, reads the answer off a three-state graph, runs the census to base `40` against brute force, and writes the one door it sees.

## Against four baselines

- **The question.** For which `N` does a digit design exceed four baselines counted exactly at that `N`: the greedy base-`3` set (the Stanley sequence), the best Salem-Spencer digit class, the best Behrend shell, and `r_3(N)` where known, [A065825](https://oeis.org/A065825) read as exact to `N = 209`. The designs are `F^L` at `N = b^L <= 10^12`, `b <= 40`, `F` a largest set of the census. Generator [lab/py/four-baselines](../lab/py/four-baselines/), `70` seconds; Behrend and greedy counts at the winner are recounted by a second method.
- **A fixed digit sum is not progression-free. Verified.** In base `5` with digits below `3`, `2, 6, 10` all have digit sum `2` and `2 + 10 = 2 * 6`. A fixed digit multiset is progression-free, since it fixes the square sum, and sits inside a Behrend shell, so the Salem-Spencer column never decides a winner; its attribution is the standard one, the source unread.
- **One winner. Verified.** Of `332` values of `N`, the design beats all four only at `37^7`, where `r_3(N)` is unknown and only greedy, Behrend and Salem-Spencer bind; at the `21` values `N = 3^L` at ratio `1` the design is the greedy set and ties.

- **The winning interval. Verified.** The level-`7` set lies in `[0, 92294880545]`, so it beats greedy and Behrend at every `N` in `[92294880546, 98807184868]`, `6512304323` values, the upper end the `10^7`-th Stanley term, while `r_3(N)` is known only to `N = 209`; the interval holds `3^23`, where greedy has `8388608`.
- **Truncation wins more. Verified.** Over every maximiser of every base `<= 40` that is a cyclic lift, `172` further table rows have a design cut to `N` above the four: base `37` at `150` rows, `39` at `14`, `35` at `7`, `25` at `1`; the lead over greedy reaches `1.5259` at `N = 3^17` (`200000` against `131072`, base `37`, `F = {0, 1, 4, 5, 11, 13, 14, 16, 20, 30}`), greedy sitting at the foot of its staircase at powers of `3`. Maximisers with a carry edge are not enumerated, so the counts are lower bounds. The strict reading `N = b^L` keeps `37^7` alone.
- **Elkin.** The design stands `2.824` over the best Behrend shell; Elkin's improvement of Behrend gains a factor that grows with `N`, in a theorem whose constant is unread, so whether the shell times that factor passes the design is not decided here, and greedy, which the design leads by `1.122`, is untouched by any such factor.
- **Not the record.** The comment of A065825 gives sets of `60974` in `[1, 19197041]` and `524288` in `[1, 515749566]` (Wroblewski, construction unread): `1.209` over greedy at the first `N`, where greedy is the best design, and `1.333` over greedy at the second, `1.311` over the best design there (`400000` by base `37` against greedy `393216`). The lead at `37^7` is `1.122`, so the row is against these four families only. Past `37^7`, base `37` loses to greedy at `L = 8, 9, 11` and at `L = 10` beats greedy by `1.164` but not Behrend (`14037750016` against `10^10`).
