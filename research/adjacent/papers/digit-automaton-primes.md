Fix a base `b` and keep the integers whose digits, read from the units end, a finite automaton accepts. With one state the set is a digit design, every digit restricted on its own, and that is where the theorems on primes with restricted digits are stated. This paper proves a Mobius bound and a prime count with an error term for sets read by an automaton with several states, under five hypotheses that are checked by hand or by a certified computation: a bound on the digits each state keeps, a split of the set below `x` into blocks of automaton words, a shifted-grid `l^1` certificate below `1/5` from every state, a contraction of the transfer matrices over a fixed number of steps, and a nearly even count of block elements prime to the base. Under them `|M_S(x)| <= C A_S(x) exp(-c sqrt(log x))` and `|psi_S(x) - kappa A_S(x)| <= C A_S(x) exp(-c sqrt(log x))` at every `x >= 2`, with `C` and `c` computable, where `A_S(x)` counts the set up to `x` and `M_S(x)`, `psi_S(x)` sum `mu` and `Lambda` over it. Two sets meet the hypotheses. The Kummer set `K = {k : p does not divide C(3k, k)}` is a two-state carry automaton and meets them at every prime `p >= 13567831`, with `kappa = p/(p+1)`. The set `R_b` of integers with no two equal adjacent base-`b` digits is a `(b+1)`-state automaton whose state is the last digit read, and meets them at every base `b >= 1153`, with `kappa = 1`. Neither set is a digit design at any power of its base. The proof is the dissection of [An Unconditional Mertens Bound at Large Base](../../coprime/papers/unconditional-mertens-at-large-base.md) and its von Mangoldt form in [An Unconditional Mertens Bound on the Half Interval](../../mobius/papers/half-interval-mobius.md), with the digit transform replaced by a product of transfer matrices. The minor-arc inputs are quoted, the bound for `mu` of Basak, Robles and Zaharescu (2023) and the bound for `Lambda` of Maynard (2022), and the zero-free regions are those of the earlier paper.

## Introduction

The figure is the Kummer set at `p = 7`, far below the range of Theorem 1.2: the picture is the set, not the bound. Its automaton reads `k` from the units digit with the carry of the doubling `2k` as its state, and a digit is refused when the addition `k + 2k` would carry. The blue cells are the integers whose digits all lie in `{0, 1, 2}`, the digits that state `0` keeps and sends back to state `0`; they form a digit design inside `K`, and theorems stated for digit designs see only them. The orange cells are what makes `K` no design. The digit `4` sends state `0` to state `1`, and state `1` refuses `2`, so `4` and `2 * 7 = 14` lie in `K` while `4 + 14 = 18` does not; a digit design holding `4` and `14` holds `18`. At every prime `p >= 13567831` Theorem 1.2 counts the primes of the whole set, not only those of the design inside it.

Definitions 2.1 and 2.2 state the digit automaton and the hypotheses (H1) to (H5) in full.

**Theorem 1.1 (the theorem on a digit automaton).** Let `S` be a set of positive integers, and suppose that a digit automaton at base `b >= 3` and data `fill`, `N_0`, `E_0`, `kappa`, `Omega`, `C_F`, `alpha_1`, `m_0` and `c_E` satisfy (H1) to (H5) for `S`; in particular `alpha_1 < 1/5` and `c_E < 1`. Then there are `C > 0` and `c > 0`, computable from `b`, the automaton and the data, such that for every `x >= 2`

```
|M_S(x)| <= C A_S(x) exp(-c sqrt(log x))   and   |psi_S(x) - kappa A_S(x)| <= C A_S(x) exp(-c sqrt(log x)) ,
```

where `A_S(x)` is the number of `n <= x` in `S` and `M_S(x)` and `psi_S(x)` are the sums of `mu(n)` and `Lambda(n)` over them. Row: [adjacent digits](../claims/adjacent-digits.md), row 3, proved in Section 3; witness [adjacent](../notes/adjacent.md), section The theorem on a digit automaton.

**Theorem 1.2 (the Kummer set).** Let `p >= 13567831` be prime and `K` the set of `k >= 1` with `p` not dividing `C(3k, k)`, with `A_K(x)`, `M_K(x)` and `psi_K(x)` its count and the sums of `mu(k)` and `Lambda(k)` over it up to `x`. Then `K` is a digit design at no base `p^j`, `j >= 1`, and there are `C > 0` and `c > 0`, computable from `p`, with

```
|M_K(x)| <= C A_K(x) exp(-c sqrt(log x))   and   |psi_K(x) - (p/(p+1)) A_K(x)| <= C A_K(x) exp(-c sqrt(log x))
```

at every `x >= 2`. It is the two-state case of Theorem 1.1, with `kappa = p/(p+1)`. Rows: [Kummer sets](../../kummer/claims/kummer-sets.md), rows 1, 5 and 6, and [adjacent digits](../claims/adjacent-digits.md), row 3; the bases `p^j` with `j >= 2` by Lemma 2.3.

**Theorem 1.3 (no two equal adjacent digits).** Let `b >= 1153`, let `R_b` be the set of `n >= 1` whose base-`b` digits have no two equal neighbours, and let `A(x)`, `M(x)` and `psi_R(x)` be its count and the sums of `mu(n)` and `Lambda(n)` over it up to `x`. Then `R_b` is a digit design at no base `b^j`, `j >= 1`, and there are `C > 0` and `c > 0`, computable from `b`, with

```
|M(x)| <= C A(x) exp(-c sqrt(log x))   and   |psi_R(x) - A(x)| <= C A(x) exp(-c sqrt(log x))
```

at every `x >= 2`. So `R_b` holds infinitely many primes, `(A(x)/log x)(1 + O(1/log x))` of them up to `x`. It is a `(b+1)`-state case of Theorem 1.1, with `kappa = 1`. Rows: [adjacent digits](../claims/adjacent-digits.md), rows 1, 7 and 8; the bases `b^j` with `j >= 2` by Lemma 2.3.

Rows are counted from the top of each claims page. Both sets are thin: `A_K(x) = x^(alpha_p + o(1))` with `alpha_p = log((p+1)/2)/log p`, and `A(x) = x^(alpha_b + o(1))` with `alpha_b = log(b-1)/log b`, the dimension of one missing digit. The saving has the shape of a zero-free region, not of a power, and `c` is tiny: it comes from the zeros of Dirichlet `L`-functions to moduli dividing a power of the base, where every possible exceptional zero belongs to one of finitely many real characters fixed by the base. The base is spent at one place, the certificate at shift `0` paid against the exponent `4/5` of the minor-arc input, and that is why the walls `13567831` and `1153` are far from base `10`. At base `10` the certificate of `R_10` reads `alpha_1` at most `0.5155374` (Fact 5.7), and Theorem 1.3 says nothing there.

What is new and what is not, as read against the sources of Section 6. First, infinitely many primes in both sets already follow at large base from theorems on digit designs inside them, at a lower count. Inside `K` the integers with every digit in `{0, .., floor((p-1)/3)}` form a design whose excluded digits are consecutive, so Theorem 1.3 of Maynard (2022) gives primes in `K` at every sufficiently large `p`, at the count `x^(log(floor((p-1)/3) + 1)/log p + o(1))`, with a threshold in `p` that is not explicit and a proof that Section 9 there sketches. At even `b` the design of alternating halves at base `b^2` lies inside `R_b`, and this collection's earlier theorem on the half interval at even base gives primes in `R_b` at every even `b >= 61270` (Section 6), at the count `x^(log(b/2)/log b + o(1))`. What is new is on the sets themselves: the asymptotic count, with its constant `p/(p+1)` or `1`, at the full exponent; the Mobius bound with a rate, for which no source read here has a statement on either set beyond `o(x)`, weaker than the trivial bound; and the theorem on a digit automaton under which the dissection carries both. Second, the minor-arc input for `mu` is one external result, Theorem 1.4 of Basak, Robles and Zaharescu (2023), posted as arXiv:2312.17435; its arXiv listing gives a journal reference, and the published text was not read. The `Lambda` input is Lemma 4.2 of Maynard (2022), a Vaughan-identity bound. Third, the walls are far from base `10`, the setting where these sets are usually met.

## Digit automata and the five hypotheses

**Definition 2.1 (a digit automaton).** A digit automaton at base `b >= 3` is a finite set `Q` of states with a start state `*` and digit kernels `D_(q q')`, subsets of `{0, .., b-1}` that are disjoint in `q'` for each `q`, read from the units digit: in state `q` a digit `d` is allowed when it lies in some `D_(q q')`, and it then moves to `q'`. With `e(x) = exp(2 pi i x)`, let `M(u)` be the `Q x Q` matrix with entries `M_(q q')(u) = sum_(d in D_(q q')) e(d u)` and `Pi_i(u) = M(u) M(b u) ... M(b^(i-1) u)`, so that `e_q^T Pi_i(u) w` is the transform of the words of length `i` read from state `q`, each weighted at its end state by the vector `w`. A state keeps the union of its kernels, and it is live when some nonempty word reaches it from `*`. Row: [adjacent digits](../claims/adjacent-digits.md), row 3.

**Definition 2.2 (the five hypotheses).** Fix `fill >= 2`, constants `N_0, E_0 >= 1` and `kappa > 0`, and a finite list `Omega` of end vectors with entries in `{0, 1}`. A set `S` of positive integers satisfies the hypotheses when:

- (H1) `*` keeps at most `b` digits and every live state at most `fill`.
- (H2) For every `x >= b` with `L` digits, the integers of `S` below `x`, with or without the integer `0`, form the disjoint union of at most `N_0` blocks `P b^j + W` at each scale `1 <= j < L` and at most `N_0` further points, where `W` lies in `[0, b^j)`, holds at most `fill^j` integers and has transform `e_*^T Pi_j(u) w` for some `w` in `Omega`; and `A_S(x) >= fill^(L-1)/N_0`.
- (H3) There are `C_F >= 1` and `alpha_1 < 1/5` with `sum_(a < b^i) |e_q^T Pi_i(s + a/b^i) w| <= C_F fill^i b^(i alpha_1)` at every `i >= 0`, every real `s`, every state `q` and every `w` in `Omega` or a unit vector `e_(q')`.
- (H4) There are `m_0 >= 1` and `c_E < 1` such that, whenever `v` lies at distance at least `3/(8b)` from `Z` and `v_2, .., v_(m_0)` are real, in the entrywise modulus of `M(v) M(v_2) ... M(v_(m_0))` every live row sums to at most `c_E fill^(m_0)` and the row of `*` to at most `c_E b fill^(m_0 - 1)`.
- (H5) Every block of (H2) at `j >= 1` has `|(b/phi(b)) #{t in W : gcd(t, b) = 1} - kappa |W|| <= E_0`.

Row: [adjacent digits](../claims/adjacent-digits.md), row 3. The integer `0`, which a block of words with leading zeros can hold, as at `K` or at a design containing the digit `0`, carries `mu(0) = Lambda(0) = 0`, so it changes no sum of `mu` or `Lambda`, and it moves the main term `kappa A_S(x)` of the prime count by at most `kappa`.

Each hypothesis feeds one step of the dissection. (H2) is the block split, (H3) at shift `0` from `*` is region A, (H3) at every shift, from every state and against the unit vectors is the hybrid `l^1` bound behind region B, (H4) is the decay of the transform near a fraction whose denominator has a prime outside the base, region C1, (H1) pays every position that the other hypotheses do not, and (H5) is the main term of the prime count. The rule between neighbouring digits enters through nothing else. The bound on `*` in (H1) is automatic, since no state keeps more than `b` digits, and it is stated because the proof pays `b` for the first step. A digit design `F` with two consecutive digits and a certificate below `1/5` is the one-state case, `Q = {*}` with `*` live, `fill = |F|`, `Omega = {1}` and `m_0 = 1`, and there Theorem 1.1 is Theorem 1.1 of the earlier paper with its von Mangoldt form; the theorem is new only where the automaton has more than one state.

**Lemma 2.3 (no design at a power of the base).** (i) At every prime `p >= 5` the set `K = {k >= 1 : p does not divide C(3k, k)}` is a digit design at no base `p^j`, `j >= 1`. (ii) At every `b >= 3` the set `R_b` is a digit design at no base `b^j`, `j >= 1`.

**Proof.** A digit design at base `B` is the set `S_F` of `n >= 1` whose base-`B` digits all lie in a set `F`, and if `x < B` and `y` lie in `S_F`, so does `x + y B`, whose base-`B` digits are `x` and those of `y`. (i) By Theorem 4.1 the digit `u = (p+1)/2` moves state `0` to state `1`, and the digit `v`, equal to `(p-1)/3` at `p = 1 mod 6` and to `(2p-1)/3` at `p = 5 mod 6`, is kept by state `0` and refused by state `1`, the kernels being the four intervals there. The digit `0` keeps state `0`. So `x = u p^(j-1)` lies in `K` and below `p^j`, `v` lies in `K`, and `x + v p^j` does not, since its word reaches the digit `v` in state `1`. (ii) Let `x` be the integer below `b^j` with digit `1` at the positions `j-1, j-3, ..` and `0` elsewhere; no two neighbouring digits of `x` agree and its top digit is `1`, so `x` lies in `R_b`, as does `1`. The digits of `x + 1 * b^j` at the positions `j - 1` and `j` are both `1`, so it does not. □

Rows: [Kummer sets](../../kummer/claims/kummer-sets.md), row 12, and [adjacent digits](../claims/adjacent-digits.md), row 12. At `j = 1` the witnesses are `u`, `v p` and `u + v p` for `K`, those of the Kummer page, section The carry automaton, and `1`, `b` and `b + 1` for `R_b`, those of [adjacent digits](../claims/adjacent-digits.md), row 1.

## The proof of Theorem 1.1

The proof runs the dissection of [the earlier paper](../../coprime/papers/unconditional-mertens-at-large-base.md), which proves the Mobius bound on a digit design, and its von Mangoldt form, Theorem 7.1(ii) of [the half-interval paper](../../mobius/papers/half-interval-mobius.md), with the seven steps below. Lemma and proposition numbers refer to the earlier paper unless the half-interval paper is named. Every step not named below reads neither a digit set nor `S`, and it runs verbatim. Two facts are used throughout. First, `||v^T B||_1 <= sum_q |v_q| r_q`, with `r_q` the sum of row `q` of the entrywise modulus of `B`. Second, a vector `e_*^T Pi_j(u)` at `j >= 1` is supported on live states, whose successors are live, so (H1) gives `||e_*^T Pi_j(u)||_1 <= b fill^(j-1)`, and every further factor costs at most `fill`.

**Proof of Theorem 1.1.**

- The blocks. (H2) replaces the split of Lemma 3.2. A block's sum of `mu` or `Lambda` is `y^(-1) sum_(a mod y) hat W(a/y) S_P(-a/y)` with `y = b^j`, by the orthogonality of Lemma 3.1, which reads only that `W` lies in `[0, y)`. In the reduction, Proposition 3.3, the scales below `L - K` weigh at most `N_0 sum_(j < L-K) fill^j <= 2 N_0 fill^(L-K) <= 2 N_0^2 fill^(1-K) A_S(x)`, by the mass floor of (H2); the further points weigh at most `N_0 log x`; and at most `N_0` blocks are kept at each scale.
- Region A. Proposition 4.4 reads the unshifted grid sum `sum_(a mod y) |hat W(a/y)|`, which is (H3) at `s = 0`, `q = *` and `w` in `Omega`.
- The hybrid bound. Lemma 5.1 splits the positions into the lowest `i_1`, the middle and the top `i_2`. Here `e_*^T Pi_k(t) w = e_*^T Pi_(i_1)(t) Pi'(V_1 t) Pi''(y t/V_2) w`, with `Pi'` and `Pi''` the products over the middle and the top positions. So `|hat W(t)| <= f(t) fill^(k - i_1 - i_2) g(y t/V_2)`, where `f(t) = ||e_*^T Pi_(i_1)(t)||_1` is the sum over `q` of `|e_*^T Pi_(i_1)(t) e_q|` and `g(v)` is the largest `|e_q^T Pi''(v) w|` over live `q`. Residues distinct mod `V_2` pay at most `sum_q sum_(a < V_2) |e_q^T Pi_(i_2)(a/V_2) w| <= |Q| C_F fill^(i_2) V_2^(alpha_1)`, by (H3) from every start state. The bottom factor enters through `4 D^2 ||f||_1 + ||f'||_1`. (H3) against the unit vectors, integrated over the shift, gives `||f||_1 <= |Q| V_1^(-1) C_F fill^(i_1) V_1^(alpha_1)`. Differentiating the product one factor at a time, the factor at position `j` costs `b^j pi b (b - 1)` in row sum, since the digits of a row are distinct and sum to at most `b(b-1)/2`; the factors below it pay the integrated `||e_*^T Pi_j||_1 <= |Q| b^(-j) C_F fill^j b^(j alpha_1)` and those above it `fill` each. So `||f'||_1 <= pi b^2 |Q| C_F fill^(i_1 - 1) i_1 V_1^(alpha_1)`, and a class `R(D, H)` pays at most `C fill^k (V_1 V_2)^(alpha_1) log y`: the bound of Lemma 5.1 with one more power of `log x`.
- Region B. Proposition 5.2 runs verbatim on that bound and asks `alpha_1 < 1/4`. The extra `log x` is absorbed by its saving `Z^(-(1/2 - 2 alpha_1))`, which is at most `Z^(-1/10)` when `alpha_1 < 1/5`.
- Region C1. Lemma 6.1 finds, for `d = e d_0` with `d_0 > 1` coprime to `b` and `(l, d) = 1`, a position in every window of `m_d` consecutive positions at which `b^i l/d` lies at distance at least `1/(2b)` from `Z`. At a point `l/d + eta` with `|eta| < b^(-2k/3)/(4b(b-1))` the phase at a position `i < 2k/3` moves by less than `1/(4b(b-1)) <= 1/(8b)`, so it stays at least `3/(8b)` from `Z`. Once `k >= 3 m_0`, the good positions of every `m_0`-th window start at least `floor(floor(2k/(3 m_d))/m_0)` disjoint runs of `m_0` positions inside `[0, k)`. (H4) contracts each run and (H1) pays every other position, so `|hat W(l/d + eta)| <= b fill^(k-1) c_E^(floor(floor(2k/(3 m_d))/m_0))`. That is the bound of Lemma 6.1 with `rho` replaced by `c_E^(1/m_0)`, up to a constant factor, and Proposition 6.3 and the choice of `C_0` in the assembly run with that replacement.
- Region C2. Proposition 6.6, and Proposition 7.6 of the half-interval paper for `Lambda`, read the transform only through `|hat W| <= |W| <= fill^k`.
- The main term. In Lemma 7.2 of the half-interval paper the principal parts over C2 sum to `(b/phi(b)) #{t in W : gcd(t, b) = 1}`, since its proof reads the transform only as a sum over the finite set `W`, and coprimality to `b` is read on `t` because `b` divides `y`. By (H5) that is `kappa |W|` within `E_0`. Over the kept blocks it is `kappa A_S(x)` within `N_0 E_0 log_b x` and `kappa` times the elements of the discarded scales, which the proof of Theorem 7.1(ii) of the half-interval paper absorbs.

With these replacements the assembly of Section 7 of the earlier paper, and its von Mangoldt form in the half-interval paper, gives both bounds, with `C` and `c` depending on `b`, the automaton and the data through `|Q|`, `fill`, `N_0`, `E_0`, `kappa`, `C_F`, `alpha_1`, `m_0` and `c_E`. □

The number of states enters only the constants, through `|Q|` and `N_0`. The proof never asks that the automaton be strongly connected, that the digit `0` be allowed in every state, or that two digits share a kernel: `R_b` has none of the three, and its contraction takes two steps.

## The Kummer set

This section supplies the hypotheses of Theorem 1.1 for `K = {k : p does not divide C(3k, k)}`. Every statement is proved on [the Kummer page](../../kummer/notes/kummer.md) and printed by [lab/py/kummer-carry](../../kummer/lab/py/kummer-carry/); the long certificate is cited there and not reproduced.

**Theorem 4.1 (the carry automaton).** At every prime `p >= 5` the integers `k` with `p` not dividing `C(3k, k)` are the words accepted by a two-state carry automaton read from the units digit: in state `c` a digit `d` moves to state `0` when `3d + c <= p - 1`, to state `1` when `2d + c >= p` and `3d + c <= 2p - 1`, and is refused otherwise. The kernels are the four intervals `D_00 = {0..floor((p-1)/3)}`, `D_01 = {(p+1)/2..floor((2p-1)/3)}`, `D_10 = {0..floor((p-2)/3)}` and `D_11 = {(p-1)/2..floor((2p-2)/3)}`; each state keeps `(p+1)/2` digits and the pair `{0, 1}`, which moves it to state `0`; the set is a width-2 rule and no digit design. Rows: [Kummer sets](../../kummer/claims/kummer-sets.md), row 1, and row 3 for the pair moving to state `0`.

**Proof.** By Kummer's theorem, `v_p(C(m + n, m))` is the number of carries in the addition `m + n` at base `p`, and `C(3k, k) = C(k + 2k, k)`. The doubling `2k` has digit `2 d_i + c_i - p c_(i+1)` with `c_0 = 0` and `c_(i+1) = 1` exactly when `2 d_i + c_i >= p`, and `c_i <= 1`; the state is this carry. With no carry of `k + 2k` arriving at position `i`, that column sums to `3 d_i + c_i - p c_(i+1)`, which is at most `p - 1` exactly in the two allowed cases. Above the top digit `2k` holds the single digit `c_L <= 1` against a zero of `k`. So `k + 2k` carries nowhere exactly when every digit is allowed, and solving the inequalities gives the four intervals. The digit `0` is allowed in both states and moves to state `0`, so leading zeros change nothing. □

In this section the pair of a general Kummer set is written `(a, r)`, keeping `b` for the base: `K_(a,r) = {k : p does not divide C(ak, rk)}`, so that `K = K_(3,1)`.

**Theorem 4.2 (the mass).** At every prime `p >= 5` exactly `((p+1)/2)^L` integers `0 <= k < p^L` have `p` not dividing `C(3k, k)`, so `A_K(p^L) = ((p+1)/2)^L`, `p^L` itself lying in `K`; and with `fill = (p+1)/2`, `pi = (2/3, 1/3)` and `lambda_2` equal to `1` at `p = 1 mod 6` and `0` at `p = 5 mod 6`, the words of length `L` ending in state `c` number exactly `fill^L pi_c + lambda_2^L (delta_(0c) - pi_c)`. Rows: [Kummer sets](../../kummer/claims/kummer-sets.md), row 3, which proves the count for every `K_(a,r)` at every prime `p > a`, and row 13 for the count by end state and the block main term; witness [the Kummer page](../../kummer/notes/kummer.md), section The mass of every Kummer set.

**Theorem 4.3 (the certificate).** At every prime `p >= 13` the set `K` carries a shifted-grid matrix transfer certificate in the row-0 basis, `C_F = 1 + eta` and `p^(alpha_1) = lambda/fill`, with `lambda` the Perron root of an explicit `2 x 2` matrix of closed forms, and `lambda/fill <= 1.180479 log p + 7.482879`. Explicitly, with `L = log(p/2)`, `n_1 = |D_01|` and the constants `nu^+ = 1.854292`, `H^+ = 5.672774`, `nu_1^+ = 1.287903` and `H_1^+ = 3.861981`,

```
A* = fill + (p/(2 pi))(2 nu^+ L + H^+) + (3/2)(p - 1) + (1 - 2/pi) p ,
B* = n_1 + (p/(2 pi))(2 nu_1^+ L + H_1^+) + (p - 1)/6 + (1 - 2/pi) p/2 ,
C* = 2 csc(pi/(2p)) ,   D* = p at p = 1 mod 6 ,   D* = C* at p = 5 mod 6 ,
lambda = (A* + D*)/2 + sqrt(((A* - D*)/2)^2 + B* C*) ,   eta = (lambda - A*)/C* ,
```

and the shifted grid sums of the words ending in each state are at most `(1 + eta) lambda^i` at every `i` and every real shift. ## No two equal adjacent digits

This section supplies the hypotheses of Theorem 1.1 for `R_b`. Every statement is proved on [the adjacent page](../notes/adjacent.md) and printed by [lab/py/adjacent-digits](../lab/py/adjacent-digits/).

**Theorem 5.1 (the automaton).** For every base `b >= 3` the integers with no two equal adjacent base-`b` digits are the accepted words of a `(b+1)`-state automaton read from the units digit whose state is the last digit read; they hold exactly `(b-1)^L` integers of `L` digits, form no digit design since `1` and `b` lie in the set and `b + 1` does not, have no digit allowed in every state and no reset word, and their automaton completed with a sink synchronizes on the word `00`. Row: [adjacent digits](../claims/adjacent-digits.md), row 1.

**Proof.** The states are `*` and the digits `0..b-1`. From `*` every digit `d` is allowed and moves to state `d`; from state `c` the digit `d` is allowed exactly when `d != c`, and moves to state `d`. So `n` lies in `R_b` exactly when its word of exact length, top digit nonzero, is accepted. Read from the top, an integer of `L` digits has `b - 1` choices of top digit and `b - 1` at each lower position. A digit `d` is refused in state `d`, and a word whose first digit is `d` is refused from state `d`. The word `00` sends every state to the sink, since `0` is refused in state `0`. □

The count matrix on the digit states is `J - I`, `J` the all-ones matrix, and `(J - I)^n = (((b-1)^n - (-1)^n)/b) J + (-1)^n I`, so the words of length `k >= 1` with bottom digit `c` and top digit `c'` number `((b-1)^(k-1) - (-1)^(k-1))/b + (-1)^(k-1) [c = c']`. Every entry of `M(u)` is a single exponential or `0`, so no kernel holds two digits.

**Theorem 5.2 (the blocks and the units digit).** Write `W_j^(f)` for the words of length `j` with no two equal adjacent digits and top digit not `f`, read as integers in `[0, b^j)`. Below every `x` with `L` digits, `R_b` is the disjoint union of at most `b - 1` blocks `f b^j + W_j^(f)`, `f >= 1`, at each scale `j < L - 1`, which hold the shorter elements, at most `b - 1` blocks `P b^j + W_j^(f)` at each scale, which hold the elements of `L` digits, `P` a prefix of `x` followed by `f`, and the point `x` when it lies in `R_b`; each block holds exactly `(b-1)^j` integers and has transform `e_*^T Pi_j(u) (1 - e_f)`. In every block `P b^j + W` of the integers with no two equal adjacent base-`b` digits, `W` the words of length `j` with top digit not `f`, `(b/phi(b))` times the number of elements coprime to `b` equals `(b-1)^j + (-1)^(j-1)(1 - (b/phi(b))[f a unit mod b])`, so the prime count constant on the set is `kappa_b = 1` at every base. Rows: [adjacent digits](../claims/adjacent-digits.md), row 13 for the split and row 4 for the units digit; witness [the adjacent page](../notes/adjacent.md), section The blocks and the units digit.

**Proof.** An element with `L` digits first falls below `x` at a position `j` where its digit `f` is below the digit `x_j` of `x`; its digits above copy those of `x`, so that prefix has no two equal neighbours, `f != x_(j+1)` when `j < L - 1` and `f >= 1` when `j = L - 1`, and every element of `P b^j + W_j^(f)` then lies in `R_b`. ## What is in print

Every source below is listed in the references and was read at source in the parts named. The wider search behind the topic, for primes or Mobius sums on sets cut by forbidden adjacent digits, is recorded on [the adjacent page](../notes/adjacent.md), section What is in print.

Digit designs. Maynard (2019), read in arXiv:1604.01041v2 in its abstract and Theorem 1.1, proves that the primes below `X` missing one decimal digit number `#A/log X` up to absolute constant factors, `#A` the count of the set; Maynard (2022), read in arXiv:1510.07711v1 in its abstract, Theorems 1.1 and 1.3 and Section 9, proves `sum_(n < q^k) Lambda(n) 1_A(n) = kappa_q(a_0) (q-1)^k + O_A((q-1)^k (log q^k)^(-A))` at one excluded digit for `q > 2000000`, and remarks that the same holds uniformly when the excluded digit depends on the position. Its Theorem 1.3 takes `s` excluded digits, `0 < s < q^(1/5 - eps)`, or consecutive excluded digits with `q - s >= q^(4/5 + eps)`, at `q` sufficiently large in terms of `eps`, and gives an asymptotic of the same shape; Section 9 proves it as a sketch, "leaving the precise details to the interested reader", and the paper derives from it the infinitely many primes with no string of `15` equal consecutive decimal digits, through a design at base `10^8`. A whole-text search of both sources finds no Mobius sum over a set: `Mobius` appears once in Maynard (2019), in "By Mobius inversion" inside a proof, and not at all in Maynard (2022). Every set these theorems treat is a digit design, at a base `q` of their choosing. Lemma 2.3 rules out the bases `p^j` for `K` and `b^j` for `R_b` only; a base that is not a power of the set's own base is not examined here. Each set contains a design at a power of its base:

- Inside `K`. Every digit of `D_00 = {0..floor((p-1)/3)}` keeps state `0` (Theorem 4.1), so the design on `D_00` lies in `K`. Its excluded digits `floor((p-1)/3) + 1, .., p - 1` are consecutive and `q - s = floor((p-1)/3) + 1 >= p^(4/5 + eps)` once `p` is large in terms of `eps`, so Maynard (2022), Theorem 1.3, gives infinitely many primes in `K` at every sufficiently large prime `p`, with no explicit threshold, at the count of the design, `(floor((p-1)/3) + 1)^k` of the first `p^k` integers against the `((p+1)/2)^k` of `K`. Theorem 1.2 gives the count on `K` itself, with the constant `p/(p+1)`, from the explicit prime `13567831`.
- Inside `R_b`. No digit design at base `b` with two digits lies in `R_b`, since a design holding `d` holds the word `dd`. At even `b = 2h` the design `F = {a + b beta : h <= a < b, 0 <= beta < h}` at base `b^2` does: its members read a top-half digit at every even position and a bottom-half digit at every odd one. It excludes `3b^2/4` digits that are not consecutive, outside both cases of Maynard (2022), Theorem 1.3. Its transform is `|hat F(t)| = |D_h(t) D_h(b t)|`, so its level-`i` grid sums at base `b^2` are the level-`2i` grid sums of `H = {0..h-1}` at base `b`, which this collection certifies below `1/5` from `b = 61270` ([digit progressions](../../mobius/claims/digit-progressions.md), row 8 for the certificate and row 9 for its wall and theorem); `F` keeps the pair `h, h + 1`, so the prime count of [the half-interval paper](../../mobius/papers/half-interval-mobius.md), Theorem 7.1(ii), gives primes in `R_b` at every even `b >= 61270`, at the count `x^(log(b/2)/log b + o(1))` ([the adjacent page](../notes/adjacent.md), bullet The embedded design). Theorem 1.3 adds the count on `R_b` itself, with constant `1` and exponent `log(b-1)/log b`, the Mobius bound, the even bases `1153..61268` and every odd base `b >= 1153`.

Han, Rahimi and Yang (2026), read in arXiv:2609.05810v1 in its abstract and its first theorem, count prime pairs `(m, m + n)` with weight `Lambda(m) Lambda(m + n)` over the differences `n` in a digit design `C_k`, and three-term progressions with common difference in `C_k`, with explicit leading constants under a non-resonance condition and an `l^1` admissibility exponent below `2/5`, verified by a transfer-operator argument for several families of digit sets. Their sets are digit designs, and they count primes in pairs, not primes in the set.

Markov digit sets. Saavedra-Araya (2024), read in arXiv:2411.07418v2 in its abstract, Theorem A and the section Forbidding combinations of digits, its arXiv listing giving the journal reference Ergodic Theory Dynam. Systems 46 (2026) 757-804, studies the residue classes of digit-restricted sets by Markov chains. Theorem A: on a missing-digit set with `gcd(g, a) = gcd(a, a') = 1`, `n mod a` and `s_g(n) mod a'` are jointly uniform exactly when `gcd(a a', d_2 - d_1, .., d_t - d_1) = 1`. For the set `A_T` of a `0/1` transition matrix `T` on the digits, a one-step shift of finite type, joint uniformity follows when `T` is `k`-regular and irreducible and some digits `d, d'` have `T(d, d) = T(d, d') = T(d', d) = 1` and `gcd(d' - d, a a') = 1`. `R_b` is `A_T` with `T = J - I`, which is `(b-1)`-regular and irreducible with zero diagonal, so that sufficient condition fails at `R_b`, though the paper remarks that it can be relaxed. Only integers are treated: no primes and no Mobius sums.

Automatic sequences. Mullner (2017), read in arXiv:1602.03042v3 in its abstract and Theorems 1.2 and 1.3, proves that every automatic sequence is orthogonal to `mu`, `sum_(n <= N) xi(n) mu(n) = o(N)` with no rate, and that the letter frequencies along the primes exist for a strongly connected automaton whose initial state is fixed by the digit `0`. The indicators of `K` and `R_b` are automatic, so `M_K(x) = o(x)` and `M(x) = o(x)`; since `A(x) = x^(alpha + o(1))` with `alpha < 1`, that is weaker than the trivial `|M| <= A`. Both completed automata have a refusing sink, so they are not strongly connected, and both sets have density `0`. Deshouillers, Drmota and Mullner (2015), read at the authors' text in its abstract, Theorem 1.2, Lemma 2.2 and Section 3, prove the full Sarnak conjecture, `o(N)` with no rate, for automatic sequences of synchronizing automata read from the most significant digit, and the sequence form of it when read from the least significant digit. Both completed automata synchronize: that of `R_b` on the word `00` (Theorem 5.1), that of `K` on the digit `p - 1`, refused in both states (Theorem 4.1). Adamczewski, Drmota and Mullner (2022), read in its abstract, prove that the logarithmic densities of every automatic sequence along the primes exist and are computable, and rational; on both sets they are `0`, and nothing is said at the scale `A(x)/log x`.

Digital functions along primes. Hanna (2017), read in arXiv:1511.02068v1 in its abstract, Theorem 1.3, Remark 1.4, Section 3 and Section 7, bounds `sum_(n <= x) Lambda(n) f(n) e(theta n)` uniformly in `theta` with an explicit saving, for `f = e(alpha a(n))` and `a(n)` in a class of digital functions that holds the block-additive ones; Remark 1.4 gives the same with `mu` in place of `Lambda`, and Corollaries 3.2 to 3.4 count the primes with `a(p) = a' mod m'`. Those level sets are automatic sets of positive density and not digit designs, so on them a Mobius bound with a rate and a prime count are in print. Its example (III) in Section 7 counts the words of one repeated letter of a fixed length, outside the case `q = beta = 2`; at length `2` that is the number of equal adjacent pairs, which is therefore equidistributed modulo `m'` along the primes. `R_b` is the level set `a(n) = 0`, of density `0`, which a statement at positive density does not reach at the scale `A(x)/log x`. It is the nearest result in print to `R_b` among the sources read.

The analytic inputs. The minor-arc bound for `mu` is Theorem 1.4 of Basak, Robles and Zaharescu (2023), read in arXiv:2312.17435v2: for a reduced `a/q` with `|alpha - a/q| <= 1/q^2`, `X >= 2` and fixed `eps > 0`, `S_mu(X, alpha) <<_eps X^(4/5 + eps) + X (log X)^3 q^(-1/2) + X^(1/2) q^(1/2) (log X)^3`. It enters regions A and B of the Mobius bound and nothing else. Its arXiv listing gives the journal reference Can. J. Math. 78 (2026) 508-543; the published text was not read here. The minor-arc bound for `Lambda` is Lemma 4.2 of Maynard (2022), a Vaughan-identity bound with the same exponent `4/5`. Kummer's theorem on carries, Kummer (1852), is cited at its record; Fact 4.6 checks the automaton against Legendre's digit sums.

The verdict. Among the sources read here, none states a Mobius bound with a rate, or a prime count at the scale `A(x)/log x`, on a set of density zero, `A(x) = x^(alpha + o(1))` with `alpha < 1`, read by a digit automaton and a digit design at no power of its base; at positive density Hanna (2017), with a rate, and Mullner (2017), Theorem 1.3, cover automatic sets such as the level sets of a block-additive function. A set whose excluded digit depends only on the position modulo `m`, which the remark of Maynard (2022) after its Theorem 1.1 covers, is a design at base `q^m`, and so is the alternating-halves set at base `b^2`. Theorems 1.2 and 1.3 are such statements on two sets, and Theorem 1.1 is the form that covers both. Infinitely many primes are not new on `K` at large `p`, by Maynard (2022), Theorem 1.3, nor on `R_b` at even `b >= 61270`, by this collection's earlier theorem; what is new is the count with its constant, the Mobius bound, and the explicit walls.

## Open problems

The walls are the certificates', not the method's. Region A reads the transform only through the certificate at shift `0`, so a sharper certificate moves a wall and nothing else changes. At `R_b` the chain pays the triangle inequality between the segment sequences of the run expansion, and the exact grid sums at base `10` read `2.4144` per digit against its `3.27746` (Fact 5.7); a certificate that reads the long runs together, or a transfer operator on the phase of the open run, would move `1153` down, and neither is written. At `K` the transfer inequality pays the triangle inequality across the two paths through the second row; readings that the Kummer page tags Conjecture ([Kummer sets](../../kummer/claims/kummer-sets.md), row 9) put the exact grid sums of `K` within `0.065` of the row-0 design and their offset, carried to the bar `1/5`, meets `p^(1/5)` at `p` near `1.15 * 10^6`. A proof of either wall lower is open.

Base `10` is out of reach of this route: there the certificate of `R_10` reads `0.5155374`, above `1/5` and above `1/4`. **Conjecture** ([adjacent digits](../claims/adjacent-digits.md), row 11): at every base `b >= 3`, `psi_R(x)` is asymptotic to `A(x)`; the readings at `b = 3, 4, 5, 7, 10` at `x = 10^8` sit within `0.0066` of `1`.

The other Kummer sets. Theorem 4.5 reduces every `K_(a,r)` at `p >= 2a - 1` to a matrix certificate below `1/5`, and the row-0 design of the general pair has limit numerator of mean `3 - 4 gcd(a, r)/a` ([Kummer sets](../../kummer/claims/kummer-sets.md), row 8). **Conjecture** (row 10 there): the row-0 operator has rate `(2/pi)(3 - 4 gcd(a, r)/a) log p + O_a(1)`, so every pair carries a certificate below `1/5` from some prime `P_0(a, r)`. The step not written is the analytic bound of Theorem 4.3 at a general union of intervals with a second block of `r(a - r) - 1` rows.

Other neighbour rules. Theorem 1.1 carries any rule between neighbouring digits, such as `|d_(i+1) - d_i| >= 2`, once its blocks, certificate and contraction are supplied; none is computed here, and no general theorem supplies a certificate from the automaton alone.

The shape of the bound. The bar `1/5` is the exponent `4/5` of both minor-arc inputs, which neither this paper nor its sources moves, and the saving `exp(-c sqrt(log x))` is the zero-free region of the characters to moduli dividing a power of the base, with a tiny `c`. The earlier paper shows that its route needs a dense digit design (its Proposition 10.2); the corresponding floor for a digit automaton, and any statement on a sparse automaton set, are open.
