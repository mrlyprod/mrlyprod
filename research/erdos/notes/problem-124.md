## Problem 124: what is in print

- The site states the question as above and credits Burr, Erdos, Graham and Li with the case `D = {3, 4, 7}`. [Burr, Erdos, Graham and Li 1996](https://doi.org/10.4064/aa-77-2-133-138), read at source, state the conjecture for `Pow(A; s)`, the nondecreasing sequence formed from all the powers `a^k`, `a` in `A`, `k >= s >= 1`, which, called nondecreasing, reads as the multiset `M_s(A)`, though Claim 1 writes it strictly increasing; their Theorem 1 gives, for `A` of positive upper density with `gcd = 1`, a finite `A'` in `A` with `Pow(A'; s)` complete, through a gap bound `2 b_N^(s+1)` for the sums of `Pow(B; s)` when `sum 1/b_i > 2` and Szemeredi's theorem. Of `{3, 4, 7}` they state, without printing the argument, that the bound of Mignotte and Waldschmidt on `abs(3^p - 4^q)` lets them show it complete at `s = 1`, largest missing integer `581`, and they print the largest missing integers `111`, `16` and `78` for `{3, 5, 7, 13}`, `{3, 6, 7, 13, 21}` and `{3, 4, 5}` at `s = 1`. A plain knapsack to `2 10^6` reads `581` for `{3, 4, 7}`, as they do, but `112`, `17` and `79` for the other three, and `111 = 81 + 27 + 3`, `16 = 9 + 7`, `78 = 64 + 9 + 5` are sums (verb `set`; `79` is also the certificate's `F`). The level `k = 0`, where every power `d^0 = 1` is allowed, is settled on the discussion thread by a Lean-checked proof through Brown's criterion `a_(n+1) <= S_n + 1`, read at source.
- The site records that `sigma(D) >= 1` is necessary, after Pomerance; the thread reconstructs the argument: by Dirichlet's simultaneous approximation, infinitely often every base has its least power `>= N` in `[N, beta N)`, and when `sigma(D) < 1` the terms below `N` then sum to less than `sigma(D) beta N < N`, so `N - 1` is no sum.
- [Bergelson and Simmons 2017](https://arxiv.org/abs/1507.02208), Theorem 1.23, read at source: the set of powers of `D` is strongly complete, complete after deleting any finite set, when `D` splits into four disjoint parts, three with `sigma >= 1` and one with `gcd = 1`; they write that they can neither prove nor disprove the conjecture. Strongly complete is complete at every `k`.
- [Fan 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.14071), Theorem 1.5, read at source: the same with two parts of `sigma >= 1` and one of `gcd = 1`, for finite `D` with no two elements powers of one integer. Both run through a criterion on disjoint infinite parts of the terms: two parts (Fan, Theorem 2.2) or three (Bergelson and Simmons, Main Theorem 2.1) with `Delta(B) = sup_(b in B) (b - sum_(b' in B, b' < b) b')` finite, plus one more part `C` with `sum_(c in C) norm(c theta) = infinity`, for every `theta` not in `Z` with `Delta(C)` finite or a countable `H_2(C)` (Fan), or for every irrational `theta` with `FS(C) + qZ = Z` for every `q` (Bergelson and Simmons), make the union complete. Main Theorem 2.1 as printed does not say its three parts are infinite, and read literally it fails: at `D = {5, 6}`, `k = 1`, the parts `{5}, {6}, {25}` meet (I), the rest of the powers meets (II) and (III), yet the sums of distinct powers number at most `x^(log_5 2 + log_6 2) < x^0.82` up to `x`; its proof and Fan's restatement use infinite parts, and so does this page.
- Both theorems are stated for the set of powers, where `(a^2)^n` and `a^(2n)` are one term, and exclude two elements powers of one integer; the site's question, a sum of one element of each `P(d_i, k)`, is the multiset `M_k(D)`, and this page certifies the multiset. The two differ on the cells where two bases are powers of one integer: `{3, 5, 6, 9}` at `k = 1` has `F = 22` as a multiset, while as a set `649` is no sum and every integer from `650` to `2 10^4` is (verbs `cell 3,5,6,9 1 20` and `set 3,5,6,9 1 20000`).
- The site page predates Fan's paper and records neither theorem. Fan's needs `sigma(D) > 2`, its third part being nonempty, and Bergelson and Simmons' `sigma(D) > 3`, so no theorem whose proof is read here settles a base set with `sigma(D) < 2`; the site's credit of `{3, 4, 7}`, where `sigma = 1`, rests on an argument at the one level `k = 1` that the paper states but does not print; the `103` base sets in `[3, 12]` minimal for `sigma > 1` and `gcd = 1` have `sigma` between `2311/2310` and `8/7` (verb `census 12 5 32 4`).

## The surplus certificate

- **Proved (the interval step).** If `P_n` contains `[T, S_n - T]` and `a_(m+1) <= S_m - 2T + 1` for every `m >= n`, then `FS(M_k(D))` contains every integer `>= T`. Proof: `P_(m+1) = P_m U (P_m + a_(m+1))` contains `[T, S_m - T] U [T + a_(m+1), S_(m+1) - T]`, one interval exactly when `T + a_(m+1) <= S_m - T + 1`, and `S_m -> infinity`. At `k = 0`, `T = 0` is Brown's criterion.
- **Proved (the surplus**, the per-part computation of Bergelson and Simmons' Section 2.7, repeated in Fan's Section 7**).** For every `n`, `S_n - a_(n+1) >= (sigma(D) - 1) a_(n+1) - C_k(D)`. Proof: `P_n` holds every term below `X = a_(n+1)`, and for each base those are `d^j` with `k <= j < J`, `d^J >= X` the least power `>= X` with `J >= k`, summing to `(d^J - d^k)/(d - 1) >= (X - d^k)/(d - 1)`.
- **Proved (the certificate).** Let `sigma(D) > 1` and `k >= 1`. If for some `n` the integer `T = 1 + max{x <= S_n/2 : x not in P_n}` satisfies `T <= a_(n+1)`, and `a_(m+1) <= S_m - 2T + 1` holds for every `m >= n` with `(sigma(D) - 1) a_(m+1) < C_k(D) + 2T - 1`, then `M_k(D)` is complete and `F(D, k) = T - 1` exactly. Proof: past that finite range the surplus gives the step, so the interval step applies; and `T - 1 < a_(n+1)` is a sum of distinct terms only of terms below `a_(n+1)`, all in `P_n`, where it is missing. The check is finite and exact: integer arithmetic with `sigma` and `C_k` over the common denominator of the `d_i - 1`.
- **Proved (exact in the gap).** If `1 < sigma(D) < 2`, then `M_k(D)` is complete if and only if the certificate exists at some `n`, so the search halts exactly on the complete levels and a level left open is open only for the cap. Proof: fix `beta > 1` with `sigma(D) beta < 2`; Dirichlet's simultaneous approximation gives arbitrarily large exponents with one power of every base, all within a factor `beta < 3` of one another, and with `N` the least of them (Fan, footnote 1) `N` is a term and the least power `>= N` of every base lies in `[N, beta N)`; at the first copy `a_(n+1) = N` the terms below `N` sum to `S_n < sigma(D) beta N < 2 a_(n+1)`; then every `x <= S_n/2` is a sum of distinct terms below `a_(n+1)` or of none, so `P_n` and `FS(M_k(D))` agree on `[0, S_n/2]`, the certificate reads `T = F(D, k) + 1`, and for `N` large the surplus clause is empty.
- **Proved (monotone).** Completeness at `(D, k)` gives completeness at `(D', k')` for every base set `D'` containing `D` and every `k' <= k`, since `M_k'(D')` contains `M_k(D)` as a multiset. So the census below needs only the base sets that are minimal for `sigma > 1` and `gcd = 1`, each at its deepest certified level.
- **Proved (the wide certificate).** Fix `W`, let `B_n = P_n meet [0, W]` and let `H_n` be the integers of `(W, S_n/2]` missing from `P_n`, so that `B_n`, `H_n` and the symmetry give all of `P_n`. Then `B_(n+1) = B_n U ((B_n meet [0, W - a_(n+1)]) + a_(n+1))`, and every integer of `(W, S_(n+1)/2]` missing from `P_(n+1)` lies in `H_n`, in `S_n - H_n`, in `S_n - ([0, W] minus B_n)` or above `S_n`, and such a candidate `z` is missing from `P_(n+1)` exactly when `z - a_(n+1)` is missing from `P_n`. Proof: `P_(n+1) = P_n U (P_n + a_(n+1))`, so `z` is missing from `P_(n+1)` exactly when `z` and `z - a_(n+1)` are missing from `P_n`; for `z <= W` both are read in `B_n`, and above `W` the four sets are, by symmetry, every integer missing from `P_n`. So the certificate runs on `W` bits and a sorted list of the middle holes, exact while the list stays below a cap, where the full array needs `S_n` bits.

## The two-part route is dead below two

- **Proved.** Let `D` be finite with `sigma(D) < 2` and `k >= 0`. Then no two disjoint infinite sub-multisets `B_1, B_2` of `M_k(D)` have both `Delta(B_1)` and `Delta(B_2)` finite, whatever becomes of the other terms. Proof: take `N` as in the gap proof above, so the terms below `N` sum to less than `sigma(D) beta N`, and let `b_i` be the least term of `B_i` that is `>= N`; the terms of `B_i` below `b_i` are those below `N`, so `Delta(B_1) + Delta(B_2) >= b_1 + b_2 - sigma(D) beta N >= (2 - sigma(D) beta) N`, unbounded in `N`.
- So Fan's Theorem 2.2, which needs two such parts, and Bergelson and Simmons' Main Theorem 2.1, which needs three, apply to no `M_k(D)` with `sigma(D) < 2` under any partition of the terms, not only the partitions by bases; the whole of `M_k(D)` has `Delta` finite once `sigma(D) >= 1`, and the obstruction is the second part. The syndetic-pair method cannot enter the gap `1 <= sigma(D) < 2` at all.
- **Verified** (verb `route 10 12`, under `0.01` s): for each of the `30` minimal base sets below `11`, the least value of `sum(M_1(D) below N)/N` over terms `N` in `[10^12, 10^30]` lies in `[1.105953, 1.537469]`, truncated, so at that `N` any two disjoint parts of the terms have one with `Delta >= 0.23 N`: the clusters the proof uses are already visible at twelve digits.

## The missing lemma

- Linear surplus means `S_n - a_(n+1) >= eps a_(n+1) - C` for all `n`, some `eps > 0` and a constant `C`; `H_1(M)` is the set of `theta` in `R/Z` with `sum_(a in M) norm(a theta) < infinity`, Fan's `H_1`-spectrum. Every `M_k(D)` with `sigma(D) > 1` and `gcd(D) = 1` has both properties, `eps = sigma(D) - 1` by the surplus lemma and `H_1 = {0}` by Fan's Section 7 argument applied base by base, so the lemma L, that linear surplus and `H_1(M) = {0}` make a multiset complete, would settle problem 124 above `sigma = 1`.
- **Refuted.** L is false. Witness: `A = {2 F_m - 1 : m >= 2} = 1, 3, 5, 9, 15, 25, 41, ...`, `F_m` the Fibonacci numbers, so `a_(n+1) = a_n + a_(n-1) + 1`. Surplus: `S_n = 2 F_(n+3) - 4 - n`, so `S_n - a_(n+1) = a_n - n - 2 >= a_(n+1)/2 - n - 5/2`, and `a_n` grows geometrically, so `eps = 1/4` works with a constant `C`. Spectrum: if `theta` is in `H_1(A)`, then `norm(a_n theta) -> 0` and `norm(theta) <= norm(a_(n+1) theta) + norm(a_n theta) + norm(a_(n-1) theta) -> 0`. Incomplete: by the window below, `a_(n+2) - S_n = n + 3` opens a window at every `n`, and `a_(m+1) >= a_m + a_(m-1)` puts the upper window `(S_(n-2), a_n)` inside the lower window of step `n`, so the non-sum `2`, in the upper windows `(0, 3)` and `(1, 5)`, recurs in every window.
- **Verified** (verb `refute 27`, under `0.1` s, pinned by a crate test): the `38` terms of `A` up to `2^27` open `36` windows, every one holding a non-sum; the least of `2 (S_n - a_(n+1)) - a_(n+1)` is `-9`; the `25` non-sums below `10^6` are the two chains `2, 7, 22, 63, 172, ...` and `2, 11, 36, 103, 280, ...`.
- **Proved (the window).** If `a_(n+2) > S_n`, the sums in `(S_n, a_(n+2))` are exactly `a_(n+1) + (P_n meet (S_n - a_(n+1), a_(n+2) - a_(n+1)))`: such an `x` uses only terms below `a_(n+2)`, and exceeds the sum of all terms before `a_(n+1)`, so it uses `a_(n+1)`. An integer of the lower window missing from `P_n` is carried to a non-sum in the upper one.
- **Proved (surplus does not heal).** The set `a_n = floor(2 (5/3)^n)`, `n >= 1`, has `S_n - a_(n+1) >= a_(n+1)/2 - n - 5`, which is linear surplus since `a_(n+1)` grows geometrically, and is incomplete. Proof: `a_(n+2) >= (50/9)(5/3)^n - 1 > 5 (5/3)^n - 5 >= S_n` opens a window at every `n`; `a_(m+1) - a_m - a_(m-1) >= (2/9)(5/3)^(m-1) - 1 > 0` for `m >= 4` puts the upper window `(S_(n-2), a_n)` inside the lower window of step `n`; a non-sum `h` there is missing from `P_n`, since `h < a_n`, so `a_(n+1) + h` is a non-sum, and one seed recurs in every other window. This is Graham's incompleteness for `t > 1`, `alpha >= max(2/t, phi)` as Fan quotes it, reproved at one point; whether its `H_1` is `{0}` is open, of the kind the site's problem 349 names when it asks whether `floor((3/2)^n)` is odd or even infinitely often.
- **Verified** (verb `graham 2 5 3 26`, under `0.1` s): the `33` terms of `floor(2 (5/3)^n)` up to `2^26` open `31` windows and every one holds a non-sum; the largest non-sum up to `2^25` is `23559582`.
- **Verified** (verb `windows`, under `0.01` s each): the power multisets run no chain. Over the terms in `[10^6, 10^30]`, `M_k({3, 4, 5})` opens a window at `27` of `123` terms at each `k = 1..4`, `M_1({3, 4, 6})` at `41` of `120`, `M_1({3, 5, 6, 7})` at `5` of `142`, and in all six no upper window `(S_m, a_(m+2))` meets the lower window `(S_n - a_(n+1), a_(n+2) - a_(n+1))` of a later open window.
- No linear-forms bound enters at `sigma > 1`: the surplus clause of the certificate closes by `(sigma - 1) a_(m+1) >= C_k + 2T - 1` alone, so Baker, Matveev or Laurent can only serve the `sigma = 1` cells. What changes with `k` is the base interval `[T, S_n - T]`, whose scale climbs from `a_n = 243` at `k = 1` to `3486784401` at `k = 4` for `{3, 4, 5}`, and `k = 5` passes a `2^34`-bit array (verb `cell 3,4,5 5 34`) but closes under the wide certificate at `W = 2^31`, at `S_n = 176844529807`.

## The two routes

- **Proved (the two routes).** An integer `x` with `a_(n+1) <= x < a_(n+2)` is no sum of `M` exactly when `y = x - a_(n+1)` and `g = S_n - x` are both missing from `P_n`, a negative `g` counting as missing; always `g + y = S_n - a_(n+1)`. Proof: `x` uses only terms below `a_(n+2)`, so it is a sum exactly when `x` or `x - a_(n+1)` lies in `P_n`, and for `0 <= x <= S_n` the symmetry of `P_n` puts `x` in `P_n` exactly when `S_n - x` is. The window route is `g < 0`, the window lemma above; the pair route is `g >= 0`, the deficit `S_n - a_(n+1)` split into two integers of `[0, S_n]` missing from `P_n`, and it needs no window.
- **Refuted.** The chain-excluded lemma: linear surplus, `H_1(M) = {0}`, and for all large `m < n` the upper window `(S_m, a_(m+2))` disjoint from the lower window `(S_n - a_(n+1), a_(n+2) - a_(n+1))`, make a multiset complete. Witness: `a_1, a_2, a_3 = 4, 5, 6` and `a_(n+2) = S_n - y_n - y_(n+1)` for `n >= 2`, `y_n = 2 - (floor(n/2) mod 2)`, so `E = 4, 5, 6, 7, 12, 18, 31, 50, 80, 129, ...`. No window: `a_(n+2) < S_n` for `n >= 2`, so every upper window from `m = 2` on is empty and the chain condition holds. Spectrum: the recurrence at `n + 1` minus the recurrence at `n` gives `a_(n+3) - a_(n+2) - a_(n+1) = y_n - y_(n+2)`, which is `1` or `-1`, so `theta` in `H_1(E)` has `norm(theta) <= norm(a_(n+3) theta) + norm(a_(n+2) theta) + norm(a_(n+1) theta) -> 0`. Surplus: `S_n - a_(n+1) = a_n + y_(n-1) + y_n >= a_n` for `n >= 3`, and `a_(n+1) <= 2 a_n + 1` by the last identity, so `eps = 1/2` works with a constant. Incomplete: `x_n = a_(n+1) + y_n` is missing from `P_(n+1)` for every `n >= 2`, by induction: `x_2 = 7` is no sum of `{4, 5, 6}`, and a sum of `x_(n+1) = a_(n+2) + y_(n+1)` in `P_(n+2)` either uses `a_(n+2)`, leaving `y_(n+1)` below every term, or lies in `P_(n+1)`, where `x_(n+1) = S_n - y_n = S_(n+1) - x_n` is missing with `x_n` by symmetry. For `n >= 3`, `x_n < a_(n+2)`, so `8, 14, 20, 32, 51, 82, ...` are no sums of `E`; each comes by the pair route, with `g = x_(n-1)` and `y = y_n`.
- **Verified** (verb `chainless 27`, under `0.1` s, pinned by a crate test): its `38` terms up to `2^27` open no window at `n >= 2`, the least of `2 (S_n - a_(n+1)) - a_(n+1)` over `n >= 1` is `-7`, `a_(n+3) - a_(n+2) - a_(n+1)` takes only `-1` and `1` for `n >= 2`, the `34` values `a_(n+1) + y_n` with `3 <= n <= 36`, those below the last term, are non-sums, and of the `38` non-sums below the last term `3` lie below `a_1` and `35` come by the pair route, none by the window route.
- **Verified** (verbs `deep 10 6 31 4`, `21.3` s on `4` threads, and `wcell`): the power multisets make their largest non-sum by the pair route. Over the `30` minimal base sets in `[3, 10]`, `F(D, k)` comes by the window route in `7` cells at `k = 1`, `1` at `k = 2`, `2` at `k = 3` and by the pair route in every certified cell from `k = 4` on, `30` at `k = 4`, `28` at `k = 5`, `2` at `k = 6`; at `k = 4` the pair route carries `128689970` non-sums against `9859830` by the window route, and over the `23` cells `deep` certifies at `k = 5`, `1598272529` against `83741167`. `{3, 4, 6}` makes `F` by the window route at `k = 1, 2, 3` and by the pair route at `k = 4`.
- So a criterion whose window clause a windowless multiset satisfies cannot reach `{3, 4, 5}`: the incompleteness proofs above run the window route for the Fibonacci set and `floor(2 (5/3)^n)` and the pair route for `E`, and the power multisets use both, the pair route for every `F` of the census from `k = 4` on. ## The windowed lemma

- **Proved (the chain).** Let `a` be sorted and `n_0 >= 1`, with `a_(n+2) = S_n - u_n - u_(n+1)` for every `n >= n_0`, each `u_(n+1)` negative or a positive integer missing from `P_(n+1)`, and `x_n = a_(n+1) + u_n` in `[0, S_(n+1)]`. If `x_(n_0)` is missing from `P_(n_0 + 1)`, then every `x_n`, `n >= n_0`, is missing from `P_(n+1)`, and is no sum whenever `x_n < a_(n+2)`. Proof: `x_(n+1) = a_(n+2) + u_(n+1) = S_n - u_n = S_(n+1) - x_n` is missing from `P_(n+1)` with `x_n`, by symmetry, and `x_(n+1) - a_(n+2) = u_(n+1)` is negative or missing from `P_(n+1)`, so `x_(n+1)` is missing from `P_(n+2) = P_(n+1) U (P_(n+1) + a_(n+2))`; an integer below `a_(n+2)` is a sum only of terms in `P_(n+1)`. Along such a sequence a window opens at `n` exactly when `u_n + u_(n+1) < 0`, `x_(n+1)` comes by the pair route with `g = x_n` when `u_(n+1) >= 0` and `x_(n+1) < a_(n+3)`, and `a_(j+3) - a_(j+2) - a_(j+1) = u_j - u_(j+2)` for `j >= n_0`. The multiset `E` above is the case `u_n = y_n`.
- **Refuted.** The windowed lemma: linear surplus, `H_1(M) = {0}`, windows `a_(n+2) > S_n` at infinitely many `n`, and for all large `m < n` the upper window `(S_m, a_(m+2))` disjoint from the lower window `(S_n - a_(n+1), a_(n+2) - a_(n+1))`, make a multiset complete. Witness: `V`, with `a_1, a_2, a_3 = 4, 5, 6` and `a_(n+2) = S_n - u_n - u_(n+1)` for `n >= 2`, where `u_n = 2` at `n = 0 mod 5` from `n = 5`, `u_n = -x_(n-2)` at `n = 1 mod 5` from `n = 6`, `u_n = x_(n-3)` at `n = 2 mod 5` from `n = 7`, and `u_n = 1` otherwise, `x_n = a_(n+1) + u_n`; so `V = 4, 5, 6, 7, 13, 19, 47, 54, 86, 153, 238, 546, ...`.
- Shape: for `n = 5q >= 5`, `x_(n-1) = a_n + 1`, and the identity of the chain lemma reads `a_(n+1) = a_n + a_(n-1) - 1`, `a_(n+2) = a_(n+1) + 2 a_n + 2`, `a_(n+3) = a_(n+2) + a_(n+1) - a_n + 1`, `a_(n+4) = a_(n+3) + a_(n+2) - a_n - 2`, `a_(n+5) = a_(n+4) + a_(n+3) + a_n`. Every increment is positive, so `V` is sorted, and `a_(j+1) <= 3 a_j + 2` for all `j`.
- Windows: `a_(n+2) - S_n = -(u_n + u_(n+1))` is `a_n - 1` at `n = 0 mod 5`, `n >= 5`, and `0, -a_n - 2, -2, -3` at the next four, and negative at `n = 2, 3, 4`; so from `n = 2` on a window opens exactly at the multiples of `5`, of width `a_n - 1 >= (a_(n+2) - 5)/4`, since `a_(n+2) <= 4 a_n + 1`. No chain: for windows `m < n`, `m <= n - 5`, and the upper window of `m` ends at `a_(m+2) <= a_(n-3) <= S_(n-2)`, below `S_n - a_(n+1) = S_(n-2) + 1`, where the lower window of `n` begins.
- Surplus: `S_j - a_(j+1) = a_j + u_(j-1) + u_j` for `j >= 3`, which over a period is `a_n + 3, a_(n-1), a_(n+2), a_(n+3) + a_n + 2, a_(n+4) + 2`, and `8, 9` at `j = 3, 4`; with `a_(n+2) <= 4 a_n + 1 <= 12 a_(n-1) + 1`, since `a_n = a_(n-1) + a_(n-2) + a_(n-5) < 3 a_(n-1)` at `n = 0 mod 5`, `n >= 10`, by the last identity of the shape, and `a_5 = 13 <= 21`, this gives `12 (S_j - a_(j+1)) >= a_(j+1) - 2` for every `j >= 3`. Spectrum: `a_(j+3) - a_(j+2) - a_(j+1) = u_j - u_(j+2) = -1` at every `j = 3 mod 5`, so `theta` in `H_1(V)` has `norm(theta) <= norm(a_(j+3) theta) + norm(a_(j+2) theta) + norm(a_(j+1) theta) -> 0`.
- Incomplete: the chain lemma applies with `n_0 = 2`. The positive `u_(n+1)` are `1` and `2`, below `a_1`, and `x_(n-1)` at `n = 0 mod 5`, which the induction has already put outside `P_n`, and `x_(n-1) = a_n + 1 < a_(n+1)`, so it is missing from `P_(n+2)`; `x_2 = 7` is no sum of `{4, 5, 6}`; every `x_i` lies in `[0, S_(i+1)]`, since `x_i = S_(i-1) - 2` where `u_i < 0` and `0 < u_i <= a_(i-2) + 1 <= S_i` elsewhere; and `a_(n+2) - x_n = a_n + u_(n-1) - u_n - u_(n+1)` is positive for every `n >= 3`. So every `x_n`, `n >= 3`, is no sum of `V`, and `x_(n+1) = S_n - 2` at `n = 0 mod 5` grows without bound.
- **Verified** (verb `windowed 27`, under `0.1` s, pinned by a crate test): the `37` terms of `V` up to `2^27` are sorted and open `7` windows from `n = 2` on, exactly at `n = 5, 10, ..., 35`, the least of `(a_(n+2) - S_n)/a_(n+2)` over them `0.255`, truncated, with no chained pair, and every one holding a non-sum; the least of `12 (S_n - a_(n+1)) - a_(n+1)` over `n >= 3` is `37`, the largest `a_(n+1) - 3 a_n` is `-7`, `a_(n+3) - a_(n+2) - a_(n+1)` is `-1` at every `n = 3 mod 5` from `3`; the `33` values `x_n` with `n >= 3` below `a_(n+2)` are non-sums, the first `8, 14, 21, 33, 68, 87, 154, 240`; of the `1352` non-sums below the last term, `3` lie below `a_1`, `494` come by the window route and `855` by the pair route.
- So the windowed lemma falls like the chain-excluded lemma: `V` opens windows at a fifth of its steps, of width at least `(a_(n+2) - 5)/4`, where `M_k({3, 4, 5})` opens them at `27` of `123` terms at each `k = 1..4`, keeps consecutive ratios at most `3 + 2/a_j`, and makes non-sums by both routes, as the power multisets do. The forced variants of `E` heal because their terms ignore the chain; `V` sets each term from a chain hole. Both `E` and `V` live on the deficit `S_n - a_(n+1)` landing on a sum of two holes of `P_n`; the next section measures where the holes of `P_n` sit for the power multisets.

## The deepest hole

- `G_n` is the set of integers of `[0, S_n/2]` missing from `P_n`, so `T_n = 1 + max G_n`, and `T_n = 0` when `G_n` is empty; `R_n = S_n - a_(n+1)` is the deficit; `T` rises at `n` when `T_(n+1) > T_n`. A missing integer `x` of `P_(n+1)` is born at `n` when `S_n/2 < x <= S_(n+1)/2`, and every positive integer has exactly one such `n`.
- **Proved (the birth law).** An integer `x` with `S_n/2 < x <= S_(n+1)/2` is missing from `P_(n+1)` exactly when `x = S_n - g` with `g` missing from `P_n`, `R_n <= 2g < S_n`, and `R_n - g` missing from `P_n`, a negative value counting as missing; every integer of `[0, S_n/2]` missing from `P_(n+1)` lies in `G_n`. Proof: `P_(n+1) = P_n U (P_n + a_(n+1))`, so `x` is missing exactly when `x` and `x - a_(n+1)` are missing from `P_n`; for `x <= S_n/2` the first puts `x` in `G_n`; for `x > S_n/2`, write `g = S_n - x < S_n/2`, which by symmetry is missing from `P_n` exactly when `x` is, negative when `x > S_n`; then `x - a_(n+1) = R_n - g`, and `x <= S_(n+1)/2` reads `2g >= R_n`.
- **Proved (the jump).** If `T` rises at `n`, then `S_n - T_n + 2 <= T_(n+1) <= S_(n+1)/2 + 1` and `R_n <= 2 T_n - 2`. If `R_n < T_n - 1 < S_n/2`, then `T` rises at `n`. Proof: the missing integers of `P_(n+1)` up to `S_n/2` lie in `G_n`, below `T_n`, so a rise is a birth `T_(n+1) - 1 = S_n - g` with `g <= T_n - 1`, negative or in `G_n`, and `R_n <= 2g`; conversely `g = T_n - 1`, in `G_n` or `-1`, is below `S_n/2` and above `R_n`, so `R_n - g < 0` and `x = S_n - g` is born, above `S_n/2 > T_n - 1`.
- **Proved (after a rise).** If `T` rises at `n`, then `R_(n+1) <= S_n <= 2 T_(n+1) - 2`, so the interval step fails at `n + 1`; and the certificate at index `N` closes at least two steps after the last rise. Proof: `R_(n+1) = S_n + a_(n+1) - a_(n+2) <= S_n`, and `2 T_(n+1) - 2 >= 2 S_n - 2 T_n + 2 >= S_n` since `2 T_n <= S_n + 2`. The certificate needs `R_N >= 2 T_N - 1`, from its first step or, when the surplus clause is already empty at `N`, from the surplus lemma, and no rise follows `N`, since `P_n` contains `[T_N, S_n - T_N]` for every `n >= N` and `T_N - 1 = F` stays missing.
- **Proved (the route of `F`).** Let `M_k(D)` be complete and `b` the step at which `F = F(D, k)` is born, `S_b < 2F <= S_(b+1)`, so `F = S_b - g` with `g` as in the birth law. If `F >= a_(b+1)`, then `F` comes by the pair route. If `F` comes by the window route, then `F < a_(b+1)`, `g > R_b` and `T_b - 1 > R_b`. Proof: if `F >= a_(b+1)`, its index `j`, with `a_(j+1) <= F < a_(j+2)`, has `j >= b`, and `g = S_b - F >= 0` since `2F <= S_b + a_(b+1) <= S_b + F`, so `S_j - F >= g >= 0`, the pair route. If `F < a_(b+1)`, then `g = S_b - F > R_b`, and either `g >= 0` lies in `G_b`, so `T_b - 1 >= g > R_b`, or `R_b < g < 0 <= T_b`.
- **Verified** (verb `jumps 10 6 31 4`, `20.1` s on `4` threads, `W = 2^31`): on every step of the `173` cells the run reaches, each minimal base set in `[3, 10]` from `k = 1` up to its first level capped at `W = 2^31`, at most `6`, no rise breaks the bounds of the jump, no step with `R_n < T_n - 1 < S_n/2` fails to rise, and no step after a rise has `R_(n+1) > 2 T_(n+1) - 2`. Over the `145` certified cells, `F >= a_(b+1)` in `17, 27, 27, 26, 22, 2` at `k = 1..6`, and `F` comes by the window route in `7, 1, 2, 0, 0, 0`, each with `T_b - 1 > R_b`; `F` is born at the last rise of `T` in `111`; the certificate closes `2, 3, 4, 5, 6` steps after the last rise in `61, 52, 27, 3, 2` cells; `T` rises at every step from the entry of the last base `max d^k` to its last rise in `21, 23, 23, 24, 17, 2`.
- The falsification run: `T_n / a_(n+1)` after the last base enters peaks at `1.9430`, at `{3, 6, 7, 9, 10}`, `k = 5`, and every certified cell with `k >= 2` passes `1.1727` (`{3, 5, 8, 10}`, `k = 3`), so no bound `T_n <= c a_(n+1)` with `c < 1.17` holds along the census from the entry of the last base, and a hole bound has to wait for the run of births to end. At the cut, each of the `28` capped cells the run reaches, `{3, 4, 5}` at `k = 6` among them, has every rise of `T` in one run still open at the cap, of `28` to `37` rises; `{3, 4, 5}` at `k = 6` stands at `30`.
- So the deepest hole never creeps: it stays, falls, which needs `a_(n+1) <= T_n - 1` to fill `T_n - 1`, or jumps past the middle of `P_n`, and the step after a jump is never an interval step; the middle of `P_n` stays holed while the births run. The pair route makes `F` in the `26` cells at `k = 4` and `22` at `k = 5` where `F >= a_(b+1)` by the route lemma; the other `4` and `1` come by the pair route from an index below `b`, which the lemma does not explain. Completeness needs a step that ends the run: by the jump, one with `T_n - 1 <= R_n` or `2 T_n - 2 = S_n` and no birth; when `T_n - 1 <= R_n`, the birth law says no birth exactly when `R_n` is no sum `g + h` of members of `G_n` with `h <= g < S_n/2`; then the certificate needs `R_N >= 2 T_N - 1` at some later `N`. A proof uniform in `k` has to produce that step, and `E` and `V` are built so that it never comes.

## The sigma-one set

- `{3, 4, 7}` is the one base set in `[3, 12]` with `sigma = 1` and `gcd = 1`. Here `C_k = (3^(k+1) + 2 4^k + 7^k)/6`, an integer: `4, 18, 92, 526` at `k = 1..4`. The surplus lemma gives only `S_m - a_(m+1) >= -C_k`, so the certificate above never closes; an exact deficit and one bound on `abs(3^a - 4^b)` close it level by level.
- **Proved (the deficit at sigma one).** Let `D = {3, 4, 7}`, `k >= 1`, `X = a_(m+1)` with base `d(X)`, and `d^(J_d)` the least power `d^j >= X` with `j >= k`. Then `S_m - X = sum_(d != d(X)) (d^(J_d) - X)/(d - 1) - C_k`, and both terms of the sum are positive. Proof: powers of `3`, `4` and `7` with positive exponents are pairwise distinct, so `S_m` is the sum of the powers below `X`, which is `(d^(J_d) - d^k)/(d - 1)` per base; `X = sum_d X/(d - 1)` since `sigma = 1`; and `d^(J_d) = X` exactly at `d = d(X)`.
- [Ellison 1971](https://www.numdam.org/item/STNB_1970-1971____A10_0/), read at source, Theorem 3 on page 12-05: `abs(2^x - 3^y) > 2^x e^(-x/10)` for all positive integers `x, y` with `x` outside `S = {1, ..., 11, 13, 14, 16, 19, 27}`. His proof bounds every solution of `0 < abs(x log 2 - y log 3) < e^(-x/20)` below `10^160` by Baker's 1968 theorem, then clears `300 <= x <= 10^160` with the continued fraction of `log 2/log 3`.
- **Proved (the tight certificate).** Let `D = {3, 4, 7}` and `k >= 1`. Suppose some `n` has `T = T_n <= a_(n+1)`. Put `L = C_k + 2T - 1`, and let `B >= 8` be an integer with `(B + 1)(ln 4 - 1/5) >= ln(3L)`. If `a_(m+1) <= S_m - 2T + 1` for every `m >= n` with `a_(m+1) <= 4^B`, then `M_k(D)` is complete and `F(D, k) = T - 1`.
- Proof: take `m >= n` with `X = a_(m+1) > 4^B` and suppose the step fails there. Then `S_m - X <= 2T - 2`, so the sum in the deficit lemma is below `L`, and each of its two positive terms is below `L`. Let `3^a` and `4^b` be the least powers `>= X` with exponents `>= k`. If `d(X) = 7`, both lie in `(X, X + 3L)`. If `X = 3^a`, then `0 < 4^b - 3^a < 3L`. If `X = 4^b`, then `0 < 3^a - 4^b < 2L`. In every case `0 < abs(4^b - 3^a) < 3L` and `4^b >= X > 4^B`, so `b >= B + 1 >= 9`. Then `x = 2b >= 18` is even and outside `S`, and Ellison gives `abs(2^(2b) - 3^a) > 2^(2b) e^(-b/5) = e^(b (ln 4 - 1/5)) >= 3L`, a contradiction. So the step holds at every `m >= n`. `P_n` contains `[T, S_n - T]` by the definition of `T` and symmetry, so the interval step makes every integer `>= T` a sum. `T - 1 < a_(n+1)` is missing from `P_n`, so it is no sum.
- **Proved (exact at sigma one).** `M_k({3, 4, 7})` is complete if and only if the tight certificate exists at some `n`, so a level the run leaves open is open only for the cap. Proof: as in the gap proof, with `sigma beta = beta < 2`, there are arbitrarily large terms `N = a_(n+1)` with `S_n < beta N < 2 a_(n+1)`, where `P_n` and `FS(M_k(D))` agree on `[0, S_n/2]`; once `S_n > 2F`, `T_n = F + 1 <= a_(n+1)`, so `L` and `B` stop moving, and once `N > 4^B` the finite clause is empty.
- **Verified** (verb `tight 4 31`, `0.4` s): the tight certificate closes at `k = 1, 2, 3, 4`. `F({3, 4, 7}, k)` reads `581, 3982888, 166025260, 14961204826`, with `37, 5207, 391070, 23496512` positive non-sums up to `F`. It closes at `a_n = 3^7, 3^15, 4^15, 4^18`, with `B = 8, 16, 19, 23`; the step holds on every term from `a_(n+1)` up to `4^B`, the least slack `S_m - 2T + 1 - a_(m+1)` being `553, 2299181, 848028876, 30780137790`; and `(B + 1)(ln 4 - 1/5) - ln(3L)` is at least `2.516`. The value `581` at `k = 1` is the one Burr, Erdos, Graham and Li state; `k = 2, 3, 4` appear in no source read here, the site's "proved it for `{3, 4, 7}`" reading their single level `s = 1` as the whole question. Controls: `tight 4 20` returns the same cells at `W = 2^20`; a plain knapsack returns `F` at `k = 1, 2` (verb `set`, and a crate test that also matches the count of non-sums); a meet in the middle finds `F` the only non-sum in `[F, F + 63]` at `k = 3, 4` (verb `split`).
- By the monotone lemma, every base set containing `{3, 4, 7}` is complete at `k <= 4`, with no bound on its other elements.
