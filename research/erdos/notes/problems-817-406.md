## Problem 817 at n = 7

- `g_3(n)` is the least `N` such that `[1, N]` holds an `n`-set `A` whose subset sums `<A>`, `0` included, contain no non-trivial 3-term progression; Korsky's preprint, read at source, proves this equivalent to the `3^n` sums `sum c_i a_i`, `c_i` in `{0, 1, 2}`, being distinct (Proposition 4.1), an admissible set, and the lower bound `(T_n - 1)/2 + sum_(j < n) T_j` (Theorem 1.1), `T_j` the central trinomial coefficient, which evaluates to `419` at `n = 7` with `T_7 = 393`, a value the paper does not print; on the discussion thread, read at source, a post gives `g_3(n + 1) <= 3 g_3(n)` through `A -> {1} U 3A`, exact values `1, 3, 8, 22, 60, 168` to `n = 6` by exhaustive search, and `419 <= g_3(7) <= 504`, and its last post the witness below and `g_3(7) <= 474`, with no exact value at `n = 7`; the OEIS entry A399720 stops at `168`. The powers of `3` are the base-3 design `{0, 1}`, admissible at every `n` and optimal only to `n = 2`.
- The search picks the elements from the top down with the ternary sums as one bit array: `a` extends a set `H` exactly when `a` and `2a` avoid the differences of its sums, the `r` elements still owed lie below the least chosen one, which must exceed `g_3(r)`, and the variance `(2/3) sum a_i^2` of a uniform ternary sum is at least that of `3^n` distinct integers, `sum a_i^2 >= (9^n - 1)/8`.
- **Verified** (verb `ternary 6 200 8`, `5.9` s on `8` threads, exhaustive): `g_3(n) = 1, 3, 8, 22, 60, 168` at `n = 1..6`, the thread's values, each `g_3(r)` of the pruning computed by the run before it; the witness at `n = 6` is `{107, 145, 159, 162, 164, 168}`, a second extremal set beside the thread's `{107, 145, 159, 162, 166, 168}`.
- **Verified** (verb `band 419 475 70 8`, `2.8` s): `A = {302, 409, 447, 459, 465, 466, 474}` is admissible, its `2187` ternary sums distinct and its `128` subset sums free of 3-term progressions, checked from the definition; so `g_3(7) <= 474`, the upper end of the thread's current `419 <= g_3(7) <= 474`, which the sweep below makes exact; with the thread's step `g_3(n) <= 474 3^(n - 7)` for `n >= 7`, `g_3(n)/3^n < 0.216736`.
- **Verified** (verb `band 419 473 100 8`, `114` s): no admissible 7-set with largest element `N` in `[419, 473]` has its six largest elements in `[N - 100, N]`, so a set beating `474` has a second-smallest element below `N - 100`, unlike every extremal set printed on the thread or here; the families `{0, 1, 3, 8, 22, 60}` and `{0, 2, 6, 9, 23, 61}` of offsets from the top plus one free offset reach `477` and `475` at best (verb `offsets 420 504`).
- The concentration cut is a window-density bound. Put `M = 3^n`, `phi(u) = (1 + 2 cos u)/3`, the characteristic function of `c - 1` for `c` uniform in `{0, 1, 2}`; for `h > 0` let `g(s) = 1 - (s/h)^2` on `[-h, h]` and `w = g * g`, so `w(theta) = h W(abs(theta)/h)` with `W(t) = 16/15 - 4t^2/3 + 2t^3/3 - t^5/30` on `[0, 2]` and `w = 0` past `2h`.
- **Proved (the concentration cut).** Let `H` be a set of positive integers, `r >= 1`, `X >= r`, `n = abs(H) + r`, `h = pi/(3X)`, `K(theta) = prod_(a in H) phi(theta a)`, `U(theta) = prod_(j < r) phi(theta (X - j))` and `K^- = max(-K, 0)`. If `int w (K U - K^- (1 - U)) > 2 pi w(0)/M` over `[-2h, 2h]`, then no `r` distinct positive integers `<= X` complete `H` to an admissible set. Proof: let `H U L` be admissible and `Y = sum e_i a_i` with `e` uniform in `{-1, 0, 1}^n`; `Y` is a uniform ternary sum minus its mean `sum a_i`, so its `M` values are distinct integers. `psi(y) = (1/2pi) int w(theta) e^(i theta y) d theta = (1/2pi) (int g(s) e^(isy) ds)^2 >= 0`, and Poisson summation gives `sum_(y in Z) psi(y) = sum_k w(2 pi k) = w(0)`, the support `[-2h, 2h]` lying inside `(-2pi, 2pi)`; so `E psi(Y) <= w(0)/M`. But `E psi(Y) = (1/2pi) int w K V`, `V = prod_(l in L) phi(theta l)`; on `abs(theta) <= 2h` every `theta l` lies in `[0, 2pi/3]`, where `phi` falls from `1` to `0`, and the `(j + 1)`-th largest element of `L` is at most `X - j`, so `U <= V <= 1` and `K V >= K U - K^- (1 - U)`; with `w >= 0` this makes `E psi(Y)` exceed `w(0)/M`.
- The verb evaluates `int w K U` by composite Simpson on at least `64` cells, less the error bound `2h step^4 max abs(f'''')/180`, `abs(f'''') <= sum_j binomial(4, j) max abs(w^(j)) B^(4 - j)` with `B` the sum of `H` and of the `X - j`, since the `j`-th derivative of `phi(theta b)` is at most `b^j`, and `max abs(W^(j)) = 16/15, 0.93, 8/3, 4, 8` for `j = 0..4`; it bounds `int w K^- (1 - U)` above cell by cell by `w` at the left end, the product of the largest `abs(phi)` at the ends, `1` where a turning point may lie inside, and `1 - U` at the right end, on the cells where a factor of `K` may be negative; and it cuts only past a relative margin `10^-9`. At each node owing `r >= 3` elements below the least chosen `m`, a binary search finds the largest `X < m` it can certify and skips every candidate `<= X`. The variance bound sees only `sum a_i^2`; the cut sees the sums pile up at the centre, which is what the spread-out sets that fill the tree fail.
- The new search holds `D(H) = {sum d_i h_i : d_i in {-2, ..., 2}}` as one signed bit array, so a candidate costs two bit reads, and finishes the last two elements word-parallel: `x > y` complete `H` exactly when `y`, `x - y`, `x + y` lie in the set of `z` with `z, 2z` outside `D(H)` and `y +- 2x`, `2y +- x` lie outside `D(H)`.
- **Verified** (verb `sweep 7 169 473 8`, `342` s on `8` threads, exhaustive): no admissible 7-set has its largest element in `[169, 473]`, and none has it below `169` since `g_3(6) = 168`; so `g_3(7) = 474`, attained by the set above. The run prunes by `g_3(r)` for `r <= 6` from `ternary`, the variance bound and the concentration cut, and needs no lower bound from the literature; the last top, `N = 473`, visits `47633835` nodes owing two elements in `13.0` s. The search without the cut finds no set with top up to `348` either (verb `tally 7 169 348 8 1`, `1.5` s), so only `[349, 473]` rests on the cut; there the plain `probe` route is the independent check where the box allows, and `tally` the control.
- **Verified** (verb `tally 7 LO HI 8 1` over `[169, 348]`, `[349, 445]`, `[446, 458]`, `[459, 467]`, `[468, 473]`, `[474, 474]`, `1.5`, `582.9`, `353.3`, `334.8`, `273.2` and `48.2` s): the search without the cut also finds no set with largest element up to `473` and only the witness at `474`, so `g_3(7) = 474` rests on the plain search and the cut is a speed device; its double-precision test does not touch the value.
- **Verified** (verb `tally 7 474 474 8 0`, `12.4` s): `{302, 409, 447, 459, 465, 466, 474}` is the only admissible 7-set with largest element `474`.
- Controls (verb `tally 6 168 176 8 1`, `0.3` s, and two crate tests): the `153` admissible 6-sets with largest element in `[168, 176]` are listed alike with and without the cut, and the cut fires on no prefix of any of them, nor of the extremal sets at `n = 5, 6, 7`; `168` has exactly the two extremal sets `{107, 145, 159, 162, 164, 168}` and `{107, 145, 159, 162, 166, 168}`.
- So `g_3(n) = 1, 3, 8, 22, 60, 168, 474` at `n = 1..7` (verbs `ternary 6 200 8` and `sweep 7 169 473 8`). At `n = 8` only the thread's step applied to the witness above and Korsky's formula bound `g_3(8)`; this page computes nothing there.
- `n = 8` is out of this box: the elements triple and the tree gains a level, while the time per top at `n = 7` already doubles about every `20`; it wants a cut at the last two levels as cheap as the bit scan it would replace.

## Problem 406 in the 3-adic integers

- The site asks whether only finitely many powers of `2` have their base-3 digits in `{0, 1}`; it records `1`, `4`, `256` as the only ones known, Saye's check that every `2^n` with `16 <= n <= 5.9 10^21` carries all three digits, and Narkiewicz's bound `1.62 x^(log_3 2)` on the count of such `n <= x`; it is open with no proof claim, and its five-comment thread adds one heuristic remark and references, no new bound (both read at source).
- `Sigma` is the set of 3-adic integers with every digit in `{0, 1}`, the closure of the base-3 design `{0, 1}` in `Z_3`. For 3-adic integers `M_1, ..., M_r`, `C(1, M_1, ..., M_r)` is the set of `lambda` in `Sigma` with every `M_i lambda` in `Sigma`, and `E^(k)` is the set of `lambda` in `Z_3` with `lambda 2^n` in `Sigma` for at least `k` values of `n`.
- [Lagarias 2009](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0512006), read at source, proposes the sets `E^(k)` as the 3-adic relaxation of the problem, proves Theorem 1.4, at most `2 X^(log_3 2)` exponents `n <= X` with `lambda 2^n` in `Sigma` for each nonzero `lambda`, and writes that whether `dim_H E^(k) > 0` for every `k` already seems a subtle question, his lower bounds stopping at `k = 3`. [Abram and Lagarias 2014](https://arxiv.org/abs/1308.3133), read at source in arXiv v1 and in the journal text, write `E^(k)` as the union of the sets `2^(-m) C(1, 2^(m_1), ..., 2^(m_(k-1)))`, compute dimensions by the carry automaton below, print `0.228392` for `C(1, 2^2, 2^8)`, and state that it is unclear whether `dim_H E^(k) > 0` for any `k >= 4`; arXiv v1 adds that `C(1, 2^2, 2^8)` is the only component of `E^(3)` known to have positive dimension and that no `C(1, 2^(m_1), 2^(m_2), 2^(m_3))` of positive dimension is known, and the journal text drops both remarks and adds that `C(1, 2^6, 2^(2k))`, `8 <= 2k <= 14`, and `C(1, 2^2, 2^8, 2^(2k))`, `10 <= 2k <= 16`, have dimension `0`. [Abram, Bolshakov and Lagarias 2017](https://arxiv.org/abs/1508.05967), read at source, leave open whether `Gamma = lim_k dim_H E^(k)` is `0`.
- Since `1`, `4` and `256` lie in `Sigma`, a fourth power `2^n` in `Sigma` puts `1` in `E^(4)`: problem 406 in its strong form says `1` is not in `E^(4)`, and `E^(4) = {0}` would settle it.
- The generator is the carry automaton of Abram and Lagarias. Reading the digits of `lambda` from the bottom, the state is the vector of carries `N_i`; a digit `a` in `{0, 1}` is allowed when every `(N_i + M_i a) mod 3` is `0` or `1`, and the next carries are `floor((N_i + M_i a)/3)`. `C(1, M_1, ..., M_r)` is the set of infinite paths from the zero state, and its Hausdorff dimension is `log_3` of the largest spectral radius among the reachable strongly connected components, their Theorem 1.6 (2); the verb brackets each radius between the least and largest ratio of a power iterate, and a component with more edges than nodes carries two first-return cycles, printed as witness words.
- **Proved (odd exponents)**, Lagarias 2009 Lemma 4.1. If `M = 2 mod 3`, then `C(1, M) = {0}`, so a set `C(1, 2^m, ...)` with an odd `m` is `{0}` and only even exponents matter.
- **Proved (the prefix test).** Let `M_1, ..., M_r` be 3-adic units. Then `C(1, M_1, ..., M_r) = {0}` exactly when some length `D` has no `x < 3^D` with digits in `{0, 1}`, lowest digit `1`, and the `D` lowest digits of every `M_i x` in `{0, 1}`; and that condition reads only `M_i mod 3^D`. Proof: a nonzero `lambda` in the set is `3^v mu` with `mu` of lowest digit `1`, the digits of `M_i mu` are those of `M_i lambda` shifted down, so `mu` lies in the set and every prefix of `mu` passes; conversely the prefixes that pass are closed under truncation, so if some pass at every length, Konig's lemma on the binary tree gives an infinite branch, a nonzero `mu` in the set. The `D` lowest digits of `M_i x` are those of `(M_i mod 3^D) x`.
- So a triple of exponents whose prefix test dies by length `D` empties `C(1, 2^a, 2^b, 2^c)` for every triple congruent to it modulo `2 3^(D - 1)`, the order of `2` modulo `3^D`: one cell settles a residue class of triples.
- **Proved (almost every set is empty).** For `r >= 2` and `X >= 2`, at most `(3X/2)^((r + 1) log_3 2)` ordered `r`-tuples of even exponents in `[2, X]` have `C(1, 2^(m_1), ..., 2^(m_r)) != {0}`, against `floor(X/2)^r` tuples in all, and `(r + 1) log_3 2 < r` for `r >= 2`; at `r = 3` the exponent is `4 log_3 2 = 2.52372`, rounded up. Proof: write `m_i = 2 t_i`, `Y = X/2`, and take `D` with `P = 3^(D - 1)` in `[Y, 3Y)`. The powers of `4` are the `P` residues `1 mod 3` modulo `3^D`, so for a fixed `x` of lowest digit `1`, `t -> 4^t x mod 3^D` is a bijection from `Z/P` onto them, and exactly `2^(D - 1)` of them have all `D` digits in `{0, 1}`. Summing over the `2^(D - 1)` prefixes `x`, at most `2^((r + 1)(D - 1)) = P^((r + 1) log_3 2) < (3Y)^((r + 1) log_3 2)` tuples of classes `t_i mod P` let some prefix pass at length `D`; every other class is empty by the prefix test, and since `P >= Y` a class holds at most one tuple with `1 <= t_i <= Y`.
- **Verified** (verb `cantor`, under `0.01` s a set, the path counts matched by multiplying out in a crate test): Table 5.2 of Abram and Lagarias in arXiv v1, Table 6.1 on p. 389 of the journal. `dim_H C(1, 2^m)` reads `0.438018, 0.255959, 0.278004, 0.306871, 0.215198, 0.243998, 0.267110` at `m = 2, 4, ..., 14`, to six places, the radius at `m = 4` the plastic number `1.324717957`, and `dim_H C(1, 2^2, 2^8) = 0.228391`, the radius `1.285199033` the root of `x^6 - x^5 - 1` they name. Their entries agree within `4 10^-6` except at `m = 8`, where they print `0.287416` and write `256 = (10111)_3`; but `256 = (100111)_3`, and `C(1, 2^8)` has radius `1.400924990` and dimension `0.306871`, its `23460` admissible prefixes of length `28` counted alike by the `35`-state automaton and by multiplying out. The typo does not explain the value: `(10111)_3 = 94`, and `C(1, 94)` has dimension `0.324658` (verb `carry 94`); Abram, Bolshakov and Lagarias 2017, Table 7.1, give `C(1, 64)` as `0.278004` and have no entry at `256`.
- **Verified** (verbs `tuples 2 40 8 16777216`, `16.2` s on `8` threads, and `deaths 2 40 79 8`): of the `190` pairs of even exponents in `[2, 40]`, `177` have `C(1, 2^a, 2^b) = {0}`, `(2, 6)`, `(4, 6)` and `(12, 14)` a countable nonzero set, and `10` positive dimension: `(2, 8), (2, 18), (2, 24), (2, 32), (2, 38), (16, 18), (22, 24), (24, 26), (28, 30), (36, 38)`, of dimensions `0.228391, 0.103480, 0.083861, 0.074977, 0.069649, 0.087278, 0.067684, 0.067080, 0.067489, 0.068997`. None passes `C(1, 2^2, 2^8)`, so the lower bound `log_3 beta_1 = 0.228391` for `dim_H E^(3)` stands in this box; arXiv v1 of Abram and Lagarias names `(2, 8)` the one component of `E^(3)` known to have positive dimension, and their journal text names none. At `(2, 18)` the zero state carries the cycle `101010100000100000000000`, low digit first, so `x = 532261 = (1000001010101)_3` has `4 x = (11000011111111)_3` and `2^18 x = (111100011000111000001101)_3`, all three in the base-3 design `{0, 1}`, as `1`, `4` and `256` are for `(2, 8)`.
- **Verified** (verbs `deaths 3 3000 79 8`, `19.1` s on `8` threads, and `tuples 3 124 8 16777216`, under `0.1` s; a crate test checks that the prefix test and the automaton agree on every pair and triple of even exponents up to `24`): each of the `561375500` triples of even exponents `2 <= a < b < c <= 3000` has `C(1, 2^a, 2^b, 2^c) = {0}`, its prefix test dying by length `50`, the deepest at `(2, 8, 332)`; up to `124` the automaton of each of the `37820` triples has at most `35` states and no cycle but the zero loop. So no nonzero `lambda` in `Z_3` has `lambda 2^n` in `Sigma` at four exponents within a span of `3000`: every component of `E^(4)` of span at most `3000` is `{0}`, and a positive dimension for `E^(4)`, or any nonzero point of it, needs a span past `3000`.
- **Verified** (verb `classes 3 D 8`, `10.0` s at `D = 7`): the classes of exponent triples modulo `2 3^(D - 1)` whose prefix test dies by length `D` are `0.691358, 0.818777, 0.894353, 0.937762, 0.963267` of all at `D = 3..7`, truncated, above the first-moment floor `1 - (16/27)^(D - 1)`, `0.648834, 0.791901, 0.876682, 0.926922, 0.956695`; each is a lower density of the triples with an empty set. Pairs reach `0.746077` at `D = 8` (verb `classes 2 8 8`), above `1 - (8/9)^7`.
- **Conjecture.** `dim_H C(1, 2^m) -> log_3(4/3) = 2 log_3 2 - 1 = 0.261859` as `m -> infinity` through the even integers. The readings at `m = 16, 18, ..., 38` lie in `[0.259662, 0.262985]` (verb `tuples 1 40 8 16777216`, `128.8` s, `16441441` states at `m = 38`, `m = 40` past the cap of `2^24` states), and `(4/3)^(D - 1)` is the first-moment count of the prefixes that pass at length `D` for one unit. Abram, Bolshakov and Lagarias leave open in their Section 7 whether the dimension of `C(1, M)` tends to `0` as the block count `b_3(M)` grows, and whether `b_3(2^n)` grows at all; along the powers of `2` to `m = 38` it stays above `0.2596`.
- The obstruction is the quantifier over all triples: the census is exact cell by cell and the first moment empties almost every class, but the classes that pass at every length form a closed set of Haar measure zero among triples of units, and whether it meets the triples `(4^s, 4^t, 4^u)` of integers `0 < s < t < u` is the question left; at `(4, 256, 4^u)` and `lambda = 1` it holds problem 406 itself.
