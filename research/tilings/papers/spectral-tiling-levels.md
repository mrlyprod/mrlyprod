Take an integer base `b` and a set `F` of digits. The numbers written in base `b` with `n` digits from `F` form a finite set `A_n`, the level `n`; scaled by `b^(-n)` the levels converge to a Cantor-type set, and the uniform measures on them converge to the self-similar measure `mu_{b,F}`. The measure is spectral when some set of frequencies `Lambda` makes the exponentials `e(lambda x) = exp(2 pi i lambda x)` an orthonormal basis of `L^2(mu_{b,F})`, as the integers do for Lebesgue measure on `[0, 1]`. This paper compares that property of the limit with a property of the levels: whether each `A_n` tiles the integers by translation. Coven and Meyerowitz read tiling off two conditions on the cyclotomic factors of a mask polynomial, and an index lemma reduces those conditions at every level to finitely many checks on `F`. When every level tiles and `|F|` is a prime power or a product `pq` of two distinct primes, `mu_{b,F}` is spectral: the proofs build Hadamard triples, in the window of Łaba and Wang where `F` is a scaled complementing set mod `b`, and outside it through the product forms of An and Lai, cut from the `p`-adic tree of `F` and from a fibre decomposition of its residues. At base 12 the digit set `{0, 1, 8, 9}` tiles at every level, is no multiple of a complementing set mod 12, has no spectrum in `12^(-n) Z` at any level, and its measure has the explicit spectrum `{0, 3/4} + 3 Lambda(12, {0, 3, 6, 9})`. In the other direction, a spectral measure whose levels are sets yields, for every `L`, `|F|^L` integers with orthogonal differences, which bounds `|F|` by a product over a covering set of primes; with `0` in `F` this proves that at five digits `mu_{b,F}` is spectral if and only if every level tiles; and since a spectrum meets finitely many cosets of `Q`, `mu_{b,{0,1,3,5,6}}`, the five-digit example An, He and Lai leave open, whose digit transform has no rational zero, is spectral at no integer base. At seven digits the vanishing sums of weight 7, read from Lam and Leung and from Poonen and Rubinstein, leave every relevant order even with at most two 2-adic exponents, and the bound closes the converse; at eleven and thirteen digits every relevant order carries a pair of primes that sums to the size, which closes it at every base with a small covering set; and an eleven-digit set at base 60 has covering bound 12, so the bound stops there, while a count of its residues still shows its measure is not spectral.

## 1. Introduction

The figure shows what a spectrum does. Each band is one exponential `e(lambda x)` measured against a moving frequency `xi`; the bands never overlap in the sense of orthogonality, and stacked they fill a flat strip of height one at every `xi` for the whole spectrum (Theorem 4.3), the figure stacking a finite part, which is Parseval's identity for every exponential and so completeness. The measure is that of `{0, 1, 8, 9}` at base 12, and the spectrum is not of the form a complementing set gives: half of it is shifted by `3/4`, and Section 4 explains why the shift is forced.

The question has a history. Jorgensen and Pedersen found that the Cantor measure of `{0, 2}` at base 4 is spectral while at odd base at most two exponentials are orthogonal. Łaba and Wang proved that a compatible pair, `F` scaled by `b^(-1)` together with a set `L` making a Hadamard matrix, gives a spectral measure (their Theorem 1.2), that a complementing set mod `b` whose size has at most two prime factors gives one (Theorem 1.4), and conjectured that a spectral `mu_{b,F}` has `F` a rational multiple of a complementing set mod `b` (Conjecture 3.1). Dai, He and Lau settled consecutive digits: `mu_{b,{0..N-1}}` is spectral if and only if `N | b`. An, He and Lai classified the spectral measures with at most four digits and left five or more open, naming `{0, 1, 3, 5, 6}`, whose transform vanishes only at irrational points. An and Lai built spectral measures from product-form Hadamard triples, among them digit sets that are no multiple of a complementing set. As Liu, Wang and Zheng quote it in their Section 1.1, as their Theorem 1.2, An and Wang's Theorem 1.7 takes a real ratio `0 < rho < 1`, primes `p, q` and a digit set `D = E_0 ⊕ (pq)^l E_1` with `l >= 0` and `E_0 ⊕ E_1 = {0..pq-1}`, a strict product-form digit set with respect to `pq`, and says that `mu_{rho,D}` is spectral if and only if `rho^(-1) = p^(l_1) q^(l_2) t_1` with `l_1, l_2, t_1` positive integers, `gcd(t_1, pq) = 1`, and `D` a complementing product-form digit set with respect to `N = p^(l_1) q^(l_2)`, in the sense Liu, Wang and Zheng give the term, exponents renamed: `D = D_0 ⊕ N^(n_1) D_1 ⊕ ... ⊕ N^(n_k) D_k` with `0 <= n_1 <= ... <= n_k` and `D_0 ⊕ ... ⊕ D_k` tiling `Z/N`. Their paper was not read for this one, no preprint of it being available; nothing below rests on it, and Section 6 says how Theorem 6.4 stands to the quoted statement.

The guess tested here replaces residues by tiling: `mu_{b,F}` is spectral exactly when every level of `F` tiles the integers. The residue form fails for tiling already at base 12: `{0, 1, 8, 9}` tiles at every level and no integer multiple of it tiles `Z/12` (Section 3). For the measure the paper proves the following, every hypothesis written into the statements of the sections named.

- If every level tiles and `|F|` is a prime power, `mu_{b,F}` is spectral (Theorem 5.2).
- If every level tiles and `|F| = pq`, `mu_{b,F}` is spectral (Theorem 6.4).
- In the Hadamard window, `b >= 3`, `0` in `F` and `F/gcd(F)` satisfying T1 and T2 with `lcm(S_{F/gcd(F)})` dividing `b`, a spectrum is `gcd(F)^(-1) Lambda(b, L)` (Theorem 3.1); outside it, a level-shift lemma gives explicit spectra at base 12 and base 30 (Theorem 4.3), and at base 12 no spectrum of any level lies in `12^(-n) Z` (Proposition 3.3).
- At four digits or fewer the classification of An, He and Lai is the every-level verdict (Proposition 7.1); the classification is theirs, the translation is the content here.
- If `0` is in `F`, `|F| = 5` and every level is a set, `mu_{b,F}` is spectral if and only if every level tiles (Theorem 8.8), and `mu_{b,{0,1,3,5,6}}` is spectral at no integer base (Corollary 8.4).
- If `0` is in `F`, `|F| = 7` and every level is a set, `mu_{b,F}` is spectral if and only if every level tiles (Theorem 9.5): a non-tiling set has every relevant order even, with at most two 2-adic exponents, so its covering bound is at most 4.
- If `0` is in `F`, `|F|` is 11 or 13, every level is a set, and some set `W` of primes of `b` meets every pair of `|F|` inside the primes of `b` with `prod_{q in W} q^(v_q(b)) < |F|`, then `mu_{b,F}` is spectral if and only if every level tiles (Theorem 10.3), in particular whenever `2^(v_2(b)) 3^(v_3(b)) < |F|`; the twelve bases below 500 the hypothesis misses at 11 begin at 60, where `{0, 10, 11, 20, 23, 35, 45, 47, 50, 55, 59}` has covering bound 12 without tiling, and its measure is not spectral by a count of its residues (Proposition 10.6).

## 2. Definitions and the inputs at source

Throughout, `b >= 2` is an integer and `F` a finite set of `k = |F| >= 2` non-negative integers. The level `n` is `A_n = {sum_{i<n} d_i b^i : d_i in F}`, counted with multiplicity, and its mask is `A_n(x) = prod_{i<n} F(x^(b^i))` with `F(x) = sum_{d in F} x^d`. When `F` lies in `{0..b-1}` every level is a set of `k^n` integers. `mu_{b,F}` is the infinite convolution of the uniform measures on `b^(-j) F`, `j >= 1`, the weak limit of the uniform measures on `b^(-n) A_n`, and `hat mu(xi) = prod_{j>=1} hat F(b^(-j) xi)/k` with `hat F(t) = sum_{f in F} e(f t)`.

**Definition 2.1 (zero orders).** `Z(F) = {m >= 2 : Phi_m divides F(x)}`, so that `hat F(a/m) = 0` with `gcd(a, m) = 1` exactly when `m` is in `Z(F)`. For a prime `p`, `Z_p(F) = {c >= 1 : p^c in Z(F)}` and `e_p = v_p(b)`. For a finite set `A` of integers, `S_A` is the set of prime powers `s` with `Phi_s | A(x)`.

**Definition 2.2 (tiling, T1, T2).** A finite set `A` tiles the integers when `A + C = Z` for some `C`, every integer written once. Coven and Meyerowitz define **T1**: `A(1) = prod_{s in S_A} Phi_s(1)`, and **T2**: `Phi_{s_1 ... s_m} | A(x)` whenever `s_1, ..., s_m` in `S_A` are powers of distinct primes. Their theorems, read at source: T1 and T2 imply that `A` tiles (Theorem A); a tile satisfies T1 (Theorem B1); a tile whose size has at most two prime factors satisfies T2 (Theorem B2). Since `prod_{s in S_A} Phi_s(x)` divides `A(x)` in `Z[x]`, `prod_{s in S_A} Phi_s(1)` always divides `A(1)`. "Every level tiles" means that every `A_n` is a set and tiles the integers.

**Lemma 2.3 (the index lemma).** `Phi_m | A_n(x)` if and only if `m/gcd(m, b^i)` is in `Z(F)` for some `i < n`.

*Proof.* A primitive `m`-th root `zeta` makes `zeta^(b^i)` a primitive root of order `m/gcd(m, b^i)`, and `A_n(zeta) = prod_{i<n} F(zeta^(b^i))` vanishes exactly when one factor does. □

For a prime power the lemma reads: `p^c` is in `S_{A_n}` exactly when `c` is in `Z_p(F)`, for `p` not dividing `b`, and exactly when `c - e_p i` is in `Z_p(F)` for some `i < n`, for `p | b`.

**Lemma 2.4 (T1 at every level).** Let every `A_n` be a set. T1 holds at every level if and only if (a) `F` satisfies T1, that is `k = prod_p p^(|Z_p(F)|)`; (b) `Z_p(F)` is empty for every prime `p` not dividing `b`; (c) for every `p | b` the elements of `Z_p(F)` are pairwise incongruent mod `e_p`. Call (a) to (c) the T1 condition. If every level tiles, the T1 condition holds and `k` divides `b`; if moreover `k` is a prime power, every level tiles if and only if the T1 condition holds; if `k` has two prime factors and every level tiles, every level satisfies T1 and T2.

*Proof.* By Lemma 2.3 the exponents of `p` in `S_{A_n}` are `Z_p(F)` for `p` not dividing `b`, and the union of the translates `Z_p(F) + e_p i`, `i < n`, for `p | b`. T1 at level `n` compares `v_p(k^n) = n v_p(k)` with the number of those exponents, for every `p`. Off the base the number is constant in `n`, so T1 at every level forces `v_p(k) = 0 = |Z_p(F)|`. On the base the union has at most `n |Z_p(F)|` elements, with equality for every `n` exactly when no two elements of `Z_p(F)` differ by a positive multiple of `e_p`, and level 1 gives `v_p(k) = |Z_p(F)|`. Conversely (a) to (c) give equality at every `p` and `n`. If every level tiles, Theorem B1 gives T1 at every level, and (c) gives `|Z_p(F)| <= e_p`, so `k | b`. At prime-power size T2 is vacuous at every level, and Theorems A and B1 give the equivalence; at two prime factors `|A_n| = k^n` has two prime factors, and Theorems B1 and B2 apply to each tiling level. □

**Lemma 2.5 (normalisation).** Replacing `F` by `F - t` or by `F/g` with `g = gcd(F)`, when `0` is in `F`, keeps `mu_{b,F}` spectral or not, every level a set or not, and every level tiling or not.

*Proof.* `mu_{b,F-t}` is a translate of `mu_{b,F}` and `mu_{b,F/g}` its image under `x -> x/g`, which carry spectra to spectra (scaled by `g`). The levels of `F - t` are translates of those of `F`, and those of `F/g` are those of `F` divided by `g`. A translate of a tile is a tile; if `A + C = Z` then `gA + (gC + {0..g-1}) = Z`, and if `gA + C = Z` then for each `r` the elements of `C` in `r + gZ` give `A + (C_r - r)/g = Z`. □

**Definition 2.6 (spectra and the completeness test).** A compactly supported probability measure `mu` is spectral with spectrum `Lambda` when `{e(lambda x) : lambda in Lambda}` is an orthonormal basis of `L^2(mu)`. Orthogonality of `e(lambda x)` and `e(lambda' x)` is `hat mu(lambda - lambda') = 0`. For an orthogonal family put `Q(xi) = sum_{lambda in Lambda} |hat mu(xi + lambda)|^2`, the Bessel sum of `e(-xi x)` against it; `Q <= 1` always, and `Q = 1` everywhere if and only if `Lambda` is a spectrum, since the exponentials span a dense subspace of `L^2(mu)` (the Jorgensen-Pedersen lemma, Jorgensen and Pedersen 1998; Łaba-Wang Lemma 2.3(b) for `Lambda(N, S)`). For a finite set `S` the uniform measure on `S` has a spectrum exactly when some `|S|` frequencies are pairwise orthogonal.

**Definition 2.7 (Hadamard triples).** For an integer `N >= 2` and finite sets `D, L` of integers with `|D| = |L|`, `(N, D, L)` is a Hadamard triple when `hat D((l - l')/N) = 0` for distinct `l, l'` in `L`, that is `|D|^(-1/2) [e(d l/N)]` is unitary; Łaba and Wang call `(N^(-1) D, L)` a compatible pair. The elements of `L` are then distinct mod `N`, and the triple depends on `L` mod `N` only. Write `Lambda(N, L) = {sum_{j<n} N^j l_j : n >= 1, l_j in L}`.

**Theorem 2.8 (Łaba and Wang, Theorem 1.2 with Lemma 2.2(b), (d), read at source).** If `(b^(-1) F, L)` is a compatible pair, `mu_{b,F}` is spectral; if moreover `gcd(F - F) = 1`, `0` is in `L` and `L` lies in `[2 - b, b - 2]`, then `Lambda(b, L)` is a spectrum of `mu_{b,F}`.

**Theorem 2.9 (An and Lai, Theorems 2.2 and 2.4, read at source).** A digit set in product form of their Definition 2.1, or in `K`-stage product form of their Definition 2.3, gives a spectral measure. Their Definition 2.1: `D = ⋃_{s<n} (a_s + N^r B_s)` with `r >= 0`, `A = {a_s}`, where `(N, A, L_1)` and every `(N, B_s, L_2)` are Hadamard triples with one `L_2` for all `s`, and every `(N, A ⊕ B_s, L_1 ⊕ L_2)` is a Hadamard triple. Their Definition 2.3: for positive integers `l_1, ..., l_k`, `D^(0) = E_0` and `D^(j) = ⋃_{d in D^(j-1)} (d + N^(l_1 + ... + l_j) E_j(d))`, where `(N, E_0, L_0)` and every `(N, E_j(d), L_j)` are Hadamard triples, and for every `1 <= m <= k` and every choice of the `d_j` the prefix `(N, E_0 ⊕ E_1(d_0) ⊕ ... ⊕ E_m(d_(m-1)), L_0 ⊕ ... ⊕ L_m)` and the suffix `(N, E_m(d_(m-1)) ⊕ ... ⊕ E_k(d_(k-1)), L_m ⊕ ... ⊕ L_k)` are Hadamard triples, `⊕` meaning that every sum is written once. Below, `N = b` and every `l_j = 1`; the proofs supply the Hadamard condition for every range of consecutive windows, which contains the single windows, the prefixes and the suffixes, and the generator checks the prefix and suffix conditions in exact arithmetic on every case it prints.

## 3. The Hadamard window and its edge

**Theorem 3.1.** Let `b >= 3`, `0` in `F`, `g = gcd(F)` and `F' = F/g`. If `F'` satisfies T1 and T2 and `lcm(S_{F'})` divides `b`, then `mu_{b,F}` is spectral with spectrum `g^(-1) Lambda(b, L)`, where `L = {sum_{s in S_{F'}} k_s b/s : 0 <= k_s < p_s}`, `p_s` the prime of `s`, with each element replaced by a representative mod `b` in `[2 - b, b - 2]`.

*Proof.* Since `0` is in `F'`, `gcd(F' - F') = gcd(F') = 1`. Each `b/s` is an integer since `s | b`, and the interval `[2 - b, b - 2]` holds `2b - 3 >= b` integers, so the representatives exist, with `0` kept. Take distinct `l, l'` in `L` with coefficients `k_s, k'_s`. Then `(l - l')/b = sum_s (k_s - k'_s)/s`. For one prime `p` with exponents `c_1 < ... < c_r` in `S_{F'}`, let `c*` be the largest where the coefficients differ; the sum over `p` is `u/p^(c*)` with `u = sum_i (k_i - k'_i) p^(c* - c_i)`, whose term at `c*` is prime to `p` and whose other terms are multiples of `p`. So `(l - l')/b = r/m` in lowest terms with `m` the product, over the primes where some coefficient differs, of the largest such `p^(c*)`. That `m` is an element of `S_{F'}` or a product of elements at distinct primes, so T2 puts `Phi_m` in the mask and `hat F'((l - l')/b) = 0`. T1 gives `|L| = prod_s p_s = |F'|`. So `(b^(-1) F', L)` is a compatible pair, Theorem 2.8 makes `Lambda(b, L)` a spectrum of `mu_{b,F'}`, and `mu_{b,F}` is its image under `x -> g x`. □

This is the proof of Łaba-Wang Theorem 1.4 with T2 assumed in place of their two-prime count. Its hypotheses make every level tile: each `c` in `Z_p(F')` is at most `e_p`, so the T1 condition holds, and a T2 instance at level `n` reduces through Lemma 2.3 at its largest index to a T2 instance of `F'`.

**Fact 3.2.** Of the 609 digit sets with `0` at bases 2 to 16 whose every level tiles, 607 carry a Hadamard triple `(b, F', L)` with `L` in `Z`, found by a clique search on `Z/b`; the other two are `{0, 1, 8, 9}` and `{0, 3, 8, 11}` at base 12. Domain: every digit set with `0` and at least two digits in `{0..b-1}`, `b = 2..16`. Generator: `lab/py/measure-spectra`, verb `census`.

The window does not reach `{0, 1, 8, 9}` at base 12. Its mask is `(1 + x)(1 + x^8) = Phi_2 Phi_16`, so `Z_2 = {1, 4}`, `e_2 = 2`, and `1`, `4` are incongruent mod 2: the T1 condition holds, the size `4` is a prime power, and every level tiles by Lemma 2.4. But `lcm(S_F) = 16` does not divide `12`, and no integer multiple `vF` tiles `Z/12`: scaling sends the exponents to `{1 + v_2(v), 4 + v_2(v)}` (Coven-Meyerowitz Lemma 1.4), while a tile of `Z/12` has every element of `S` dividing `12` (their Lemma 2.1), and `2^(4 + v_2(v))` never does. So `{0, 1, 8, 9}` is no rational multiple of a complementing set mod 12, the form Łaba-Wang Conjecture 3.1(c) predicts for a spectral measure, while its measure is spectral (Theorem 4.3). The window also cannot be reached by passing to a power of the base:

**Proposition 3.3.** No level `A_n` of `{0, 1, 8, 9}` at base 12 has a spectrum inside `12^(-n) Z`, so no Hadamard triple `(12^n, A_n, L)` with `L` in `Z` exists at any level.

*Proof.* A spectrum of the set `A_n` may be read mod 1, its points distinct there. For `gamma - gamma' = d/12^n` the order of `d/12^n` mod 1 is `m = 12^n/gcd(d, 12^n) = 2^a 3^c`, and orthogonality asks `m` in `Z(A_n)`. By Lemma 2.3 that holds exactly when `m/gcd(m, 12^i) = 2^((a-2i)_+) 3^((c-i)_+)` is `2` or `16` for some `i < n`, that is `a = 2i + 1` or `a = 2i + 4` with `c <= i`; with `a <= 2n` the possible `a` form the set `{1, 3, ..., 2n - 1} ∪ {4, 6, ..., 2n}` of `2n - 1` values, all positive. So a spectrum `Gamma`, as a subset of `Z/12^n`, maps injectively to `Z/2^(2n)`, two points with one image differing by an element of odd order, and the `2`-adic valuations `2n - a` of its differences take at most `2n - 1` values. A subset of `Z/2^(2n)` whose differences have their valuations in a set `V` has at most `2^|V|` points, its binary tree branching only at the levels of `V`; and `2^(2n - 1) < 4^n = |A_n|`. □

## 4. The level shift

**Lemma 4.1.** Let `mu = nu * sigma`, where `nu` is the uniform measure on a finite set `S` with a spectrum `Lambda_0` of `|S|` points, `sigma` has a spectrum `Gamma` containing `0`, and `gamma (s - s')` is an integer for every `gamma` in `Gamma - Gamma` and `s, s'` in `S`. Then `Lambda_0 + Gamma` is a spectrum of `mu`.

*Proof.* For `gamma` in `Gamma - Gamma`, `e(s gamma) = e(s' gamma)` for all `s, s'` in `S`, so `hat nu(xi + gamma) = c_gamma hat nu(xi)` with `|c_gamma| = 1`. Take `lambda_0 + gamma != lambda'_0 + gamma'`. If `lambda_0 != lambda'_0`, then `hat nu(lambda_0 - lambda'_0 + gamma - gamma') = c hat nu(lambda_0 - lambda'_0) = 0`; otherwise `hat sigma(gamma - gamma') = 0`; either way `hat mu = hat nu hat sigma` vanishes at the difference. The same computation with `hat mu(0) = 1` shows that distinct pairs `(lambda_0, gamma)` give distinct sums. Since `0` is in `Gamma`, `|hat nu(xi + lambda_0 + gamma)| = |hat nu(xi + lambda_0)|`, and `Q(xi) = sum_{lambda_0} |hat nu(xi + lambda_0)|^2 sum_gamma |hat sigma(xi + lambda_0 + gamma)|^2 = sum_{lambda_0} |hat nu(xi + lambda_0)|^2 = 1`, the inner sum being `1` because `Gamma` is a spectrum of `sigma`, the outer because `Lambda_0` is a spectrum of `nu` on the `|S|`-dimensional space `L^2(nu)`. □

## 5. Prime-power digit counts

**Lemma 5.1 (the tree lemma).** For a finite set `A` of integers and `j >= 1`, `Phi_(p^j) | A(x)` if and only if, in each class mod `p^(j-1)`, the `p` classes mod `p^j` above it hold equally many elements of `A`. If `|A| = p^r` and `A` satisfies T1, then `S_A = {p^(c_1), ..., p^(c_r)}` with `c_1 < ... < c_r`, each class of `A` mod `p^(j-1)` meets all `p` classes above it, equally, when `j` is some `c_i`, and exactly one of them otherwise.

*Proof.* A polynomial of degree below `p^j` is a multiple of `Phi_(p^j)(x) = sum_{s<p} x^(s p^(j-1))` exactly when it is `sum_{t < p^(j-1)} q_t x^t Phi_(p^j)(x)`, that is when its coefficients are constant on each coset of `p^(j-1) Z/p^j Z`; apply this to `A(x)` mod `x^(p^j) - 1`. T1 with `|A| = p^r` and `Phi_(q^c)(1) = q` forces `S_A` to be `r` powers of `p`. Follow one element: the count of its class mod `p^j` starts at `p^r` at `j = 0`, is divided by exactly `p` at each `c_i`, never grows, and is `1` for large `j`. The `r` divisions already bring `p^r` to `1`, so at every other level the count is unchanged, and the class goes whole into one class above it. □

**Theorem 5.2.** Let `F` be a finite set of non-negative integers with `|F| = p^r`, `p` prime, whose every level at base `b` tiles. Then `mu_{b,F}` is spectral.

*Proof.* By Lemma 2.4, `e = e_p >= 1` and the exponents `c_1 < ... < c_r` of `Z_p(F)` are pairwise incongruent mod `e`; let `rho_i` in `1..e` be the residue of `c_i` and `b = p^e b'`. Cut each digit into base-`p^e` windows, `f = sum_{j<=K} r_j(f) p^(ej)` with `0 <= r_j(f) < p^e`, `K` the last window over `F`, so that `b'^K f = sum_{j<=K} b^j b'^(K-j) r_j(f)`. For a lower part `d`, a class mod `p^(ej)` met by `F`, let `R_j(d)` be the set of the distinct values `r_j(f)` over the `f` in that class, which are the classes mod `p^(e(j+1))` that `F` meets inside it, and `E_j(d) = b'^(K-j) R_j(d)`. By Lemma 5.1 the classes split `p` ways at the levels `c_i` and not elsewhere, so the number of distinct classes mod `p^(e(j+1))` below a class at a level `l` in `[ej, e(j+1)]` depends only on `l`; hence the set `R_j(d)` splits equally, `p` ways, exactly at the levels `c_i - je` that lie in `1..e`, which are the `rho_i` of the window's `i`, and Lemma 5.1 puts `Phi_(p^(rho_i))` in its mask. The factor `b'^(K-j)` is prime to `p`, so `Phi_(p^(rho_i))` divides `E_j(d)(x)` as well. Put `L_j = {sum_i k_i b/p^(rho_i) : 0 <= k_i < p}` over the window's `i`. For a range of consecutive windows `m..m'` and any choice of window sets, the mask of `E_m(d_m) + ... + E_(m')(d_(m'))` is the product of their masks and holds `Phi_(p^(rho_i))` for every `i` of the range; those `rho_i` are distinct, so the argument of Theorem 3.1 at the single prime `p` makes the difference of two distinct elements of `L_m ⊕ ... ⊕ L_(m')` over `b` have order `p^(rho)` for some `rho_i = rho` of the range, a zero of the sum's mask. So the sum with `L_m ⊕ ... ⊕ L_(m')` is a Hadamard triple; the sizes match, `p` to the number of the range's `i`, and since a Hadamard matrix has rank equal to its size, the sum of window sets is direct. This is the `K`-stage product form of Theorem 2.9 for `b'^K F = {sum_j b^j e_j}`, so `mu_{b, b'^K F}` is spectral, and `mu_{b,F}` is its image under `x -> x/b'^K`. □

At `{0, 1, 8, 9}` and base 12 the windows are base 4: `R_0 = {0, 1}` with `rho = 1`, and `R_1(d) = {0, 2}` with `rho = 2`, for both lower parts.

## 6. Two-prime digit counts

**Lemma 6.1 (the fibre lemma).** Let `N = p^alpha q^beta` with `p != q` primes and `alpha, beta >= 1`. If `Phi_N` divides a polynomial `P` with non-negative integer coefficients, then the exponents of `P` mod `N`, with multiplicity, form a union of `p`-fibres `{x + kN/p : k < p}` and `q`-fibres `{x + kN/q : k < q}`.

*Proof.* Put `zeta = e(1/N)` and `omega = zeta^(N/pq)`, of order `pq`. The degree of `Q(zeta)` over `Q(omega)` is `phi(N)/phi(pq) = N/pq`, so `x^(N/pq) - omega` is the minimal polynomial of `zeta` over `Q(omega)`, and `P(zeta) = sum_{r < N/pq} zeta^r G_r(omega)` vanishes exactly when every `G_r(omega)` does, `G_r(y) = sum_u g_r(u) y^u` holding the counts `g_r(u)` of the exponents `r + (N/pq) u`, `u` in `Z/pq`. Its Galois conjugates vanish too, so the transform of `g = g_r` on `Z/pq = Z/p x Z/q` vanishes at every frequency with both coordinates nonzero, and `g(i, j) = g(0, j) + (g(i, 0) - g(0, 0))`. A function of `j` alone counts `p`-fibres, adding `N/p` to an exponent moving only `i`, and a function of `i` alone counts `q`-fibres. Let the row term `g(i, 0) - g(0, 0)` be least at `i*`; subtracting that least value from every row term and adding it to every column term leaves row terms at least `0` and the column term `g(i*, j) >= 0`, which is where non-negativity enters. □

The statement is Lam-Leung Theorem 3.3(2) with the reduction of their Theorem 3.1 to the subgroup of order `pq`, which they trace to de Bruijn; the proof is given to keep the paper self-contained.

**Lemma 6.2 (the shape).** Let `|F| = pq` with `p != q` primes, `0` in `F`, `gcd(F) = 1`, and every level of `F` at base `b` tiling. Then, naming the primes suitably, `Z_q(F) = {1}`, `Z_p(F) = {c}`, and `F = ⋃_{j<q} (x_j + p^(c-1) q K_j)` with the `x_j` distinct mod `q` and each `K_j` a set of `p` integers forming a complete residue system mod `p`.

*Proof.* By Lemma 2.4, `Z_p(F) = {c}`, `Z_q(F) = {d}`, and `F` satisfies T1 and T2, so `Phi_(p^c q^d)` divides `F`. By Lemma 6.1 at `N = p^c q^d`, `F` mod `N` is `A` `p`-fibres and `B` `q`-fibres with `pA + qB = pq`, so `p | B`, `q | A` and `F` is `q` `p`-fibres or `p` `q`-fibres. A `q`-fibre is one point mod `p^c`, so in the second case `F` mod `p^c` is at most `p` distinct residues; by Lemma 5.1 every class mod `p^(c-1)` met by `F` meets all `p` classes above it, so `F` lies in one class mod `p^(c-1)`, that of `0`, and `p^(c-1)` divides `gcd(F) = 1`: `c = 1`. Symmetrically the first case gives `d = 1`. Name the primes so that the exponent-1 prime is `q` and `F` mod `p^c q` is `q` `p`-fibres `x_j + p^(c-1) q {0..p-1}`. Assigning each element to its fibre writes it as `x_j + p^(c-1) q k + p^c q t = x_j + p^(c-1) q (k + p t)`, which defines `K_j`. Each group lies in one class mod `q`, and `Phi_q | F` puts `p` elements in every class mod `q`, so the `x_j` are distinct mod `q`. □

**Lemma 6.3 (three evaluations).** For `F` of the shape of Lemma 6.2:
- for `1 <= alpha <= c - 1`, `Phi_(p^alpha q) | F` if and only if the `x_j` lie in one class mod `p^alpha`;
- for `beta >= 1`, `Phi_(p^c q^(1+beta)) | F` if and only if every `K_j` lies in one class mod `q^beta`;
- for `1 <= alpha <= c - 1` and `beta >= 1`, `Phi_(p^alpha q^(1+beta))` does not divide `F`.

*Proof.* Let `zeta` have the order in question and `eta = zeta^(p^(c-1) q)`, so that `F(zeta) = sum_j zeta^(x_j) K_j(eta)`. First case: `eta = 1` and `F(zeta) = p sum_j zeta^(x_j)`; write `zeta = zeta_1 zeta_2` with orders `p^alpha` and `q`. The `zeta_2^(x_j)` are the `q` distinct `q`-th roots of unity, whose only linear relation over `Q(zeta_1)` has equal coefficients, `Phi_q` staying irreducible there; so the sum vanishes exactly when the `zeta_1^(x_j)` agree. Second case: `eta` has order `p q^beta`, the degree of `Q(zeta)` over `Q(eta)` is `p^(c-1) q`, so `x^(p^(c-1) q) - eta` is minimal and `1, zeta, ..., zeta^(p^(c-1) q - 1)` are independent over `Q(eta)`; the `x_j`, distinct mod `q`, are distinct mod `p^(c-1) q`, so `F(zeta) = 0` exactly when every `K_j(eta) = 0`, which by the first case with `p` and `q` exchanged says that `K_j` lies in one class mod `q^beta`. Third case: `eta = zeta_2^(p^(c-1) q)` has order `q^beta`, with `zeta = zeta_1 zeta_2` of orders `p^alpha` and `q^(1+beta)`; over `E = Q(zeta_1, zeta_2^q)`, which contains `eta`, `zeta_2` has degree `q`, so its powers `zeta_2^0, ..., zeta_2^(q-1)` are independent over `E`, and `zeta^(x_j)` is `zeta_2^(x_j mod q)` times an element of `E`, with the `x_j mod q` distinct. So `F(zeta) = 0` would need every `K_j(eta) = 0`, that is `Phi_(q^beta)` dividing the mask of a set of `p` integers, whose value `p` at `1` would then be a multiple of `q`. □

With `y_j = x_j + p^(c-1) q kappa_j`, `s = (b/b_P)^a` and `b^a = s p^(e a) q^(f a)`,

## 8. The converse

Write `Theta` for the finite set of zeros of `hat F` in `[0, 1)`, so that the zero set of `hat mu` is `⋃_{j>=1} b^j (Theta + Z)`. An integer `d` is a zero of `hat mu` exactly when `b^j/gcd(d, b^j)` is in `Z(F)` for some `j >= 1`; say then that `d` reaches that order at level `j`. Every order an integer reaches has all its primes dividing `b`; call the orders of `Z(F)` with this property the `b`-orders of `F`, and `j(m)` the least `j` with `m | b^j`.

**Lemma 8.1 (the class count).** If `A_n` is a set of `k^n` integers, every spectrum `Lambda` of `mu = mu_{b,F}` meets at least `k^n` classes of `R/b^n Z`.

*Proof.* `hat mu(xi) = hat mu_n(xi) hat mu(b^(-n) xi)`, with `mu_n` uniform on `b^(-n) A_n`; `hat mu_n` has period `b^n` and is `1` on `b^n Z`. Two points of one class `Lambda_r` differ by `b^n z`, so `0 = hat mu(b^n z) = hat mu(z)`: the set `b^(-n) Lambda_r` is orthogonal in `L^2(mu)`, and Bessel's inequality gives `sum_{lambda in Lambda_r} |hat mu(b^(-n)(x - lambda))|^2 <= 1`. Splitting `1 = sum_lambda |hat mu(x - lambda)|^2` by classes gives `1 <= sum_r |hat mu_n(x - r)|^2` for every `x`. If the classes are finitely many, integrate over a period: the mean of `|hat mu_n|^2` is `k^(-n)`, the elements of `A_n` being distinct, so their number is at least `k^n`. □

**Lemma 8.2 (rational cosets).** A spectrum of `mu_{b,F}` through `0` meets finitely many cosets of `Q`.

*Proof.* In the `Q`-vector space `R/Q`, every point of the spectrum and every difference of two points maps into `{0}` or a set `b^j x`, `j >= 1`, with `x` the image of an irrational element of `Theta`. If infinitely many points had distinct images, infinitely many would map to `b^j x` for one `x`, with `j` in an infinite set `K`. For `j > j'` in `K` the difference maps to `(b^j - b^(j')) x`, which is `b^i x'` with `x'` the image of an element of `Theta`, necessarily `x' = c x` with `c` rational, and so `b^(j - j') - 1 = c b^(i - j')`. Finitely many `c` occur, and an integer prime to `b` equals `c b^s` for at most one `s`, so `j - j'` takes finitely many values on `K`, which is impossible. □

**Theorem 8.3 (integer orthogonal sets).** If every `A_n` is a set and `mu_{b,F}` is spectral, then for every `L >= 1` some `k^L` integers, pairwise incongruent mod `b^L`, have every difference a zero of `hat mu`.

*Proof.* Take a spectrum `Lambda` through `0`, `C` its number of cosets of `Q` (Lemma 8.2), `Lambda_v` its points in one coset, and `M` the lcm of `Z(F)` (`M = 1` if `Z(F)` is empty). Differences in `Lambda_v` are rational zeros `b^j(theta + i)` with `theta` a rational zero of `hat F`, so they lie in `M^(-1) Z`, and `Gamma = M(Lambda_v - lambda_v)` is a set of integers whose classes mod `M b^n` are those of `Lambda_v` mod `b^n Z`. Two points `c + M b^(tL) x` and `c + M b^(tL) x'` of `Gamma` differ by `M b^j (theta + i)`, `theta + i` not an integer, so `j > tL` and `x - x' = b^(j - tL)(theta + i)` is a zero of `hat mu`. So if `g_L <= b^L` is the largest number of integers, pairwise incongruent mod `b^L`, with all differences zeros of `hat mu`, a class of `Gamma` mod `M b^(tL)` holds at most `g_L` classes mod `M b^((t+1)L)`, and `Lambda` meets at most `C M g_L^(ceil(n/L))` classes mod `b^n`. Lemma 8.1 gives `k^n <= C M g_L^(n/L + 1)` for every `n`, so `g_L >= k^L`. □

**Corollary 8.4.** If every `A_n` is a set and `mu_{b,F}` is spectral, then `k <= b` and `F` has a `b`-order. If `Z(F)` is empty, `mu_{b,F}` is not spectral, with no hypothesis on the levels. In particular `mu_{b,{0,1,3,5,6}}` is spectral at no integer base `b >= 2`.

*Proof.* Theorem 8.3 at `L = 1` gives `k` integers distinct mod `b`, so `k <= b`; and `k >= 2` of them have a difference that is an integer zero of `hat mu`, which reaches a `b`-order. If `Z(F)` is empty, `hat mu` has no rational zero, so a spectrum through `0` holds at most one point in each coset of `Q`, and by Lemma 8.2 it is finite, while `L^2(mu_{b,F})` is infinite-dimensional, the support being infinite. The mask `1 + x + x^3 + x^5 + x^6` has no cyclotomic factor (generator: `lab/py/measure-spectra`, verb `converse`), which covers the bases 2 to 6, where levels repeat points, as `1 + 4 * 1 = 5 + 4 * 0` at base 4. □

An-He-Lai Example 5.2 takes a real ratio `0 < rho < 1` and expects no `mu_{rho,{0,1,3,5,6}}` to be spectral; Corollary 8.4 settles the integer ratios `rho = 1/b`, and a non-integer `rho^(-1)` stays open. The corollary also recovers the non-spectrality of `{0, 2}` at odd base, where `Z = {4}` has no `b`-order; Jorgensen and Pedersen prove more there, at most two orthogonal exponentials.

**Theorem 8.5 (the covering bound).** Let every `A_n` be a set and `W` a set of primes of `b` meeting every `b`-order of `F`, that is sharing a prime with each; for `q` in `W` let `rho_q` count the classes mod `e_q` met by `v_q(m)` over the `b`-orders `m` divisible by `q`. If `mu_{b,F}` is spectral, then `k <= prod_{q in W} q^(rho_q)`.

*Proof.* Take the `k^L` integers of Theorem 8.3, and `J` the largest `j(m)` over the `b`-orders. A difference `d` reaching `m` at level `j` has `m | b^j`, so `j >= j(m)`, and some prime `q'` of `b` has `v_(q')(d) < L e_(q')`. If `q'` does not divide `m` then `v_(q')(d) >= j e_(q')`, so `j < L`; if it does, `v_(q')(m) = j e_(q') - v_(q')(d) > (j - L) e_(q')` while `v_(q')(m) <= j(m) e_(q')`; either way `j < J + L`. For `q` in `W` dividing `m`, `v_q(d) = j e_q - v_q(m)` lies in `[0, (J + L) e_q)` and in one of `rho_q` classes mod `e_q`, so it takes at most `rho_q (J + L)` values, a set `V_q`. Every difference reaches a `b`-order divisible by some `q` in `W`, and two integers whose difference has `q`-adic valuation `delta` differ in their `q`-adic digit at position `delta`; so the digits at the positions `V_q`, over `q` in `W`, tell all the integers apart, and `k^L <= prod_q q^(rho_q (J + L))` for every `L`. Let `L` grow. □

**Proposition 8.6.** If `F` passes the T1 condition, in particular if every level tiles, its covering bound, the least `prod_{q in W} q^(rho_q)` over sets `W` meeting every `b`-order, is at least `k`.

*Proof.* Each `p` with `Z_p(F)` nonempty divides `b`, so the orders `p^c`, `c` in `Z_p(F)`, are `b`-orders; a covering set contains `p`, and `rho_p >= |Z_p(F)|` since those `c` are pairwise incongruent mod `e_p`. So the bound is at least `prod_p p^(|Z_p(F)|) = k`. □

**Lemma 8.7.** Let `|F| = p` be prime, `0` in `F` and `gcd(F) = 1`. The only prime power that can lie in `Z(F)` is `p`, and it does exactly when `F` is a complete residue system mod `p`; then every level tiles exactly when `p | b`.

*Proof.* If `Phi_(q^c) | F`, Lemma 5.1 makes the count of every class mod `q^(c-1)` met by `F` a multiple of `q`; the total `p` forces `q = p` and one class, that of `0`, so `p^(c-1) | gcd(F) = 1` and `c = 1`, and `Phi_p | F` says that `F` meets every class mod `p` once. Then `S_F = {p}`, T1 holds, and the T1 condition reads `p | b`; at prime size Lemma 2.4 makes it the every-level verdict. □

**Theorem 8.8 (five digits).** Let `F` be a set of five non-negative integers with `0` in `F`, whose every level `A_n` at base `b` is a set. Then `mu_{b,F}` is spectral if and only if every level of `F` tiles.

*Proof.* If every level tiles, Theorem 5.2 applies. Conversely, by Lemma 2.5 take `gcd(F) = 1` and suppose not every level tiles. By Lemma 8.7 no `b`-order is a prime power: the only candidate is `5`, and if `5` is in `Z(F)` and divides `b`, every level tiles. Let `m` be a `b`-order. Lam-Leung Theorem 4.8, read at source, writes the residues of `F` mod `m`, a vanishing sum of `m`-th roots of unity with non-negative coefficients and weight 5, as minimal vanishing sums, each a rotated fibre of a prime of `m` or of weight above the third prime of `m`, at least 6. A single 5-fibre puts `F` in one class mod `m/5`, so `m/5 | gcd(F) = 1` and `m = 5`, a prime power. So `F = P ∪ T` with a pair `P` whose difference `d_P` is `m/2` mod `m` and a triple `T` whose differences are `± m/3` mod `m`; then `a = v_2(m) = v_2(d_P) + 1`, and `T` lies in one class mod `2^a`. Let `(P', T')` be the splitting at a second `b`-order, with exponent `a'`. If `P' = P` then `a' = a`. If `P'` lies in `T`, then `P` lies in `T'`, and `a' - 1 = v_2(d_(P')) >= a` and `a - 1 >= a'` cannot both hold. Otherwise `P = {f_1, f_2}` and `P' = {f_1, f_3}` with `f_3` in `T`, `f_2` in `T'`, and some `f_4` in `T ∩ T'`; in `f_1 - f_2 = (f_1 - f_3) + (f_3 - f_4) + (f_4 - f_2)` the terms have 2-adic valuations `a' - 1`, at least `a`, at least `a'`, so `a' < a` would make the right side of valuation `a' - 1` against `a - 1` on the left; and in `f_1 - f_3 = (f_1 - f_2) + (f_2 - f_4) + (f_4 - f_3)`, `a < a'` fails the same way. So every `b`-order is even with one exponent `a` of 2, `W = {2}` covers them with `rho_2 = 1`, and Theorem 8.5 would give `5 <= 2`. At odd `b` an even order is never a `b`-order, so `F` has none, and Corollary 8.4 applies. Either way `mu_{b,F}` is not spectral. □

**Example 8.9.** Theorem 8.3 at `L = 1` alone does not decide the converse. `F = {0, 1, 251, 375, 501}` at base `510 = 2 * 3 * 5 * 17` has residues `{0, 0, 1, 1, 1}` mod 5, so not every level tiles; mod 750 it is the 2-fibre `{0, 375}` and the 3-fibre `{1, 251, 501}`, and `6, 30, 150, 750` are its `b`-orders. The integers `0, 17, 1734, 1751, 176868` are pairwise incongruent mod 510, and every difference reaches 30 at level 1, 150 at level 2 or 750 at level 3, so five integers with orthogonal differences exist at `L = 1`; yet all four orders have `v_2 = 1`, the covering bound with `W = {2}` is 2, and `mu_{510,F}` is not spectral by Theorem 8.5. Likewise `{0, 1, 9, 10}` at base 12 fails T1, `4` against `Phi_2(1) = 2`, while `0, 2, 8, 10` are pairwise incongruent mod 12 with orthogonal differences; its orders `2, 6, 18` all have `v_2 = 1`, and its covering bound is `2 < 4`. Generator for the orders and the integers: `lab/py/measure-spectra`, verb `converse`.

## 9. Seven digits

Two inputs from the literature, both read at source, carry the next three primes; the first was already used in Theorem 8.8.

**Theorem 9.1 (Lam and Leung, Theorems 4.8 and 6.5 with Example 6.7; Poonen and Rubinstein, Lemma 1, Theorem 3 and Table 1; read at source).** A vanishing sum of `m`-th roots of unity with non-negative integer coefficients is a sum of minimal ones, those with no proper non-empty vanishing sub-sum. A minimal one is symmetric, a rotated fibre `{x + i m/p : i < p}` of a prime `p` of `m`, or asymmetric, and then the order of its roots has at least three primes `p_1 < p_2 < p_3` and its weight is at least `(p_1 - 1)(p_2 - 1) + p_3 - 1`, which is `6` for `2, 3, 5` and at least `8` for every other triple; the asymmetric sum of weight 6 is unique up to rotation, and `z^5 + z^6 + z^7 + z^17 + z^18 + z^24 + z^25` at `m = 30` is a minimal asymmetric sum of weight 7. The minimal relations `sum a_i zeta_i = 0` with positive integer coefficients `a_i` and distinct roots of unity `zeta_i`, of weight `sum a_i` at most 12, are up to rotation the 107 of Poonen and Rubinstein's Table 1; at weight 7 they are `R_7`, the 7-fibre, and the two relations `(R_5 : 2 R_3)`: a 5-fibre `{zeta e(i/5) : i < 5}` in which two of the five points `zeta_i` are each replaced by the pair `zeta_i e(1/6), zeta_i e(-1/6)`, since `zeta = zeta e(1/6) + zeta e(-1/6)`, the two relations differing in whether the replaced points are adjacent on the fibre. By Mann's theorem, their Lemma 1, a minimal relation with `k` distinct roots is a rotation of one whose roots have order dividing `prod_{p <= k} p`, so the table at weight 7 covers every order `m`, the repeated residues of a multiset grouped into coefficients.

**Lemma 9.2 (three shapes).** Let `|F| = 7`, `0` in `F`, `gcd(F) = 1`, every `A_n` a set, and not every level tiling. Then every `b`-order `m` of `F` is even, and `F` mod `m`, with multiplicity, is one of
- (a) a 5-fibre `Y` and a 2-fibre `P`;
- (b) a 3-fibre `T` and two 2-fibres `P_1`, `P_2`;
- (c) with `30 | m`, the three points `U = {y + i m/5 : i in I}` and the four points `V = {y + i m/5 + m/6, y + i m/5 - m/6 : i < 5, i not in I}`, for some `y` and some set `I` of three classes mod 5.

*Proof.* By Lemma 8.7 the only prime power `Z(F)` can hold is `7`, and `7` with `7 | b` makes every level tile; so no `b`-order is a prime power. For a `b`-order `m`, `F` mod `m` is a vanishing sum of weight 7 with non-negative coefficients, hence by Theorem 9.1 a sum of minimal ones, each a fibre of weight `2`, `3`, `5` or `7`, or asymmetric with `30 | m` and weight `6` or `7`. If every summand is a fibre, the weight splits as `7`, `5 + 2` or `3 + 2 + 2`; otherwise an asymmetric summand occurs, of weight 7, since `6 + 1` is no sum of weights of minimal sums. A 7-fibre puts `F` in one class mod `m/7`, so `m/7` divides `gcd(F) = 1` and `m = 7`, excluded. A minimal asymmetric sum of weight 7 is `(R_5 : 2 R_3)` by Theorem 9.1, which is (c); that its roots have order 30, so `30 | m`, comes from Poonen and Rubinstein's Table 1 through Mann's theorem, which places every weight-7 relation at an order dividing 210, not from the census of Fact 9.6. In (a) and (b) a 2-fibre holds two points differing by `m/2` mod `m`, and in (c) `6 | m`: `m` is even. □

**Lemma 9.3 (the 2-adic and 3-adic reading).** Let `m` be a `b`-order of `F` under the hypotheses of Lemma 9.2 and `a = v_2(m)`. A 2-fibre `{f, f'}` has `v_2(f - f') = a - 1`, and the odd part of `m` divides `f - f'`. A 3-fibre or a 5-fibre lies in one class mod `2^a`, and the differences of a 3-fibre have `v_3 = v_3(m) - 1`. In shape (c), `F` lies in one class mod `2^(a-1)`, `U` lies in one class mod `2^a` and in one class mod `3^(v_3(m))`, `V` lies in the other class mod `2^a`, and of the three splittings of `V` into two pairs, the one pairing equal signs has both differences divisible by `3^(v_3(m))`, while the other two have every difference of `v_3 = v_3(m) - 1`.

*Proof.* A 2-fibre has `f - f' = (m/2)(2t + 1)`. The points of a 3-fibre or a 5-fibre differ by multiples of `m/3` or `m/5`, which `2^a` divides, and the differences of a 3-fibre are `(m/3)(3t ± 1)`. In (c), `m/5` is divisible by `2^a` and by `3^(v_3(m))`, while `m/6` is `2^(a-1)` times an odd number and `3^(v_3(m) - 1)` times a number prime to 3; so the points of `U` agree mod `2^a 3^(v_3(m))`, those of `V` lie in the class of `y + m/6` mod `2^a`, and all of `F` in the class of `y` mod `2^(a-1)`. Two points of `V` of equal sign differ by `(i_1 - i_2) m/5 + tm`, two of one `i` by `± m/3 + tm`, and two of different `i` and different sign by `(i_1 - i_2) m/5 ± m/3 + tm`. □

**Lemma 9.4 (two exponents).** Under the hypotheses of Lemma 9.2, `v_2(m)` takes at most two values over the `b`-orders `m` of `F`.

*Proof.* Call the partition of `F` into its classes mod `2^j` the depth `j`; a larger depth refines a smaller one, and the number of parts never decreases with depth. At a `b`-order `m` with `a = v_2(m)`, Lemma 9.3 reads depths `a - 1` and `a` off the shape. In (c), depth `a - 1` is `{F}` and depth `a` is `{U, V}`, of sizes `3, 4`. In (a), `P` splits at depth `a` and `Y` lies inside one part there, so depth `a - 1` is `{F}` with depth `a` of sizes `6, 1`, or `{P, Y}` with depth `a` of sizes `1, 1, 5`. In (b), each `P_i` splits at depth `a` and `T` lies inside one part there, so depth `a - 1` is a coarsening of `{P_1, P_2, T}`: `{F}` with depth `a` of sizes `5, 2`; `{P_1 ∪ P_2, T}` with depth `a` of sizes `2, 2, 3`, each 2-part holding one point of each `P_i`; `{P_1 ∪ T, P_2}` or its mirror with sizes `4, 1, 1, 1`; or `{P_1, P_2, T}` with sizes `1, 1, 1, 1, 3`. In every shape depth `a` has more parts than depth `a - 1`. Suppose `a_1 < a_2 < a_3` are three values, at orders `m_1, m_2, m_3`. Depth `a_2 - 1` refines depth `a_1`, which has at least two parts, so depth `a_2 - 1` is not `{F}`: it is `{P, Y}` of sizes `2, 5`, or `{P_1 ∪ P_2, T}`, `{P_1 ∪ T, P_2}`, `{P_1, P_2, T}` of sizes `4, 3`, `5, 2`, `2, 2, 3`; the same holds at `m_3`. Depth `a_3 - 1` refines depth `a_2`, which has at least three parts, while depth `a_3 - 1` has at most three; so both have exactly three parts, and a refinement with as many parts as the partition it refines equals it. The three-part options are `1, 1, 5` and `2, 2, 3` at depth `a_2` and `2, 2, 3` at depth `a_3 - 1`, so depth `a_3 - 1` equals depth `a_2`, both of sizes `2, 2, 3`: `m_2` has shape (b) with depth `a_2 - 1 = {P_1 ∪ P_2, T}` and depth `a_2 = {{u_1, u_2}, {u_1', u_2'}, T}`, where `u_i, u_i'` are the points of `P_i`; and the three fibres of `m_3`, each inside one part of depth `a_3 - 1`, are the three parts `{u_1, u_2}`, `{u_1', u_2'}` and `T`, with `T` the 3-fibre. Depth `a_2 - 1`, of sizes `4, 3`, refines depth `a_1`, which has at least two parts, so depth `a_1` has exactly two parts and equals depth `a_2 - 1`; the two-part options at depth `a_1` are `3, 4`, `6, 1` and `5, 2`, so `m_1` has shape (c) with `U = T` and `V = P_1 ∪ P_2`. By Lemma 9.3 at `m_1`, `T = U` lies in one class mod `3^(v_3(m_1))`; as the 3-fibre of `m_2` and of `m_3` its differences have `v_3 = v_3(m_i) - 1`, so `v_3(m_i) >= v_3(m_1) + 1`, and the 2-fibres of `m_i` have their difference divisible by the odd part of `m_i`, hence by `3^(v_3(m_1) + 1)`. But `{P_1, P_2}` and `{{u_1, u_2}, {u_1', u_2'}}` are two different splittings of `V` into pairs, and by Lemma 9.3 at `m_1` at most one of the three splittings has its differences divisible by `3^(v_3(m_1))`; so a 2-fibre of `m_2` or of `m_3` has a difference of `v_3 = v_3(m_1) - 1`, a contradiction. □

**Theorem 9.5 (seven digits).** Let `F` be a set of seven non-negative integers with `0` in `F`, whose every level `A_n` at base `b` is a set. Then `mu_{b,F}` is spectral if and only if every level of `F` tiles.

*Proof.* If every level tiles, Theorem 5.2 applies. Conversely, by Lemma 2.5 take `gcd(F) = 1`, and suppose not every level tiles. By Lemma 9.2 every `b`-order is even, so at odd `b` there is none and Corollary 8.4 applies. At even `b`, `W = {2}` meets every `b`-order, and by Lemma 9.4 `v_2(m)` takes at most two values over them, so `rho_2 <= 2` and Theorem 8.5 would give `7 <= 4`. Either way `mu_{b,F}` is not spectral. □

The hypothesis `0` in `F` enters Lemma 9.2 where a 7-fibre is turned into `m = 7`: without it `{1, 8, 15, 22, 29, 36, 43}` at base 49 has `Z(F) = {49}`, a single 7-fibre at an odd order, and tiles at every level, as Lemma 2.5 predicts from `{0..6}`. Nothing in the section reads a zero of `hat F` off the roots of unity: the irrational zeros of `hat F` enter the covering bound only through the constant `C` of Lemma 8.2.

## 10. Eleven and thirteen digits

Theorem 9.1 carries the next two primes as well. The weights 11 and 13 are too small for most asymmetric sums and divisible by neither 2 nor 3, so a union of fibres of one prime never has that weight, which pins every `b`-order to a pair of primes with 2 or 3 among them; the covering bound then reads off the `{2, 3}`-part of `b`, and a set at the first base it misses shows where the bound stops and what replaces it.

**Definition 10.1 (pairs).** Call `{q, r}` a pair of `p`, for primes `q != r`, when `p = uq + vr` with integers `u, v >= 1`. The pairs of 11 are `{2, 3}, {2, 5}, {2, 7}, {3, 5}`; those of 13 are these and `{2, 11}, {3, 7}`. A set of primes meets a pair, or an order, when it shares a prime with it.

**Lemma 10.2 (the pairs).** Let `|F| = p` be 11 or 13, `0` in `F`, `gcd(F) = 1`, every `A_n` a set, and not every level tiling. Then the primes of every `b`-order `m` of `F` contain a pair of `p`. In particular `m` is divisible by 2 or by 3; an odd `m` by 15, or at `p = 13` by 15 or 21; and an `m` prime to 3 by 10 or 14, or at `p = 13` by 10, 14 or 22.

*Proof.* By Lemma 8.7 no `b`-order is a prime power. By Theorem 9.1, `F` mod `m` is a sum of minimal vanishing sums, each a fibre of a prime of `m` or asymmetric with three primes `q_1 < q_2 < q_3` of `m` and weight at least `(q_1 - 1)(q_2 - 1) + q_3 - 1`. If every sum is a fibre, the set `U` of primes used satisfies `p = sum_{q in U} u_q q` with every `u_q >= 1`. One prime gives a `p`-fibre, which puts `F` in one class mod `m/p`, so `m/p` divides `gcd(F) = 1` and `m = p`, excluded. Two primes form a pair of `p`. Three or more primes sum to at least `2 + 3 + 5 = 10`, so at `p = 11` they do not occur, `11 - 10 = 1` being no sum of primes and `2 + 3 + 7` exceeding 11, and at `p = 13` only `13 = 2 + 3 + 3 + 5` occurs, with `U = {2, 3, 5}`, which contains the pair `{2, 3}`. If an asymmetric sum occurs, its weight is at most `p <= 13`, so `(q_1 - 1)(q_2 - 1) + q_3 - 1 <= 13`, which forces `q_1 = 2` and either `q_2 = 3` with `q_3` in `{5, 7, 11}` or `q_2 = 5` and `q_3 = 7`: the primes of `m` contain `{2, 3}` or `{2, 5}`, pairs of both 11 and 13. Every pair holds 2 or 3; the odd pairs are `{3, 5}` and, at `p = 13`, `{3, 7}`; the pairs without 3 are `{2, 5}`, `{2, 7}` and, at `p = 13`, `{2, 11}`. □

**Theorem 10.3 (eleven and thirteen digits).** Let `|F| = p` be 11 or 13, `0` in `F`, and every level `A_n` at base `b` a set. Suppose some set `W` of primes of `b` meets every pair of `p` whose two primes divide `b`, and `prod_{q in W} q^(e_q) < p`. Then `mu_{b,F}` is spectral if and only if every level of `F` tiles. In particular this holds whenever `2^(e_2) 3^(e_3) < p`; when `b = 2^alpha 3^gamma` with `alpha <= 3` or `gamma <= 2`, 144 and 216 among them; at `p = 11` when 5 does not divide `b` and `e_2 <= 3`; at `p = 13` when neither 5 nor 7 divides `b` and `e_2 <= 3`; and at every base whose primes hold no pair of `p`, prime powers among them.

*Proof.* If every level tiles, Theorem 5.2 applies. Conversely, by Lemma 2.5 take `gcd(F) = 1`, and suppose not every level tiles. By Lemma 10.2 the primes of every `b`-order contain a pair of `p`, whose primes divide `b`, so `W` meets every `b`-order; `rho_q` counts classes mod `e_q`, so `rho_q <= e_q`, and Theorem 8.5 would give `p <= prod_{q in W} q^(rho_q) <= prod_{q in W} q^(e_q) < p`. So `mu_{b,F}` is not spectral. For the cases: every pair holds 2 or 3, so `W = {2, 3}` always serves; at `b = 2^alpha 3^gamma` the only pair inside the primes of `b` is `{2, 3}`, met by `{2}` and by `{3}`, with `2^alpha < 11` exactly when `alpha <= 3` and `3^gamma < 11` exactly when `gamma <= 2`, the same thresholds at 13; without `5 | b` the pairs of 11 inside the primes of `b` are among `{2, 3}` and `{2, 7}`, met by `{2}`; at `p = 13` without 5 and 7 they are among `{2, 3}` and `{2, 11}`, met by `{2}`; and with no pair inside, `W` is empty, its product `1`. □

**Fact 10.4.** The bases up to 500 at which no set `W` of Theorem 10.3 exists are 60, 120, 180, 240, 300, 336, 360, 400, 420, 432, 450 and 480 at `p = 11`, and 120, 126, 168, 180, 240, 252, 336, 360, 378, 400, 432, 450 and 480 at `p = 13`. Generator: `lab/py/measure-spectra`, verb `prime`.

**Lemma 10.5 (one level).** Let `m` be a `b`-order of `F` and `j(m)` the least `j` with `m | b^j`. If an integer `d` not divisible by `b` reaches `m` at level `j`, then `j = j(m)`, `v_q(d) = j(m) e_q - v_q(m)` for every prime `q` of `m`, and `v_q(d) >= j(m) e_q` for every prime `q` of `b` not dividing `m`.

*Proof.* `b^j/gcd(d, b^j) = m` reads `v_q(m) = j e_q - min(v_q(d), j e_q)` at every prime `q` of `b`, and `m | b^j` gives `j >= j(m)`. Since `b` does not divide `d`, some prime `q'` of `b` has `v_(q')(d) < e_(q')`, so `v_(q')(m) > (j - 1) e_(q') >= 0`: `q'` divides `m`, and `v_(q')(m) <= j(m) e_(q')` gives `j - 1 < j(m)`. So `j = j(m)`. For `q` dividing `m`, `v_q(m) > 0` forces `min(v_q(d), j e_q) = v_q(d)`; for `q` not dividing `m` it forces `min(v_q(d), j e_q) = j e_q`. □

**Proposition 10.6 (the base-60 set).** Let `F = {0, 10, 11, 20, 23, 35, 45, 47, 50, 55, 59}` and `b = 60 = 2^2 * 3 * 5`. The cyclotomic factors of `F(x)` are exactly `Phi_15`, `Phi_18` and `Phi_20`, so `F` fails T1 and not every level tiles; the covering bound of `F` is `12`; and `mu_{60,F}` is not spectral.

*Proof.* Mod 15 the residues of `F` are the 5-fibre `{2, 5, 8, 11, 14}` and the 3-fibre `{0, 5, 10}` twice; mod 18 the 3-fibre `{5, 11, 17}` and the 2-fibres `{0, 9}`, `{1, 10}`, `{2, 11}`, `{5, 14}`; mod 20 the 5-fibre `{3, 7, 11, 15, 19}` and the 2-fibres `{0, 10}` twice and `{5, 15}`. So `Phi_15`, `Phi_18` and `Phi_20` divide `F(x)`, and no other `Phi_m` does: a factor has `phi(m) <= deg F = 59`, and the generator tests every such `m` exactly (verb `prime`). No prime power is an order, so T1 fails, `11` against the empty product, and by Lemma 2.4 not every level tiles. The three orders are `b`-orders, with `j(15) = j(20) = 1` and `j(18) = 2`. A covering set `W` meets 15, 18 and 20, so it holds two of `2, 3, 5`: `W = {2, 3}` has `rho_2 = 2`, the values `v_2 = 1` at 18 and `v_2 = 2` at 20 being distinct mod `e_2 = 2`, and `rho_3 = 1`, every value being one class mod `e_3 = 1`, which gives `4 * 3 = 12`; `W = {2, 5}` gives `20` and `W = {3, 5}` gives `15`. So the bound is `12 >= 11`, and Theorem 8.5 does not decide. Since `F` lies in `{0..59}`, every level is a set; so if `mu_{60,F}` were spectral, Theorem 8.3 at `L = 1` would give 11 integers, pairwise incongruent mod 60, every difference a zero of `hat mu`, hence reaching 15, 18 or 20, and not divisible by 60, so Lemma 10.5 applies: a difference reaching 15 has `v_2 >= 2`, `v_3 = 0`, `v_5 = 0`, so it is divisible by 4 and prime to 15; one reaching 18 has `v_2 = 3`, `v_3 = 0`, `v_5 >= 2`, so it is divisible by 200 and prime to 3; one reaching 20 has `v_2 = 0`, `v_3 >= 1`, `v_5 = 0`, so it is odd, divisible by 3 and prime to 5. Two of the integers in one class mod 3 have a difference divisible by 3, which therefore reaches 20 and is odd: they differ in parity. So each class mod 3 holds at most two of the integers, at most 6 in all, against 11. □

The set refutes the guess, stated as a conjecture in an earlier draft of this paper, that the covering bound is at least `|F|` exactly when `F` passes the T1 condition: Proposition 8.6 is the direction that holds, and the other fails at base 60. With it falls the route to the converse at 11 digits through the bound alone, which stops at the hypothesis on `b` of Theorem 10.3. What decides the set is a count of residues: call the residue clique bound of `F` at base `b` the largest number of residues mod `b` through `0` whose pairwise differences each carry, mod every `q^(e_q)`, the valuations Lemma 10.5 prescribes for some `b`-order, that is `min(v_q(d), e_q) = min(j(m) e_q - v_q(m), e_q)` for `q | m` and `q^(e_q) | d` for the other primes `q` of `b`. By Theorem 8.3 at `L = 1` and Lemma 10.5, a spectral `mu_{b,F}` whose levels are sets has residue clique bound at least `|F|`; for the base-60 set it is 3.

## 11. Open problems

The forward direction is open at a size `p^alpha q^beta` with `alpha + beta >= 3` or with three or more primes, when an exponent exceeds `e_p` after the gcd: Lemma 6.1 then allows `p`-fibres and `q`-fibres together, and the groups need not be distinct mod `q`. The evidence is a census: of the 6898 designs that tile at every level among the digit sets with `0` at bases 2 to 16 of a size dividing the base and at bases 17 to 30 of size 4, 8, 9 or with two primes dividing the base, leaving out the cells size 8 and 12 at base 24, size 9 at base 27, size 14 at base 28 and sizes 10 and 15 at base 30, 6844 carry an integer Hadamard triple `(b, F/gcd(F), L)` and 54 are in An-Lai stage form, none neither (`lab/py/measure-spectra`, verb `census`). **Conjecture 11.1.** If every level of `F` at base `b` tiles, then `F/gcd(F)` carries an integer Hadamard triple at base `b` or `F` or `F/gcd(F)` is in An-Lai stage form with windows of base `b_P`, and `mu_{b,F}` is spectral. At three or more primes even the tiling side leans on the open necessity of T2; an unrefereed preprint (arXiv 2609.14026) claims that every tile satisfies T2, and nothing here rests on it.

The converse at prime size is proved at `p <= 7` and, at 11 and 13, under the hypothesis of Theorem 10.3 on the base. Of the bases below 500 it misses, Fact 10.4, the sets in `{0..b-1}` at 60 and 120 are decided by Fact 10.7; the ten others at 11 and the twelve others at 13 stay open, 180 the least at 11 and 126 at 13. The conjecture of an earlier draft of this paper, that the covering bound is at least `|F|` exactly under the T1 condition, is refuted by Proposition 10.6 and withdrawn. What replaces it is the count of residues: **Conjecture 11.2.** If `|F| = p` is prime, `0` is in `F` and every level `A_n` is a set, then `mu_{b,F}` is spectral if and only if every level tiles, that is, by Lemma 8.7, if and only if `p | b` and `F/gcd(F)` is a complete residue system mod `p`. The route is to show that a set with `gcd(F) = 1` whose `b`-orders come from two or three different pairs has residue clique bound below `p`, as every set of the boxes of Fact 10.7 does with room, the bound never passing 3. The next prime, `p = 17`, is where the pattern of Lemma 10.2 breaks: `{5, 7}` is a pair of 17 that avoids 2 and 3, and an asymmetric sum at the primes `3, 5, 7` has Lam-Leung weight `(3 - 1)(5 - 1) + 7 - 1 = 14 <= 17`, so a `b`-order prime to 6 is no longer excluded, and no two-prime covering set is forced; the clique route is the only one in view there.

Five and seven digits with a level that repeats a point, and `{0, 1, 3, 5, 6}` at a non-integer ratio, are not decided. The necessary condition of An and Wang, as Liu, Wang and Zheng quote it in their Theorem 2.3, makes `rho^(-1)` an integer for a spectral `mu_{rho,D}` when the zero set of the mask of `D` lies in a lattice `alpha Z`; the zero set of a mask with integer digits is periodic mod 1, so an irrational zero puts it in no lattice, and the condition as quoted does not reach `{0, 1, 3, 5, 6}`. Joined, the two directions are **Conjecture 11.3**: for an integer base `b >= 2` and a finite set `F` of non-negative integers containing `0`, `mu_{b,F}` is spectral if and only if every level is a set that tiles the integers; the direction from spectral to tiling levels is a question of An and coauthors quoted by Wu and Xiao, who settle it for equidifferent digit sets of prime size.
