## Spectral levels

**Proved.** If T1 and T2 hold at every level, every level is spectral, by Łaba's Theorem 1.5(i) applied to each `A_n`; with the classification, every level is spectral whenever every level tiles and `|F|` has at most two prime factors. The spectrum is explicit: sums of `j/s` over `s` in `S_{A_n}`, `0 <= j < p` for `s` a power of `p`, as in Łaba-Wang's proof of their Theorem 1.4.

**Verified.** On the 6866 levels with `|A_n| <= 36` at bases 2 to 12 and levels 1 to 3, a level is spectral exactly when it tiles, on all 6749 levels the search decides. The search is complete for rational spectra: a spectrum through `0` has every element `theta` with `A_n(e(theta)) = 0`, so a rational one lies in `(1/M_n)Z` for `M_n = lcm Z(A_n)`, and a clique search on `Z/M_n` with the edges `M_n/gcd(d, M_n)` in `Z(A_n)` finds one or proves none. It is complete for all spectra when every root of `F` on the unit circle is a root of unity, since a root of `A_n` on the circle is then one too; PARI finds the other kind of root on 77 of the 4083 digit sets, and their 117 non-tiling levels with no rational spectrum stay undecided. The spectra found are re-checked numerically (`spectral`).

**Conjecture.** A level of a dimension-one design is spectral if and only if it tiles. This is Fuglede's conjecture on a structured family of integer sets; its tiling side is the classification above.

## The limit measure

The limit of the levels is the self-similar measure `mu_{b,F}`, the weak limit of the uniform measure on `b^(-n) A_n`. The guess for it: `mu_{b,F}` is spectral exactly when every level of `F` at base `b` tiles. Łaba and Wang conjectured the stronger form, that a spectral `mu` has `F` a rational multiple of a complementing set mod `b` (their Conjecture 3.1(c)); at base 12 `{0, 1, 8, 9}` is no such multiple, while its measure is spectral (the level shift, below) and meets the condition of Liu-Wang-Zheng Theorem 1.3, an unrefereed arXiv preprint, so the residue form fails for the measure of this witness too. An-Lai Example 1.3 and An-He-Lai Section 1.1 quote from Dutkay and Jorgensen the measure of `{0, 1, 8, 9}` at base 4, a spectral measure whose digits are not distinct mod the base, which their Remark 3.11, read at source, gives against Conjecture 3.1(c); An-Lai Example 8.4 gives `{0, 1, 16, 17}` at base 24, and the spectrality of the base-12 measure is An-Lai Theorem 2.6 and An-He-Lai Theorem 4.2, the four-digit sets `{0, a, 2^t l, a + 2^t l'}` with `a, l, l'` odd and `t` not a multiple of `e_2`.

**Proved.** For consecutive digits `F = {0..N-1}`, every level tiles if and only if `N | b`: `Z(F)` is the divisors of `N` above 1, `Z_p(F) = {1..v_p(N)}`, the T1 condition reads `v_p(N) <= e_p`, and then `lcm(S_F) = N` divides `b`, so T2 holds at every level. Dai, He and Lau prove that `mu_{b,{0..N-1}}` is spectral if and only if `N | b`, so the guess holds on this family. For `{0, 2}` the T1 condition needs `b` even, and Jorgensen and Pedersen prove that at odd base `R` at most two exponentials are orthogonal in `L^2(mu)`, the base-3 Cantor measure among them, while base 4 is spectral. (**Verified**: `N | b` against the every-level verdict on all 2016 pairs `2 <= N <= b <= 64`; `measure`.)

**Verified.** For the product-form digit sets `{0..N-1} + m{0..N'-1}`, T1 and T2 at every level agree with the spectral condition of Liu-Wang-Zheng Theorem 1.3, stated in an unrefereed arXiv preprint, `N | p`, `N' | p` and `N | m/gcd(m, p^d)` with `d` the largest `i >= 0` such that `m N'/gcd(m N', p^i)` shares a factor with `N'`, on all 576422 cases with base `p <= 24`, `2 <= N, N' <= 12` and `N <= m <= p^2`; no case passes the T1 condition and fails T2 with three primes, so on the box the every-level verdict is a tiling verdict (`measure`). Here digits exceed the base and a level can be a multiset; T1 and T2 force a set, since the Coven-Meyerowitz complement `B` of the proof of Theorem A gives `A_n(x) B(x) = 1 + x + ... + x^(M-1)` mod `x^M - 1`, `M = |A_n| |B|`, with non-negative coefficients (their Lemma 1.3), so every coefficient of `A_n(x)` is `0` or `1`. The zero set of a product-form mask comes from the index lemma for one power, `Z({0..N-1}) + Z({0..N'-1}(x^m))`. A comparison that read only levels up to 8 would disagree on `{0, 1} + 256{0, 1}` at base 18, whose T1 depth is exactly 8: the T1 condition is what reads every level.

**Conjecture.** For an integer base `b >= 2` and a finite digit set `F` in the non-negative integers containing `0`, the measure `mu_{b,F}` is spectral if and only if every level `A_n` is a set that tiles the integers. The direction from spectral to tiling levels is a question of An and coauthors, quoted by Wu and Xiao, an arXiv preprint, which settles it for equidifferent digit sets of prime size and shows the converse fails for Cantor-Moran measures with varying digit sets; the other direction, from tiling levels to a spectrum, holds at prime-power size, at `pq` digits, at four digits or fewer and on every design of the census, below; the direction from a spectrum to tiling levels holds at four digits or fewer by An-He-Lai and at five and seven digits with `0` in `F` and levels that are sets, below.

## The measure in the Hadamard window

Write `Lambda(b, L) = {sum_{j<n} b^j l_j : n >= 1, l_j in L}` for a finite set `L` of integers containing `0`. Łaba and Wang prove that when `(b^(-1) F, L)` is a compatible pair, that is `hat F((l - l')/b) = 0` for distinct `l, l'` in `L` and `|L| = |F|`, the measure `mu_{b,F}` is spectral, and that `Lambda(b, L)` is a spectrum when moreover `gcd(F - F) = 1`, `0` is in `L` and `L` lies in `[2 - b, b - 2]`; for `b >= 3` a choice of representatives mod `b` arranges the last condition, and either representative of a residue works (their Theorem 1.2 and Lemma 2.2(b), (d)). At `b = 2` the interval is `{0}`, and the only digit sets are `{0, 1}` up to scaling, whose measure is Lebesgue measure on `[0, 1]` with spectrum `Z`.

**Proved.** If `b >= 3`, `0` is in `F`, `F' = F/gcd(F)` satisfies T1 and T2 and `lcm(S_{F'})` divides `b`, then `mu_{b,F}` is spectral with spectrum `gcd(F)^(-1) Lambda(b, L)`, where `L` is `{sum_{s in S_{F'}} k_s b/s : 0 <= k_s < p_s}`, `p_s` the prime of `s`, with each element replaced by a representative mod `b` in `[2 - b, b - 2]`. Since `0` is in `F'`, `gcd(F' - F') = gcd(F') = 1`. Each `b/s` is an integer since `s | b`. For distinct `l, l'` the number `(l - l')/b` is `sum (k_s - k'_s)/s`, and grouping by primes it is `r/m` with `gcd(r, m) = 1` and `m` the product, over the primes where some `k_s` differs, of the largest such `s`; T1 and T2 put `Phi_m` in the mask of `F'`, so the pair is compatible, and `mu_{b,F}` is the image of `mu_{b,F'}` under `x -> gcd(F) x`. This is the proof of Łaba-Wang Theorem 1.4 with T2 assumed in place of the two-prime count, and by the T2 section above it is the case where `F'` tiles `Z/b` through its Coven-Meyerowitz complement. (**Verified**: 607 of the 609 every-level designs at bases 2 to 16 carry a Hadamard triple `(b, F', L)` with `L` in `Z`, found by a clique search on `Z/b`; `census` of [lab/py/measure-spectra](../lab/py/measure-spectra/).)

Iterating does not widen the window: the spectrum above for the level `A_n` at base `b^n` needs every exponent of `p` in `S_{A_n}` at most `n e_p`, and the top one is `max Z_p(F) + e_p(n - 1)`. The two designs without one at bases up to 16 are `{0, 1, 8, 9}` and `{0, 3, 8, 11}` at base 12.

**Proved.** No level of `{0, 1, 8, 9}` at base 12 has a spectrum inside `12^(-n) Z`, so no integer Hadamard triple `(12^n, A_n, L)` exists at any level. By the index lemma an order `m` dividing `12^n` lies in `Z(A_n)` exactly when `m/gcd(m, 12^i)` is `2` or `16` for some `i < n`, so every such order is even and its exponent of `2` lies in `{1, 3, ..., 2n - 1} ∪ {4, 6, ..., 2n}`, a set of `2n - 1` values. A spectrum `Gamma` in `Z/12^n` then maps injectively to `Z/2^(2n)`, since two points with one image differ by an element of odd order, and the `2`-adic valuations of its differences take `2n - 1` values; a set whose binary tree branches at `2n - 1` levels has at most `2^(2n - 1) < 4^n` points.

## The level shift

**Proved.** Let `mu = nu * sigma`, where `nu` is the uniform measure on a finite set `S` with a spectrum `Lambda_0` of `|S|` points, `sigma` has a spectrum `Gamma` containing `0`, and `gamma (s - s')` is an integer for every `gamma` in `Gamma - Gamma` and `s, s'` in `S`. Then `Lambda_0 + Gamma` is a spectrum of `mu`. Write `hat mu(xi) = ∫ e(xi x) dmu(x)` for the Fourier transform of a measure, and likewise `hat nu`, `hat sigma`. The condition makes `|hat nu|` invariant under `Gamma - Gamma`, so two points with different `Lambda_0` parts are orthogonal through `hat nu` and two with the same part through `hat sigma`; and `Psi(xi) = sum_lambda |hat mu(xi + lambda)|^2`, Bessel's sum for `e(xi x)` against the family, is `sum_{lambda_0} |hat nu(xi + lambda_0)|^2 sum_gamma |hat sigma(xi + lambda_0 + gamma)|^2 = sum_{lambda_0} |hat nu(xi + lambda_0)|^2 = 1`, so every exponential lies in the closed span, which is all of `L^2(mu)`.

For a direct sum `F = G ⊕ H` and `q >= 1`, rearranging the infinite convolution gives `mu_{b,F} = nu * mu_{b, G + b^(-q) H}` with `nu` uniform on `S = sum_{j=1..q} b^(-j) H`: the factors `b^(-j) H` with `j <= q` stay in `nu`, the rest move up `q` levels. This is the decomposition in the proof of Liu-Wang-Zheng Theorem 3.5, here for any direct sum.

**Proved.** `mu_{12,{0,1,8,9}}` is spectral with spectrum `{0, 3/4} + 3 Lambda(12, {0, 3, 6, 9})`. Take `G = {0, 1}`, `H = {0, 8}`, `q = 1`: `S = {0, 2/3}` has spectrum `{0, 3/4}`, and `G + H/12 = (1/3){0, 2, 3, 5}`. The mask of `{0, 2, 3, 5} = {0, 3} ⊕ {0, 2}` is `Phi_2 Phi_4 Phi_6`, so `(12^(-1){0, 2, 3, 5}, {0, 3, 6, 9})` is compatible, and Łaba-Wang Theorem 1.2 makes `Lambda(12, {0, 3, 6, 9})` a spectrum of `mu_{12,{0,2,3,5}}`; scaling by `1/3` makes `3 Lambda(12, {0, 3, 6, 9})` a spectrum of the second factor, and it lies in `3Z`, where `gamma * 2/3` is an integer. The same steps give `{0, 5/6, 5/3} + 5 Lambda(30, {0, 5, 10, 15, 20, 25})` for `{0, 1} ⊕ 12{0, 1, 2}` at base 30, a design of `pq` digits outside the Hadamard window, its exponent of `3` being `2 > e_3 = 1`. (**Verified**: both spectra are orthogonal in exact arithmetic, and `1 - min Psi` over 32 points of `[-1, 1]` falls to `1.73e-05` at depth 6 and `9.30e-07` at depth 5; the controls `Lambda(4, {0, 3})` for `{0, 2}` at base 4, which keeps `Psi(1) = 0` through the cycle at `1` of Łaba-Wang Theorem 1.3, and `Lambda(12, {0, 3, 6, 9})` alone for `{0, 1, 8, 9}`, which is not orthogonal, fail; `spectra`.) After scaling the digits by 3 and by 5, both spectra are the self-replicating spectra `L_2/N ⊕ Lambda(N, L_1 ⊕ L_2)` that An-Fu-Jiang Corollary 1.4, an unrefereed arXiv preprint, gives for the product-form Hadamard triples `(12, {0, 3, 24, 27}, {0, 6} ⊕ {0, 3})` and `(30, {0, 5, 60, 65, 120, 125}, {0, 15} ⊕ {0, 5, 10})`; their Example 4.1 does the same for `{0, 1, 16, 49}` at base 24.

## The measure at prime-power size

**Proved (the tree lemma).** Let `|A| = p^r` with T1, so that `S_A` is `{p^(c_1), ..., p^(c_r)}`, `c_1 < ... < c_r`. Then each class of `A` mod `p^(j-1)` meets all `p` classes mod `p^j` above it, equally, when `j` is some `c_i`, and exactly one of them otherwise. `Phi_(p^j)` divides `A(x)` exactly when the `p` classes mod `p^j` inside each class mod `p^(j-1)` hold equally many elements of `A`, since a polynomial reduced mod `x^(p^j) - 1` is a multiple of `Phi_(p^j)(x) = sum_{s<p} x^(s p^(j-1))` exactly when its coefficients are constant on each coset of `p^(j-1) Z / p^j Z`. Follow one element: the count of its class starts at `p^r`, is divided by exactly `p` at each `c_i`, never grows, and is `1` far out; the `r` divisions already bring `p^r` to `1`, so no other level splits a class.

**Proved.** If `|F| = p^r` and every level of `F` at base `b` tiles, then `mu_{b,F}` is spectral. By the T1 section `e = e_p >= 1` and the `c_i` are pairwise incongruent mod `e`; write `tau_i` for the residue of `c_i` in `1..e` and `b = p^e b'`. Cut each digit into base-`p^e` windows, `f = sum_j r_j(f) p^(ej)`, so that `b'^K f = sum_{j <= K} b^j b'^(K-j) r_j(f)` for `K` the last window. For a class `d` mod `p^(ej)` met by `F`, let `R_j(d)` be the set of the distinct values `r_j(f)` over the `f` in that class, which are the classes mod `p^(e(j+1))` that `F` meets inside `d`; by the tree lemma the number of those classes below a class mod `p^l` met by `F`, `ej <= l <= e(j+1)`, is `p` to the number of `c_i` in `(l, e(j+1)]`, the same for every class, so `R_j(d)` splits equally, `p` ways, exactly at the levels `c_i - je` in `1..e`, and `Phi_(p^(tau_i))` divides its mask for those `i`, and so the mask of `E_j(d) = b'^(K-j) R_j(d)`, the factor being prime to `p`. With `L_j = {sum k_i b/p^(tau_i)}` over the window's `i`, every `(b, E_j(d), L_j)` is a Hadamard triple by the argument of the Hadamard window, and since the `tau_i` of all windows are distinct, so is every sum `E_m(d_m) ⊕ ... ⊕ E_(m')(d_(m'))` with `L_m ⊕ ... ⊕ L_(m')`; the sizes match, and a Hadamard matrix has rank equal to its size, so each sum is direct. An-Lai Definition 2.3, read at source, asks for each single window and, for every `1 <= m <= K`, for the prefix `E_0 ⊕ ... ⊕ E_m` with `L_0 ⊕ ... ⊕ L_m` and the suffix `E_m ⊕ ... ⊕ E_K` with `L_m ⊕ ... ⊕ L_K`; the argument here gives every range of consecutive windows, which contains those. So this is a `K`-stage product-form Hadamard triple in their sense, with `N = b` and every `l_j = 1`, their Theorem 2.4 makes `mu_{b, b'^K F}` spectral, and `mu_{b,F}` is its image under `x -> x/b'^K`.

The base-12 witness is the case `r = 2`. The set `{0, 1, 8, 25} = ({0} + 4{0, 2}) ∪ ({1} + 4{0, 6})` of An-Lai Example 1.6 is no direct sum, and at base 28 it tiles at every level, so its measure is spectral by this theorem and by none of the sections above; the candidate spectrum `{0, 7/4} + 7 Lambda(28, {0, 7, 14, 21})`, read off its exponents `{1, 4}` as for the base-12 witness, is orthogonal and gives `1 - min Psi = 5.38e-04` at depth 6 (`spectra`), and whether a spectrum read from the exponents alone is complete for every tree set is not decided here. (**Verified**: every gcd-1 tree set of size `2^r` with an exponent above `e_2` at bases 12, 20, 24, 28, 36, 40, 44, 48, and of size `9` with an exponent above `e_3` at bases 36, 45, 72, tiles at every level and passes conditions (i) and (ii) of Definition 2.3 in exact arithmetic, 314 of 314, as do the 40 census designs of prime-power size without a Hadamard triple; `hunt` and `census` of [lab/py/measure-spectra](../lab/py/measure-spectra/).)

## The measure at four digits or fewer

An-He-Lai classify the spectral self-similar measures with at most four maps, read at source: for four integer digits their Theorem 4.2 with the sufficiency of their Section 4.1 is an if and only if, `mu_{b,F}` spectral exactly when `b = 2^beta m` with `beta >= 1` and `m` odd and `F` is, up to scaling, `{0, a, 2^t l, a + 2^t l'}` with `a, l, l'` odd and `beta` not dividing `t` (their Theorem 1.7 states the necessity); for two and three digits their Theorem 5.1 gives the necessity, `b` even for `{0, a}`, and `3 | b` with `F` a rational multiple of a complete residue system mod 3 for three digits, and a compatible pair gives the sufficiency. What this page proves is that their condition is the every-level verdict.

**Proved.** If `|F| <= 4`, the condition of An-He-Lai Theorems 4.2 and 5.1, read at source, holds exactly when every level of `F` at base `b` tiles, so by their classification `mu_{b,F}` is spectral if and only if every level tiles.
- `|F| = 2`, `F = {0, a}`: every level tiles exactly when `b` is even, by the T1 condition with `Z_2(F) = {v_2(a) + 1}`.
- `|F| = 3`: a set of size 3 divisible by `Phi_(3^c)` is `3^(c-1) C` with `C` a complete residue system mod 3, so every level tiles exactly when `F` has this form and `3 | b`.
- `|F| = 4`: by the tree lemma at `p = 2`, T1 for `F` says that `F/2^v`, `v = v_2(gcd F)`, has two even and two odd elements, the evens differing by `2^t` times an odd number and the odds by `2^t` times an odd number for one `t >= 1`, the exponents being `v + 1` and `v + 1 + t`; the T1 condition then reads that `b` is even and `t` is not a multiple of `beta = v_2(b)`.

(**Verified**: every four-digit set `{0, a_1, a_2, a_3}` in `{0..b-1}` at bases 4 to 36, 58905 sets of which 2642 tile at every level, has the every-level verdict equal to the An-He-Lai condition; `four`.) So the guess for the limit measure holds at every size up to 4, in both directions.

## The measure at `pq` digits

**Proved (the fibre lemma).** Let `N = p^alpha q^beta` with `alpha, beta >= 1`. If `Phi_N` divides a polynomial `P` with non-negative integer coefficients, then the exponents of `P` mod `N`, with multiplicity, are a union of `p`-fibres `{x + kN/p : k < p}` and `q`-fibres `{x + kN/q : k < q}`. Put `zeta = e(1/N)` and `omega = zeta^(N/pq)`, of order `pq`; the minimal polynomial of `zeta` over `Q(omega)` is `x^(N/pq) - omega`, both degrees being `N/pq`, so `P(zeta) = sum_{r < N/pq} zeta^r G_r(omega)` vanishes exactly when every `G_r(omega)` does, `G_r` holding the counts `g` on the coset `r + (N/pq)Z`, a copy of `Z/p x Z/q`. There `G_r(omega) = 0` says that the transform of `g` vanishes off the two axes, so `g(i, j) = g(0, j) + (g(i, 0) - g(0, 0))`: a column term, counting `p`-fibres, plus a row term, counting `q`-fibres; subtracting the least row term, taken at a row `i*`, from every row and adding it to every column makes both non-negative: the new row terms are at least `0`, and the new column term is `g(i*, j) >= 0`, which is where the non-negativity of `P` enters. The statement is classical: it is Lam-Leung Theorem 3.3(2) with the reduction of their Theorem 3.1 to the subgroup of order `pq`, which they trace to de Bruijn, read at source; the proof given is self-contained.

**Proved (the shape at `pq` digits).** Let `|F| = pq` with `p != q` primes, `0` in `F`, `gcd(F) = 1`, and every level of `F` at base `b` tiling. Then, naming the primes suitably, `Z_q(F) = {1}`, `Z_p(F) = {c}`, and `F = ⋃_{j<q} (x_j + p^(c-1) q K_j)` with the `x_j` distinct mod `q` and each `K_j` a complete residue system mod `p`. By the T1 section and Theorem B2, `Phi_(p^c)`, `Phi_(q^d)` and `Phi_(p^c q^d)` divide `F`, and the fibre lemma at `N = p^c q^d` writes `F` mod `N` with `pq = p A + q B` fibres' worth of points, so, since `p` divides `B`, `F` is `q` `p`-fibres or `p` `q`-fibres. A `q`-fibre `x + p^c q^(d-1){0..q-1}` is one point mod `p^c`, so in the second case `F` mod `p^c` meets at most `p` residues, which the `p` fibres may share; by the tree lemma every class mod `p^(c-1)` met by `F` meets all `p` classes mod `p^c` above it, so counting classes puts `F` in one class mod `p^(c-1)`, which holds `0`: `p^(c-1)` divides `gcd(F)` and `c = 1`. Symmetrically the first case forces `d = 1`. In either case one prime has exponent `1` and the fibres run along the other; call the first `q`, so that `F` is `q` `p`-fibres `x_j + p^(c-1) q {0..p-1}` mod `p^c q`, and write each element as `x_j + p^(c-1) q k + p^c q t = x_j + p^(c-1) q (k + p t)`. Each group lies in one class mod `q`, and `Phi_q` asks for `p` elements in every class, so the `x_j` are distinct mod `q`.

**Proved (three evaluations).** For `F` of this shape:
- for `1 <= alpha <= c - 1`, `Phi_(p^alpha q)` divides `F` if and only if the `x_j` lie in one class mod `p^alpha`;
- for `beta >= 1`, `Phi_(p^c q^(1 + beta))` divides `F` if and only if every `K_j` lies in one class mod `q^beta`;
- for `1 <= alpha <= c - 1` and `beta >= 1`, `Phi_(p^alpha q^(1 + beta))` does not divide `F`.

Let `zeta` have the order in question and `eta = zeta^(p^(c-1) q)`, so that `F(zeta) = sum_j zeta^(x_j) K_j(eta)`. In the first case `eta = 1`, `F(zeta) = p sum_j zeta^(x_j)`, and with `zeta` the product of a root of order `p^alpha` and one of order `q`, the `q` powers of the second are all `q`-th roots of unity, whose only linear relation over `Q(e(1/p^alpha))` has equal coefficients: the sum vanishes exactly when the `e(x_j/p^alpha)` agree. In the second case `eta` has order `p q^beta` and the minimal polynomial of `zeta` over `Q(eta)` is `x^(p^(c-1) q) - eta`, so the `zeta^(x_j)`, the `x_j` being distinct mod `p^(c-1) q`, are independent over `Q(eta)` and `F(zeta) = 0` exactly when every `K_j(eta) = 0`, which by the argument of the first case with `p` and `q` exchanged says each `K_j` lies in one class mod `q^beta`. In the third case `eta` has order `q^beta`, the powers `zeta^(x_j)` carry `q` distinct roots of order `q^(1+beta)` up to factors in `Q(e(1/p^alpha), eta)`, independent over that field, so `F(zeta) = 0` would need some `K_j(eta) = 0`, that is `Phi_(q^beta)` dividing a set of `p` elements, and `q` does not divide `p`.

**Proved.** If `|F| = pq` with `p != q` primes and every level of `F` at base `b` tiles, then `mu_{b,F}` is spectral: with `F'` the translate of `F` through `0` divided by its gcd and the primes named so that `Z_q(F') = {1}` and `Z_p(F') = {c}`, either `c <= e_p` and the Hadamard window applies, or `w e_p < c <= (w + 1) e_p` and `(b, (b/b_P)^w F', L_1 ⊕ L_2)` is a product-form Hadamard triple of An-Lai Definition 2.1 with `r = w`, `b_P = p^(e_p) q^(e_q)`, `L_1 = (b/q){0..q-1}` and `L_2 = (b/p^(c - w e_p)){0..p-1}`, whose direct sum `L_1 ⊕ L_2` is the set of sums `l_1 + l_2`. Translating and dividing by the gcd keep the measure spectral or not and every level tiling, so it suffices to treat `F = F'`, of the shape above; write `e = e_p` and `f = e_q`. If `c <= e`, then `lcm(S_F) = p^c q` divides `b` and the Hadamard window applies. If `c > e`, let `w >= 1` with `w e < c <= (w + 1) e`. T2 at level `w + 1` asks for `Phi_(p^c q^(1 + f w))`, the exponent `c` read at `i = 0` and `1 + f w` at `i = w`, and by the index lemma its witnesses are the `l <= w` with `p^(c - e l) q^(1 + f(w - l))` in `Z(F)`. At `l = w` the first evaluation puts the `x_j`, and with them all of `F`, in the class of `0` mod `p^(c - e w)`, against `gcd(F) = 1`; the third rules out `0 < l < w`; so `l = 0`, and by the second evaluation every `K_j` lies in one class mod `q^(f w)`, `K_j = kappa_j + q^(f w) K'_j` with `K'_j` again complete mod `p`. With `y_j = x_j + p^(c-1) q kappa_j` and `s = (b/b_P)^w`,

`s F = ⋃_j (s y_j + b^w B_j)`, `B_j = p^(c-1-w e) q K'_j`.

The set `A = {s y_j}` is complete mod `q`, so `(b, A, L_1)` is a Hadamard triple with `L_1 = (b/q){0..q-1}`; each `B_j` is divisible by `Phi_(p^(c - w e))`, so `(b, B_j, L_2)` is one with `L_2 = (b/p^(c - w e)){0..p-1}`, integers since `c - w e <= e`; and `(b, A ⊕ B_j, L_1 ⊕ L_2)` is one, since a difference with both parts nonzero has order `p^(c - w e) q`, where `B_j(zeta) = K'_j(zeta^(q p^(c-1-w e)))` is `K'_j` at a primitive `p`-th root, which is `0`; `L_1 ⊕ L_2` is direct mod `b`, and `A ⊕ B_j` is direct since `A` is distinct mod `q` and `B_j` is divisible by `q`. This is a product-form Hadamard triple of An-Lai Definition 2.1 with `r = w`, and their Theorem 2.2 makes `mu_{b, sF}` spectral, so `mu_{b,F}` is spectral. The base-30 family is the case `w = 1`: `{0, 1} ⊕ 12{0, 1, 2}` has `p = 3`, `c = 2`, groups `x_j = 0, 1` and `K_j = {0, 2, 4}` in one class mod 2.
