Fix a base `b >= 2` and a digit set `F` inside `{0..b-1}` with `k = |F| >= 2`. The level `n` of the dimension-one design is the finite set `A_n = {sum_{i<n} d_i b^i : d_i in F}` of `k^n` integers, and its mask is `A_n(x) = prod_{i<n} F(x^(b^i))` with `F(x) = sum_{d in F} x^d`. A finite set `A` tiles the integers when `A + C = Z` with every integer written once; it is spectral when some set of `|A|` frequencies `theta` has `A(e(theta - theta')) = 0` for every two of them, `e(t) = exp(2 pi i t)`. Every verdict on this page is read from one object, the zero set of [the digit transform](../../mobius/notes/mobius.md) `hat F(t) = sum_{f in F} e(f t)` at roots of unity, and every number is printed by [lab/py/level-tiles](../lab/py/level-tiles/) or, for the limit measure, [lab/py/measure-spectra](../lab/py/measure-spectra/).

## Chapters

- [Which levels tile](tiling-levels.md): Which levels of a dimension-one design tile the integers: the index lemma reads every level's cyclotomic divisors off the zero set of the digit transform, the tiling levels form an initial segment, a closed condition decides T1 at every level, the guess that the digit set must tile its residues fails at base 12, a horizon decides T2, and the classification decides every digit set whose size has at most two prime factors.
- [The limit measure](limit-measure.md): When every level tiles, every level is spectral and the limit measure is spectral at prime-power size, at `pq` digits and at four digits or fewer, through the Hadamard window and the level shift, with an explicit spectrum at base 12 for `{0, 1, 8, 9}`, no multiple of a complementing set.
- [The converse](converse.md): A spectral limit measure whose levels are sets needs integer orthogonal sets of every size `|F|^h`, which through the covering bound proves that every level tiles at five and seven digits, and at eleven and thirteen digits at every base whose `{2, 3}`-part is below the size, the bound alone fails at base 60, and the gap names what both directions leave open: a size `p^alpha q^beta` with `alpha + beta >= 3`, or with three primes.

## The census

The census runs every digit set containing `0` with at least two digits at every base from 2 to 16, 65519 sets, and prints per base the number that tile `Z/b`, the number that tile at every level, the number that pass the T1 condition, the counterexamples to the guess before and after the gcd, the T2 horizon and the histogram of tiling depths (`census`). The totals: 588 tile `Z/b`, 609 tile at every level, 641 pass the T1 condition; the 32 that pass it and fail T2 are all of size 6 at base 12, and fail already at level 1. At a prime base only the full digit set tiles every level, since the size must divide the base. Every tiling verdict at levels up to 3 and bases up to 12, 803 of them, carries a Coven-Meyerowitz complement whose sum with the level is checked to cover every residue mod `lcm(S)` once, and 492 levels at bases up to 8 and levels up to 2 agree with an exhaustive complement search over `Z/N`, `|A| | N <= 512`, which finds a complement for every tiling verdict and none for the others; two more levels pass the search's step cap (`search`).

## Literature

- Coven and Meyerowitz, the T1 and T2 conditions, Theorems A, B1 and B2, the scaling lemma 1.4, the residue lemma 1.3 and the period lemmas 2.1 and 2.3, read at source on arXiv; the index lemma generalises their Lemma 1.1(7).
- Łaba, Theorem 1.5, read at source on arXiv: T1 and T2 give a spectrum; a spectrum inside `p^(-alpha) Z` forces a prime-power size and T1.
- Łaba and Wang, Theorems 1.2 to 1.4 with Lemmas 2.1 to 2.3, Lemma 2.2(b), (d) among them, and the proof of Theorem 1.4: a compatible pair gives a spectral measure, `Lambda(N, S)` is a spectrum unless a cycle of nonzero integers exists, and a complementing set mod `b` with at most two prime factors in its size gives a spectral measure; Conjecture 3.1.
- An and Lai, Adv. Math. 431 (2023) 109257, Definitions 1.5, 2.1 and 2.3, Theorems 2.2, 2.4 and 2.6, Examples 1.3, 1.6 and 8.4 and the reductions of Section 3: product-form Hadamard triples of one and of `k` stages give spectral measures, and so do the four-digit sets of Theorem 2.6.
- Dutkay and Jorgensen, Math. Z. 261 (2009), Example 3.9 and Remark 3.11, read at source on arXiv: `mu_{4,{0,1,8,9}}` is Lebesgue measure on `[0, 1] ∪ [2, 3]`, spectral with spectrum `Z + {0, 1/4}`, against Łaba-Wang Conjecture 3.1(c).
- An, He and Lai, Section 1.1, Theorems 1.7, 4.2 and 5.1 with the sufficiency proof of Section 4.1: the spectral self-similar measures with at most four maps; Conjecture 1.6, a modified Łaba-Wang conjecture; Example 5.2 and the closing remark of Section 5: five digits open, `{0, 1, 3, 5, 6}` with irrational zeros only, two and three digits settled in Theorem 5.1 through their Theorem 1.2 and An-Wang, and the modified Łaba-Wang conjecture called hard above four digits because irrational zeros appear from five digits on.
- Jorgensen and Pedersen, Section 6 and Theorem 6.1: base 4 spectral, odd base at most two orthogonal exponentials.
- Dai, He and Lau, Theorem 1.1: `mu_{1/q, {0..N-1}}` is spectral if and only if `N | q`; Section 6 restates the Łaba-Wang conjecture.
- Dutkay, Haussermann and Lai, the abstract and Theorem 1.3, read at source on arXiv: Hadamard triples give spectral self-affine measures in every dimension.
- An, Fu and Jiang, an unrefereed arXiv preprint, the abstract, Theorems 1.2, 1.3 and 1.5, Corollary 1.4 and Example 4.1: when a self-replicating set `L_2/N ⊕ Gamma` is a spectrum of a product-form Hadamard triple measure, and an explicit spectrum for every such measure.
- Liu, Wang and Zheng, an unrefereed arXiv preprint, Section 1.1, Theorem 1.3 and the proof of Theorem 3.5: the spectral classification of `{0..N-1} + m{0..N'-1}` and the decomposition of the level shift.
- Wu and Xiao, Section 1 with Theorems 1.3 and 1.4: the question whether a spectral Cantor-Moran measure has tiling levels, and its answer for equidifferent digit sets of prime size.
- An, Li and Zhang, Pacific J. Math. 334 (2025), the abstract and Theorems 1.2 and 1.5, read at source on arXiv: a Cantor-Moran measure with consecutive digit sets `{0..N_n-1}` is spectral if and only if `N_n | b_n` for `n >= 2`, if and only if every level is an integer tile.
- Lam and Leung, Theorems 2.2, 3.1, 3.3 and 4.8 with Corollary 3.4, Theorem 6.5 and Example 6.7: a vanishing sum of `N`-th roots of unity with non-negative coefficients is a non-negative combination of `p`-fibres and `q`-fibres when `N = p^alpha q^beta`, after de Bruijn; a minimal vanishing sum is a single fibre or has at least three primes `p_1 < p_2 < p_3` and weight at least `(p_1 - 1)(p_2 - 1) + p_3 - 1`; the sum of that weight is unique up to rotation, and `z^5 + z^6 + z^7 + z^17 + z^18 + z^24 + z^25` is a minimal asymmetric sum of weight 7 at `m = 30`.
- Poonen and Rubinstein, Section 3, Lemma 1 (Mann), Theorem 3 and Table 1: the 107 minimal relations `sum n_i zeta_i = 0` with positive integer `n_i` and distinct roots of weight up to 12, up to rotation; at weight 7 the fibre `R_7` and the two relations `(R_5 : 2 R_3)`.
- An and Wang, J. Funct. Anal. 280 (2021) 108821, not read here: its publisher listing describes a characterisation of the spectral self-similar measures associated with an integer tile; Liu-Wang-Zheng Section 1.1 quote its Theorem 1.7 on strict product-form digit sets with respect to `pq`, which may overlap the `pq` theorem.
- Li, Wu, Hu, Wang, Wang and Zhu, an unrefereed arXiv preprint, abstract read: it claims that T1 and T2 characterise the integer tiles of every size, so that every tile satisfies T2; a preprint is not a theorem here, and the statements above that call this open stand.
