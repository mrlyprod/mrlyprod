## The converse at prime size

The converse asks that a spectral `mu_{b,F}` have every level tiling. At source it is settled on few families: Łaba-Wang Theorem 1.4 is a sufficiency only, a complementing set mod `b` whose size has at most two prime factors giving a spectral measure, with the converse their Conjecture 3.1; Dai-He-Lau Theorem 1.1 settles consecutive digits `{0..N-1}`, An-Li-Zhang Theorems 1.2 and 1.5 their Cantor-Moran measures, and Wu-Xiao Theorem 1.3 the necessity for equidifferent digits of prime size; An-He-Lai Theorem 5.1 settles two and three digits, so prime size `p = 2, 3`, and their closing remark leaves five or more digits open, naming `{0, 1, 3, 5, 6}`, whose transform vanishes only at irrational points. This section proves a necessary condition at every size, which zeros off the roots of unity cannot spoil, sharpens it to a covering bound, and with it proves the converse at five digits.

Write `Theta` for the finite set of zeros of `hat F` in `[0, 1)`, so that the zero set of `hat mu(xi) = prod_{j>=1} hat F(b^(-j) xi)/k` is `⋃_{j>=1} b^j (Theta + Z)`; an integer `d` lies in it exactly when `b^j/gcd(d, b^j)` is in `Z(F)` for some `j >= 1`, and then `d` reaches that order at level `j`.

**Proved (the class count).** If `A_n` is a set of `k^n` integers, as when `F` lies in `{0..b-1}`, then a spectrum `Lambda` of `mu = mu_{b,F}` meets at least `k^n` classes of `R/b^n Z`. Write `hat mu(xi) = hat mu_n(xi) hat mu(b^(-n) xi)` with `mu_n` uniform on `b^(-n) A_n`; `hat mu_n` has period `b^n` and is `1` on `b^n Z`. Two points of one class `Lambda_r` differ by `b^n z`, so `0 = hat mu(b^n z) = hat mu(z)`, the set `b^(-n) Lambda_r` is orthogonal in `L^2(mu)`, and Bessel's inequality bounds `sum_{lambda in Lambda_r} |hat mu(b^(-n)(x - lambda))|^2` by `1`. Splitting Parseval's `sum_lambda |hat mu(x - lambda)|^2 = 1` by classes gives `1 <= sum_r |hat mu_n(x - r)|^2` for every `x`; with finitely many classes, the mean of `|hat mu_n|^2` over a period is `k^(-n)`, so their number is at least `k^n`.

**Proved (finitely many rational cosets).** A spectrum through `0` meets finitely many cosets of `Q`. In the `Q`-vector space `R/Q` its points and their differences map into `{0}` and the sets `b^j x`, `j >= 1`, with `x` the image of an irrational element of `Theta`. If infinitely many points mapped to `b^j x` for one `x`, then for two such `j > j'` the difference `(b^j - b^(j')) x` is some `b^i v x` with `v x` the image of an element of `Theta`, so `b^(j - j') - 1 = v b^(i - j')`. An integer prime to `b` equals `v b^s` for at most one `s`, and `v` takes finitely many values, so `j - j'` takes finitely many values over infinitely many `j`, which is impossible.

**Proved (integer orthogonal sets).** If every `A_n` is a set and `mu_{b,F}` is spectral, then for every `h >= 1` some `k^h` integers, pairwise incongruent mod `b^h`, have every difference a zero of `hat mu`. Take a spectrum `Lambda` through `0`, its points `Lambda_v` in one coset of `Q`, and `M` the lcm of `Z(F)`. Differences in `Lambda_v` are rational zeros, in `M^(-1) Z`, so `Gamma = M(Lambda_v - lambda_v)` is a set of integers whose classes mod `M b^n` are those of `Lambda_v` mod `b^n Z`. Two points `gamma_0 + M b^(th) x` and `gamma_0 + M b^(th) x'` of `Gamma` differ by `M b^j (theta + i)` with `theta` a rational zero, not an integer, so `j > th` and `x - x'` is a zero of `hat mu` at level `j - th`. One point of each class mod `M b^((t+1)h)` met inside a class mod `M b^(th)` thus gives integers incongruent mod `b^h` with orthogonal differences; if `g_h` bounds their number, `Lambda` meets at most `C M g_h^(ceil(n/h))` classes mod `b^n`, `C` its number of cosets of `Q`, and the class count gives `k^n <= C M g_h^(n/h + 1)` for every `n`, so `g_h >= k^h`. A zero of `hat F` off the roots of unity costs only the constant `C`.

**Proved.** If every `A_n` is a set and `mu_{b,F}` is spectral, then `k <= b` and `Z(F)` holds an order whose primes all divide `b`; otherwise `hat mu` has no integer zero, `g_1 = 1 < k`, and `mu_{b,F}` is not spectral. When `Z(F)` is empty no set hypothesis is needed: `hat mu` has no rational zero, so a spectrum through `0` holds at most one point in each of its finitely many cosets of `Q` and is finite, while `L^2(mu)` is infinite-dimensional, the support of `mu` being infinite. So `mu_{b,{0,1,3,5,6}}` is not spectral at any integer base `b >= 2`, the bases 2 to 6, where levels repeat points (`1 + 4 * 1 = 5 + 4 * 0` at base 4), included: the mask `1 + x + x^3 + x^5 + x^6` has no cyclotomic factor (`converse` of [lab/py/measure-spectra](../lab/py/measure-spectra/)). An-He-Lai Example 5.2 takes a real contraction ratio in `(0, 1)` and believes the self-similar measure of `{0,1,3,5,6}` is spectral at none; this proves it at every ratio `1/b` with `b >= 2` an integer, a non-integer reciprocal stays open. The converse is in print for consecutive digits, by Dai-He-Lau Theorem 1.1 and for Cantor-Moran measures An-Li-Zhang Theorems 1.2 and 1.5, for equidifferent digits of prime size, by Wu-Xiao Theorem 1.3, and at two to four digits, by An-He-Lai; the five- and seven-digit theorems below extend it to every digit set of that size containing `0` whose levels are sets, at integer bases. Neither that statement for a general digit set nor this integer case was found in the sources read, at the statements the refs name: Łaba-Wang, Dai-He-Lau, Dutkay-Jorgensen, Dutkay-Haussermann-Lai, An-Lai, An-He-Lai, An-Li-Zhang, Wu-Xiao, Liu-Wang-Zheng and An-Fu-Jiang. The same corollary rebuilds the non-spectrality of `{0, 2}` at odd base, where `Z = {4}` has no order with primes in `b`; Jorgensen and Pedersen prove more, at most two orthogonal exponentials.

**Proved (one level).** Let `m` be in `Z(F)` with every prime of `m` dividing `b`, and `j(m)` the least `j` with `m | b^j`. If `b` does not divide `d` and `d` reaches `m` at level `j`, then `j = j(m)` and `v_q(d) = j(m) e_q - v_q(m)` for every prime `q` of `m`. For `q | b`, `v_q(m) = j e_q - min(v_q(d), j e_q)`, and `m | b^j` gives `j >= j(m)`. Some `q` has `v_q(d) < e_q`; it divides `m`, since otherwise `v_q(d) >= j e_q`; so `(j - 1) e_q < v_q(m) <= j(m) e_q` and `j < j(m) + 1`. So integers pairwise incongruent mod `b` whose differences all reach `m` have every difference of one `q`-adic valuation, and number at most `q`, the least prime of `m`.

**Proved (prime size).** Let `|F| = p` be prime, `0` in `F`, every `A_n` a set, and `F' = F/gcd(F)`.
- The only prime power in `Z(F')` is `p`, and it is there exactly when `F'` is a complete residue system mod `p`; then every level tiles exactly when `p | b`.
- Every other order in `Z(F')` has its least prime below `p`.
- If `mu_{b,F}` is spectral, then some `p` integers, pairwise incongruent mod `b`, have every difference reaching an order of `Z(F')`; if one difference reaches `p`, every level tiles.
- So if `Z(F')` holds at most one order that is not a prime power and has all its primes in `b`, then `mu_{b,F}` is spectral if and only if every level tiles.

Scaling keeps the measure spectral or not, the levels sets and every level tiling, so take `F = F'`, which keeps `0` in `F` and has `gcd(F) = 1`; without `0` the statement fails, `{1, 6, 11, 16, 21}` at base 25 having `Z(F') = {25}` while every level tiles. A factor `Phi_(q^c)` makes `F` a union of `q`-fibres mod `q^c` by the tree lemma, so `q = p` and `F` is one class mod `p^(c-1)`, which forces `c = 1`; then T1 holds with `S_F = {p}`, and the T1 section reads every level tiling as `p | b`. For another order `m`, Lam-Leung Theorem 4.8, read at source, writes `F` mod `m` as minimal vanishing sums, each a fibre of a prime of `m` or of weight above the third prime of `m`; if every prime of `m` were at least `p`, the weight `p` would leave one `p`-fibre, putting `F` in one class mod `m/p` against `gcd(F) = 1`. The integers are the integer orthogonal sets at `h = 1`. A difference reaching `p` makes `p` divide `b^j`, so `p | b`, and `p` is in `Z(F)`. With one other order `m` available, if no difference reaches `p`, all reach `m`, and the one-level lemma caps the set at the least prime of `m`, below `p`; the forward direction is the theorem at prime-power size.

**Verified.** Over every digit set with `0` and gcd `1` in `{0..b-1}` of size `5` at bases 5 to 42, `7` at 7 to 26, `11` at 11 to 20 and `13` at 13 to 20, 803630, 654288, 167960 and 77520 sets, every level tiles on 8131, 784, 1 and 1, the verdict read as `p` in `Z(F)` by the first bullet, and on each of the others a clique search on `Z/b`, where `d` is an edge when its pattern mod each `q^(e_q)` is that of an order of `Z(F)` at its level, bounds the integers of the theorem by `3`, below `p`; so on these boxes `mu_{b,F}` is spectral if and only if every level tiles. Of the non-tiling sets, 1674200 have no non-prime-power order with primes in `b` and 20085 have one, decided by the theorem above, and 196 have more, decided by the search alone (`converse`).

**Refuted.** The lemma that no `p` integers pairwise incongruent mod `b` have every difference reaching a non-prime-power order of `Z(F')`, which with the theorem above would give the converse at prime size from `h = 1` alone. `F = {0, 1, 251, 375, 501}` at base `510 = 2 * 3 * 5 * 17` has residues `{0, 0, 1, 1, 1}` mod 5, so not every level tiles; mod 750 it is the 2-fibre `{0, 375}` and the 3-fibre `{1, 251, 501}`, and the same holds mod 150, 30 and 6, so `6, 30, 150, 750` are orders with primes in `b`, at levels 1, 1, 2, 3, and `converse` finds no other. The integers `0, 17, 1734, 1751, 176868` are pairwise incongruent mod 510, and every difference reaches 30 at level 1, 150 at level 2 or 750 at level 3 (`converse`). The orders at higher levels let the clique branch at three depths, each split simultaneous in the 2-adic and the 3-adic tree.

**Proved (the covering bound).** Let every `A_n` be a set and `W` a set of primes meeting every order of `Z(F)` whose primes divide `b`; for `q` in `W` let `rho_q` count the classes mod `e_q` met by `v_q(m)` over those orders `m` divisible by `q`. If `mu_{b,F}` is spectral, then `k <= prod_{q in W} q^(rho_q)`. Take the `k^h` integers of the integer orthogonal sets, pairwise incongruent mod `b^h`. A difference `d` reaching `m` at level `j` has `m | b^j`, so `j >= j(m)`; some `q'` has `v_q'(d) < h e_q'`, and either `q'` does not divide `m` and `j e_q' <= v_q'(d)` gives `j < h`, or `(j - h) e_q' < v_q'(m) <= j(m) e_q'`; so `j < J + h` with `J` the largest `j(m)`. For `q` in `W` dividing `m`, `v_q(d) = j e_q - v_q(m)` lies in `[0, (J + h) e_q)` and in one of `rho_q` classes mod `e_q`, so it takes at most `rho_q (J + h)` values. Every difference reaches an order divisible by some `q` in `W`, and two of the integers whose difference has `q`-adic valuation `delta` differ in their `q`-adic digit at position `delta`, so their digits at those positions, over `q` in `W`, tell all of them apart: `k^h <= prod_q q^(rho_q (J + h))` for every `h`. The witness above has `W = {2}` and `v_2(m) = 1` on all four orders, so `rho_2 = 1`, `5 > 2`, and its measure is not spectral; `{0, 1, 9, 10}` at base 12, which at `h = 1` has the four integers `0, 2, 8, 10` incongruent mod 12 with orthogonal differences though T1 reads `4` against `Phi_2(1) = 2`, has orders `2, 6, 18`, all with `v_2 = 1`, and covering bound `2 < 4`.

**Proved (five digits).** If `|F| = 5`, `0` is in `F` and every `A_n` is a set, then `mu_{b,F}` is spectral if and only if every level tiles. Take `gcd(F) = 1` and suppose not every level tiles; by the prime-size theorem no order of `Z(F)` with primes in `b` is a prime power. For such an order `m`, Lam-Leung Theorem 4.8 writes `F` mod `m` as minimal vanishing sums, each a fibre of a prime of `m` or of weight above the third prime, at least 6; the weight 5 is a single 5-fibre, which puts `F` in one class mod `m/5`, so `m/5` divides `gcd(F) = 1` and `m = 5` is a prime power, or a 2-fibre and a 3-fibre. So `F = P ∪ T` with the pair `P` differing by `m/2` and the triple `T` by `m/3` mod `m`: `v_2(m) = v_2(d_P) + 1` for the difference `d_P` of `P`, and `T` lies in one class mod `2^(v_2(m))`. Two such splittings `(P, T)`, `(P', T')` with exponents `c`, `c'` have `c = c'`. If `P' = P`, both equal `v_2(d_P) + 1`. If `P' ⊂ T` then `P ⊂ T'`, and `c - 1 >= c'`, `c' - 1 >= c` cannot both hold. Otherwise `P = {f_1, f_2}`, `P' = {f_1, f_3}` with `f_3` in `T`, `f_2` in `T'`, and some `f_4` in `T ∩ T'`; in `f_1 - f_2 = (f_1 - f_3) + (f_3 - f_4) + (f_4 - f_2)` the terms have 2-adic valuations `c' - 1`, at least `c`, at least `c'`, so `c' < c` would give `c - 1 = c' - 1`, and `c' > c` fails with the roles exchanged. So every order of `Z(F)` with primes in `b` is even with one exponent of 2, and the covering bound with `W = {2}`, `rho_2 <= 1`, reads `5 <= 2` (at odd `b` no order has its primes in `b`, and the bound is 1): the measure is not spectral. The forward direction is the theorem at prime-power size.

**Proved.** If `F` passes the T1 condition, in particular if every level tiles, its covering bound is at least `|F|`. Each `p` with `Z_p(F)` nonempty divides `b`, so the orders `p^c`, `c` in `Z_p(F)`, have their primes in `b`; a covering set `W` contains `p`, and `rho_p >= |Z_p(F)|` since those `c` are pairwise incongruent mod `e_p`; so the bound is at least `prod_p p^(|Z_p(F)|) = |F|`.

**Verified.** On the census boxes above the covering bound is below `p` on every non-tiling set, 795499, 653504, 167959 and 77519 of them; over every four-digit set with `0` and gcd `1` in `{0..b-1}` at bases 4 to 36, it is below 4 on all 48897 that do not tile at every level, so the bound alone gives the necessity half of the An-He-Lai classification on that box (`converse`).

**Refuted (a conjecture on the census boxes, refuted at base 60 in the eleven-and-thirteen section below).** The covering bound of `F` at base `b` is at least `|F|` exactly when `F` passes the T1 condition. Checked on every gcd-1 set with `0` in `{0..b-1}` of the census boxes at sizes 4, 5, 7, 11 and 13, of size 6 at bases 30 and 42, 8684 and 49566 passing and 107942 and 682990 failing, and of size 8 at base 24, 2188 passing and 242638 failing (`converse`). With the covering bound it would make a spectral measure whose levels are sets pass the T1 condition, which is the converse at every prime-power size and leaves only T2 at the other sizes.

The converse at prime size is proved at `p <= 7`, by An-He-Lai at 2 and 3 and below at five and seven digits with `0` in `F` and levels that are sets, and decided on every census set at 11 and 13. Above 7 the proof stops at the covering set: at `p = 11` the splitting `3 + 3 + 5` lives at odd orders `15 | m`, so no single prime meets every order, and a bound `2^(rho_2) 3^(rho_3)` or `2^(rho_2) 5^(rho_5)` below 11 would need both counts to be `1`, which fails at base 60 (eleven and thirteen digits, below).

## The converse at seven digits

Two facts from the literature, both read at source. Lam-Leung's Lower Bound Theorem 4.8: a minimal vanishing sum of `m`-th roots of unity with non-negative integer coefficients is symmetric, a fibre `{x + i m/p : i < p}` of a prime `p` of `m`, or `m` has at least three primes `p_1 < p_2 < p_3` and the sum has weight at least `(p_1 - 1)(p_2 - 1) + p_3 - 1`, which is `6` for `2, 3, 5` and at least `8` for every other triple; their Uniqueness Theorem 6.5 makes the weight-6 sum unique up to rotation, and their Example 6.7 gives the weight-7 asymmetric sum `z^5 + z^6 + z^7 + z^17 + z^18 + z^24 + z^25` at `m = 30`. Poonen-Rubinstein's Theorem 3 with Table 1: the minimal relations `sum n_i zeta_i = 0`, `n_i` positive integers and `zeta_i` distinct roots of unity, of weight `sum n_i` up to 12 are, up to rotation, the 107 of their table; at weight 7 they are `R_7`, the 7-fibre, and the two relations `(R_5 : 2 R_3)`, a 5-fibre with two of its points `zeta` each replaced by `zeta e(1/6)` and `zeta e(-1/6)`, since `zeta = -zeta e(1/3) - zeta e(2/3)`. Their Lemma 1, Mann's theorem, rotates a minimal relation with `k` distinct roots into roots of unity of order dividing `prod_{p <= k} p`, so the table at weight 7 covers every order `m`, repeated residues included.

**Proved (three shapes).** Let `|F| = 7`, `0` in `F`, `gcd(F) = 1`, every `A_n` a set, and not every level tiling. Then every order `m` of `Z(F)` whose primes divide `b` is even, and `F` mod `m` is one of
- (a) a 5-fibre `Y` and a 2-fibre `P`;
- (b) a 3-fibre `T` and two 2-fibres `P_1`, `P_2`;
- (c) with `30 | m`, the three points `U = {y + i m/5 : i in I}` and the four points `V = {y + i m/5 + m/6, y + i m/5 - m/6 : i < 5, i not in I}`, for some `y` and some 3-set `I` of classes mod 5.

By the prime-size theorem the only prime power that `Z(F)` can hold is `7`, and `7` with `7 | b` makes every level tile, so no order with primes in `b` is a prime power. For such an `m`, `F` mod `m` is a vanishing sum of weight 7 with non-negative coefficients, hence a sum of minimal ones, each a fibre of weight `2`, `3`, `5` or `7`, or asymmetric with `30 | m` and weight `6` or `7`. The weight `7` splits as `7`, `5 + 2` or `3 + 2 + 2`; `6 + 1` and `4 + 3` are not sums of these weights. A 7-fibre puts `F` in one class mod `m/7`, so `m/7` divides `gcd(F) = 1` and `m = 7`, excluded; a minimal relation of weight 7 that is not `R_7` is `(R_5 : 2 R_3)`, which is (c). In (a) and (b) the 2-fibre holds two points differing by `m/2` mod `m`, in (c) `6 | m`: `m` is even.

**Proved (the 2-adic and 3-adic reading).** Write `c = v_2(m)`. A 2-fibre `{f, f'}` has `f - f' = (m/2)(2t + 1)`, so `v_2(f - f') = c - 1` and the odd part of `m` divides `f - f'`. A 5-fibre or a 3-fibre lies in one class mod `m/5` or `m/3`, so in one class mod `2^c`, and the three points of a 3-fibre have `v_3(f - f') = v_3(m) - 1` on every difference. In (c), `m/5` is divisible by `2^c` and by `3^(v_3(m))`, and `m/6` is `2^(c-1)` times an odd number and `3^(v_3(m) - 1)` times a number prime to 3; so `F` lies in one class mod `2^(c-1)`, `U` in one class mod `2^c` and in one class mod `3^(v_3(m))`, and `V` in the other class mod `2^c`. Of the three ways to split `V` into two pairs, the pairs of equal sign, `{y + i_1 m/5 + m/6, y + i_2 m/5 + m/6}` with its mirror, have differences `(i_1 - i_2) m/5 + tm`, divisible by `3^(v_3(m))`, while the pairs of one `i` have differences `m/3 + tm` and the pairs of different `i` and different sign have differences `(i_1 - i_2) m/5 + m/3 + tm`, all with `v_3 = v_3(m) - 1`.

**Proved (two exponents).** Under the hypotheses of the three shapes, `v_2(m)` takes at most two values over the orders `m` of `Z(F)` whose primes divide `b`. Call the partition of `F` into its classes mod `2^j` the tree at depth `j`; deeper is finer. At an order `m` with `c = v_2(m)` the shapes read on depths `c - 1` and `c`: in (c) depth `c - 1` is the one node `F` and depth `c` is `{U, V}`, of sizes `3, 4`; in (a) `P` splits at depth `c` and `Y` is a node there, so depth `c - 1` is `{F}` with depth `c` of sizes `6, 1`, or `{P, Y}` with sizes `1, 1, 5`; in (b) each `P_i` splits at depth `c` and `T` is a node there, so depth `c - 1` is a coarsening of `{P_1, P_2, T}`, and depth `c` has sizes `5, 2` under `{F}`, `2, 2, 3` under `{P_1 ∪ P_2, T}`, each 2-node holding one point of each `P_i`, `4, 1, 1, 1` under `{P_1 ∪ T, P_2}` or its mirror, and `1, 1, 1, 1, 3` under `{P_1, P_2, T}`. In every shape depth `c` has more nodes than depth `c - 1`. Suppose `c_1 < c_2 < c_3` are three values at orders `m_1, m_2, m_3`. Depth `c_2 - 1` refines depth `c_1`, so it has two nodes or more, and so does depth `c_3 - 1`; hence `m_2` and `m_3` have depth `c - 1` of sizes `2, 5` in shape (a) or `4, 3`, `5, 2`, `2, 2, 3` in shape (b). Depth `c_3 - 1` refines depth `c_2`, of sizes `1, 1, 5`, `2, 2, 3`, `4, 1, 1, 1` or `1, 1, 1, 1, 3`; a refinement has at least as many nodes, depth `c_3 - 1` has at most three and depth `c_2` at least three, so both have three and a refinement with equal node counts is an equality, which leaves `2, 2, 3` equal to itself, `2, 2, 3` differing from `1, 1, 5`. So depth `c_3 - 1` equals depth `c_2`, `m_2` has shape (b) with depth `c_2 - 1` equal to `{P_1 ∪ P_2, T}`, and `m_3` has shape (b) with 3-fibre `T` and 2-fibres the two 2-nodes `{p_1, p_2}` and `{p_1', p_2'}` of depth `c_2`, `p_i, p_i'` the points of `P_i`. Depth `c_2 - 1`, of sizes `4, 3`, refines depth `c_1`, whose sizes are `3, 4`, `6, 1`, `1, 1, 5`, `5, 2`, `2, 2, 3`, `4, 1, 1, 1` or `1, 1, 1, 1, 3`; only `3, 4` fits, so `m_1` has shape (c) with `U = T` and `V = P_1 ∪ P_2`. Now `U` lies in one class mod `3^(v_3(m_1))`, while as the 3-fibre of `m_i`, `i = 2, 3`, its differences have `v_3 = v_3(m_i) - 1`, so `v_3(m_i) >= v_3(m_1) + 1`, and the 2-fibres of `m_i` have differences divisible by `3^(v_3(m_i))`. But `{P_1, P_2}` and `{{p_1, p_2}, {p_1', p_2'}}` are two different splittings of `V` into pairs, at most one of them the equal-sign splitting, so a 2-fibre of `m_2` or of `m_3` has a difference with `v_3 = v_3(m_1) - 1 < v_3(m_i)`.

**Proved (seven digits).** If `|F| = 7`, `0` is in `F` and every `A_n` is a set, then `mu_{b,F}` is spectral if and only if every level tiles. Scaling keeps the measure spectral or not, the levels sets and every level tiling, so take `gcd(F) = 1`. If not every level tiles, every order of `Z(F)` with primes in `b` is even and `v_2` takes at most two values on them, so `W = {2}` meets every such order with `rho_2 <= 2`, and the covering bound reads `7 <= 4` (at odd `b` there is no such order and the bound is `1`): the measure is not spectral. The forward direction is the theorem at prime-power size.

## The converse at eleven and thirteen digits

The two facts of the seven-digit section carry the next two primes: Lam-Leung's bound `(q_1 - 1)(q_2 - 1) + q_3 - 1` on an asymmetric minimal sum, and the fibre lemma that at an order with at most two primes every minimal sum is a fibre. The weights 11 and 13 are too small for most asymmetric sums and too odd for pure powers of 2 and 3, which pins every relevant order to a pair of primes with 2 or 3 among them; the covering bound then reads off the `{2, 3}`-part of `b`, and a search at the first bases it leaves shows exactly where it stops.

Call `{q, r}` a pair of `p` when `p = uq + vr` with `u, v >= 1`: the pairs of 11 are `{2, 3}, {2, 5}, {2, 7}, {3, 5}`, those of 13 these and `{2, 11}, {3, 7}` (`prime` of [lab/py/measure-spectra](../lab/py/measure-spectra/)).

**Proved (the pairs).** Let `|F| = p` be 11 or 13, `0` in `F`, `gcd(F) = 1`, every `A_n` a set, and not every level tiling. Then the primes of every order `m` of `Z(F)` whose primes divide `b` contain a pair of `p`; in particular `m` is divisible by 2 or by 3, an odd `m` by 3 and by 5, or at `p = 13` by 5 or 7, and an `m` prime to 3 by 2 and by 5 or 7, or at `p = 13` by 5, 7 or 11. By the prime-size theorem no such `m` is a prime power. `F` mod `m` is a sum of minimal vanishing sums, each a fibre of a prime of `m` or asymmetric with three primes `q_1 < q_2 < q_3` of `m` and weight at least `(q_1 - 1)(q_2 - 1) + q_3 - 1`. If every sum is a fibre, the primes `U` used satisfy `p = sum_{q in U} u_q q` with every `u_q >= 1`; a single prime gives the `p`-fibre, which puts `F` in one class mod `m/p`, so `m/p` divides `gcd(F) = 1` and `m = p`, excluded; two primes form a pair; three or more primes sum to at least `2 + 3 + 5 = 10`, so at `p = 11` they do not occur and at `p = 13` only `13 = 2 + 3 + 3 + 5` with `U = {2, 3, 5}`, which holds the pair `{2, 3}`. If an asymmetric sum occurs, its three primes have `(q_1 - 1)(q_2 - 1) + q_3 - 1 <= 13`, so `q_1 = 2` and either `q_2 = 3` with `q_3` in `{5, 7, 11}` or `q_2 = 5, q_3 = 7`: the primes of `m` contain `{2, 3}` or `{2, 5}`. Every pair holds 2 or 3; the odd pairs are `{3, 5}` and at `p = 13` `{3, 7}`; the pairs without 3 are `{2, 5}, {2, 7}` and at `p = 13` `{2, 11}`.

**Proved (eleven and thirteen digits).** Let `|F| = p` be 11 or 13, `0` in `F`, every `A_n` a set, and `e_q = v_q(b)`. If some set `W` of primes of `b` meets every pair of `p` whose two primes divide `b`, where `W` meets a pair or an order when it shares a prime with it, and `prod_{q in W} q^(e_q) < p`, then `mu_{b,F}` is spectral if and only if every level of `F` at base `b` tiles. In particular this holds when `2^(e_2) 3^(e_3) < p`, when `b = 2^(e_2) 3^(e_3)` with `e_2 <= 3` or `e_3 <= 2`, 144 and 216 among them, at `p = 11` when `5` does not divide `b` and `e_2 <= 3`, at `p = 13` when neither 5 nor 7 divides `b` and `e_2 <= 3`, and at every base whose primes hold no pair, prime powers among them. Scaling keeps the measure spectral or not, the levels sets and every level tiling, so take `gcd(F) = 1`. If not every level tiles, every relevant order has a pair of `p` among its primes, so `W` meets it, and `rho_q`, a number of classes mod `e_q`, is at most `e_q`; the covering bound reads `p <= prod_{q in W} q^(rho_q) <= prod_{q in W} q^(e_q) < p`, so the measure is not spectral. The forward direction is the theorem at prime-power size. Without `5 | b` there is no odd pair inside the primes of `b` at `p = 11`, and without `5 | b` and `7 | b` none at `p = 13`, so `W = {2}` serves and the bound is `2^(e_2)`, which needs `e_2 <= 3` at both primes, as `432 = 2^4 3^3` shows; at `b = 2^(e_2) 3^(e_3)` both `{2}` and `{3}` serve. No prime power is an order, so T1 fails and not every level tiles; `W` must hold two of `2, 3, 5`, and `W = {2, 3}` has `v_2 = 1, 2` at `e_2 = 2`, so `rho_2 = 2`, `rho_3 = 1`, and the bound is `12 >= 11` (`prime`). ## The gap

What the sections above leave open in dimension one, from tiling levels to a spectrum, is a size `p^alpha q^beta` with `alpha, beta >= 1` and `alpha + beta >= 3`, or a size with three or more primes, together with an exponent above `e_p` after the gcd, where the Hadamard window does not reach; the converse is taken up at the end of this section. The stage form still decides such a design when it applies: cut digits into windows of base `b_P`, the part of `b` on the primes of `|F|`, scale window `j` by `(b/b_P)^(K-j)`, and check An-Lai's conditions directly, as for the `pq` design `{0, 1} ⊕ 12{0, 1, 2}` at base 30, with windows `5{0, 1}` and `{0, 2, 4}` and window spectra `L^(0) = {0, 15}`, `L^(1) = {0, 5, 10}`.

**Verified.** Over every digit set with `0` at bases 2 to 16 of a size dividing the base, and at bases 17 to 30 of size `4`, `8`, `9` or of a size with two distinct prime factors dividing the base, leaving out the cells size 8 and 12 at base 24, size 9 at base 27, size 14 at base 28 and sizes 10 and 15 at base 30, 6898 designs tile at every level: 6844 carry a Hadamard triple `(b, F', L)` after the gcd, 54 are in stage form for `F` or `F'`, 40 of prime-power size and 14 of size 6 at base 30, and none is neither. So every one of them has a spectral measure, by Łaba-Wang Theorem 1.2 or An-Lai Theorem 2.4 (`census` of [lab/py/measure-spectra](../lab/py/measure-spectra/)).

**Proved.** The cells left out hold no design that tiles at every level and is neither in the Hadamard window after the gcd nor in stage form. Size 8 at base 24 is a prime-power size, where the construction of the theorem above is a stage form; base 27 is a prime-power base, where the T1 section puts every such design in the window. In the cells of a size with two distinct prime factors, where the claim is that no such design lies outside the window, every level tiling gives T1 and T2 for `F`, and the distinct cyclotomic factors of `F` have total degree at most `b - 1`. Size 12 at base 24 outside the window needs `c_2 >= 4` or `d >= 2` in `Z_2 = {c_1, c_2}`, `Z_3 = {d}`, and the factors at `2^(c_1)`, `2^(c_2)`, `3^d`, `2^(c_1) 3^d`, `2^(c_2) 3^d` have degree at least `27 > 23`. Size 14 at base 28 needs an exponent of `2` at least 3, and `Phi_8 Phi_7 Phi_56` has degree `34 > 27`, while `Phi_49` alone has 42. Size 15 at base 30 needs `Phi_9 Phi_5 Phi_45`, degree `34 > 29`, or `Phi_25 Phi_75`, more. Size 10 at base 30 has `Z_5 = {1}`, since `Phi_25 Phi_50` has degree 40; `Z_2 = {c}` with `c >= 4` costs `Phi_16 Phi_80`, degree 40; `c = 3` needs `Phi_8 Phi_5 Phi_40` and, for the T2 instance `200` at level 2, `Phi_20` (the other witness `Phi_200` has degree 80), degree `32 > 29`; `c = 2` needs `Phi_4 Phi_5 Phi_20` and, for the instance `100` at level 2, `Phi_10`, so the counts of `F` mod 20 have a transform carried by the orders 1 and 2, every element of `F` is even, and `F/2` has `Z_2 = {1}`, inside the window.

A falsifier would be a design whose every level tiles while every candidate spectrum fails completeness. At prime-power size and at `pq` digits none exists by the theorems above, and the census finds none at the other sizes it reaches. The `pq` proof stops at `p^alpha q^beta` with `alpha, beta >= 1` and `alpha + beta >= 3`: the fibre lemma then allows `p`-fibres and `q`-fibres together, since `p^alpha q^beta = p A + q B` has several solutions, and T1 gives several exponents to one prime, so dividing by the gcd no longer forces an exponent `1`, and the groups `x_j` need not be distinct mod `q`. The obstruction sits between windows: a stage-form sum `E_0 ⊕ E_1` has a product mask, so a factor `Phi_(p^c q^d)` with `p^c` from one window and `q^d` from another must already divide one window's set, while T2 at every level asks the digit set for such composite factors across windows.

**Conjecture.** If every level of `F` at base `b` tiles, then `F/gcd(F)` carries an integer Hadamard triple at base `b` or `F` or `F/gcd(F)` is in An-Lai stage form with windows of base `b_P`, and `mu_{b,F}` is spectral; checked on every design at bases 2 to 16 and on sizes 4, 8, 9 and two-prime sizes at bases 17 to 30.

The converse, from a spectral measure to tiling levels, is decided at four digits or fewer by An-He-Lai; at five and seven digits with `0` in `F` and levels that are sets by the covering bound of the sections above; at eleven and thirteen digits, with the same hypotheses, at every base whose primes admit a covering set below the size, the pairs theorem, and at bases 60 and 120 by the search, while the covering-bound route alone is refuted at base 60 by a set of bound 12 whose measure falls to a count of its residues; at other prime sizes it is proved when at most one non-prime-power order of `Z(F')` has its primes in `b`, and decided on the census; at other sizes the covering bound is a necessary condition, which at four digits alone gives the An-He-Lai necessity on the census.

The paper [Spectral Measures from Tiling Levels](../papers/spectral-tiling-levels.md) writes the limit-measure sections out for an outside reader: the window, the level shift with both spectra, the prime-power and `pq` theorems, the four-digit translation, the converse through the covering bound with the five-, seven-, eleven- and thirteen-digit theorems, the base-60 set that refutes the bound-alone route, and the residue count that decides it.

In dimension two the Hadamard triples of Dutkay, Haussermann and Lai give spectral self-affine measures in every dimension, so the window statement has a plane analogue, but the Coven-Meyerowitz conditions have no counterpart on `Z^2` to read the tiling side; the plane codes are not decided here.
