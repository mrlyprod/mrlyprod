Fix a base `b >= 3`. The set `R_b` holds the integers `n >= 1` whose base-`b` digits have no two equal neighbours. Write `A(x)` for the number of `n <= x` in `R_b`, `M(x)` for the sum of `mu(n)` over them and `psi_R(x)` for the sum of `Lambda(n)`. The set is read by a `(b+1)`-state automaton whose state is the last digit, it keeps `b - 1` digits in every state, as a one-missing-digit set does, and it is no digit design, so no theorem on a digit design at base `b` reaches it and neither does the Kummer theorem of [kummer](kummer.md): the automaton has no digit allowed in every state, no kernel holding two digits and no reset word. At even `b` a digit design at base `b^2` lies inside it and already carries primes from `b = 61270`, at a lower count (bullet The embedded design); what this page adds is the count `A(x)/log x` on `R_b` itself, the Mobius bound, and the bases `1153..61268` and every odd base `b >= 1153`. This page states the inputs that the dissection of [mobius](mobius.md) reads off any digit automaton, proves the theorem from them, and supplies them for `R_b`: blocks cut by the top digit, a units digit uniform up to a bounded error, a contraction over two steps, and a certificate from a run expansion of the transform below `1/5` from base `1153`. Every number below is printed by [lab/py/adjacent-digits](../lab/py/adjacent-digits/).

## The automaton

- **The automaton. Proved.** Read the digits `d_0, d_1, ..` of `n` from the units end. The states are a start state `*` and the digits `0..b-1`. From `*` every digit `d` is allowed and moves to state `d`; from state `c` the digit `d` is allowed exactly when `d != c`, and moves to state `d`. Then `n` lies in `R_b` exactly when its word of exact length, top digit nonzero, is accepted: the state is the digit just read, and the one refusal is a digit equal to it. In the language of [beneath](beneath.md) this is the width-2 rule of the memory dial at base `b` that forbids exactly the `b` windows `(d, d)`.
- **The mass. Proved.** Read from the top, an integer of exactly `L` digits in `R_b` has `b - 1` choices of top digit and `b - 1` choices at each lower position, so `R_b` holds exactly `(b-1)^L` integers of `L` digits and `A(b^L - 1) = (b-1)((b-1)^L - 1)/(b-2)`; the accepted words of length `L` with leading zeros allowed number `b (b-1)^(L-1)`. So `A(x)` grows like `x^(alpha_b)`, `alpha_b = log(b-1)/log b`, the dimension of a one-missing-digit set. The count matrix on the digit states is `J - I`, `J` the all-ones matrix, with `(J - I)^n = (((b-1)^n - (-1)^n)/b) J + (-1)^n I`, by induction from `J^2 = b J`: the words of length `k >= 1` with bottom digit `c` and top digit `c'` number `((b-1)^(k-1) - (-1)^(k-1))/b + (-1)^(k-1) [c = c']`. Its Perron root is `b - 1` with the uniform vector on both sides, its other eigenvalue `-1`, and it is primitive at `b >= 3`, since `(J - I)^2 = (b - 2) J + I` is positive.
- **Not a digit design. Proved.** `1` and `b` lie in `R_b` and `b + 1` does not. As words of length two they are `(1, 0)`, `(0, 1)` and `(1, 1)`, units digit first, and a digit design holding the first two holds the third. A prime missing one digit may repeat a digit, and a member of `R_b` may use every digit, so no theorem on a digit design at base `b` reaches `R_b`.
- **The embedded design. Proved.** At even `b = 2h` let `F = {a + b beta : h <= a < b, 0 <= beta < h}`, a digit design at base `b^2`. Its members read, at base `b`, a top-half digit at every even position and a bottom-half digit at every odd one, so two neighbours never agree, the top digit is nonzero, and `S_F` lies in `R_b`. Its transform is `abs(hat F(t)) = abs(D_h(t) D_h(b t))`, so its level-`i` grid sums at base `b^2` are the level-`2i` grid sums of `H = {0..h-1}` at base `b`; the half interval at even base on [mobius](mobius.md), section The digit progressions, certifies them below `1/5` from `b = 61270`, `F` keeps the pair `h, h + 1`, and `kappa_F = (b/phi(b))(phi(b)/2) h/h^2 = 1`. So the prime count of [coprime](coprime.md) already gives infinitely many primes in `R_b` at every even `b >= 61270`, at the lower count `x^(log(b/2)/log b + o(1))`. The odd-base version, through the intervals `I_fill` and `I_(fill-1)`, is not written here. **Verified**: at `b = 4, 6, 8, 10` the three-digit strings of `F` at base `b^2` all lie in `R_b`, `F` keeps `h` and `h + 1`, `kappa_F = 1` exactly, and `abs(hat F_i) = abs(hat H_(2i))` at `i = 1, 2` and twenty random `t` to `3.4 * 10^-11` (verb `count`).
- **Leading zeros and synchronisation. Proved.** No digit is allowed in every state, so a leading zero changes membership: the word of `n` with one zero placed above it is accepted, the top digit being nonzero, and with two zeros it is refused. So `R_b` below `b^L` is not the set of accepted words of length `L`, which is where the Kummer automaton, whose digit `0` is allowed in every state and returns to state `0`, differs; the blocks below carry the top digit at every scale. The automaton has no reset word, since a word whose first digit is `d` is refused from state `d`. Completed with a refusing sink it is synchronizing: the word `00` sends every state to the sink. `R_b` lies among the words that never reach it, the sparse support that [kummer](kummer.md) reads for its own completed automaton.
- **The check. Verified.** The automaton against direct membership below `b^6` at `b = 3, 4, 5, 10`, `0` mismatches and `597870` members at `b = 10`; the counts `(b-1)^L` and `b (b-1)^5` there; the witness `1, b, b + 1` at every `b = 3..39`; `(J - I)^n` in closed form at `b = 3..8`, `n = 0..8` (verb `count`).

## The theorem on a digit automaton

The Kummer theorem of [kummer](kummer.md) reads a carry automaton through four inputs and never through binomial coefficients. This section states those inputs for any digit automaton and proves the Mobius bound and the prime count from them; the sections after it supply each input for `R_b`.

- **The setting.** A digit automaton at base `b >= 3` is a finite set `Q` of states with a start state `*` and digit kernels `D_(q q')`, subsets of `{0..b-1}` disjoint in `q'` for each `q`, read from the units digit. Let `M(u)` be the `Q x Q` matrix with entries `M_(q q')(u) = sum_(d in D_(q q')) e(d u)`, `e(x) = exp(2 pi i x)`, and `Pi_i(u) = M(u) M(b u) ... M(b^(i-1) u)`, so that `e_q^T Pi_i(u) w` is the transform of the words of length `i` read from state `q`, weighted at their end state by `w`. A state keeps the union of its kernels; a state is live when some nonempty word reaches it from `*`.
- **The hypotheses.** Fix `fill >= 2`, constants `N_0, E_0 >= 1`, `kappa > 0` and a finite list `Omega` of end vectors with entries in `{0, 1}`. A set `S` of positive integers satisfies them when:
  - (H1) `*` keeps at most `b` digits and every live state at most `fill`.
  - (H2) For every `x >= b` with `L` digits, `S` below `x` is the disjoint union of at most `N_0` blocks `P b^j + W` at each scale `1 <= j < L` and at most `N_0` further points, where `W` lies in `[0, b^j)`, holds at most `fill^j` integers and has transform `e_*^T Pi_j(u) w` for some `w` in `Omega`; and `A_S(x) >= fill^(L-1)/N_0`.
  - (H3) There are `C_F >= 1` and `alpha_1 < 1/5` with `sum_(a < b^i) abs(e_q^T Pi_i(s + a/b^i) w) <= C_F fill^i b^(i alpha_1)` at every `i >= 0`, every real `s`, every state `q` and every `w` in `Omega` or a unit vector `e_(q')`.
  - (H4) There are `m_0 >= 1` and `c_E < 1` such that, whenever `v` lies at distance at least `3/(8b)` from `Z` and `v_2, .., v_(m_0)` are real, in the entrywise modulus of `M(v) M(v_2) ... M(v_(m_0))` every live row sums to at most `c_E fill^(m_0)` and the row of `*` to at most `c_E b fill^(m_0 - 1)`.
  - (H5) Every block of (H2) at `j >= 1` has `abs((b/phi(b)) #{t in W : gcd(t, b) = 1} - kappa abs(W)) <= E_0`.
- **The theorem. Proved.** If `S` satisfies (H1) to (H5), there are `C, c > 0` computable from `b` and the data with `abs(M_S(x)) <= C A_S(x) exp(-c sqrt(log x))` and `abs(psi_S(x) - kappa A_S(x)) <= C A_S(x) exp(-c sqrt(log x))` at every `x >= 2`, `A_S`, `M_S` and `psi_S` the count and the sums of `mu` and `Lambda` over `S` up to `x`. Proof. Run the unconditional dissection of [mobius](mobius.md) and its von Mangoldt form on [coprime](coprime.md), section The dissection for the von Mangoldt function, with the replacements below; every step they do not name reads neither a digit set nor the set and runs verbatim. Throughout, `norm(v^T B)_1 <= sum_q abs(v_q) r_q`, `r_q` the sum of row `q` of the entrywise modulus of `B`; a vector `e_*^T Pi_j(u)` at `j >= 1` is supported on live states, whose successors are live, so (H1) gives `norm(e_*^T Pi_j(u))_1 <= b fill^(j-1)` and every further factor costs at most `fill`.
  - The blocks. (H2) gives the split, and a block's sum is `y^(-1) sum_(a mod y) hat W(a/y) S_P(-a/y)`, `y = b^j`, by orthogonality mod `y`, which reads only that `W` lies in `[0, y)`. The scales below `L - K` weigh at most `N_0 sum_(j < L-K) fill^j <= 2 N_0 fill^(L-K) <= 2 N_0^2 fill^(1-K) A_S(x)`, and the further points at most `N_0 log x`.
  - Region A. The unshifted grid sum of a block is (H3) at `s = 0`, `q = *` and `w` in `Omega`.
  - The hybrid bound. Split `e_*^T Pi_k(t) w = e_*^T Pi_1(t) Pi_2(V_1 t) Pi_3(y t/V_2) w` over the lowest `i_1`, the middle and the top `i_2` positions, so `abs(hat W(t)) <= f(t) fill^(k - i_1 - i_2) g(y t/V_2)` with `f(t) = norm(e_*^T Pi_1(t))_1`, the sum over `q` of `abs(e_*^T Pi_1(t) e_q)`, and `g(v)` the largest `abs(e_q^T Pi_3(v) w)` over live `q`. Residues distinct mod `V_2` pay at most `sum_q sum_(a < V_2) abs(e_q^T Pi_3(a/V_2) w) <= abs(Q) C_F fill^(i_2) V_2^(alpha_1)`, (H3) from every start state. The bottom factor enters through `4 D^2 norm(f)_1 + norm(f')_1`: (H3) with unit end vectors, integrated over the shift, gives `norm(f)_1 <= abs(Q) V_1^(-1) C_F fill^(i_1) V_1^(alpha_1)`; differentiating the product one factor at a time, the factor at position `j` costs `b^j pi b (b - 1)`, since the digits of a row are distinct and sum to at most `b(b-1)/2`, the factors below it pay the integrated `norm(e_*^T Pi_j)_1 <= abs(Q) b^(-j) C_F fill^j b^(j alpha_1)` and those above `fill` each, so `norm(f')_1 <= pi b^2 abs(Q) C_F fill^(i_1 - 1) i_1 V_1^(alpha_1)`. A class `R(D, H)` pays at most `C fill^k (V_1 V_2)^(alpha_1) log y`, the shape of the hybrid bound there with one more power of `log x`, which the saving `Z^(-1/10)` of region B absorbs; region B, which asks `alpha_1 < 1/4`, runs verbatim.
  - Region C1. For `d = e m'` with `m' > 1` coprime to `b` and `l` nonzero mod `m'`, every window of `m_d` consecutive positions holds a position where `b^l l/d` sits at distance at least `1/(2b)` from `Z`, by the proof of Lemma A' on [coprime](coprime.md). At a C1 point `l/d + eta` the hypothesis `abs(eta) < y^(-2/3)/(4b(b-1))` of the perturbed Lemma A' moves that phase by less than `1/(4b(b-1)) <= 1/(8b)` at `l < 2k/3`, leaving it at least `3/(8b)` from `Z`. The good positions of every `m_0`-th window start at least `floor(floor(2k/(3 m_d))/m_0)` disjoint runs of `m_0` positions inside `[0, k)` once `k >= 3 m_0`; (H4) contracts each run and (H1) pays `fill` elsewhere, so `abs(hat W(l/d + eta)) <= b fill^(k-1) c_E^(floor(floor(2k/(3 m_d))/m_0))`. That is the bound the C1 bullet of mobius reads, with `c'` replaced by `c_E^(1/m_0)` up to a constant, and the assembly takes `C_0` with that replacement.
  - Region C2 reads only `abs(hat W) <= abs(W) <= fill^k`.
  - The main term. The principal characters of C2 give a block `(b/phi(b)) #{t in W : gcd(t, b) = 1}`, by the identity of coprime, which holds over any finite set of `t`, coprimality to `b` being read on `t` since `b` divides `y`. By (H5) that is `kappa abs(W)` within `E_0`, and over the blocks `kappa A_S(x)` within `N_0 E_0 log_b x` and `kappa` times the discarded scales. □
- **Kummer's theorem is the two-state case.** At `(3, 1)` take `Q = {0, 1}` with `*` the state `0`, `fill = (p+1)/2`, `Omega = {e_0, e_1}` and the blocks by state, at most `2 fill + 2` per scale. (H3) is its certificate, its top-factor bound covering both start states; (H4) holds with `m_0 = 1`, since its `l^infinity` input gives every row at most `fill - 2 + 2 cos(3 pi/(8p))` at distance `3/(8p)` from `Z`; (H5) holds with `kappa = p/(p+1)` and `E_0 = 1` by its main-term bullet. The one-missing-digit sets and the half interval of mobius are the one-state case, `Q = {*}` with `*` live, `m_0 = 1` and the consecutive pair.
- **What changes from the every-pair theorem of kummer.** That theorem is stated for Kummer automata and reads three features they share: the digit `0` is allowed in every state and returns to state `(0, 0)`, so leading zeros change nothing and the blocks are words of a fixed length; the pair `{0, 1}` sits inside one kernel of every state, a one-step contraction; and a units digit `0` resets every state to `(0, 0)`, which counts the block elements divisible by `p`. `R_b` has none of the three. Its blocks carry the top digit as an end vector, the integers shorter than `x` being blocks with a one-digit prefix; its contraction takes two steps, `m_0 = 2`; its main term is read off the exact counts of `(J - I)^n`. The number of states enters only the constants, through `abs(Q)` and `N_0`.

## The blocks and the units digit

- **The blocks. Proved.** Write `W_j^(f)` for the words of length `j` with no two equal adjacent digits and top digit not `f`, read as integers in `[0, b^j)`, with `W_0^(f) = {0}`; it holds `b (b-1)^(j-1) - (b-1)^(j-1) = (b-1)^j` integers at `j >= 1`, and its transform is `e_*^T Pi_j(u) (1 - e_f)`, `1` the all-ones vector on the digit states. Let `x` have `L` digits `x_j`. An element `n < x` of `R_b` with fewer than `L` digits has some top digit `f >= 1` at position `j < L - 1`, so it lies in `f b^j + W_j^(f)`, and every element of that block lies in `R_b`. An element with `L` digits first falls below `x` at a position `j` where its digit `f` is below `x_j`, the digits above copying those of `x`; that prefix has no two equal neighbours, `f != x_(j+1)` when `j < L - 1` and `f >= 1` when `j = L - 1`, so the element lies in `P b^j + W_j^(f)` with `P` the prefix followed by `f`, and the block lies in `R_b`. So `R_b` below `x` is the disjoint union of at most `b - 1` blocks of each kind at each scale `j < L` and the point `x` when `x` lies in `R_b`: (H2) holds with `fill = b - 1`, `N_0 = 2b`, the blocks at scale `0` being single points, at most `2b - 1` of them with `x`, `Omega = {1 - e_f : f < b}`, each block holding exactly `(b-1)^j` integers, and `A(x) >= (b-1)^(L-1)` from the integers of `L - 1` digits.
- **The units digit, and `kappa_b = 1`. Proved.** Let `U` be the units mod `b`, `phi = phi(b)`. By the word count of the first section the elements of `W_j^(f)` whose units digit lies in `U` number `sum_(c in U) sum_(c' != f) (((b-1)^(j-1) - (-1)^(j-1))/b + (-1)^(j-1) [c = c']) = phi (b-1)^j/b + (-1)^(j-1)(phi/b - [f in U])`, so `(b/phi) #{t in W_j^(f) : gcd(t, b) = 1} = (b-1)^j + (-1)^(j-1)(1 - (b/phi)[f in U])`, the block's size within `b/phi`. That is (H5) with `kappa = 1` and `E_0 = b/phi(b)`, at every base. The units digit of a block element is uniform up to that error because the uniform vector is the left Perron vector of `J - I`: every digit is refused in exactly one state, and the stationary law weighs the states equally. The correlation between the units digit and the digit above it changes the law of pairs and not the law of the units digit. A one-missing-digit set, which refuses the same digit `e_0` in every position, has `kappa = (b/phi)(phi - [e_0 in U])/(b - 1)` by the theorem of coprime, `10/9` at base `10` missing `5`; `R_b` refuses each digit in one state and reads `1`.
- **The check. Verified.** The block split against direct membership at `420` values of `x` at `b = 3, 4, 5, 7, 10`, at most `2(b-1)` blocks per scale, each of `(b-1)^j` integers; the units-digit identity exactly at all `450` cells `b = 3..12`, `j = 1..6`, every `f` (verb `count`).

## The two-step contraction

- **No kernel holds two digits. Proved.** Each digit of state `c` moves to its own state, so every entry of `M(u)` is a single exponential or `0` and the entrywise modulus of `M(u)` is `J - I` on the digit states, with the row of `*` all ones, at every `u`. No one-step bound beats `fill = b - 1`: the consecutive pair that Lemma A' contracts is present in every state at `b >= 4`, `{0, 1}` or `{2, 3}`, but its two digits lead to two states and never meet in one entry.
- **Two steps meet. Proved.** In `M(v) M(v')` the entry from state `c` to state `c''` sums over the middle digit: `e(c'' v') sum_(c' != c, c' != c'') e(c' v)`, of modulus `abs(S_b(v) - e(c v) - [c'' != c] e(c'' v))` with `S_b(v) = sum_(d < b) e(d v)`. So a digit row of the entrywise modulus sums to at most `(b - 1)(abs(S_b(v)) + 2) + abs(S_b(v)) + 1 <= b abs(S_b(v)) + 2b - 1`, and the row of `*`, whose entry to `c''` has modulus `abs(S_b(v) - e(c'' v))`, to at most `b abs(S_b(v)) + b`. At distance at least `3/(8b)` from `Z`, `abs(S_b(v)) = abs(sin(pi b v)/sin(pi v)) <= sigma_b = sin(3 pi/8)/sin(3 pi/(8b))`: on the main lobe the Dirichlet kernel decreases, and beyond it `abs(S_b(v)) <= csc(pi/b) < sigma_b`. So (H4) holds with `m_0 = 2` and `c_E = (b sigma_b + 2b - 1)/(b - 1)^2`, the row of `*` being at most `(b sigma_b + b)/(b(b - 1)) <= c_E` of `b fill`. At `b >= 1153`, `sin w >= w (1 - w^2/6)` gives `b sigma_b <= 0.784214 b^2 (1 + 10^-6)`, so `c_E <= (0.7842148 + 2/b)/(1 - 2/b) < 0.7874`; its limit is `(8/(3 pi)) sin(3 pi/8) = 0.784213`. The contraction is stronger than the pair's `1 - (2/fill)(1 - cos(pi/(4b)))`, which tends to `1`, because two steps cancel the whole digit sum `S_b(v)` and not one pair.
- **The check. Verified.** At `1600` random pairs with the first frequency at distance at least `3/(8b)` from `Z`, `b = 5, 10, 31, 101`, the two-step rows sit at most `0.948152` of `b abs(S_b(v)) + 2b - 1`; `c_E < 1` and decreasing at every `b = 19..5000`, `1.000923` at `18` and `0.787312` at `1153`; at `b = 31`, level `12`, `332` reduced `l/d` over `11` moduli carrying a prime outside `b`, each plus a rational perturbation inside the hypothesis of Lemma A' and phases computed exactly, `abs(hat W_k(l/d + eta))` sits at most `2.417 * 10^-9` of `b (b-1)^(k-1) c_E^(floor(floor(2k/(3 m_d))/2))` (verb `check`).

## The run expansion

- **The product. Proved.** With `D(u)` the diagonal matrix of `e(c u)`, `c < b`, the transform of the words of length `i >= 1` with end weight `w` on the digit states is `T_i(u; w) = 1^T D(u) (J - I) D(b u) (J - I) ... (J - I) D(b^(i-1) u) w`, which is `e_*^T Pi_i(u) w`: the row of `*` is `1^T D(u)` and a digit row of `M(u)` is the same row of `(J - I) D(u)`. Since every entry is a single exponential, the triangle inequality across the states, which [kummer](kummer.md) pays once per digit in its transfer inequality, gives here only the trivial bound; the cancellation sits between the paths and is read by expanding `J - I`.
- **The run expansion. Proved.** Put `rep_r = (b^r - 1)/(b - 1)`. Then `T_i(u; w) = sum_(r = 1..i-1) (-1)^(r-1) S_b(rep_r u) T_(i-r)(b^r u; w) + (-1)^(i-1) sum_c w_c e(c rep_i u)`. Proof. `1^T D(u) J = S_b(u) 1^T`, so expanding the first `J - I` gives `T_i(u; w) = S_b(u) T_(i-1)(b u; w) - 1^T D(u + b u) (J - I) D(b^2 u) ... w`, and the second term has the same form with `D(u)` replaced by `D(rep_2 u)`; induct on `r`. □ So `T_i` is a signed sum over the compositions of the `i` positions into runs, a run of length `r` starting at position `j` contributing `S_b(rep_r b^j u)` with sign `(-1)^(r-1)`: the transform of the words in which that run repeats one digit, the sign correcting the overcount, an inclusion-exclusion over the forbidden equalities.
- **Full blocks merge. Proved.** Consecutive runs of length `1` at positions `j..j+l-1` multiply to `prod S_b(b^m u) = S_(b^l)(b^j u)`, the transform of the full interval `[0, b^l)`. Group each maximal stretch of runs of length `1` into one full block. Then `T_i(u; 1)` is a signed sum over the sequences of segments covering the `i` positions in which a segment is a full block of length `l >= 1` or a long run of length `r >= 2` and no two full blocks are adjacent, the segment at position `j` contributing `S_(b^l)(b^j u)` or `(-1)^(r-1) S_b(rep_r b^j u)`. The merge is what keeps the cost of a full block linear in `l log b`, as in the digit-uniform chain of [coprime](coprime.md), where runs of the full digit set merge in the same way.
- **The check. Verified.** The run expansion and the matrix product meet the direct transform at nine random frequencies at every level up to `7`, `6`, `5`, `4`, `4` at `b = 3, 4, 5, 7, 10`, largest error `3.39 * 10^-10` (verb `identity`).

## The certificate

- **The chain. Proved.** Let `phi_l` be the supremum over real `tau` of `sum_(q < b^l) abs(S_(b^l)((tau + q)/b^l))`, the shifted grid sum of a full block, and `rho_r` that of `sum_(q < b^r) abs(S_b((tau + q)/b^r))`. Write `Sigma_i(s) = sum_(a < b^i) abs(T_i(s + a/b^i; 1))`. Put `c_0 = g_0 = 1`, `g_i = sum_(r = 2..i) rho_r c_(i-r)`, `h_i = sum_(l = 1..i) phi_l g_(i-l)` and `c_i = g_i + h_i`. Then `Sigma_i(s) <= c_i` at every `i` and every real `s`. Proof. By the triangle inequality over the segment sequences of the run expansion it suffices to bound the grid sum of one product, and that by peeling its lowest segment, of length `m`. Induct on the number of segments, a full block being followed by a long run or nothing. □ So if `x = 1/z` has `P(x)(1 + Phi(x)) <= 1`, with `Phi(x) = sum_(l >= 1) phi_l x^l` and `P(x) = sum_(r >= 2) rho_r x^r`, then `g_i <= sum_r rho_r z^(i-r)(1 + sum_l phi_l z^(-l)) <= z^i` by induction and `Sigma_i(s) <= (1 + Phi(1/z)) z^i`.

## The theorem on R_b

- **The theorem. Proved.** At every base `b >= 1153` there are `C, c > 0` computable from `b` alone with `abs(M(x)) <= C A(x) exp(-c sqrt(log x))` and `abs(psi_R(x) - A(x)) <= C A(x) exp(-c sqrt(log x))` at every `x >= 2`: the Mobius function cancels on the integers with no two equal adjacent base-`b` digits against their own count, and the sum of `Lambda` over them is their count, with constant `kappa_b = 1`. So at every such base there are infinitely many primes with no two equal adjacent digits, and up to `x` they number `(A(x)/log x)(1 + O(1/log x))`. Proof. The theorem on a digit automaton, with its hypotheses checked one by one. (H1): `*` keeps `b` digits and every digit state `b - 1 = fill`, by the first section. (H2): the blocks bullet, with `N_0 = 2b` and `Omega = {1 - e_f}`. (H3): the certificate, with `C_F = (C_1 + C_e)/(1 - y) + 1` and `alpha_1 < 1/5` from `1153` by the wall. (H4): the two-step contraction, `m_0 = 2` and `c_E < 0.7874`. (H5): the units digit, `kappa = 1` and `E_0 = b/phi(b)`. For the count of primes, the prime powers up to `x` weigh `O(x^(1/2) log x)`, below the error since `A(x) >= (b-1)^(L-1) > (x/b)^(alpha_b)` with `alpha_b = log(b - 1)/log b > 1/2`, so the sum of `log p` over the primes of `R_b` up to `x` is `A(x)(1 + O(exp(-c sqrt(log x))))`; partial summation against `A(t) = O(t^(alpha_b))` gives the count. □
- **What the theorem reads off `R_b`.** The certificate below `1/5` at `s = 0` for region A, at every shift and from every state for the hybrid bound, `alpha_1 < 1/4` for region B, the two-step contraction for C1, and the block counts `(b-1)^j` for C2, the mass floor and the units-digit counts for the main term. The rule between neighbouring digits enters through nothing else.
- **What stays open.** Below `1153` the certificate does not reach `1/5`, and at base `10` it reads `0.5155`, so the primes of `R_10` are not proved infinite here. The exact grid sums of the readings below sit well under the chain, which pays the triangle inequality between the segment sequences of the run expansion, so a certificate that reads the long runs together, or a transfer operator on the phase of the open run, moves the wall down; neither is written here. The theorem on a digit automaton carries any other rule between neighbouring digits, such as `abs(d_(i+1) - d_i) >= 2`, once its certificate and contraction are supplied; none is computed here. **Conjecture.** At every base `b >= 3`, `psi_R(x)` is asymptotic to `A(x)`: the readings at `b = 3, 4, 5, 7, 10` at `x = 10^8` sit within `0.0066` of `1`, the farthest `0.993479` at `b = 3`, while at `10^6` base `3` reads `1.0425` (verb `primes`).

## What is in print

- **The search.** Queries for primes with no two equal adjacent digits, primes avoiding a digit block or pattern, digital blocks along primes, primes in sparse automatic sets, primes in sets cut by a subshift of finite type or a forbidden adjacent pair, and primes with restricted digits beyond missing digits, through the arXiv listings to the present, found no source on primes or Mobius sums on `R_b`, or on any set defined by forbidden adjacent digit pairs, at the scale `A(x)/log x`. What the search found is below, each read at source as stated.
- **The sequence. Verified.** [A050757](https://oeis.org/A050757) lists the primes with no pair of consecutive equal decimal digits, the primes of `R_10`; the entry, read whole, has no references and no comments.
- **Missing digits. Verified.** [Maynard 2022](https://doi.org/10.1093/imrn/rnab002), Theorem 1.1, read at source: the asymptotic for `Lambda` over one missing digit at base `q > 2 * 10^6` with a log-power error, which the text says holds uniformly when the excluded digit depends on the position; `R_b` excludes a digit that depends on the neighbouring digit, which that statement does not cover.
