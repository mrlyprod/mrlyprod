The [design ladder](design-ladder.md) reads a design at `n` and catches each of its three rungs with one test of its battery: one base with a kernel test, two bases with the dilation square, rounds with a base mismatch or an exercised nonlinearity. A design obeys `x(bn) = x(n)` exactly, and its battery compares `x(n)` with `x(n + h)`, `x(Bn + r)` and `x(b^i n)`. A reading reads the design along a polynomial or along the primes, `y(n) = x(P(n))`, and it can leave the automatic class: Thue-Morse along the squares is normal ([Drmota, Mauduit and Rivat 2019](../refs.md)), so no finite automaton generates it. Yet `(2n)^2 = 4n^2` and `t(4m) = t(m)`, so `t((2n)^2) = t(n^2)` at every `n`, and the dilation test returns `1` on a normal sequence. Normal is not random. This chapter asks which readings escape all three tests and answers in three parts. A battery pair is a near-head of a polynomial reading, `P(psi(n)) - b^L P(phi(n))` a constant at the design's base `b`, exactly when the polynomial is a monomial plus a constant centred at the pair's fixed point and the pair's ratio is a power of `b` (the rigidity lemma), and there its correlation converges to a local constant computed from the base-`b` digits of its values. At every other pair the reading is free, and the two free readings of Thue-Morse run are still caught. Its correlations carry mass at one third, the sum of `C_t(h)` over the multiples of 3 below `2^k` being exactly `(16/45)(3/2)^k + 8/15` at even `k` and `+ 7/15` at odd `k`, and the conjecture is that a pair whose remainder runs over multiples of 3 leaks that mass at a rate near `N^(-0.415)`, slower than the noise. Rudin-Shapiro along `n^3 + n` fails too, with a white design, so that mass is not the only way to fail. Rudin-Shapiro passes along `n^2 + n` and along the primes to `N = 2^26`. That is the candidate for a fourth rung, and it stays a conjecture, since a proof needs a joint normality that no theorem gives.

The generator is the study `lab/rs/readings`, verbs `identities`, `battery`, `local`, `falsify` and `leak`. The figure draws two readings as pairs of square blocks, `y(n)` on the left and `y(2n)` on the right for `n < 1024`: Thue-Morse along the squares above, where the right block is a copy of the left, and Rudin-Shapiro along `n^2 + n` below, where it is not.

## The objects

Three designs, as signs `+-1`. Thue-Morse `t(n) = (-1)^(s_2(n))`, `s_2` the number of binary ones; Rudin-Shapiro `r(n) = (-1)^(e(n))`, `e` the number of adjacent pairs `11` in binary; and the ternary parity `t3(n) = (-1)^(d(n))`, `d` the number of ternary digits equal to `2`. The first and the last are signed designs in the ladder's sense, so `x(b^L m + s) = x(m) x(s)` at every `0 <= s < b^L`, with `b = 2` for `t` and `b = 3` for `t3`; Rudin-Shapiro reads one pair across the boundary, `r(2^L m + s) = r(m) r(s) (-1)^(m_0 s_(L-1))`. All three obey `x(bm) = x(m)`.

A reading is an integer polynomial `P` with `P(n) >= 0` for `n >= 0`, or the consecutive primes, `p_0 = 2`; the stream is `y(n) = x(P(n))`. The battery is the ladder's: the shift test over `1 <= h <= 64`, the dilation test over the 44 pairs `(n, Bn + r)`, `2 <= B <= 9`, `r < B`, the kernel test over the two pairs `(bn, b^2 n)`, `b = 2, 3`, the dilation square `y(n) y(2n) y(3n) y(6n)` and the LZ78 rate. A battery pair is one of the 46 pairs of affine maps `(phi, psi)` that the dilation and kernel tests compare.

- A battery pair is a **near-head** of the reading at the design's base `b` when `P(psi(n)) = b^L (P(phi(n)) + q) + s` as polynomials, for integers `L >= 1`, `q` and `0 <= s < b^L`.
- A reading is **free** at a pair that is not a near-head, and free when no battery pair is a near-head.
- A pair is **exact** when `y(psi(n)) y(phi(n))` is constant in `n`, and **twisted** when it is periodic in `n` and not constant.
- A **contraction** is an integer affine map `phi(n) = lambda n + mu`, `lambda >= 2`. The head scan of the study reads `y(phi^j n) y(phi^(j+1) n)` over `n < N` for `lambda` in `{2, 3, 4, 8, 9}`, `mu < lambda` and `j = 0, 1, 2`, and reports the products that are periodic with period at most 81. The battery's own kernel test is the contraction `bn` at depth `j = 1`.

## Monomials

**If `x(bm) = x(m)` at every `m` and `P(n) = c n^k`, then `y(bn) = y(n)` at every `n`. Proved.** `P(bn) = b^k P(n)`, and `x(bm) = x(m)` applies `k` times. So the dilation test at `(b, 0)` and the kernel test at `(bn, b^2 n)` compare a sequence with itself and return `1` on every window where `y` is nonconstant. The square is `1` at every `n` as well, at either base: at `b = 2` the factors pair as `y(2n) = y(n)` and `y(6n) = y(3n)`, at `b = 3` as `y(3n) = y(n)` and `y(6n) = y(2n)`.

The battery at `N = 2^15` reads `t(n^2)` as mean `-0.0150`, shift `0.0280` at `h = 15`, dilation `1.0000` at `(2, 0)`, kernel `1.0000`, square `+1.0000`, and LZ78 rates `1.297, 1.249, 1.214` bits per bit at `2^14, 2^16, 2^18` against the control's `1.215` at `2^18`. Drmota, Mauduit and Rivat prove more than normality: their Theorem 2 bounds `sum_(n<N) e((1/2) sum_l alpha_l s_2((n + l)^2))` by `N^(1 - eta)` for every nonzero `alpha`, so every shift correlation of `t(n^2)` tends to `0`, with an exponent `eta` that is never given a value. The compressor sees a normal sequence, as it must; one dilation sees everything. The squares have been offered as the remedy for the low complexity of automatic sequences ([Sun and Winterhof 2019](../refs.md), [Popoli 2020](../refs.md), [Merai and Winterhof 2022](../refs.md)), measured there by the correlation measures of [Mauduit and Sarkozy 1998](../refs.md), which compare `x(n + d_1)` with `x(n + d_2)` and are blind to `n -> 2n`; a dilation test is not found in the sources read. [Mauduit and Rivat 2015](../refs.md) use `b(2^kappa n) = b(n)` as a tool, to read off their Fourier property. The digital functions of [Mullner 2018](../refs.md) are sums of `F` over every window of digits, the windows indexed by all of `Z`, with `F(0, ..., 0) = 0`, so each has `b(qn) = b(n)`, and by the theorem above each of his normal readings `b(n^2) mod m'` keeps `y(qn) = y(n)`: across his whole class, normal along the squares and caught by one dilation.

## The rigidity lemma

**Let `P` have degree `k >= 2` and `chi(u) = lambda u + mu` with `lambda > 0` rational, `lambda != 1`, and fixed point `u_0 = mu/(1 - lambda)`. Then `P(chi(u)) - lambda^k P(u)` is constant if and only if `P(u) = q (u - u_0)^k + q_0`, and the constant is then `q_0 (1 - lambda^k)`. Proved.** Write `Q(w) = P(u_0 + w) = sum_i q_i w^i`. Since `chi(u_0 + w) = u_0 + lambda w`, `P(chi(u)) - lambda^k P(u) = sum_i q_i (lambda^i - lambda^k) w^i` with `w = u - u_0`, and `lambda^i != lambda^k` for `i != k`, so every `q_i` with `0 < i < k` vanishes. Call such a `P` a centred monomial plus a constant, with centre `u_0`.

**A battery pair `(phi, psi)` is a near-head of a polynomial reading of degree `k >= 2` at a prime base `b` exactly when `P` is a centred monomial plus a constant whose centre is the fixed point of `chi = psi phi^(-1)` and whose slope ratio `lambda` has `lambda^k` a power of `b`. For the dilation `(B, r)` this asks for the centre `-r/(B - 1)` and `B` a power of `b`; for the kernel pair `(bn, b^2 n)` it asks for the centre `0`. Proved.** `P(psi(n)) - b^L P(phi(n))` is `(P(chi(u)) - b^L P(u))` at `u = phi(n)`, and `phi` is a bijection of the rationals, so it is constant exactly when the lemma applies with `lambda^k = b^L`, the ratio of the leading coefficients. The constant is then `b^L q + s` with `0 <= s < b^L`. For the dilation `chi(u) = Bu + r` and `B^k = b^L`; for the kernel `chi(u) = bu`.

So the battery's two-point tests see exactly the centres `0, -1/(B - 1), ..., -1` with `B` a power of the base, and everything else is free. The centre decides, not the degree: `n^2 + n = (n + 1/2)^2 - 1/4` has its centre at `-1/2`, a ternary integer and not a binary one, and is a near-head of `t3` at `(3, 1)` and `(9, 4)` and free for `t` and `r`.

**For `b` prime, let `P` be a centred monomial plus a constant with centre `u_0 = p/d` in lowest terms. An integer contraction `phi(n) = lambda n + (1 - lambda) u_0` with `lambda` a power of `b` exists exactly when `d` is prime to `b`, and along it a reading of `t` or `t3` keeps `y(phi^(j+1) n) = eps_j y(phi^j n)` at every `n >= max(0, u_0)` for every large `j`. Proved.** The map has integer coefficients exactly when `d` divides `b^i - 1`, which some `i` achieves when `d` is prime to `b`, and none when `b` divides `d`. Then `P(phi^j(n)) = lambda^(kj) (P(n) - q_0) + q_0`, so `m_j = P(phi^j(n))` is congruent modulo `b^(ikj)` to an integer `c_j` that does not depend on `n`, and `P(phi^(j+1)(n)) = b^(ik) (m_j + q) + s` with `q` and `s` not depending on `j`. Here `c_j` is the residue of the integer `q_0 (1 - lambda^(kj))`, so `c_j + q` lies in `[0, b^(ikj))` for every large `j`, and then the addition `m_j + q` leaves the digits above the window alone: `x(m_j + q) x(m_j) = x(c_j + q) x(c_j)`, a constant sign. The battery's kernel test is the case `u_0 = 0`, `lambda = b`, `j = 1`: it sees `n^2 + 1`, whose window is wide enough at `j = 1`, but not `n^2 + 5`, whose identity starts at `j = 2`, nor `(n + 1)^2 + 1`, whose centre is `-1`. The head scan finds all three.

| reading | centre | `t` | `r` | `t3` |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `n^2`, `n^3` | `0` | exact at `(2, 0)`, `(4, 0)`, `(8, 0)` and the kernel | the same | exact at `(3, 0)`, `(9, 0)` and the kernel |
| `n^2 + 1` | `0` | `0` at the dilations, kernel exact | exact everywhere | `-1/3` at the dilations, kernel exact |

The linear reading `n` of `t` and `t3` is exact at every dilation by a power of its base; `r` along `n` is exact at 8 of its 15 near-head pairs and twisted with mean `0` at the other 7, since `r(2m + 1) = (-1)^m r(m)`.

## Local constants

At a near-head the reading is not free, and its correlation has a limit that the digits decide.

**Let `x` be `t` or `t3` at its base `b`, or `r` at `b = 2`, let `F` be a nonconstant integer polynomial with `F(n) >= 0` and `q` an integer. The mean of `x(F(n) + q) x(F(n))` over `n < N` converges. For each `J`, call a class of `n` modulo `b^J` (modulo `2^(J+1)` for `r`) good when `a = F(n) mod b^J` has `0 <= a + q < b^J`; on a good class the product is the constant `x(a + q) x(a)`, times `(-1)^(h ((a + q)_(J-1) + a_(J-1)))` for `r`, `h` the binary digit `J` of `F(n)`. With `kappa_J` the sum of these constants over the good classes divided by the number of classes, and `eps_J` the share of bad classes, the limit lies in `[kappa_J - eps_J, kappa_J + eps_J]`, and `eps_J -> 0`. Proved.** On a good class the addition does not reach the digits above the window, so the product depends on `F(n)` modulo the window, which depends on `n` modulo the period, `F` having integer coefficients. Over a whole number of periods the mean lies in the interval exactly, and over any `N` within `2 b^(J+1)/N` of it, so every limit point of the means lies in every interval. A bad class has `F(n) + e = 0` modulo `b^J` for one of `abs(q)` values of `e`. The `n` in the `b`-adic integers with `F(n) + e = 0` modulo `b^J` form a decreasing sequence of open and closed sets, `b` prime, whose intersection is the finite set of roots of `F + e`, of Haar measure `0`; their measures, the shares of bad classes, tend to `0`, and the intervals shrink to one point.

At a near-head `P(psi(n)) = b^L (m + q) + s` with `m = P(phi(n))`, so `y(psi(n)) = x(s) x(m + q)` for a signed design, times `(-1)^((m + q)_0 s_(L-1))` for `r`, so for `t` and `t3` the raw mean of `y(psi(n)) y(phi(n))` tends to `x(s)` times the local constant of `F = P o phi`. For `r` the boundary factor `(-1)^((m + q)_0 s_(L-1))` varies with `n` when the top digit of `s` is `1`; it stays inside the class sum, which the same proof bounds and `local` computes. `r` along `n` at `(2, 1)` is the example: `r(2n + 1) r(n) = (-1)^n`, mean `0`, where `x(s) = r(1) = 1`. When `eps_J = 0` from some `J` on, every class is good, and the product is periodic in `n`: a twisted identity, exact when the period is `1`. The Pearson coefficient of the battery also subtracts the product of the two means; where those means tend to `0` it has the same limit, and the run prints both.

`local` takes every near-head battery pair of 13 polynomial readings (the seven polynomials of the list and the six probes `n^2 + 2`, `n^2 + 3`, `n^2 + 4`, `n^2 + 5`, `(n + 1)^2 + 1`, `n^3 + n`) under the three designs, 143 pairs: 91 are exact, `+-1` with `eps_J = 0`; 29 lie strictly between `0` and `1`; 23 are `0`. It computes `kappa_J` at `J = 20` (`13` at base 3) and the raw mean over `N = 2^22` (`3^14`), a whole number of periods, so the raw mean must fall inside the enclosure, and every one does. Two examples:

- `t` along `n^2 + 4`: `P(2n) = 4(P(n) - 3)`, so the dilation `(2, 0)` compares `t(n^2 + 4)` with `t(n^2 + 1)`. For odd `n`, `n^2 = 1 mod 8` and the two differ by one binary one; for `n = 2a`, `t(4a^2 + 1) t(4a^2 + 4) = (-1)^a`. The product is `+1` at `n = 0 mod 4` and `-1` otherwise, a twisted identity of period 4 with mean `-1/2`; the battery prints `0.5002` at `(2, 0)`.
- `t3` along `n^2 + 5` reads `0.555556` at `(3, 0)` with `eps_J = 2.51e-6`, a constant that no identity explains: the carries escape the window on a set of classes whose share shrinks with `J`.

The same theorem with `F(n) = n^2`, at `J = 26` digits (`16` at base 3), gives the pair law of a design along the squares, the limit of the mean of `x(n^2 + c) x(n^2)`:

| `c` | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6 | 7 | 8 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `t` | `0` | `-1` | `1` | `-1/2` | `-1/2` | `1/2` | `1/6` | `-2/3` |
| `r` | `1` | `-1/2` | `0` | `1/2` | `1/2` | `0` | `0` | `-1/4` |
| `t3` | `-1/3` | `-1/3` | `1/3` | `1/3` | `-5/9` | `-5/9` | `-1/9` | `1/3` |
| `C_t(c)` | `-1/3` | `-1/3` | `1/3` | `-1/3` | `0` | `1/3` | `0` | `-1/3` |

The cells of `t` and `r` at `c <= 6` and of `t3` at `c = 1` are exact, `eps_J = 0`. The other cells are not proved equal to the rational shown: `local` prints for each the bound `abs(kappa_J - p/q) + eps_J` on its distance from the limit, at most `1.40e-7`, and `7.95e-8` for `t` at `c = 7` and `8`. The last row is Thue-Morse's own correlation. Along `floor(n^c)`, `1 < c < 7/5`, the pairs keep the Thue-Morse law, `-1/3` at offset `1` ([Deshouillers, Drmota and Morgenbesser 2012](../refs.md)). Along the squares they do not, at any of the eight offsets: a square is `0` or `1` modulo `4`, its binary digit `1` is always `0`, and adding `2` always adds one binary one. The squares are not equidistributed modulo powers of 2, and the pair law is the Thue-Morse law read through their 2-adic distribution. That such limits exist is also a case of the densities of automatic sequences along the squares ([Adamczewski, Drmota and Mullner 2022](../refs.md)), which are proved there in the logarithmic sense; the argument here gives the natural density, because the product is locally constant off a set of classes of vanishing measure.

## Identities between readings

Every identity below is a statement about signs, proved by one look at the low digits, and checked by `identities` at every `n < 2^24` with 0 violations.

- `t(n^2 + 1) = -(-1)^n t(n^2)`: for even `n`, `n^2 = 0 mod 4` and adding `1` sets digit `0`; for odd `n`, `n^2 = 1 mod 8` and `...001 + 1 = ...010` keeps the number of ones.
- `t(n^2 + 2) = -t(n^2)`: the digit `1` of a square is `0`. And `t(n^2 + 3) = t(n^2)`: for even `n` two ones are set, and for odd `n`, `8a + 1 + 3 = 8a + 4` trades one one for one.
- `t(2n^2 + 1) = -t(n^2)`: the digits of `n^2` followed by a `1`.
- `t((2n + 1)^2) = -t(n^2 + n)`, since `(2n + 1)^2 = 4(n^2 + n) + 1`. So the odd half of the squares stream is the stream along `n^2 + n`, the even half is the squares stream itself, and the squares stream is the `n^2 + n` stream spread over the odd parts: `t((2^a (2m + 1))^2) = -t(m^2 + m)`.
- `r(n^2 + 1) = r(n^2)`: a square is `0 mod 4` or `1 mod 8`, and `...00 + 1` and `...001 + 1 = ...010` create and destroy no pair `11`. And `r(2n^2 + 1) = (-1)^n r(n^2)` from `r(2m + 1) = (-1)^m r(m)` and `n^2 = n mod 2`.
- `r((2n + 1)^2) = r(n^2 + n)` from `r(4m + 1) = r(m)`.
- `t3(n^2 + 1) = -t3(n^2)` unless `3` divides `n`: the last ternary digit of a square is `0` or `1`, and adding `1` turns `1` into `2`.
- `t3` along `n^2 + n` has `y(3n + 1) = -y(n)`, since `(3n + 1)(3n + 2) = 9(n^2 + n) + 2`; along `(2n + 1)^2`, `y(3n + 1) = y(n)`, since `(6n + 3)^2 = 9(2n + 1)^2`; along `2n^2 + 1`, `y(3n) = y(9n) = t3(2n^2)`, since `2(3n)^2 + 1 = 9(2n^2) + 1` and `2(9n)^2 + 1 = 81(2n^2) + 1`.

So the eight readings of Thue-Morse, with the probes `n^2 + 2` and `n^2 + 3`, are five streams up to sign and twist: `n`, the squares (with `n^2 + 1`, `n^2 + 2`, `n^2 + 3` and `2n^2 + 1`), `n^2 + n` (with `(2n + 1)^2`), `n^3` and the primes. For Rudin-Shapiro `n^2 + 1` collapses onto `n^2` exactly.

## Out of the automatic class

The ladder calls a sequence `b`-automatic when its `b`-kernel, the set of the subsequences `n -> x(b^k n + r)`, `0 <= r < b^k`, is finite.

**A `b`-automatic sequence over an alphabet `A` with a kernel of `kappa` elements has at most `b k abs(A)^(2 kappa)` factors of length `k`. Proved.** Take `j` with `b^(j-1) < k <= b^j`. A factor of length `k` starting at `b^j q + rho`, `rho < b^j`, lies in `[b^j q, b^j (q + 2))`, and `x(b^j q + s)` for `s < b^j` is the kernel element `n -> x(b^j n + s)` read at `q`. So the factor is fixed by `rho` and by the two vectors of all kernel elements read at `q` and at `q + 1`: at most `b^j abs(A)^(2 kappa) < b k abs(A)^(2 kappa)` choices. This is the linear complexity of automatic sequences that the ladder cites unread, proved in the ladder's own terms.

**`t(n^2)` is not 2-automatic and `t3(n^2)` is not 3-automatic. Proved.** Since `(2^k n + 1)^2 = 2^(k+1) (2^(k-1) n^2 + n) + 1`, the kernel element `n -> t((2^k n + 1)^2)` is `-t(2^(k-1) n^2 + n)`. At `n = 2^(k-1) + 1`, `k >= 3`, `2^(k-1) n^2 + n = 2^(3k-3) + 2^(2k-1) + 2^k + 1` has four ones; for `k' > k`, `n < 2^(k'-1)` and `2^(k'-1) n^2 + n` has the ones of `n^2` above those of `n`, `3 + 2 = 5`. So the kernel elements at `k >= 3` are pairwise distinct and the kernel is infinite. In base 3, `n -> t3((3^k n + 1)^2) = t3(3^k n^2 + 2n)`; at `n = (3^k + 1)/2`, `2n = 3^k + 1` and the value is `t3(n^2 + 1)`, while for `k' > k` it is `t3(n^2) t3(3^k + 1) = t3(n^2)`; since `n = 2 mod 3`, `n^2` ends in the ternary digit `1` and `t3(n^2 + 1) = -t3(n^2)`. `identities` splits all 703 pairs `3 <= k < k' <= 40` and all 276 pairs `1 <= k < k' <= 24`.

**Every reading of `t` along a polynomial of degree at least 2, and the readings of `r` along `n^2`, `n^2 + 1`, `2n^2 + 1`, `n^2 + n` and `(2n + 1)^2`, are automatic in no base. Proved.** For `t` this is a case of [Shen 2022](../refs.md), Theorem 1: every block occurs in `t(H(n))` for every `H` of degree at least 2 with `H(N)` in `N`, against the linear bound; it covers `n^3` and `n^3 + n` as well. For `r` it follows from the normality of `r(n^2)` ([Mullner 2018](../refs.md), Theorem 1.4 with `q = 2`, `m' = 2`), which gives all `2^k` factors of length `k`. A reading equal to the squares stream times a periodic sign is automatic exactly when the squares stream is, which covers `n^2 + 1` and `2n^2 + 1`. For `w(n) = r(n^2 + n) = r((2n + 1)^2)`, the squares stream is `y(2m) = y(m)` and `y(2m + 1) = w(m)`, so a factor of `y` of length `2k` is fixed by its parity, a factor of `y` of length `k` and one of `w` of length `k`: `4^k <= 2 . 2^k p_w(k)`, and `w` has at least `2^(k-1)` factors of length `k`. The same argument from the normality of `t(n^2)` ([Drmota, Mauduit and Rivat 2019](../refs.md), Theorem 1) recovers the five quadratic readings of `t`. For the ternary parity the normality theorem does not apply, since its hypothesis `gcd(q - 1, m') = 1` fails at `q = 3`, `m' = 2`, and only the 3-kernel witness is proved. Along the cubes, `t(n^3)` has density `1/2` ([Spiegelhofer 2023](../refs.md)), and its normality is open.

## The leak at one third

The free readings are not all alike, and the difference is in the design.

**Let `U_r(H)` be the sum of `C_t(h)` over `0 <= h < H`, `h = r mod 3`. Then `U(2H) = M U(H) + b(H)` with `M = [[1, -1/2, -1/2], [-1/2, -1/2, 1], [-1/2, 1, -1/2]]`, and the sum of `C_t(h)` over the multiples of 3 below `2^k` is exactly `(16/45)(3/2)^k + 8/15` at even `k >= 2` and `(16/45)(3/2)^k + 7/15` at odd `k`, so their mean is `(16/15)(2^k)^(-log2(4/3)) + O(2^(-k))`, `log2(4/3) = 0.415`, against `2/(3 . 2^k)` over all lags. Proved.** The Thue-Morse correlations obey `C_t(2q) = C_t(q)` and `C_t(2q + 1) = -(C_t(q) + C_t(q + 1))/2` (the ladder's block recursion, `C_t(0) = 1`, `C_t(1) = -1/3`). Splitting `h < 2H` by parity, `2q = r mod 3` puts `q = 2r mod 3` and `2q + 1 = r` puts `q = 2r - 2`, which gives the rows of `M`; the boundary vector is `b(H) = (-[H = 2] C_t(H)/2, -[H = 1] C_t(H)/2, 1/2 - [H = 0] C_t(H)/2)`, residues modulo 3, the `1/2` being `C_t(0)` dropped from the shifted sum. `M` is symmetric with eigenvalues `0`, `3/2`, `-3/2` and eigenvectors `(1, 1, 1)`, `(2, -1, -1)`, `(0, 1, -1)`. At `H = 2^k`, `C_t(2^k) = -1/3` and `2^k = 1` or `2 mod 3` with the parity of `k`, so `b` alternates between `(0, 1/6, 1/2)` and `(1/6, 0, 1/2)`. The coefficient `a_k` of `(2, -1, -1)` obeys `a_(k+1) = (3/2) a_k + beta_k/6` with `beta_k = -2/3` for even `k` and `-1/6` for odd `k`, `a_0 = 1/3` from `U(1) = (1, 0, 0)`, so `(2/3)^k a_k -> 1/3 - (1/6)((2/3)(6/5) + (1/6)(4/5)) = 8/45`, and summing the geometric tails exactly, `a_k = (8/45)(3/2)^k + 7/45` at even `k` and `+ 11/90` at odd `k`. The `-3/2` eigenvector has first coordinate `0`, and the component along `(1, 1, 1)` is a third of `sum_(h < 2^k) C_t(h) = 2/3`. So, for `k >= 1`, `U_0(2^k) = 2 a_k + 2/9 = (16/45)(3/2)^k + 8/15` at even `k` and `+ 7/15` at odd `k`. `leak` checks the recursion against direct sums within `2.27e-12` at every `k <= 24`, prints `U_0(2^k)(2/3)^k = 0.355556` at `k = 40..61`, and checks the closed form in exact integers, on `6 . 2^k U(2^k)`, at every `k = 1..70`. The growth `(3/2)^k` sits in the residue classes modulo 3, not in the total, so it is a growth at frequency one third: the real part of `sum_(h < 2^k) C_t(h) omega^h`, `omega` a primitive cube root of unity, is `3 a_k`. In the spectral language of the ladder, the Thue-Morse measure is the Riesz product of the factors `1 - cos(2 pi 2^j theta)`, and at `theta = 1/3` every factor is `3/2`.

A free battery pair leaves a remainder. For `n^2 + n` at the dilation `(4, r)`, `P(4n + r) - 16 P(n) = 4(2r - 3) n + r(r + 1)`, so `y(4n + r) = t(16 A + R(n))` with `A = n^2 + n`, `R` that remainder and `t(16 A) = t(A) = y(n)`: the product is a Thue-Morse correlation at the lag `R(n)` read at the point `16A`. Write `R(n) = 16 a + c` with `0 <= c < 16`; then `t(16A + R) t(16A) = t(c) t(A + a) t(A)`. If the high digits of `A` were uniform, the mean would be a weighted mean of `C_t(a)` with weights `t(c)`, where `a` is near `(2r - 3) n/4` and `c` repeats with period 4 in `n`. As functions of the lag the weights then repeat with period `abs(2r - 3)`, and when 3 divides `2r - 3`, at `r = 0` and `r = 3`, they have a component at frequency one third, where the proposition puts the mass. The same holds at `(8, r)` with slope `2r - 7`, divisible by 3 at `r = 2` and `r = 5`.

**Conjecture: a free battery pair of a Thue-Morse reading whose remainder is linear with slope divisible by 3 has a correlation of order `N^(-log2(4/3))`; a reading with such a pair then fails the battery at every large `N`.** `falsify` runs the free readings at `N = 2^20, 2^22, 2^24, 2^26`. For `t` along `n^2 + n` the largest Pearson dilation correlation is `0.009261, 0.005130, 0.002895, 0.001760`, always at `(4, 0)` or `(4, 3)` with `(8, 5)` third, slopes `-0.426, -0.413, -0.359` step by step and `-0.399` overall in `log2 N` where the noise has `-0.5`, the steps moving away from `-0.5` as `N` grows, so that `z` grows, and `z = 9.48, 10.51, 11.86, 14.42` against the control's `3.83, 3.34, 2.33, 2.81`. Its kernel and square pass, `z <= 1.33`, and its shift test sits at the band's edge, `z = 3.64, 4.12, 3.42, 4.37`, at the lags `30, 30, 21, 24`, all multiples of 3. `t` along `n^3 + n`, whose kernel pair leaves `P(4n) - 8 P(2n) = -12 n`, fails worse: kernel `z = 29.60, 36.75, 42.69, 65.52`, slope `-0.309`. And `t(n^2)` itself, whose shift pairs leave `(n + h)^2 - n^2 = h(2n + h)`, has its largest shift correlation at `h = 15` at each size run, `2^15, 2^20, 2^22, 2^24, 2^26`, from `0.0280` down to `0.0011`, with slope `-0.384` from `2^20` to `2^26`: the power saving of Drmota, Mauduit and Rivat is real, and slower than the noise there.

Rudin-Shapiro is white, `C_r(h) = 0` at every `h >= 1`, and its spectral measure is Lebesgue measure (the ladder): no frequency is heavier than another for a remainder to leak. `r` along `n^2 + n`, free at every battery pair, has largest `z` over 64 shift, 44 dilation and 2 kernel correlations and the square `2.14, 2.56, 2.68, 3.41` at the four sizes, against the control's `3.83, 3.34, 2.76, 2.81`. Whiteness is not enough for a cubic: `r` along `n^3 + n` fails its kernel test at `z = 9.41, 16.75, 17.43, 17.10`, and `t3` along `n^3 + n` at up to `41.35`, by a mechanism this chapter does not identify.

## The primes

A reading along the consecutive primes has no polynomial structure, and no battery pair is a near-head. Its dilation and kernel tests pass for all three designs at every size, `z <= 3.97`. Its shift test is another matter. `t` reads `0.0545, 0.0522, 0.0503, 0.0491` at `h = 2` for `N = 2^20 .. 2^26`, slope `-0.025`, and `t3` reads `0.0552, 0.0524, 0.0492, 0.0467` at `h = 1`, slope `-0.040`, `z` up to `402`; `r` passes, `z <= 3.32`. A heuristic reading: consecutive primes are odd, and `t(2a + 1 + g) t(2a + 1) = t(a + g/2) t(a)` for even `g`, so a gap `g` contributes the Thue-Morse correlation at `g/2`; the gaps of consecutive primes favour multiples of 6, and a weighted mean of `C_t` over lags with a bias modulo 3 feels the proposition's mass, spread over a range of order `log p_N` and so decaying in `log N`, not in `N`. For `t3`, a gap divisible by 6 keeps the last ternary digit and moves the digits above by `g/3`, so the commonest gap alone contributes `C3(2) = -1/3`. Both readings stay a conjecture. [Aloui, Mauduit and Mkaouar 2021](../refs.md) study the blocks `t(p), t(p + 1), ..., t(p + k)` at one prime, not at consecutive primes, and the normality of `t` along the primes is open ([Popoli 2023](../refs.md)).

## The fourth rung

The ladder's three rungs read a design at `n`. A reading along a polynomial or the primes leaves the automatic class, and the battery still sorts the readings into three kinds.

- **Centred monomials plus a constant** keep a near-head at every battery pair centred where they are, and their correlations tend to proved local constants: `1` for the monomials, `-1/2` for `t` along `n^2 + 4`, a twisted `0` for `t` along `n^2 + 1` that the kernel test catches at depth 1. A centre the battery does not visit, `t` along `(n + 1)^2 + 1`, keeps an exact identity the battery cannot see.
- **Free readings of Thue-Morse**: both run, along `n^2 + n` and `n^3 + n`, fail at every size run, the first at the pairs the leak at one third predicts. The clean criterion, no near-head means no failure, is refuted by `t` along `n^2 + n`. Two polynomials are thin ground for the conjecture that every free reading of Thue-Morse fails, and `r` along `n^3 + n` fails with a white design, so the mass at one third is not the general cause.
- **Free readings of Rudin-Shapiro** along `n^2 + n`, equivalently `(2n + 1)^2`, and along the primes pass every test to `N = 2^26`, and the quadratic one leaves the automatic class in every base. **Conjecture: every battery correlation of Rudin-Shapiro along `n^2 + n` is `O(N^(-1/2 + epsilon))`, the rate of independent signs; at the sizes run its largest `z` stays at the control's level.** This is the candidate fourth rung, and the obstruction to a proof is named: the dilation correlation of a free pair is a mean over a polynomial set of a product at two polynomials, and its decay needs the joint normality of `(P(n), P(Bn + r))`, which no theorem read here gives. The one family of free dilation pairs proved to vanish in the sources read is that of Drmota, Mauduit, Rivat and Spiegelhofer, `sum g(p^2 n^2) conj(g(q^2 n^2)) = o(N)` for distinct primes `p`, `q` prime to the base ([Drmota, Mauduit, Rivat and Spiegelhofer 2020](../refs.md)), a dilation of the squares by an odd ratio; the near-power-of-two pairs, where the leak lives, are not among them.

## Doors

- **The leak theorem.** Prove that the `(4, 0)` correlation of `t` along `n^2 + n` is of order `N^(-log2(4/3))`. The proposition gives the mass; what is missing is the joint distribution of the high digits of `n^2 + n` with `n`, a carry lemma for two polynomials.
- **Rudin-Shapiro along the cubic.** `r` along `n^3 + n` fails its kernel test at `z` near 17 with a white design. Find the statistic that carries it, likely a correlation of `r` restricted to a class of the low digits.
- **The generalized battery.** A kernel test along every contraction `lambda n + mu` catches every head reading whose identity starts inside its window. Prove that no finite battery catches every head reading, the depth growing with `log_b` of the constant and the contraction with the denominator of the centre.
- **The pair law along the squares.** Its exact entries are dyadic rationals, and the others sit within `1.40e-7` of `1/6`, `-2/3`, `-5/9` and `-1/9`: give the closed form as an integral over the 2-adic distribution of the squares, and decide when it is rational.
- **The primes.** Compute the gap-weighted local constants from the prime-pair heuristic and compare with the measured `0.049`.
- **The ternary squares.** Is `t3(n^2)` normal? The normality theorem's hypothesis fails at `gcd(2, 2) = 2`, the 3-kernel is infinite, and the battery cannot tell, since `t3(9n^2) = t3(n^2)` catches it first.
