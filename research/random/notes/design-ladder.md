A design is a rule on digits carried through every level by the Kronecker product ([the core](../../core/notes/core.md)), so a design read left to right is a stream of bits, and a stream of bits can be asked to pass for random. This chapter asks which generators built from designs alone can pass, and answers with a ladder of three no-go rungs, each with its structural reason and the one test that catches it. Rung 1, one base: every design stream, every code, every XOR of designs at the same base and every finite-state map of one obeys an exact dilation identity, so a single kernel test returns correlation `1` on every window where its coordinate is nonconstant, and for a design so does the dilation test at residue `0`, while the shift tests can be blind: Rudin-Shapiro has zero correlation at every nonzero lag and dies at once. Rung 2, two bases: an XOR of designs at the bases `2` and `3` is automatic in neither base and can be white under every shift, yet the four-point dilation square `x(n) x(2n) x(3n) x(6n)` equals `1` at every `n`, its factor complexity is at most quadratic, and a one-base kernel pair strips one layer exactly and leaves a sum that grows as `phi^k` on `2^k` terms. Rung 3, rounds: a cellular automaton fed by a design stays automatic when its rounds are bounded, when its rule is linear over `F_p` at a base that is a power of `p`, and when its rule is linear on the configurations it actually reaches, from some round on. So a stream leaves its seed's automatic class only through unbounded rounds and either a base mismatch or a nonlinearity the seed exercises at unbounded depth, and the exercise is not sufficient: rule 18 is nonlinear and draws the gasket of rule 90 from one cell, and rules 22 and 182 exercise theirs at unbounded depth and draw automatic diagrams.

The generator is the study `lab/rs/design-ladder`, verbs `codes`, `rudin`, `battery`, `moire`, `rounds`, `shadow`, `escape` and `lemma`. The figure draws rule 18 from one cell above rule 18 from the Thue-Morse seed: the same nonlinear rule, which draws the gasket of rule 90 when the orbit never makes two ones in a row (one cell, the Cantor seed) and is conjectured to leave the automatic class on the Thue-Morse seed.

## The three tests

A stream is `x : N -> {-1, 1}`, read on `n < N`. For an iid uniform stream each statistic below has mean `0` and standard deviation `1/sqrt(N)` once its points are distinct, so the run in the last section calls a value past `4/sqrt(N)` a failure; at `N = 2^15` that band is `0.0221`.

- **The shift test** at lag `h` is the Pearson correlation of the pairs `(x(n), x(n + h))`, `n < N`; its limit `C(h)`, when it exists, is the correlation sequence, the Fourier transform of the stream's spectral measure. Every standard correlation battery is a family of shift tests.
- **The dilation test** at `(B, r)` is the Pearson correlation of `(x(n), x(Bn + r))`, `n < N`. It reads `B N` terms to compare `N` pairs.
- **The kernel test** at `(b, i, j)` is the Pearson correlation of `(x(b^i n), x(b^j n))`, `i < j`; the dilation test at `B = b^d, r = 0` is the case `i = 0`.
- **The dilation cube** at multipliers `P_1, ..., P_s` and modulus `M` is the mean of `prod over S of x(M n prod_(i in S) P_i)`, the product over the `2^s` subsets `S` of `{1..s}`. At `s = 2`, `P = (2, 3)`, `M = 1` it is the **dilation square** `x(n) x(2n) x(3n) x(6n)`.

Three names fix the objects. A *signed design* at base `b` with code `c`, a subset of the digits, is `x(n) = prod_i v(n_i)` over the base-`b` digits of `n`, `v = (-1)^c`, the sign tile carried through the Kronecker power; at level `L` the product runs over `L` digits, leading zeros included. Thue-Morse is the base-2 signed design of code `{1}`, and `t3` below is the base-3 signed design of code `{2}`, the parity of the number of ternary digits equal to `2`. A *digit-restricted design* is `A_L(n) = [every one of the L digits of n lies in D]`, the indicator the tree's Kronecker power renders, and its stream is `(-1)^(A_L(n))`. This chapter calls a sequence *b-automatic* when its *b-kernel*, the set of subsequences `n -> x(b^k n + r)` over `k >= 0` and `0 <= r < b^k`, is finite; that this is the same as being generated by a finite automaton reading base-`b` digits is the kernel criterion of [Allouche and Shallit 2003](../refs.md), not read here, and it is the bridge to Cobham's theorem as the [two bases](../../cobham/notes/cobham.md) topic states it. Every design of either kind is `b`-automatic, since its kernel at depth `1` is `{x, -x}` or `{A, 0}` up to the level.

## Rung 1: one base

**If `x` is `b`-automatic with a `b`-kernel of `m` elements, there are `0 <= e < e + d <= m` with `x(b^e n) = x(b^(e+d) n)` at every `n`, so the two coordinates of the kernel test at `(b, e, e + d)` agree at every `n` and every sample size. Proved.** The `m + 1` sequences `n -> x(b^k n)`, `k = 0..m`, all lie in the kernel, so two of them coincide. Nothing here is asymptotic: the identity holds term by term, so the test returns correlation `1` on the first window it reads where `x(b^e n)` is nonconstant (for `(-1)^n` at base `2` the only coincidence is the constant `x(2n) = x(4n) = 1`), where an iid stream meets it with a probability exponentially small in `N`.

The depth `e = 0` cannot always be taken. For `y(n) = (-1)^n t(n)`, `t` Thue-Morse, `y(2^d n + r) = (-1)^r t(2^d n + r) = +-t(n)` for `d >= 1`, so `y(n) y(2^d n + r) = +-(-1)^n` and every two-point dilation of `y` at a power of `2` is balanced to the last term: `rudin` prints `max abs Pearson = 0.000000` over `d <= 3`, `r < 2^d`, `n < 2^20`. Yet `y(2n) = t(n) = y(4n)`, and the kernel pair `(2n, 4n)` prints `1.000000`. The shift test catches this `y` anyway, since `C_y(h) = (-1)^h C_t(h)`.

### Designs need depth one

**For a signed design, `x_L(bn + r) = v(0) v(r) x_L(n)` at every `n < b^(L-1)` and every residue `r`. Proved.** The digits of `bn + r` are `r` followed by the digits of `n`, and the top digit of `n` at level `L` is a leading `0`, so `x_L(bn + r) = v(r) prod_(i < L-1) v(n_i)` and `x_L(n) = v(0) prod_(i < L-1) v(n_i)`. The dilation test at `(b, r)` is therefore exactly `v(0) v(r) = +-1` at every residue and every window. An XOR of signed designs at one base is the signed design of the product tile `v_1 v_2`, so it is covered. **For a digit-restricted design with `0` in `D`, `A_L(bn + r) = [r in D] A_L(n)`, so the dilation test is exactly `1` at every residue in `D` and its second coordinate is constant at the others. Proved**, by the same digit split; when `0` is not in `D` the first `b^(L-1)` cells of the render are void and the mean test already fails.

`codes` runs every base-2 and base-3 level-1 code. It checks the signed identity at every pair `(n, bn + r)`, `n < b^(L-1)`, `L = 20` at base 2 and `13` at base 3, `1048576` and `1594323` pairs, with `0` violations at every code. A code is written as its digit set; the class is the tile `v v(0)`, which fixes the infinite stream up to the global sign `v(0)^L`.

| base | code | class | dilation bias by `r` | `C(1), ..., C(b)` | `max abs C(h)`, `1 <= h <= 4096` |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2 | `{}`, `{0, 1}` | constant | `1, 1` | `1, 1` | `1` |
| 2 | `{0}`, `{1}` | Thue-Morse | `1, -1` | `-1/3, -1/3` | `1/3` at `h = 1` |
| 3 | `{}`, `{0, 1, 2}` | constant | `1, 1, 1` | `1, 1, 1` | `1` |
| 3 | `{1}`, `{0, 2}` | `(-1)^n` | `1, -1, 1` | `-1, 1, -1` | `1` |
| 3 | `{2}`, `{0, 1}` | parity of the `2`s | `1, 1, -1` | `0, -1/3, 0` | `1/3` at `h = 2` |
| 3 | `{0}`, `{1, 2}` | parity of the nonzero digits | `1, -1, -1` | `0, -1/3, 0` | `1/3` at `h = 2` |

The base-3 code `{1}` is a design that is periodic: `3` is odd, so the parity of the ternary digits equal to `1` is the parity of `n`. It is the one design on the table that is automatic in every base, as Cobham's theorem allows only for eventually periodic streams. The digit-restricted half of `codes` prints `Pearson(A_L(n), A_L(bn + r)) = 1.000000` at every `r` in `D` for the codes `{0}` at base 2 and `{0}`, `{0, 1}`, `{0, 2}` at base 3, and a constant coordinate everywhere else; the Cantor set is the last of these.

### The shift test sees the signed designs

**For a signed design with `v(0) = 1`, `C(bh) = C(h)` and some lag `1 <= s < b` has `C(s) != 0`. So no nonconstant signed design is white, and its correlation returns to `C(s)` at every lag `b^k s`. Proved.** Split `n = bm + r`: the pair `(bm + r, bm + r + bq + s)` stays in one block of `b` when `r + s < b` and carries one when not, which gives the exact identity `S(bN, bq + s) = al_s S(N, q) + al_(b-s) S(N, q + 1)` on the sums `S(N, h) = sum_(n < N) x(n) x(n + h)`, with `al_s = sum_(r < b-s) v(r) v(r + s)`, `al_0 = b`. Dividing by `bN`, `C(bq) = C(q)`, `C(1) = al_1/(b - al_(b-1))`, and `C(s) = (al_s + al_(b-s) C(1))/b` for `s < b`. If `C(1) = 0` then `C(b - 1) = al_(b-1)/b = v(0) v(b-1)/b != 0`. The census prints the values: `-1/3` at lag `1` for Thue-Morse, `0` and `-1/3` at lags `1` and `2` for both ternary classes, and the render's `C(h)` agrees with the recursion within `1.02e-5` at `N = 524288` and `5.65e-6` at `N = 531441` for `h <= 8`.

This is the invariance the [dilated stacks](../../dilations/notes/dilations.md) chapter proves for the digit measure of a weighted design, `mu^(bt) = mu^(t)` at integers, read on the correlation measure of the sign stream instead: the spectral measure of a signed design has `sigma^(b h) = sigma^(h)` and is never Rajchman. On the tree's map this is the place slot, one integer ratio, read as a statistical test.

### Rudin-Shapiro: white under every shift, predictable under one dilation

Rudin-Shapiro is `r(n) = (-1)^(the number of adjacent pairs 11 in the binary digits of n)`, with `r(2n) = r(n)` and `r(2n + 1) = (-1)^n r(n)`. It is `2`-automatic but not a signed design: its rule reads pairs of adjacent digits.

**`sum_(n < 2^k) r(n) r(n + h) = 0` for every `k >= 2` and `1 <= h <= 2^(k-2)`; for every `N`, `abs(sum_(n < N) r(n) r(n + h)) <= 4h (log2 N + 1)`; so `C(h) = 0` at every `h >= 1` and the spectral measure is Lebesgue measure. Proved.** Write `S(N, h)` for the sum and `D(N, h) = sum_(n < N) (-1)^n r(n) r(n + h)`. Splitting `n` by parity with the two rules gives four exact identities:

```
S(2N, 2h)     = (1 + (-1)^h) S(N, h)
S(2N, 2h + 1) = (-1)^h D(N, h) + D(N, h + 1)
D(2N, 2h)     = (1 - (-1)^h) S(N, h)
D(2N, 2h + 1) = (-1)^h D(N, h) - D(N, h + 1)
```

If `S(N, j) = 0` for `1 <= j <= J` and `D(N, j) = 0` for `0 <= j <= J`, the identities give `S(2N, j) = 0` for `1 <= j <= 2J` and `D(2N, j) = 0` for `0 <= j <= 2J`, the lag `0` entering only through `D(2N, 0) = 0`. At `N = 4`, where `r = 1, 1, 1, -1, 1`, the three sums `S(4, 1)`, `D(4, 0)` and `D(4, 1)` are `0`, so `J = 1` at `k = 2` and `J = 2^(k-2)` at `k`. For a general `N`, cut `[0, N)` into at most `log2 N + 1` dyadic blocks `[u 2^j, (u + 1) 2^j)`. On a block `r(u 2^j + m) = r(u) r(m) (-1)^(u_0 m_(j-1))`, so the pairs inside the block sum to the aperiodic autocorrelation `A_P(h)` of the block `P_j = (r(m))_(m < 2^j)` when `u` is even and `A_Q(h)` of its companion `Q_j = (r(m) (-1)^(m_(j-1)))_(m < 2^j)` when `u` is odd, plus at most `h` pairs that leave the block. The pair obeys `P_(j+1) = (P_j, Q_j)`, `Q_(j+1) = (P_j, -Q_j)`, so on the unit circle `abs(P_(j+1)(z))^2 + abs(Q_(j+1)(z))^2 = 2 (abs(P_j(z))^2 + abs(Q_j(z))^2) = 2^(j+1)`, a constant, and `A_P(h) + A_Q(h) = 0` at every `h != 0`. Since `S(2^j, h)` is `A_P(h)` plus `h` pairs, `abs(A_P(h)) = abs(A_Q(h)) <= h` once `h <= 2^(j-2)`, and a block with `2^j < 4h` holds fewer than `4h` pairs. Each block contributes at most `4h`.

**Verified** by `rudin`: `S(2^k, h) = 0` exactly for every `h <= 64` and `k = 10..20`; the largest `abs(S(N, h))` over all `N <= 2^20` and `h <= 64` is `118`, at `h = 59`, against `sqrt(2^20) = 1024`; and `r(2n) = r(n)` and `r(4n + 1) = r(n)` hold at all `2^20` values of `n`, so `Pearson(r(n), r(2n)) = 1.000000`. One dilation reads what no fixed lag can. A lag comparable to the sample can: for `n < 2^(k-1)` the rule gives `r(n + 2^k) = r(n)`, so the shift test at lag `h = 2N` returns `1`, a dilation in disguise.

## Rung 2: two bases

The moire XOR multiplies a base-2 layer by a base-3 layer. Two are run: `z = t . t3`, Thue-Morse times the parity of the ternary `2`s, and `y = r . t3`, Rudin-Shapiro times the same. Here `.` is the termwise product of signs, which is the XOR of the bits.

**`z` and `y` are neither `2`-automatic nor `3`-automatic, nor automatic in any base `k` with `k^a = 2^c` or `k^a = 3^c`. Proved.** A product of two `b`-automatic sequences is `b`-automatic, its kernel lying inside the products of kernel elements. If `z` were `2`-automatic, `t3 = z . t` would be `2`-automatic and `3`-automatic, so eventually periodic by Cobham's theorem ([Cobham 1969](../refs.md)); an eventual period `P` forces `C(P) = 1`, while the bound below gives `abs(C3(h)) <= 3^(-1/2)` at every `h >= 1`. If `z` were `3`-automatic, `t = z . t3` would be eventually periodic, against `abs(C_t(h)) <= 1/3`, which follows from `C_t(2q + 1) = -(C_t(q) + C_t(q + 1))/2` and `C_t(1) = -1/3` by induction. For `y`, Rudin-Shapiro has `C(h) = 0 != 1`. A base `k` with `k^a = 2^c` reduces to the base `2`, since a `k`-kernel holds the `k^a`-kernel and a `2^c`-automatic sequence is `2`-automatic. Whether `z` or `y` is automatic in a base such as `5` or `6` is left open here.

### Correlations factor at coprime bases

**Let `x` be a signed design at base `b` or Rudin-Shapiro at `b = 2`, and `w` a signed design at a base `b'` with `gcd(b, b') = 1`. Then `C_(xw)(h)` exists and equals `C_x(h) C_w(h)` at every lag. Proved.** For a signed design with `v(0) = 1`, `x(n) = x(n mod b^j) x(floor(n/b^j))`, so `F(n) = x(n) x(n + h)` equals `x(rho) x(rho + h)`, `rho = n mod b^j`, whenever `rho + h < b^j`: `F` agrees with a `b^j`-periodic function outside a set of density `h/b^j`. Rudin-Shapiro does the same with period `2^(j+1)`, since `r(u 2^j + rho) = r(u) r(rho) (-1)^(u_0 rho_(j-1))` and only the bit `u_0` survives the product. The same holds for `G(n) = w(n) w(n + h)` at the period `b'^l`. By the Chinese remainder theorem the residues of `n` modulo `b^j` and `b'^l` are jointly uniform over a full period, so the mean of the product of the two periodic functions is the product of their means, and letting `j` and `l` grow closes the gap `h/b^j + h/b'^l`. This is the mechanism by which the flat carpet layers at coprime odd scales are exactly uncorrelated in the crate (`moire::pairs::correlation`), carried from periodic layers to designs.

So the plain shift test catches `z`: `C_z(2) = C_t(2) C3(2) = (-1/3)(-1/3) = 1/9`, `C_z(6) = -1/9`, and `moire` prints `C2(h) C3(h)` for `h = 1..12` as `0, 1/9, 0, -1/27, 0, -1/9, 0, -1/27, 0, 0, 0, 1/27`; the empirical `C_z(h)` at `N = 2^22` agrees within `7.97e-4` at every `h <= 256`. And `y` is white: `C_y(h) = 0 . C3(h) = 0` at every `h >= 1`, and the empirical maximum over `h <= 256` at `N = 2^22` is `6.96e-4`, against `1/sqrt(N) = 4.88e-4`.

Do the correlations of `z` tend to `0`? [Kamae 1977](../refs.md), read at source in its Numdam scan, proves that the shift systems of `exp(2 pi i a s_p(n))` and `exp(2 pi i b s_q(n))` are disjoint and have mutually singular spectral measures, for `(p, q) = 1` and `a`, `b` irrational. The signs here are `a = 1/2` and a ternary digit count, so the theorem does not reach them; the factorisation above is what this chapter uses, and the limit is decided on the two factors. **`abs(C3(h)) <= 3^(-w_3(h)/2)`, `w_3(h)` the number of nonzero digits of `h` in balanced ternary, so `abs(C_z(h)) <= 3^(-1 - w_3(h)/2)` at every `h >= 1`. Proved.** The block recursion for `t3` reads `C3(3q) = C3(q)` and `C3(3q + delta) = -C3(q + delta)/3` for `delta = +-1`; adding or removing `1` changes the balanced-ternary weight by exactly one, so `w_3(q + delta) >= w_3(q) - 1 = w_3(h) - 2`, and induction closes, with `C3(1) = -C3(1)/3 = 0` at the start. The bound is attained: `moire` prints the maximum of `abs C3` over `h < 2^20` at each even weight as `1/3, 1/9, 1/27, 1/81, 1/243`, and `0` at every odd weight, where `h` is odd and `C3(h) = 0`. The binary side decays too, and [two bases](two-bases.md) proves it in binary runs: the maximum of `abs C2` at non-adjacent-form weight `1..11` runs `0.333, 0.333, 0.167, 0.125, 0.0729, 0.0495, 0.0306, 0.0200, 0.0127, 0.00817, 0.00521`, a ratio near `0.64` per digit. **`C_z(h) -> 0`. Proved in [two bases](two-bases.md), with an effective rate whose constant is not computed.** The largest values come at `h` sparse in both bases at once, `1049760 = 2^20 + 2^10 + 2^7 + 2^5` and `1222100000000` in base 3, with `max abs C_z = 1.55e-3` on `[2^20, 2^21)`, `3.33e-3` on `[2^15, 2^21)` and `1.39e-2` on `[2^10, 2^21)`. The proof there: a binary decay lemma `abs C_t(h) <= K rho^(r_2(h))` in binary runs, `rho < 1`, and the finiteness of the integers with at most `K` signed powers of `2` and at most `K` signed powers of `3`, an `S`-unit equation, give the limit, the finiteness being the `S`-unit theorem; a limit reached through integers sparse in two bases can only come slowly, which fits the tail printed.

### The dilation square

**Let `x = x_1 ... x_s` with each `x_i` a `b_i`-automatic sign sequence, the bases arbitrary. There are `P_i = b_i^(d_i)`, `d_i >= 1`, and a modulus `M = prod b_i^(e_i)` with `prod over S of x(M n prod_(i in S) P_i) = 1` at every `n`, the product over the `2^s` subsets `S` of `{1..s}`. Proved.** Rung 1 gives `x_i(b_i^(e_i) u) = x_i(b_i^(e_i + d_i) u)` at every `u`. Fix `i`, and pair each subset `S` without `i` with `S + {i}`: the argument `M n prod_S P_j` is a multiple of `b_i^(e_i)`, so the factor `x_i` takes the same value at both, and the `x_i` part of the cube is a product of squares. When the products `prod_S P_i` are distinct, as for pairwise coprime bases, the `2^s` points are distinct at `n >= 1` and the cube has mean `0` on an iid stream. One base needs two points; `s` bases need `2^s`. For `z` and `y` every `e_i = 0` and `d_i = 1`, so `x(n) x(2n) x(3n) x(6n) = 1` at every `n`: the run prints `square mean +1.0000` for both, and `-0.0030` for the Rule 30 centre column and `+0.0010` for the control.

### One-base tests on two bases

A dilation at one base strips that layer exactly. Since `t3(3^c n + r) = t3(r) t3(n)` for `r < 3^c`, `z(n) z(3^c n + r) = t3(r) . t(n) t(3^c n + r)`, a test on the Thue-Morse layer alone along multiplication by `3^c`, and symmetrically `z(n) z(2^a n + r) = t(r) . t3(n) t3(2^a n + r)`. Writing `g(n) = x(An + a) x(Bn + r)` for a signed design `x` at base `b`, the split `n = bm + d` turns every such sum into a linear recursion `S(bN) = M S(N)` on the `AB` states `(a, r)`, with an integer matrix `M`. **Every dilation `z(n) z(Bn + r)`, `0 <= r < B`, with `B = 2, 4, 8, 16, 3, 9, 27` has mean `0`. Proved by computation**: `moire` bounds the spectral radius by `norm(M^64)^(1/64)` and prints `rho <= 1.0789`, `1.7503`, `1.7871`, `1.8011` at `B = 2, 4, 8, 16` on the ternary layer and `rho <= 1.4311`, `1.4997`, `1.4607` at `B = 3, 9, 27` on the binary layer, each below its base, so the sums grow at most as `N^(theta + epsilon)` with `theta <= 0.0692`, `0.5096`, `0.5285`, `0.5356`, `0.5171`, `0.5847`, `0.5466`. The run at `N = 2^15` sees no excess: the largest dilation correlation of `z` over `2 <= B <= 9` is `0.0111`.

The kernel pairs are sharper. `z(2n) z(4n) = t3(2n) t3(4n)`, whose sum over `n < 3^k` is exactly `1` at every `k <= 38`. But `z(3n) z(9n) = t(3n) t(9n)`, the ternary layer gone, and **its sum over `n < 2^k` is `c_1 phi^k + c_2 psi^k + O(2^(k/2))` with `c_1 != 0`, `phi` the golden ratio, so the raw kernel sum at `(3, 1, 2)` has a z-score `S/sqrt(N)` growing as `N^(log2 phi - 1/2) = N^0.1942` along `N = 2^k`; the Pearson form also subtracts the means of `z(3n)` and `z(9n)`, which this proof does not bound. Proved by computation.** The sums obey `S_(k+4) = 2 S_(k+3) - 2 S_(k+2) + S_(k+1) + 2 S_k`, checked at every `k <= 60`; the recursion has `27` states, so its sequences satisfy a linear recurrence of order `27`, and an order-4 law that holds on `61` consecutive terms holds forever. The characteristic polynomial is `(x^2 - x - 1)(x^2 - x + 2)`, whose second factor has roots of modulus `sqrt 2`. Then `T_k = S_(k+2) - S_(k+1) + 2 S_k` removes them and is an integer Fibonacci sequence, `c_1 = 0` would force `T = 0`, and `T_0 = 4 - 2 + 2 = 4` from `S = 1, 2, 4, 6, 8, ...`. `moire` prints `S(2^60)/phi^60 = 1.263038` and a growth exponent `0.694232` against `log2 phi = 0.694242`, and the predicted correlation `1.263 (2^15)^(log2 phi - 1)` is `0.0528`, which the run reads as the kernel value `0.0508` of `z`. For `y` the base-3 dilation leaves Rudin-Shapiro along `3n + c`, and those sums are large without a proof: `sum_(n < 2^21) r(n) r(3n + 2) = -14682`, `10.1` standard deviations, and the run flags `y` at `(B, r) = (3, 2)` with `0.0277`, `5.0` standard deviations.

### Zero entropy

**The factor complexity of a product is at most the product of the complexities, and each layer is linear: `p(n) <= 4bn` for a signed design and `p(n) <= 32n` for Rudin-Shapiro, so `p_z(n), p_y(n) = O(n^2)` and the topological entropy of both is `0`. Proved.** A factor of length `n <= b^k` of a signed design sits inside two consecutive level-`k` blocks, which are `x(j) B_k` and `x(j + 1) B_k` for the level-`k` block `B_k`, so it is fixed by its offset below `b^k` and the pair of signs, at most `4 b^k <= 4bn` choices; Rudin-Shapiro's level-`k` blocks are `+-P_k`, `+-Q_k`, at most `16` ordered pairs. The census is exact where every pair of layer factors occurs jointly, which `moire` checks in a prefix of length `2^22`; the layer counts come from the prefix `18 b^k`, which holds every offset and every sign pair, and the Thue-Morse column is [A005942](https://oeis.org/A005942).

| 8 | 22 | 28 | 176 | 2.75 | 56 | 232 |

At every `n` checked the joint pairs are complete, `p_t p_t3` of them, and `p_z(n)` is `0.49` of that product at `n = 64`, as the global sign pair `(u, w) -> (-u, -w)` predicts for two layers closed under complement. Rudin-Shapiro's prefix count is `8n - 8` at `n = 8..64` from both prefixes; that is a count, not a proof of the formula. Zero entropy is what a compressor sees in the limit: the LZ78 rate of `z` falls `1.143, 1.001, 0.840` bits per bit at `2^14, 2^16, 2^18` and of `y` `1.245, 1.140, 0.989`, while the `splitmix64` control holds `1.296, 1.251, 1.215`, the phrase overhead of an incompressible stream.

## Rung 3: rounds

A round is one step of an elementary cellular automaton, a rule `f` with `a(t + 1, n) = f(a(t, n - 1), a(t, n), a(t, n + 1))` over `F_2`; the seed `a(0, n)` is a design on `n >= 0` and `0` on `n < 0`, and a generator reads one line of the spacetime diagram, a row `n -> a(T, n)` or a column `t -> a(t, n_0)`. The **reach** `W_t` is the set of windows `(l, c, r)` that occur in row `t`, the exterior included. A rule `g = a l + b c + d r + e` over `F_2` is **affine**; sixteen of the 256 rules are. The first shape put on trial, that a design-fed stream escapes the automatic class if and only if some round is nonlinear and the rounds are unbounded, fails in the "if" direction and holds in the "only if" direction at a matching base only.

**Bounded rounds. If the seed is `b`-automatic, every row `a(T, .)` is `b`-automatic, whatever the rule and its radius. Proved.** After `T` rounds of a radius-`w` rule, `a(T, n) = G(s(n - Tw), ..., s(n + Tw))` for one function `G`, a sliding block code. The kernel of a shift `n -> s(n + j)` is finite: `s(b^k n + r + j)` with `r + j = b^k i + r'`, `0 <= r' < b^k`, is the kernel element `s(b^k . + r')` read at `n + i`, and `i` ranges over at most `2 abs(j) + 2` values; the zeros on `n < 0` change only finitely many first terms. A function of finitely many `b`-automatic sequences is `b`-automatic, its kernel lying inside the tuples of kernel elements. So a fixed number of rounds, linear or not, stays on rung 1 and dies under its kernel test, at a depth that grows with `T`. **Verified** by the kernel count of `rounds`, the number of distinct kernel words `(s(2^k n + r))_(n < 24)` over `r < 2^k`, accumulated over `k = 0..10`: on the Thue-Morse seed, rows `1, 2, 3, 4` of rule 30 stop at `8, 11, 14, 21` words and row `4` of rule 110 at `11`, the seed itself at `2`, while the `splitmix64` control reads `1, 3, 7, 15, ..., 2047`, a new word at every residue.

**Linear rounds at a matching base. If the rule is linear over `F_p`, `a(t + 1, n) = sum_(abs(j) <= w) phi_j a(t, n + j)` modulo `p`, and the seed is `p`-automatic, then every column `t -> a(t, n_0)` is `p`-automatic, at every number of rounds. Proved.** Write the row `t` as the Laurent series `Phi(x)^t u(x)`, `Phi = sum_j phi_j x^(-j)`, `u` the seed, and `Lambda_f` for the section `u -> sum_m u(pm + f) x^m`. Over `F_p`, `Phi(x)^p = Phi(x^p)`, so `Phi^(pt + e) u = Phi(x^p)^t Phi^e u` and the cell `(pt + e, pm + f)` of the diagram of `u` is the cell `(t, m)` of the diagram of the seed `Lambda_f(Phi^e u)`. The seeds reached this way stay inside the `F_p`-span of the shifts `x^i g`, `g` in the `p`-kernel of `u`, `abs(i) <= w + 2`: multiplying by `Phi^e` adds at most `(p - 1) w` to a shift and a section divides it by `p`, rounding up, and `Lambda_f` of a kernel element is a kernel element. That span is finite, so the two-dimensional `p`-kernel of the diagram is finite, and a column, read at depths past `log_p n_0`, cycles through finitely many seeds. Rule 90 is `Phi = x + x^(-1)` over `F_2` and rule 150 is `x + 1 + x^(-1)` ([Martin, Odlyzko and Wolfram 1984](../refs.md)); from one cell rule 90 draws Pascal's triangle modulo `2`, the gasket, which `rounds` checks on every cell `t < 4096`. [Allouche, von Haeseler, Peitgen and Skordev 1996](../refs.md), read at source, prove the double sequence of a linear automaton over `Z/pZ` from a finite seed `p`-automatic; the seeds here are automatic, not finite. **Verified**: the centre columns of rules 90 and 150 on the Thue-Morse seed stop at `5` and `4` kernel words at base 2 and rule 90 from one cell at `2`, while at base 3 the same columns keep growing, `38` and `1078` words at depth `6`, as Cobham's theorem requires of a stream that is not periodic.

**Linear on the reach. Let the seed be 2-automatic, `g` an affine rule and `t_0` a round such that `f = g` on every window of `W_t` for all `t >= t_0`. Then every row and column of the orbit of `f` is 2-automatic. Proved.** From round `t_0` every window of row `t` lies in `W_t`, where `f` and `g` agree, so row `t + 1` is `g` of row `t`: the orbit of `f` is the orbit of `g` from row `t_0`. Row `t_0` is 2-automatic by the bounded-rounds theorem, and zero below `-t_0 w` or a constant there when `f(0, 0, 0) = 1`; subtract the constant, shift by `t_0`, and the linear theorem applies. The constant evolves by `c_(t+1) = (a + b + d) c_t + e`, ultimately periodic in `t`, and a 2-automatic stream plus an ultimately periodic one is 2-automatic. The contrapositive is the necessary condition: **a design-fed elementary stream that is not 2-automatic exercises a nonlinearity at unbounded depth, that is, for every affine `g` infinitely many rounds meet a window on which `f` and `g` differ. Proved.**

**Whether `f` is linear on its reach from round `0` with shadow `g` is decided by `g` alone: it holds exactly when `f = g` on `M(g, s)`, the windows of the `g`-orbit of the seed `s` over all rounds and cells. Proved.** If `f = g` on `M(g, s)` the two orbits agree by induction on `t`, so the reach of `f` lies in `M(g, s)`; conversely, if the reach of `f` lies where `f = g` the orbits agree and `M(g, s)` is that reach. So the rules linear on their reach are those agreeing with some affine `g` on `M(g, s)`, and `M(g, s)` is a finite closure. **Proved by computation.** The cell `(2t + e, 2m + u)` of the diagram of a seed `v` is the cell `(t, m)` of the diagram of the section `Lambda_u(Phi^e v)`, so a window of three adjacent cells is, one binary digit down, a window of three cells of three sections at offsets in `{-1, 0, 1}`. The sections stay in the `F_2`-span of the shifts by at most one of the seed and, for Thue-Morse, of the indicator of `n >= 0`, since `t(2m) = t(m)` and `t(2m + 1) = 1 + t(m)`. Halving `T` and `n` reaches `(0, 0)` or `(0, -1)`, so the windows are the base values of finitely many reachable states, and the constant of an affine `g` costs one tag bit. `shadow` prints the exact set beside the set seen by simulation to `t <= 4096` for the 16 affine orbits of both seeds: 32 pairs, 0 differences.

**Of the 256 elementary rules, 90 are linear on their reach from one cell and 20 from the Thue-Morse seed. Proved by computation.** From one cell the 90 are the 16 affine rules and 74 more, grouped by shadow: rule 90 shares its shadow with 18, 26, 82, 146, 154, 210 and 218, eight rules that draw the gasket; rules 0, 170, 204 and 240 each have fifteen non-affine twins whose cell dies, shifts or stays; rule 255 has seven whose row is all ones from round 1; the other ten affine rules stand alone. By hand, for rule 18 `= (l + r)(1 + c)`: it equals rule 90 on every window without two adjacent ones, and from one cell the live cells at time `t` sit at positions of the parity of `t`, so no window holds `11` and the parity pattern is inherited. From the Thue-Morse seed the 20 are the 16 affine rules and 42, 76, 112 and 128, which differ from 170, 204, 240 and 0 only on the window `111`, and Thue-Morse, cube-free, never makes it. Simulation to `t <= 4096` finds the same two sets, and no rule meets a new window after round 3 from one cell or round 1 from Thue-Morse. `rounds` prints 0 cells differing from rule 90 on `t < 4096` for rules 18 and 146, against `88574` and `265720` for rules 22 and 126, all counted on `n >= 0`, and the figure asserts the identity on `t <= 63`. **Verified** by `escape`: the largest depth-10 kernel count of a column of a rule linear on its reach is 5 from one cell and 13 from Thue-Morse, against 2047, and none of the 110 pairs grows. The Cantor seed sits on even positions, every member having digits `0` and `2`, so rule 18 on it is rule 90 on it and the two columns print the same kernel counts.

**Exercising a nonlinearity at unbounded depth does not force escape. Refuted.** Of the 166 rules not linear on their reach from one cell, 56 burn in, their last exercise against the best affine `g` at round 2 or earlier, and 110 exercise to the end of the run, round 4094 or later; no rule lies between. `escape` reads three columns of each, `n_0 = -1, 0, 1`, to `t < 2^15`, and calls a column periodic when it has a period of at most 512 after a preperiod of at most an eighth of its length, and a plateau when the kernel count of its last depth equals that three depths earlier. Of the 110, 92 have all three columns ultimately periodic, 13 have a column that does not plateau (rules 30, 45, 73, 75, 86, 89, 101, 110, 124, 135, 137, 149 and 193, with 403 to 2047 kernel words) and 5 have an aperiodic column and plateau at 4 to 5 words: rules 22, 126, 129, 161 and 182. Rules 22, 126, 129, 161 and 182 are proved automatic in [Pascal images](pascal-images.md), each a different way for a nonlinearity to be exercised without effect.

**A repair, Conjecture. On a single cell an aperiodic column leaves the plateau exactly when the exercise has positive density.** The density is the share of interior cone cells, at rounds `t > 2048`, whose window lies outside the agreement set of the best affine `g`. The five that stay, all proved automatic, read at most `0.0092` (rule 22 `0.0092`, rule 182 `0`, rules 126, 129 and 161 `8e-8`), the thirteen that leave read `0.0778` to `0.2491`, and the 92 periodic rules up to `0.2498`: a gap of a factor 8 over 18 rules, 11 up to mirror images and equal diagrams, is evidence, not a law. On the Thue-Morse seed it fails: in the bulk, away from the edge of the seed (columns `2^14, +1, +2`, `t < 2^14`), 44 of the 102 rules exercised to round 4094 or later plateau at 9 to 39 words, 35 also at word length 48, with no period of 512 or less, at densities `0.047` to `0.219`. The plateau test misses kernels of about 80 words: rule 161 from the edge of that seed agrees with an affine rule from round 73 to round 32768, so the theorem makes it automatic if that persists, and it reads 82 to 98 and is called growth.

What does escape is a Conjecture, read on kernel counts that are evidence, not proof, since an automatic stream with a large kernel looks the same at depth `10`. The mismatched base: rule 90 and rule 150 over `F_2` on the base-3 Cantor seed reach `803` and `874` words at base 2 and `891` and `1092` at base 3, growing at every depth. The nonlinearity that bites: rule 18 on the Thue-Morse seed, whose adjacent ones break the parity pattern, reaches `1074` and `1041`, and rule 30 from one cell and from the Thue-Morse seed reads `2047` at base 2 and `1093` at base 3, as the control does; its column agrees with [A051023](https://oeis.org/A051023) on the first `36` terms. **That these columns are automatic in no base is a Conjecture**; for Rule 30 not even non-periodicity is proved here.

## The falsification run

`battery` runs eight streams at `N = 2^15` pairs per test, so the null band `4/sqrt(N)` is `0.0221`. The shift column is the largest `abs C_N(h)` over `1 <= h <= 64`, the dilation column the largest over `2 <= B <= 9`, `r < B`, with its `(B, r)`, the kernel column the largest of `(x(bn), x(b^2 n))` over `b = 2, 3`, the square column the mean of `x(n) x(2n) x(3n) x(6n)`, and the last column the LZ78 rate at `2^18` bits. A cell whose coordinate is constant is skipped. The carpet stream reads the Sierpinski carpet along its Morton order, the base-9 digit-restricted design that drops the digit `4`, the centre pair `(1, 1)`. A row of the infinite carpet at a finite height is periodic, and the middle row of a level-`L` render is the Cantor stream again, so the Morton order is the carpet read as one stream.

## Doors

- **The binary decay lemma.** Settled: [two bases](two-bases.md) proves `abs C_t(h) <= K rho^(r_2(h))` in binary runs, which with the balanced-ternary bound and the `S`-unit finiteness gives `C_z(h) -> 0`, at an effective rate whose constant is not computed.
- **No base at all.** Prove `z` and `y` automatic in no base, the bases `5`, `6`, `10` included. Superlinear complexity would do it, since an automatic stream has linear complexity, a classical fact not read here; the census is exact to `n = 64` but a lower bound for every `n` needs the joint pairs complete at every length.
- **Rudin-Shapiro along `3n`.** The sums `sum_(n < N) r(n) r(3n + c)` reach `10.1` standard deviations at `N = 2^21`; an exact recursion on the carry states of `3n + c` would decide whether they grow faster than `sqrt N`, an analogue for Rudin-Shapiro of the power growth of Thue-Morse along `3n`, not read here.
- **The mismatched linear round.** Rule 90 on the base-3 Cantor seed is a sum of Pascal columns started at the Cantor points; prove its column automatic in no base, which would show a design-fed linear round escaping.
- **A schedule of shadows.** Rule 126 follows rule 90 at every round but `2^k - 2`, yet its closed form in [Pascal images](pascal-images.md) needs no schedule. Partial products of a 2-automatic schedule of Laurent polynomials need not be automatic; decide when they are.
- **The frontier and the block code.** Rule 182 exercises its nonlinearity only on the edge of the light cone, rule 22 is a block code of rule 90, and the difference map sends rule 126 to rule 18. State the theorem for the interior of the cone, or up to a sliding block conjugacy, and classify the elementary rules whose diagram from one cell is such an image of a linear one.
- **The density law.** Prove that on a single cell an aperiodic column is automatic exactly when the exercise has density zero, or break it with a rule between `0.0092` and `0.0778`.
- **The forensic theorem.** A signed design satisfies `x(bn + r) = +-x(n)` exactly, so a window starting at `0` names its base. Prove that a window starting anywhere does, and that no base `B` other than a power of `b` gives an exact dilation identity on a nonperiodic signed design.

A digit-restricted generator leaks its base to a dilation test; mixing two bases erases the leak but not the determinism; and a design-fed automaton leaves its seed's automatic class only through a base that is not a power of its characteristic or a nonlinearity the seed actually exercises at unbounded depth, a condition that is necessary and, by rules 22 and 182, not sufficient.
