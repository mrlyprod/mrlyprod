## Introduction

![The 65536 grids of the 4 x 4 torus as one square, cut into concentric square bands, one band for each cycle of the stamped round `t_c h`, `c = 0x0406`, of the level-2 carpet split, written through the carpet neighbourhood under the primes Source and moved by the slide spread, the stamp being one element of the group chosen for its long prime cycle; the area of a band is proportional to the length of its cycle, the longest outermost. The blue band, more than half the square, is one cycle whose length is a prime, and by Lemma 7.1 that one cycle makes the group of the round and its stamps the alternating group. The orange bands, alternately strong and pale, are the other cycles, shrinking inward to a fixed point at the centre.](paper-design-rounds)

Take the `n x n` torus of bits and colour some of its cells by a design: the coloured cells are the written side `A`, the others the read side `B`. A round counts, at every written cell, the set bits among the read cells around it, flips the written cell when the count lies in a fixed set, moves the whole grid by a map of the torus and adds a fixed grid, the stamp. The written cells are computed from cells the step leaves alone, so the step undoes itself and the round is a permutation of the `2^(n^2)` grids: a Feistel round whose two halves are the two sides of a design. Two questions come before any statistical test of such a generator. Which permutations can a schedule of rounds and stamps make? And how fast do two inputs stop looking related?

The first answer is a sign law. Every stamp is even, every map of the torus that moves cells is even once `n^2 >= 3`, and the Feistel step is even as soon as it writes two cells; a round that writes one cell is odd exactly when its one mess bit takes the value `1` on an odd number of read grids, which forces that cell to read the whole read side (Theorem 3.1). So rounds that write two cells or more generate at most the alternating group `A_M`, `M = 2^(n^2)`, and the question is when they get there. The group of a schedule with every stamp contains the translations, so it is transitive, and by the subspace criterion of Caranti, Dalla Volta and Sala its blocks are the cosets of a subspace (Lemma 4.1). Five laws read off the design produce such a subspace or a product action (Theorem 4.3): a read-closed set of cells, the module spanned by the mess, a self-complementary rule, a kept parity of a cell partition, and a coset partition that no read crosses. On the two catalogs computed here, every split of the `3 x 3` torus and every two-letter word of the `4 x 4` torus under three neighbourhoods, four rules and six maps, these laws are the only way a nonlinear round misses the alternating group: `147` of the `3240` nonlinear `3 x 3` cells generate `A_512` and `198` of the `2142` nonlinear `4 x 4` cells generate `A_65536`, every one certified by a prime cycle and Jordan's theorem, and every other nonlinear cell meets a law (Facts 7.3 to 7.5). The equivalence is proved by census on these two catalogs only; on every torus it is Conjecture 10.1.

Three results hold on every torus. The first law has a mess-free core: a subspace inside the written side is a block of the round exactly when the spread maps it onto itself, whatever the rule, and the splits that contain one are counted in closed form, so that the rotation and the transposition of the torus break almost every split as `n` grows (Theorems 5.1 and 5.2). Conversely, a strongly connected graph of second differences, an isolated reader for every read cell and one live read give a primitive group on every torus for any schedule (Theorem 6.5); they certify the checkerboard pair, the two-sided Feistel network on the colours of the board, at every even `n >= 6` (Theorem 6.7), and a pair on the `5 x 5` torus whose group is `A_(2^25)` (Fact 7.7). Finally, with a uniform stamp after every round, the difference of two inputs is a doubly stochastic Markov chain, irreducible, for a single round, exactly when the group is 2-transitive (Theorems 8.1 and 8.4); it mixes exactly when it is also aperiodic, and its rate is `|lambda_2(Q)|`, the second eigenvalue modulus of the period product `Q`. The second singular value of one round carries no information: by the classical one-round Feistel characteristic it is `1` once two cells are written (Theorem 8.5). Affine rounds never mix, and twelve affine `3 x 3` cells are 2-transitive yet stay at the largest distance forever (Theorem 8.6 and Fact 8.7). Every mixing cell of the two catalogs is alternating, so neither catalog holds a mixing round that is affine for a hidden sum (the remark after Fact 8.9). By the standard light-cone and fan-in arguments, since a difference spreads by at most one cell a round under every local map, a local schedule needs a number of rounds linear in the side `n = 2^nu`, that is `Omega(2^nu)`, and any schedule at all needs `Omega(nu)` (Theorems 8.10 and 8.11).

What is classical, and what is not. That Feistel-type round functions are even permutations and can generate the alternating group is the content of Coppersmith and Grossman 1975 and Even and Goldreich 1983, and Wernsdorf proved that the one-round functions of the DES, and later the round functions of Rijndael, generate the alternating group; all four are cited unread. The parity of maps that flip one bit by a function of the others is classical, the setting of Coppersmith and Grossman 1975 as the secondary accounts describe it; the sign law is proved here from scratch in the form the design needs, and the count of Corollary 3.2 is elementary. The block criterion is Section 2.1 of Caranti, Dalla Volta and Sala 2009, read at source and proved again in Lemma 4.1; law I and the trap of Section 5 apply it to coordinate subspaces. The same authors, read at source, derive the alternating group for AES-like ciphers from primitivity through Li's list of primitive groups with a regular abelian subgroup (2009b), and Aragona, Calderini and Civino 2020, read at source, show that only linear partitions propagate through a long-key Feistel network. Jordan's theorem is used as stated in the literature. The Markov property of differences under independent uniform round keys is the Markov-cipher property of Lai, Massey and Murphy 1991, cited unread and proved again in Theorem 8.1. The distance of Proposition 8.2 is half the pairwise decorrelation distance of Vaudenay 2003, read at source, and measures the almost pairwise independence that Liu, Tessaro and Vaikuntanathan 2021 study for substitution-permutation networks, read at the abstract. The direction from a round function generating the alternating or the symmetric group to an irreducible difference chain is credited to Hornauer, Stephan and Wernsdorf 1994, cited unread and proved again, in a 2-transitive form, in Theorem 8.4. Theorem 8.5 restates the classical one-round Feistel characteristic, Theorem 8.6 the folklore that an affine map has a deterministic difference table, and the two light-cone bounds are the standard locality and fan-in arguments applied to designs. A round affine for another group law on the grids is the hidden-sum trapdoor of Calderini, Civino and Sala 2021, read at source. What depends on the design is not taken from these sources: the five laws as read off a design and their exhaustion of the two catalogs, the count of trapped splits, the isolation theorem with its families on every even torus, the `5 x 5` group, and the distance curves and exact cone depths of design rounds. Section 9 compares the two in detail.

## The rounds

Throughout, `n >= 3`, the torus is `Z_n x Z_n`, a cell is `p = (i, j)`, `N = n^2` and `M = 2^N`. A grid is a vector `x` of `V = F_2^N`, `x_p` its bit at `p`, `e_p` the unit vector at `p`, and for a set `C` of cells `V_C` is the subspace of grids supported on `C`. The two unit offsets of the torus are `e_1 = (1, 0)` and `e_2 = (0, 1)`, so that `p + e_1` is a cell and `e_(p + e_1)` the unit grid at it. A grid is written as a hexadecimal mask with bit `n i + j` for the cell `(i, j)`. Sums of grids are taken in `F_2`.

**Definition 2.1 (split, neighbourhood, Source, mess).** A split is a nonempty proper set `A` of cells, the written side, and `B` is its complement, the read side; for a grid `x`, `x_A` and `x_B` are its restrictions to `A` and `B`, vectors of `F_2^A` and `F_2^B`. A neighbourhood is a set `D` of nonzero offsets in `{-1, 0, 1}^2`: the carpet (all eight), the net (the four orthogonal) or the void (the four diagonal), the base-3 level-1 designs `495`, `186` and `341` with their centre removed. A written cell `a` reads `N(a) = (a + D) ∩ B`, of size `m_a`. A Source is a set `S` of counts, `primes = {2, 3, 5, 7}`, `binary = {1, 2, 4, 8}`, `fibonacci = {0, 1, 2, 3, 5, 8}` or `odds = {1, 3, 5, 7}`, and `g(t) = [t in S]`. The mess is

```
m(x) = sum over a in A of f_a(x) e_a ,    f_a(x) = g(|x ∩ N(a)|) ,
```

with `|x ∩ N(a)|` the number of cells of `N(a)` whose bit is `1`. A written cell is live when `g` is not constant on `0, ..., m_a`, and dead otherwise. Under `odds` every `f_a` is a parity and the mess is linear; a cell whose Source is not `odds` is called nonlinear.

**Definition 2.2 (spread, stamp, round).** A spread is a permutation `sigma` of the cells acting by `L e_p = e_(sigma(p))`; the six used are `id`, `shift (i, j+1)`, `drop (i+1, j)`, `slide (i+1, j+1)`, `turn (j, n-1-i)` and `flip (j, i)`. A stamp is a translation `t_c(x) = x + c`. The Feistel step is `F(x) = x + m(x)`, the stampless round is `h = L F`, and the round is `R = t_c L F`.

**Lemma 2.3 (reversibility).** `F` is an involution, every round is a bijection of `V`, and `R^(-1) = F L^(-1) t_c`.

**Proof.** `m(x)` depends only on `x_B`, and `F` changes only `x_A`, so `m(F(x)) = m(x)` and `F(F(x)) = x + m(x) + m(x) = x`. `L` and `t_c` are bijections and `t_c` is its own inverse. □

**Definition 2.4 (schedules and their groups).** A schedule is a list of stampless rounds `h_1, ..., h_k`, each with its own split, neighbourhood, Source and spread, and its group is `G = <T, h_1, ..., h_k>` with `T = {t_c : c in V}`. It contains every stamped round `t_c h_j` and every word in them. A single is a schedule of one round; a pair is the schedule that writes `A` from `B` and then `B` from `A`, both with the identity spread, the two-sided Feistel network.

**Definition 2.5 (the catalogs).** The `3 x 3` catalog takes every split, the `510` masks `1, ..., 510`. The `4 x 4` catalog takes every two-letter base-2 word `k_1 (x) k_2`, `k_1, k_2` in `1, ..., 15`, the cell `(2u + s, 2v + t)` with `u, v, s, t` in `{0, 1}` being written when bit `2u + v` of `k_1` and bit `2s + t` of `k_2` are set, the full word dropped: `224` masks. A cell of a catalog is a split with a neighbourhood, a Source and a spread. A split is kept once per orbit of the torus symmetries, translations times the eight symmetries of the square, that commute with the spread; such a symmetry conjugates the whole round, since the neighbourhoods are symmetric, and keeps its cycle type and its group. This leaves `4320` cells at `3 x 3`, `3240` of them nonlinear, and `2856` at `4 x 4`, `2142` nonlinear. The level-2 carpet of the figure is the word `7 (x) 7 = 0x135f`.

The undo of Lemma 2.3, the involution and the bijection are checked on every state of all `7176` cells by the round census, with no fault.

## The sign law

**Theorem 3.1 (the sign law).** Let `N >= 3` and let `w` be the number of read grids `y` in `F_2^B` with `m(y) != 0`, the mess being a function of `y = x_B`. Every stamp `t_c` with `c != 0` and every spread is an even permutation of `V`, and the Feistel step has sign `(-1)^(w 2^(|A| - 1))`. So every round that writes two cells or more is even, a round that writes one cell has sign `(-1)^w`, and the rounds of a schedule and all stamps generate a subgroup of `A_M` unless some round writes one cell with `w` odd.

**Proof.** A stamp with `c != 0` is a fixed-point-free involution, `2^(N-1)` transpositions, even since `N >= 2`. A spread is a product of transpositions of two coordinates `p != q`, and such a swap fixes the grids with `x_p = x_q` and exchanges the others in pairs, `2^(N-2)` transpositions, even since `N >= 3`. The Feistel step keeps every fibre `{x : x_B = y}`, a set of `2^|A|` grids, and acts on it as the translation by `m(y)`, a vector of `V_A`: the identity when `m(y) = 0`, and `2^(|A|-1)` transpositions otherwise. Summing over the `w` fibres with `m(y) != 0` gives `w 2^(|A|-1)` transpositions. □

**Corollary 3.2 (the one-cell round).** If `A = {a}` and `a` reads `k` cells, then `w = 2^(|B| - k) sum over s in S of C(k, s)`, so the round is odd only when `a` reads all of `B` and `sum over s in S of C(N - 1, s)` is odd. On the `4 x 4` torus no round of the catalog is odd. On the `3 x 3` torus the odd rounds of the catalog are exactly the single-cell splits with the carpet and `binary`.

**Proof.** A read grid has a nonzero mess exactly when its pattern on `N(a)` has a count in `S`: `C(k, s)` patterns of count `s`, each with `2^(|B| - k)` completions off `N(a)`. If `k < |B|` the power of two makes `w` even. On the `4 x 4` torus a cell reads at most `8` of the other `15`. On the `3 x 3` torus only the carpet reads all `8` other cells, and `sum over s in S of C(8, s)` is `148`, `107`, `150` and `128` for `primes`, `binary`, `fibonacci` and `odds`, odd only for `binary`; the net and the void read `4` cells, and their weights are `2^4` times `10`, `11`, `15` and `8`. □

The census finds `9` odd cells at `3 x 3` after the symmetry reduction, all of them single-cell carpet `binary` splits, and none at `4 x 4`; every round of the census is checked against Theorem 3.1 and its weight against Corollary 3.2. The sign law is why the alternating group, not the symmetric one, is the target: for `n >= 4` no design round is odd at all unless a cell reads the whole of `B`, which no neighbourhood of size `8` can do.

**Proposition 3.3 (the even null).** A uniform even permutation of `M >= 2` points has mean cycle count `H_M + (-1)^M / (M (M - 1))`, `H_M` the harmonic number, and for `M >= 3` mean fixed-point count `1`.

**Proof.** Let `c(pi)` count the cycles of `pi` in `S_M`. The classical identity `sum over pi of x^(c(pi)) = x (x + 1) ... (x + M - 1)` and `sgn(pi) = (-1)^(M - c(pi))` give `sum over pi of sgn(pi) x^(c(pi)) = x (x - 1) ... (x - M + 1)`. Differentiating at `x = 1` only the factor `x - 1` contributes, so `sum over pi of sgn(pi) c(pi) = prod over k != 1 of (1 - k) = (-1)^M (M - 2)!`, while `sum over pi of c(pi) = M! H_M`. The even permutations are `M!/2`, and their total cycle count is half the sum of the two sums, so their mean is `H_M + (-1)^M (M - 2)!/M!`. For fixed points, `sum over pi of sgn(pi) fix(pi) = M sum over tau in S_(M-1) of sgn(tau) = 0` once `M - 1 >= 2`, so the even mean equals the full mean, `1`. □

The census checks both means over every even permutation of `M = 2, ..., 8` points, whose numbers are A001710. At `M = 512` the correction is `3.8e-6`, so the fair null of a design round is, to that precision, the uniform permutation: `H_512 = 6.8165` and `H_65536 = 11.6676` cycles, a mean longest cycle covering `0.62494` of the points at `M = 512`, computed exactly, and tending to the Golomb-Dickman constant `0.62433` (A084945; the limit is Shepp and Lloyd 1966, cited unread), and one fixed point.

A single round is not a random permutation; the rest of the paper asks what its group is and how many rounds randomise a difference.

## Blocks and the five laws

The group `G` of a schedule contains the translations, so it is transitive, and the question is whether it is primitive. Write `D_u f(x) = f(x + u) + f(x)` for the derivative of a map `f` of `V` along `u`.

**Lemma 4.1 (block subspaces).** Let `G` be a permutation group of `V` that contains `T`. Every block system of `G` is the partition of `V` into the cosets of a subspace `U`, and `G` keeps the cosets of `U` exactly when `D_u h(x)` lies in `U` for every `u` in `U`, every `x` and every generator `h` of `G` outside `T`. So `G` is imprimitive exactly when some subspace `U`, `0 != U != V`, has this property; such a `U` is called a block.

**Proof.** Let `Delta` be the block through `0` of a block system. For `u` in `Delta` the translation `t_u` maps `0` to `u`, so `t_u(Delta)` meets `Delta` and equals it: `u + Delta = Delta`. So `Delta` is closed under addition, a subspace `U`, and the translates `c + U`, which cover `V`, are blocks of the same system. A permutation `g` maps every coset of `U` onto a coset exactly when `g(x + u) + g(x)` lies in `U` for all `x` and all `u` in `U`. Translations keep every coset partition, and a partition kept by every generator of a finite group is kept by the group. □

This is the subspace fact of Caranti, Dalla Volta and Sala 2009, Section 2.1. For a design round the derivative has one shape, which every later section uses.

**Lemma 4.2 (the derivative identity).** For the stampless round `h = L F`,

```
D_u h(x) = L (u + D_u m(x)) ,
```

and `D_u m` depends on `u` only through `u_B`. If `U` is a block and `u` lies in `U`, then `L D_c D_u m(z)` lies in `U` for all `c` and `z`, and so does every higher derivative of `D_u m` taken along at least one more direction. Blocks are closed under intersection, so every `v != 0` lies in a smallest subspace `U(v)` with the block property, the closure of `v` under sums and derivatives of the generators, and `G` is primitive exactly when `U(v) = V` for every `v != 0`.

**Proof.** `F(x + u) + F(x) = u + m(x + u) + m(x)` and `L` is linear; `m` reads only `B`. For the second claim, `D_u h(z) + D_u h(z + c) = L D_c D_u m(z)` is a sum of two vectors of `U`; repeat for more directions. If `U_1` and `U_2` have the block property so does `U_1 ∩ U_2`, and a proper block contains `U(v)` for each of its nonzero `v`. □

The round census computes `U(v)` for every `v != 0` of every cell, first by random derivatives, which can only prove `U(v) = V`, then exhaustively over every `x` and a basis of the subspace; a proper closure is verified again as a block. A primitive verdict is therefore a proof, not a sample.

**Theorem 4.3 (the five laws).** Let `h = L F` be a single round with spread `sigma`, and `G = <T, h>`. Under each of the following, `G` is not the alternating group `A_M`. Laws I to IV give a block; law V a product action.

- **I, a read-closed set.** A nonempty proper union `O` of `sigma`-orbits contains every written cell whose mess depends on a cell of `O`. Then `V_O` is a block. With the identity spread `O = A` always qualifies.
- **II, the mess module.** Group the live written cells into classes with equal functions `f_a`, and let `E` be the span of the indicator grids of the classes. If the smallest `L`-invariant subspace `X` containing `E` is proper, it is a block when `E != 0`; when `E = 0` the mess is constant and the line of the all-ones grid `1` is a block.
- **III, the complement.** If `g(t) = g(m_a - t)` for every written cell `a` and every `0 <= t <= m_a`, the line of `1` is a block.
- **IV, a kept parity.** Let `P_1, ..., P_r` partition the cells, some part having two cells or more, and `pi(x)` be the vector of parities `|x ∩ P_i| mod 2`. Then `ker pi` is a block exactly when `pi(h(x))` depends only on `pi(x)`.
- **V, a product.** If the cells split into `k >= 2` cosets `C_1, ..., C_k` of a subgroup of the torus, `sigma` permutes the cosets, and every written cell reads only inside its own coset, then `G` lies in the wreath product `Sym(2^(N/k)) wr S_k` in its product action, of order less than `M!/2`.

**Proof.** In each of I to IV the subspace is proper and nonzero, and by Lemma 4.1 it suffices to check `D_u h(x)` for `u` in it, which Lemma 4.2 writes as `L(u + D_u m(x))`.

I. For `u` in `V_O`, `D_u m(x)` is supported on the written cells whose mess depends on a cell of `supp u`, which lie in `O`; so `u + D_u m(x)` lies in `V_O`, and `L V_O = V_(sigma(O)) = V_O`. With `sigma = id` every cell is an orbit, and no written cell's mess depends on a written cell, so `O = A` qualifies.

II. Cells of one class have the same `f_a`, so `m(x)` is a constant plus a combination of the class indicators with coefficients depending on `x`, and every `D_u m(x)` lies in `E`. For `u` in `X`, `u + D_u m(x)` lies in `X` and `L X = X`. If `E = 0` the mess is constant, `D_u h(x) = L u`, and `L 1 = 1`.

III. `D_1 f_a(x) = g(t) + g(m_a - t) = 0` with `t = |x ∩ N(a)|`, a dead cell contributing nothing, so `D_1 h(x) = L 1 = 1`.

IV. The cosets of `ker pi` are the fibres of `pi`; translations map fibres to fibres, and `h` does exactly when `pi(h(x))` is a function of `pi(x)`. `pi` is onto `F_2^r`, so `ker pi` has codimension `r >= 1` and, a part having two cells, dimension at least `1`.

V. Write `V` as the product of the `k` factors `V_(C_i)`, each `F_2^(N/k)`. Translations act factorwise, and so does `F`, since the mess of a written cell of `C_i` reads only `C_i`; `L` carries `V_(C_i)` onto `V_(sigma(C_i))` and so permutes the factors. Hence `G` lies in the wreath product, of order `(q!)^k k!` with `q = 2^(N/k)`. The partitions of `qk` points into `k` blocks of `q` number `(qk)!/((q!)^k k!) >= 3` for `q, k >= 2`, the block of one point alone having `C(qk - 1, q - 1) >= 3` choices, and `qk <= q^k = M`, so `(q!)^k k! <= (qk)!/3 < M!/2`. □

Laws II and III are the design forms of two obstructions familiar for any round function: a mess whose derivatives live in a proper invariant space, and a linear structure of the rule. Law I is the obstruction of geometry, and Section 5 isolates its core. Law IV is the only one that needs a partition of the cells, and the catalogs need it only at `4 x 4`. A product action can be primitive, and six `3 x 3` cells and three `4 x 4` cells are; law V is what keeps them from the alternating group.

## The trap inside the written side

Law I asks for a set of cells that the mess never reads out of. The simplest such set lies inside the written side, where the mess reads nothing at all, and there the law needs no hypothesis on the mess.

**Theorem 5.1 (the trap).** On the `n x n` torus for every `n >= 3`, let `h = L F` be a round with split `A` and spread `sigma`, under any neighbourhood and any Source. A nonzero subspace `X` of `V_A` is a block of `G = <T, h>` exactly when `L X = X`. The `L`-invariant subspaces of `V_A` are the `L`-invariant subspaces of `V_O`, where `O` is the union of the `sigma`-orbits contained in `A`; so `V_O` is the largest block inside `V_A`, and one exists exactly when `A` contains a whole orbit. The bits outside `O` after any word of stamped rounds are then a function of the bits outside `O` before it. For a schedule, every nonzero subspace that lies in every written side and is kept by every spread is a block.

**Proof.** For `w` in `V_A`, `w_B = 0`, so `D_w m = 0` by Lemma 4.2 and `D_w h(x) = L w` for every `x`. The block condition of Lemma 4.1 for `X` is therefore `L X ⊆ X`, which is `L X = X` since `L` is injective, and `X` is proper since `V_A` is. If `X` in `V_A` is `L`-invariant and `w` lies in `X`, then `L^k w` lies in `V_A` for every `k`, so `supp w` lies in `sigma^(-k)(A)` for every `k`, that is in `O`; and `V_O` is `L`-invariant. For the trapdoor, `B` lies outside `O`, so `m(x)` depends only on bits outside `O`, and `sigma` maps the complement of `O` onto itself; so the bits of `h(x)` outside `O` are a function of the bits of `x` outside `O`, and a stamp adds a constant to them. For a schedule the same computation holds round by round. □

The trap is law I in the case where the closed set lies inside `A`, and its proof never looks at the mess: no Source, no neighbourhood and no number of rounds repairs a split that contains a spread orbit. The quotient by `V_O` is the same machine on the cells outside `O`, with `A` minus `O` written.

**Theorem 5.2 (the count of trapped splits).** Let the spread have orbits `o` on the `n x n` torus. The proper splits with a trap number `2^N - 1 - prod over o of (2^|o| - 1)`, and the trapped masks make up the fraction `1 - prod over o of (1 - 2^(-|o|))` of all `2^N` masks. The six spreads have these orbits:

| spread | orbits | trapped fraction of all masks |
|---|---|---|
| `id` | `N` fixed cells | `1 - 2^(-N)` |
| `shift`, `drop`, `slide` | `n` orbits of `n` cells: the rows, the columns, the diagonals | `1 - (1 - 2^(-n))^n` |
| `turn`, `n` even | `n^2/4` orbits of `4` | `1 - (15/16)^(n^2/4)` |
| `turn`, `n` odd | the centre and `(n^2 - 1)/4` orbits of `4` | `1 - (15/16)^((n^2 - 1)/4) / 2` |
| `flip` | the `n` diagonal cells and `n(n - 1)/2` pairs | `1 - 2^(-n) (3/4)^(n(n - 1)/2)` |

**Proof.** A mask contains no whole orbit when it misses a cell of each, `2^|o| - 1` choices per orbit; the empty mask is among these, and the full mask is the one trapped mask that is not a split. For the orbits: the translations are fixed-point free of order `n` on each row, column or diagonal; `turn` has order `4`, and `turn^2(i, j) = (n - 1 - i, n - 1 - j)` fixes a cell exactly when `2i = 2j = n - 1` in `Z_n`, never for even `n` and only at the centre for odd `n`, so every other orbit has four cells; `flip` fixes the diagonal and swaps the rest in pairs. □

The schedule study counts the trapped splits by brute force over every mask at `n = 3, 4, 5` and finds the formula: the translations trap `168` of the `510` proper splits at `3 x 3`, `14910` of `65534` at `4 x 4` and `4925280` of `33554430` at `5 x 5`; `turn` traps `286`, `14910` and `22163806`; `flip` `484`, `64806` and `33495382`; the identity traps all of them. It also computes the closure `U(e_p)` for a cell `p` in every orbit of every `3 x 3` split, under every spread, neighbourhood and nonlinear Source, `123930` closures, and finds every orbit inside `A` to be exactly its own block and no other unit closure to stay inside `V_A`, with no fault. Generator: `lab/rs/schedules`, verb `trap`.

So the spreads split in two as `n` grows. A translation traps a vanishing fraction of the masks, about `n 2^(-n)` and `0.000244` at `n = 16`, because its orbits are whole rows or diagonals. The turn and the flip trap almost every mask, `0.983925` and `1 - 2^(-16) (3/4)^120`, about `1 - 1.6e-20`, at `n = 16`, because their orbits have at most four cells and a random mask contains one with probability tending to `1`. A design round with the turn or the flip is imprimitive for almost every split however strong its mess. A pair has no trap at all, since `V_A` and `V_B` meet only in `0`: the first reason to write both sides.

## Primitivity on every torus

The block census of Lemma 4.2 ends at `4 x 4`: the `5 x 5` torus has `2^25` grids and as many vectors to close. This section reads primitivity off the shape of a schedule instead, for every `n`. Fix a schedule `h_1, ..., h_k`, round `j` writing `A_j` from its complement with mess `m_j`, rule `g_j` and spread `sigma_j`.

**Definition 6.1 (live reads and five conditions).** A cell is live-read in round `j` when some live written cell of round `j` reads it, and inert in round `j` otherwise. A symmetric function `t -> g(t)` of `m` variables is affine when `g(t) = g(t + 2)` for `0 <= t <= m - 2`, that is a constant, the parity or its complement, and self-complementary when `g(t) = g(m - t)` for every `t`. Write `Delta g(t) = g(t) + g(t + 1)`. For a live-read `p` let `S_j(p)` be the span of the second differences `D_c D_(e_p) m_j(z)` over all `c` and `z`, a subspace of the grids supported on the live readers of `p`; for an inert `p`, `S_j(p) = 0`.

- **R, connected reads.** The digraph on the cells with an edge `p -> sigma_j(p)` for every `j` and an edge `p -> sigma_j(a)` whenever a live `a` reads `p` in round `j` is strongly connected.
- **Sp, spanning second differences.** For every `j` and every `p` live-read in round `j`, `S_j(p)` is the whole space of grids on the live readers of `p`.
- **W, no affine direction.** Every live window rule, `g_j` read on the `m_a` cells of a live `a`, is neither affine nor self-complementary.
- **Iso, isolation.** Every cell `q` live-read in a round `j` has a live reader `a` in that round and a nonempty set `Q` of cells of `N(a)` other than `q` such that `a` is the only live written cell of round `j` that reads every cell of `Q`, and `Delta^|Q| g_j`, read on the `m_a - |Q|` cells of `N(a)` outside `Q`, is neither affine nor self-complementary.
- **Gamma, the second-difference graph.** The digraph `Gamma` with an edge `p -> sigma_j(a)` whenever `e_a` lies in `S_j(p)`, and an edge `p -> sigma_j(p)` whenever `p` is inert in round `j` or `D_(e_p) m_j(0)` lies in `S_j(p)`, is strongly connected.

Under Sp, `D_(e_p) m_j(0)` lies in `S_j(p)` and every reads edge is an edge of `Gamma`, so R and Sp give Gamma.

**Lemma 6.2 (linear structures of a symmetric rule).** Let `f(x) = g(|x|)` be a symmetric Boolean function of `m >= 2` variables that is not affine. If `D_v f` is constant for some `v != 0`, then `v` is the all-ones vector, and the all-ones vector has this property exactly when `g(t) + g(m - t)` does not depend on `t`. In particular, if `f` is neither affine nor self-complementary, `D_v f` is not identically zero for any `v != 0`.

**Proof.** Suppose `D_v f = c` with `v` neither `0` nor all ones. Take `i` with `v_i = 1`, `j` with `v_j = 0`, and `tau` the swap of the two coordinates. Since `f` is symmetric, `D_(tau v) f(x) = D_v f(tau x) = c`, so `D_(v + tau v) f = c + c = 0`, and `v + tau v = e_i + e_j`. By symmetry every `e_k + e_l` then has a zero derivative; these span the even-weight vectors, so `f(x)` depends only on the parity of `|x|` and `f` is affine, a contradiction. For the all-ones vector, `D_1 f(x) = g(|x|) + g(m - |x|)`, constant exactly as stated, and identically zero exactly when `f` is self-complementary. For `m <= 1` every function is affine, so the last sentence holds for every `m`. □

The schedule study lists the linear structures of every symmetric function of `m = 2, ..., 10` variables by brute force and finds no other: at `m = 10`, `2044` functions are not affine and `60` of them have the all-ones structure. Generator: `lab/rs/schedules`, verb `lemma`.

**Lemma 6.3 (unit propagation).** If `Gamma` is strongly connected, the only block containing a unit vector is `V`.

**Proof.** Let `e_p` lie in a block `U`. If `p` is inert in round `j`, no live cell reads `p`, `D_(e_p) m_j = 0` and `D_(e_p) h_j = L_j e_p = e_(sigma_j(p))`, which lies in `U`. Otherwise, by Lemma 4.2, `L_j D_c D_(e_p) m_j(z)` lies in `U` for all `c` and `z`, so `L_j S_j(p)` lies in `U`, and `e_(sigma_j(a))` lies in `U` whenever `e_a` lies in `S_j(p)`. If moreover `D_(e_p) m_j(0)` lies in `S_j(p)`, then `L_j e_p = L_j(e_p + D_(e_p) m_j(0)) + L_j D_(e_p) m_j(0)` is a sum of `D_(e_p) h_j(0)` and a vector of `L_j S_j(p)`, both in `U`. These are the edges of `Gamma`, so `U` contains `e_r` for every `r` reachable from `p`, which is every cell. □

**Proposition 6.4 (condition R is necessary).** If the reads digraph is not strongly connected, the schedule has a block.

**Proof.** Some nonempty proper set `O` of cells has no edge leaving it, for instance the cells reachable from a cell that does not reach all. For `u` in `V_O`, `D_u m_j(x)` is supported on the live readers of cells of `O`, so `L_j(u + D_u m_j(x))` is supported on `sigma_j(O)` and on the `sigma_j`-images of those readers, all in `O` since they are heads of edges leaving `O`. So `V_O` is a block. □

This is law I for any schedule. The converse direction needs more than R, and the isolation condition is what it needs.

**Theorem 6.5 (the isolation theorem).** For any schedule of design rounds on the `n x n` torus, any `n >= 3`, any spreads and any Sources: if `Gamma` is strongly connected, some cell is live-read in some round, and condition Iso holds, then the group of the rounds and every stamp is primitive.

**Proof.** Let `U` be a nonzero block.

Step 1, a read vector. Take `u != 0` in `U`. If every cell of `supp u` is inert in every round, then `D_u m_j = 0` and `D_u h_j = L_j u`, so every `L_j u` lies in `U`; repeat on the images. Either some vector so obtained has in its support a cell live-read in some round, or the union `O` of the supports of all of them is a nonempty set kept by every `sigma_j` and inert in every round. From a cell inert in round `j` the only edge of `Gamma` is `p -> sigma_j(p)`, since `S_j(p) = 0`, so no edge of `Gamma` leaves such an `O`; strong connectivity makes `O` every cell, and then no cell is live-read anywhere, which is excluded. So `U` contains a vector `w` with `w_q = 1` for a cell `q` live-read in some round `j`.

Step 2, a unit vector. Take `a` and `Q` from condition Iso for `q` and `j`, `k = |Q| >= 1`, and let `D_Q` be the derivative along every `e_r` with `r` in `Q`. By Lemma 4.2, `L_j D_Q D_w m_j(z)` lies in `U` for every `z`. Its coordinate at a live written cell `a' != a` vanishes, since `a'` misses a cell `r` of `Q`, `f_(a')` does not depend on `x_r`, and derivatives commute; a dead cell contributes a constant, whose derivatives vanish. At `a`, for `z` with `t` set bits on the cells of `N(a)` outside `Q`, the `2^k` points `z + e_T`, `T ⊆ Q`, have the same counts as `z` with its `Q`-part replaced by every pattern, so

```
D_Q f_a(z) = sum over i of C(k, i) g(t + i) = Delta^k g(t)   (mod 2) ,
```

a symmetric function `f'` of the `m_a - k` cells of `N(a)` outside `Q`. Hence `D_Q D_w f_a = D_w f' = D_(w') f'` with `w'` the part of `w` on those cells, which is nonzero since it contains `q`. By condition Iso and Lemma 6.2, `D_(w') f'` is not identically zero; choose `z` with value `1`. Then `D_Q D_w m_j(z) = e_a`, and `e_(sigma_j(a))` lies in `U`.

Step 3. By Lemma 6.3, `U = V`. So there is no proper block, and by Lemma 4.1 the group is primitive. □

The two steps are the two sources of blocks. A block with no read vector is the trap of Section 5, generalised to cells inert in every round, and `Gamma` kills it; a block with a read vector is killed in Step 2, but only through isolation. A read vector of large weight has second differences on several written cells at once, and without a set `Q` that singles one of them out nothing forces a unit vector into the block. Whether isolation can be dropped is not settled here; the second open problem gives the context. For a pair the gap has a precise shape.

**Proposition 6.6 (split blocks of a pair).** For a pair whose mess derivatives span each written side, every block `U` lies in a block `X + Y` with `X` in `V_A`, `Y` in `V_B`, the derivatives of `m_A` along `Y` in `X` and those of `m_B` along `X` in `Y`.

**Proof.** For `u` in `U`, `D_u F_A(x) = u + D_(u_B) m_A(x)` and `u` lie in `U`, so `D_(u_B) m_A(x)` lies in `U ∩ V_A`. Let `X` and `Y` be the projections of `U` to `V_A` and `V_B`; then `U ∩ V_A ⊆ X`, so for `v` in `X + Y`, `D_v F_A(x) = v + D_(v_B) m_A(x)` lies in `X + Y`, and the same holds for `F_B`. So `X + Y` has the block property. If it were `V`, then `Y = V_B`, `U` would contain every derivative of `m_A`, which span `V_A`, and likewise `V_B`, so `U = V`. □

Read dually, a proper block of a pair is a set of parities of the written side that the mess maps to functions of a set of parities of the read side, and back.

**Theorem 6.7 (every even n).** On the `n x n` torus, for every even `n >= 6`, the following pairs generate a primitive group with every stamp: the checkerboard pair, `A` the cells with `i + j` even, with the carpet or the net neighbourhood and the `binary` or `fibonacci` Source; and the stripes pair, `A` the cells with `j` even, with the carpet neighbourhood and the `primes` or `binary` Source.

**Proof.** The hypotheses of Theorem 6.5 are checked. Locality comes first. Every check below is an incidence among cells whose offsets from one cell have coordinates in `[-2, 2]`: which of them are equal, and which reads which. Two such incidences can differ between the torus and the plane only when a nonzero vector with coordinates in `[-4, 4]` vanishes modulo `n`, which needs `n <= 4`. So for `n >= 5` every check reads the same as on the plane, and for even `n` both colourings are well defined on the torus.

The checkerboard. Both neighbourhoods read, from a written cell, its four orthogonal neighbours, which are the other colour, so `m = 4` everywhere; all four Sources are non-constant on `0, ..., 4`, so every cell is live and every cell is live-read in the round that writes the other colour. The reads digraph is the plus graph between the colours, connected and symmetric. For isolation, let `q` be live-read, `a = q - e_2` one of its readers, and `Q = {a + e_1, a - e_1}`: a cell reading both is orthogonally adjacent to both, which on the plane is `a` alone; `q` is not in `Q`. On the `2` remaining variables `Delta^2 g(t) = g(t) + g(t + 2)`, `t = 0, 1, 2`, which is affine, and self-complementary, exactly when `g(0) = g(4)`; `binary` has `g(0) = 0`, `g(4) = 1` and `fibonacci` `g(0) = 1`, `g(4) = 0`. For spanning, take a live-read `p` and `c = e_(p + 2e_1)`: the reader `p + e_1` of `p` reads `p + 2e_1`, and no other reader of `p` does, so `D_c D_(e_p) m(z) = Delta^2 g(t) e_(p + e_1)`, `t` the number of set bits among the two other reads of `p + e_1`. `Delta^2 g` on `0, 1, 2` is `1, 1, 0` for `binary` and `0, 0, 1` for `fibonacci`, not identically zero, so `e_(p + e_1)` lies in `S(p)`, and by symmetry so do the other three readers: Sp holds, and with R it gives Gamma.

The stripes. With the carpet a written cell reads the six cells of the two neighbouring columns, `m = 6`, every cell is live and live-read, and the reads digraph joins each cell to its horizontal and diagonal neighbours, connected and symmetric. For a live-read `q = a + e_2` with `a` in a written column, `Q = {a + (-1, -1), a + (1, 1)}` isolates `a`: a cell Moore-adjacent to both has both coordinates within one of `a`'s. `Delta^2 g` on the remaining `4` variables is `1, 1, 1, 0, 0` for `primes` and `1, 1, 0, 0, 1` for `binary`, neither affine nor self-complementary. Spanning is the computation of `S(p)`, a span of vectors on the readers of `p` that depends only on the cells within distance `2` of `p`; by locality it is the same at every even `n >= 6`, and the schedule study computes all five conditions for both families at `n = 6, 8, 10` and finds them met. Generator: `lab/rs/schedules`, verb `family`. □

At `n = 4` the hypotheses fail: `a + 2e_1 = a - 2e_1`, and `Q` has two common readers. The block census of the `4 x 4` pairs, Fact 7.6, finds the `4 x 4` checkerboard alternating for `binary` and `fibonacci` and imprimitive for `primes`, which keeps the parity of the whole torus. `primes` fails isolation on the checkerboard at every `n`: `g(0) = g(4) = 0` makes `Delta^2 g` affine on an opposite pair, a single cell has four readers, an adjacent pair has a second common reader, and three cells leave one variable, on which every function is affine. `fibonacci` on the stripes has `Delta^2 g = 0, 0, 1, 0, 0`, self-complementary, and isolation fails. The figure of the schedule chapter draws the second-difference graph of the checkerboard carpet `binary` pair at `n = 10`.

**Proof.** The conditions are finite and are computed exactly for all `81` cells by the schedule study, verb `five`; Theorem 6.5 applies to the seven. □

**Fact 6.9 (the catalogs against the conditions).** Of the `108` nonlinear `3 x 3` pair cells, the `96` that miss `A_512` all fail R (`62`) or Sp (`34`: `32` with a proper mess module, and `2`, `0x004e` with the net and `0x001b` with the void, both `binary`, keeping a one-dimensional block spanned by a vector that is neither a unit nor all ones); the `12` alternating ones also fail Sp, so Sp is not necessary. ## The alternating group

Primitivity is the hard half. The other half is classical: Jordan's theorem says that a primitive group of degree `M` containing a cycle of prime length `p <= M - 3` contains `A_M`; the statement is read in the encyclopedia entry cited in the References, which cites Isaacs, Finite Group Theory, page 245, and Jordan's 1873 paper is unread. A single long cycle supplies primitivity as well.

**Lemma 7.1 (the long prime).** Let `G` be a transitive group of degree `M` with an element `g` that has a cycle of prime length `p`, `M/2 < p <= M - 3`. Then `G` is primitive and contains `A_M`; if `G` is generated by even permutations, `G = A_M`.

**Proof.** The other cycles of `g` cover `M - p < p` points, so none has length divisible by `p`, and the power of `g` by the product of their lengths is a `p`-cycle `c`, since that product is prime to `p`. Suppose `G` keeps a block system with blocks of size `s`, `2 <= s <= M/2`. The `M/s <= M/2 < p` blocks are permuted by `c`, whose orbits on them have size `1` or `p`; so `c` fixes every block, and its orbit of `p` points lies in a single block, of size `s < p`. This is impossible, so `G` is primitive, and Jordan's theorem gives `A_M <= G`. □

**Lemma 7.2 (the lone prime).** If an element of a primitive group of degree `M` has exactly one cycle whose length is divisible by a prime `p <= M - 3`, and that cycle has length `p`, the group contains `A_M`.

**Proof.** The power of the element by the least common multiple of its other cycle lengths, which is prime to `p`, is a `p`-cycle; apply Jordan's theorem. □

When every round of a design schedule is even, as Theorem 3.1 makes it for every round that writes two cells or more, its group is generated by even permutations and either certificate gives `G = A_M` exactly; in the catalogs only the nine odd `3 x 3` cells fall outside, and they are imprimitive. Lemma 7.2 needs primitivity from elsewhere, the closures of Lemma 4.2 or Theorem 6.5; Lemma 7.1 needs nothing but the cycle.

**Fact 7.3 (the `3 x 3` census).** Over the `4320` cells of the `3 x 3` catalog, the group of a round and every stamp is `A_512` for exactly `147` cells, all nonlinear, each primitive by the closures of Lemma 4.2 and certified by Lemma 7.2, `135` from a lone prime cycle of a stamped round `t_c h` and `12` from a word of `2` to `4` stamped rounds; `6` cells are primitive products; every other nonlinear cell, `3087`, is imprimitive, the first law holding being I for `2901`, II for `183` and III for `3`. The `1080` `odds` cells are affine: `1068` imprimitive and `12` primitive. No cell generates `S_512`: the `9` odd cells are imprimitive. The alternating cells are `49` each under `shift`, `drop` and `slide` and none under `id`, `turn` and `flip`. Generator: `lab/rs/rounds`, verb `three`.

**Fact 7.4 (the `4 x 4` census).** Over the `2856` cells of the `4 x 4` catalog, the group is `A_65536` for exactly `198` cells, `192` certified from a stamped round and `6` from a word, after primitivity by the closures; `3` cells are primitive products; all `714` `odds` cells are imprimitive; and the other `1941` nonlinear cells are imprimitive, the first law holding being I for `1802`, II for `113`, III for `8` and IV for `18`. Each of those `18` keeps the parities of the cosets of a torus subgroup or of the `2 x 2` tiles, `13` of them the parity of the whole torus. No round is odd. The alternating cells are `49` each under `shift` and `drop`, `25` under `slide` and `75` under `turn`, which fixes no cell at `4 x 4`. Generator: `lab/rs/rounds`, verb `four`.

**Fact 7.5 (no law, no miss).** In both catalogs no primitive cell meets law I, II, III or IV, and no primitive product cell and no affine cell shows a lone prime cycle in its `512` stamped rounds and `512` random words; the product law, run on every cell, holds in `491` nonlinear and `28` `odds` cells at `3 x 3` and in `926` and `256` at `4 x 4`; and the `12` primitive `odds` cells at `3 x 3` meet none of the five laws. With Theorem 4.3 the three Facts say: on every nonlinear cell of both catalogs, the group of the round and its stamps is the alternating group exactly when none of the five laws holds. One direction is the theorem; the other is the census, since every imprimitive nonlinear cell meets one of laws I to IV and the primitive ones that are not alternating are the nine products of law V. The affine cells are never alternating, their groups lying in the affine group of `F_2^N`, far smaller than `A_M`, and the twelve primitive ones show that the five laws do not explain affine cells. The equivalence is a census of two catalogs, not a theorem; whether it holds on every torus is Conjecture 10.1.

**Fact 7.6 (the pairs).** Writing a split and then its complement with the identity spread, the `12` splits of `3 x 3` up to symmetry and complement give `A_512` in `12` of the `108` nonlinear cells (`binary` `8`, `primes` `4`) and the `23` words of `4 x 4` give `A_65536` in `10` of the `207` nonlinear cells (`binary` `6`, `fibonacci` `3`, `primes` `1`); every other cell is imprimitive. **Fact 7.7 (`A_(2^25)` at `5 x 5`).** The `5 x 5` pair that writes the columns `0` and `2` and then the other three, with the carpet neighbourhood and the `primes` Source, generates `A_(2^25)` with every stamp. This is the one alternating group of the paper that the block census could not have reached: `2^25` vectors, each closed under the derivatives of a round on `2^25` grids. It rests on Theorem 6.5 or on one cycle, and each route certifies it without the other.

## The difference chain

A generator built from rounds is fed a seed and a counter, so after the group the question is how fast two different inputs stop looking related. In this section the stamp is not the split's design but a fresh uniform element `c_t` of `V` at every round, a round key, and a schedule of `r` rounds maps an input `x` to `E(x) = t_(c_r) h_r ... t_(c_1) h_1 (x)`. For a map `h` of `V` let

```
P_h(a, b) = #{y : D_a h(y) = b} / 2^N ,    a, b != 0 ,
```

the difference table of `h` divided by `2^N`, a matrix on the `2^N - 1` nonzero differences, and let `mu` be the uniform law on them.

**Theorem 8.1 (the difference chain).** Fix inputs `x != x'`, let `x_t` and `x'_t` be their images after `t` rounds and `d_t = x_t + x'_t`. Then `d_1 = D_(x + x') h_1(x)` is a fixed nonzero difference, and every nonzero difference occurs as `d_1` for some pair of inputs. For `t >= 1`, `x_t` is uniform and independent of `(d_1, ..., d_t)`, and conditionally on `(d_1, ..., d_t)` the next difference has law `P_(h_(t+1))(d_t, .)`: the differences form a Markov chain with kernels `P_(h_2), P_(h_3), ...` from the start `d_1`.

**Proof.** `d_1 = h_1(x) + h_1(x')` involves no stamp, since `c_1` is added to both images. Given `x` and a nonzero `b`, the input `x' = h_1^(-1)(h_1(x) + b)` gives `d_1 = b`. For the induction, `x_t = h_t(x_(t-1)) + c_t` with `c_t` uniform and independent of `c_1, ..., c_(t-1)`, while `x_(t-1)` and `d_1, ..., d_t` are functions of the inputs and of `c_1, ..., c_(t-1)` alone, `d_t = D_(d_(t-1)) h_t(x_(t-1))`. So `x_t` is uniform and independent of `(d_1, ..., d_t)`, and `d_(t+1) = D_(d_t) h_(t+1)(x_t)` has, given the history, the law of `D_(d_t) h_(t+1)(y)` with `y` uniform, which is the row `d_t` of `P_(h_(t+1))`. □

This is the Markov-cipher property of Lai, Massey and Murphy 1991, re-proved; the first round only relabels the start, and the stamps begin to work in round `2`.

**Proposition 8.2 (the distance from pairwise independence).** After round `r >= 1` the total-variation distance between the law of `(E(x), E(x'))` and the uniform law on ordered pairs of distinct grids equals `TV(law of d_r, mu)`. So the distance of the worst input pair is

```
d(r) = max over a != 0 of TV(e_a P_(h_2) P_(h_3) ... P_(h_r), mu) ,    d(1) = 1 - 1/(2^N - 1) .
```

**Proof.** The output pair is `(x_r, x_r + d_r)` with `x_r` uniform and independent of `d_r` by Theorem 8.1, and a uniform pair of distinct grids is `(X, X + D)` with `X` uniform and `D` independent and uniform on the nonzero grids. The map `(x, d) -> (x, x + d)` is a bijection, and two product laws with the same uniform first factor are at the distance of their second factors. By Theorem 8.1 the start `d_1` ranges over every nonzero difference. □

**Proposition 8.3 (double stochasticity and the singular-value bound).** Every `P_h` of a bijection `h` is doubly stochastic, so `mu` is stationary for every schedule, and `P_h` is the transpose of `P_(h^(-1))`. For a schedule of period `k` with period product `Q = P_(h_2) ... P_(h_(k+1))`,

```
d(1 + k s) <= (1/2) sqrt(2^N - 1) sigma_2(Q)^s ,
```

`sigma_2(Q)` being the norm of `Q` on the sum-zero vectors, its second singular value; the rate of the curve is `|lambda_2(Q)|`, the largest modulus of an eigenvalue on that space, at most `sigma_2(Q)` and different from it in general, since `Q` need not be normal.

**Proof.** A row sums to `1` by definition. The column of `b` sums to `#{(a, y) : h(y) + h(y + a) = b} / 2^N`, and for each `y` exactly one `a` works since `h` is a bijection, `a = 0` never when `b != 0`. Substituting `z = h(y)` turns `h(y) + h(y + a) = b` into `h^(-1)(z) + h^(-1)(z + b) = a`, which is the transpose. A doubly stochastic matrix maps the sum-zero vectors to themselves, on both sides, and `e_a - mu` is sum-zero with `|e_a - mu|_2 <= 1`; total variation is half the `l_1` norm, at most `sqrt(2^N - 1)/2` times the `l_2` norm, and `e_a Q^s - mu = (e_a - mu) Q^s`. □

For a single round the bound is used with `Q = P^(k*)`, `k*` the least power with `sigma_2(P^(k*)) < 1`; Theorem 8.5 shows why a power is needed.

**Theorem 8.4 (irreducible means 2-transitive).** For a single round `h`, the chain `P_h` is irreducible exactly when `G = <T, h>` is 2-transitive. If it is reducible, a start in a proper closed class `C` keeps `d(r) >= 1 - |C|/(2^N - 1)` for every `r`; in particular a round whose group is imprimitive, or primitive and not 2-transitive, never mixes.

**Proof.** Since `G` contains `T`, a `G`-orbit of ordered pairs of distinct grids is a union of classes `{(y, y + a) : y in V}`, one for each difference `a`; let `D(a)` be the differences in the orbit of `(0, a)`. An edge `a -> b` of the support graph of `P_h` is a `y` with `D_a h(y) = b`: translate `(0, a)` to `(y, y + a)`, apply `h`, and translate by `h(y)` to reach `(0, b)`. Conversely every element of the finite group `G` is a word in `h` and translations, translations keep the difference, and `h` moves it along an edge. So `D(a)` is the set of differences the chain reaches from `a`, and `G` has one orbit on distinct pairs exactly when every difference reaches every other. A start in a closed class `C` keeps its law on `C`, at distance at least `1 - |C|/(2^N - 1)` from `mu`. An imprimitive or a primitive non-2-transitive group has more than one orbit on distinct pairs. □

The direction from the group to the chain is classical for round functions that generate the alternating or the symmetric group, which are 2-transitive: it is credited to Hornauer, Stephan and Wernsdorf 1994, cited unread, and re-proved here.

The ladder, for a single round, has three rungs. First, imprimitive implies `sigma_2(P^j) = |lambda_2(P)| = 1` for every `j`: a block `U` makes `C = U \ {0}` a closed class; the rows of `C` put all their mass `|C|` into `C`, which is the whole mass its columns receive, so no row outside `C` enters `C`, and the sum-zero vector `|C^c| 1_C - |C| 1_(C^c)` is fixed by `P`. For a design round `sigma_2(P) = 1` holds in any case, by Theorem 8.5. Second, 2-transitive is equivalent to irreducible, by Theorem 8.4. Third, the chain mixes, `d(r) -> 0`, exactly when `|lambda_2(P)| < 1`, and exactly when it is irreducible and aperiodic, by the convergence theorem for finite Markov chains; one self-loop certifies aperiodicity, a difference `a` with `D_a h(y) = a` for some `y`, and so do two cycles of coprime lengths in the support graph. For a schedule of period `k` the third rung holds with the period product `Q` in place of `P`. The rate of the curve is `|lambda_2(Q)|`, the limit of `sigma_2(Q^s)^(1/s)` as `s` grows, and never the second singular value of one round.

**Theorem 8.5 (one round has second singular value 1).** Every design round that writes two cells or more has `sigma_2(P) = 1`. More generally `sigma_2(P^j) = 1` once two cells `p` have `p, sigma(p), ..., sigma^(j-1)(p)` all in `A`, and `d(j + 1) = 1 - 1/(2^N - 1)` once one such cell exists; in particular `d(2) = 1 - 1/(2^N - 1)` for every round.

**Proof.** A difference `a` with `a_B = 0` passes deterministically: by Lemma 4.2, `D_a h(y) = L a` for every `y`, so its row is the point mass at `L a`. Two such starts `a != a'`, which exist once `|A| >= 2`, give the sum-zero vector `e_a - e_(a')`, mapped to `e_(La) - e_(La')` of the same norm, so `sigma_2(P) >= 1`, and `sigma_2 <= 1` for a doubly stochastic matrix. If the support of `a` lies in the cells whose first `j` spread images lie in `A`, the same holds for `j` steps, `e_a P^j` is a point mass, and two such cells give `j`-step deterministic differences `e_p` and `e_q`. One such cell `p` gives `e_p P^j = e_(sigma^j(p))`, a point mass at distance `1 - 1/(2^N - 1)` from `mu`, so `d(j + 1) >= 1 - 1/(2^N - 1)` by Proposition 8.2; and no law `nu` on the `2^N - 1` nonzero differences is farther, since some `b` has `nu(b) >= 1/(2^N - 1) = mu(b)`, so `TV(nu, mu) = 1 - sum_b min(nu(b), mu(b)) <= 1 - 1/(2^N - 1)`. Every round writes a cell, which is the case `j = 1`. □

This is the classical probability-one one-round characteristic of a Feistel network, restated for the chain. So a spectral gap after one round is never available for a design round: the light differences that live on the written side pass through the Feistel step untouched. The guess that `sigma_2(P) = 1` marks the imprimitive cells is therefore false: it holds on every cell that writes two cells, alternating or not, and on all `147` alternating `3 x 3` cells (Fact 8.8); only a power of the round separates the cells that mix.

**Theorem 8.6 (affine rounds never mix).** If the mess is linear or constant, the round is affine, `D_a h(y) = K a` for a fixed linear bijection `K`, and `P` is the permutation matrix of `K` on the nonzero vectors: the difference is deterministic forever and `d(r) = 1 - 1/(2^N - 1)` for every `r`. The chain is irreducible, and the group 2-transitive, exactly when `K` permutes the `2^N - 1` nonzero vectors in one cycle, a Singer cycle.

**Proof.** If `m` is affine, `h(y) = L(y + m(y))` is affine with linear part `K = L(I + m - m(0))`, so `D_a h(y) = K a`, independent of `y`. A permutation matrix is irreducible exactly when its permutation is one cycle, and Theorem 8.4 converts that into 2-transitivity. □

That an affine round never mixes is folklore: its difference table is a permutation. The twelve cells of Fact 8.7 make it concrete: 2-transitive, yet at distance `1 - 1/(M - 1)` forever.

**Fact 8.7 (the `3 x 3` chains).** Of the `4320` `3 x 3` cells, exactly `147` nonlinear cells and `12` affine cells have an irreducible chain. The `147` are the alternating cells of Fact 7.3, a second certificate of their 2-transitivity by one search of the support graph, sharing nothing with Jordan's theorem; the `12` are its `12` primitive affine cells, `odds` with the net under `shift` and under `drop` and with the void under `slide`, four each, which by Theorem 8.6 are 2-transitive and never mix. Every reducible nonlinear cell has a closed class of at most `255` differences and keeps `d(r) >= 1 - 255/511 = 0.500978` for every `r`. Every row of every kernel is compared with the direct table `h(y) + h(y + a)`, and `0` rows differ; an independent rebuild in numpy from the direct tables finds the same `147` and `12`. Generators: `lab/rs/mixing`, verb `three`; `lab/rs/mixing/dense.py`.

The kernel is built without the dense table: a row depends on `a` only through `a_B`, and for each `a_B` the law of `m(y) + m(y + a_B)` over the read grids is exact from two Walsh-Hadamard transforms, the autocorrelations of the component functions `u . m`.

**Fact 8.8 (the curves of the `147` alternating `3 x 3` cells).** From every start, the rounds to `d < 1/4` run from `7` to `29`, median `10`, and to `d < 0.01` from `13` to `76`, median `22`, equal cell by cell between the sparse route and the dense one; `sigma_2(P) = sigma_2(P^2) = 1` on every cell, `k*` is `3` for `135` cells and `5` or `6` for twelve `binary` cells, `sigma_2(P^(k*))` runs from `0.449163` to `0.829949`, and `|lambda_2(P)|`, the growth rate of seeded power iteration, from `0.586` to `0.932`, median `0.748`, the range rounded outward, within `7e-4` of the dense eigenvalues on the `21` cells the dense route checks; the slowest cell is `0x0054` with the net, `fibonacci` and `shift`, with dense `|lambda_2| = 0.931500` and `76` rounds to `0.01`. Generators: `lab/rs/mixing`, verb `three`; `lab/rs/mixing/dense.py`.

**Fact 8.9 (the `4 x 4` chains).** Of the `2142` nonlinear `4 x 4` cells, `1802` are reducible by law I without building the chain, `142` more are reducible by search, and exactly `198` are irreducible, the alternating cells of Fact 7.4; their kernels have from `0.56` to `4.0` million nonzero entries, against `4.3` billion for a dense table. On `23` alternating cells, every ninth in catalog order and the figure cell, each with `32` seeded kernel rows checked against the direct table, the distance taken as the worst of `18` starts, the `16` single cells, the whole fill and the whole void, and so a lower bound for `d(r)`: the rounds to `1/4` run from `8` to `61`, median `11`, and to `0.01` from `11` to `106`, with one cell past `120`; `k*` is `3` or `4`; `sigma_2(P^(k*))` runs from `0.2374` to `0.8202`; and `|lambda_2(P)|` from `0.4737` to `0.9512`, median `0.7252`, the range rounded outward, each the largest-modulus Ritz value of restarted Arnoldi on the sum-zero space at residual `||P x - lambda x|| <= 1e-10`, a second seed within `1e-12`. The figure cell `0x135f` with the carpet, `primes` and `slide` has `sigma_2(P^4) = 0.369446`, `|lambda_2| = 0.6518`, and needs `11` rounds to `1/4` and `19` to `0.01`. Generator: `lab/rs/mixing`, verb `four`.

Hidden sums. A chain that mixes makes its group 2-transitive (Theorem 8.4), and a 2-transitive group of degree `M = 2^N` that contains the translations falls into few cases. By Li's list, Theorem 1.1 of Li 2003, cited unread and read as Section 4 of Caranti, Dalla Volta and Sala 2009b states it, a primitive group of degree `2^N` with a regular abelian subgroup is affine, or a product `(S_1 x ... x S_r).O.P` in product action with `2^N = m^r` and `r > 1`, or almost simple, and in the last case, through Guralnick 1983 as that section recalls it, the alternating or the symmetric group, or `PSL(2, 2^N - 1)` with `N` prime. A group in product action keeps the number of coordinates in which two points of the product differ, so it is not 2-transitive; `N = n^2` is not prime; and a round that writes two cells is even (Theorem 3.1). So a mixing design round that writes two cells or more, on any torus, generates `A_M` with its stamps, or a group affine for a group law `∘` on the grids, other than the sum, for which the translations are affine maps: the hidden-sum trapdoor of Calderini, Civino and Sala 2021, which makes every word of stamped rounds affine for `∘`. The sum itself is excluded, since a round affine for it never mixes (Theorem 8.6). The census has no mixing cell that is not alternating: its irreducible cells are the `147` and `198` alternating ones and the twelve affine `3 x 3` cells, which never mix, and all `714` `odds` cells at `4 x 4` are imprimitive (Facts 7.4, 8.7 and 8.9). So on the two catalogs mixing implies alternating, and no cell of either is a mixing hidden-sum trapdoor. Whether a mixing design round affine for a hidden sum exists on a larger torus is open.

The median cell needs about as many rounds on `16` bits as on `9`. That cannot last, and the reason is geometric.

**Theorem 8.10 (the light cone).** Suppose every round of a schedule reads within `l_infinity` distance `m`, and its spread maps every ball of radius `t` into a ball of radius `t + s`; let `c = m + s`. Then

```
d(r) >= 1 - (2^(w^2) - 1) / (2^(n^2) - 1) ,    w = min(n, 2 c (r - 1) + 1) ,
```

so `d(r) > 1 - 2^(1 - 2n)` while `2c(r - 1) + 1 <= n - 1`, and a schedule needs at least `1 + (n - 1)/(2c)` rounds to bring `d` to `1/2`. Every spread of the catalog, and every translation by any vector, has `s = 0`, and all three neighbourhoods have `m = 1`, so `c = 1`: at side `n = 2^nu` a local schedule needs `Omega(2^nu)` rounds.

**Proof.** `D_a m(y)` vanishes outside the written cells that read a cell of `supp a`, all within distance `m` of it, so if `supp a` lies in a ball of radius `rho` then `a + D_a m(y)` lies in the ball of radius `rho + m` about the same centre, and the spread carries it into a ball of radius `rho + c`, whose centre depends on the old centre and not on `y`. From the start `e_p`, after `r - 1` steps every reachable difference lies in one fixed ball of radius `c(r - 1)`, which has at most `w^2` cells, and a law supported on the `2^(w^2) - 1` nonzero grids of that ball is at distance at least `1 - (2^(w^2) - 1)/(2^(n^2) - 1)` from `mu`. For `w <= n - 1` the ratio is below `2^((n-1)^2 - n^2) = 2^(1 - 2n)`. The translations are isometries of the `l_infinity` metric of the torus, and `turn` and `flip` are affine with an orthogonal integer linear part, so they are too. □

Shifting by `2^j` in round `j`, a slide at every scale, is a translation each round and keeps `c = 1`: moving the cone does not widen it. A spread breaks the linear cone only by stretching distances, and then a weaker bound holds for every spread.

**Theorem 8.11 (depth for every spread).** For any schedule whose neighbourhoods have at most `8` offsets, with any spreads, `d(r) > 1/2` whenever `9^(r - 1) < n^2`; so `d(r) <= 1/2` needs `r >= 1 + log_9(n^2)`, which is `Omega(nu)` at side `2^nu`. Moreover, for a single spread, the support reachable from a start, `Z -> sigma(Z ∪ {a in A : a reads a cell of Z})`, never shrinks, and if it reaches a fixed set short of the torus that set is read-closed and spread-invariant, a block of law I.

**Proof.** A cell of `B` is read by at most `8` written cells and a cell of `A` by none, so the reachable support grows at most ninefold per step, and the spread is a bijection. A support of at most `n^2 - 1` cells carries `2^(n^2 - 1) - 1` of the `2^(n^2) - 1` nonzero grids, less than half of `mu`, so the distance exceeds `1/2`. If `Z* = sigma(Z* ∪ Y)` with `Y` the written cells reading `Z*`, then `|Z* ∪ Y| = |Z*|` forces `Y ⊆ Z*` and `sigma(Z*) = Z*`; every written cell whose mess depends on `Z*` lies in `Z*`, which is law I. □

Theorems 8.10 and 8.11 are the standard light-cone and fan-in depth arguments, applied to designs. A cone can also stall without being fixed, moving with the spread: the product cell `0x0054` with the void, `primes` and `shift` never covers the `3 x 3` torus and has no law-I set. What breaks the linear cone is a spread that stretches. On the board split `{i + j even}`, the design of the two-sided Feistel network, every written cell reads its four orthogonal neighbours on the other colour, and `shift` carries the fill onto the void and back.

**Proposition 8.12 (the hyperbolic spread).** The map `(i, j) -> (2i + j + 1, 3i + 2j)` is a bijection of `Z_n x Z_n` for every `n`, and for even `n` it exchanges the two colours of the board; its matrix has eigenvalues `2 +- sqrt 3` and stretches its unstable direction by `3.73` per round.

**Proof.** The matrix `[[2, 1], [3, 2]]` has determinant `1`, so it is invertible modulo every `n`, and `(2i + j + 1) + (3i + 2j) = 5i + 3j + 1` has the parity of `i + j + 1`. □

**Fact 8.13 (cones and depths).** The exact reachable support covers the torus, from every start, in these numbers of rounds after the first, the support map of Theorem 8.11 applied once a round, at `nu = 2, ..., 6`: the board under `shift` in `5, 9, 17, 33, 65`, the board under the hyperbolic spread in `4, 6, 7, 8, 10`, and the level-`nu` carpet `{i AND j = 0}` under `slide` in `8, 15, 30, 61, 123`; the digit rotation `(rot i + 1, rot j + 1)`, `rot` the cyclic shift of the `nu` binary digits, and the cat map `(2i + j + 1, i + j + 1)` are certified never to cover the carpet, a start lying in a closed set. At `n = 4` the exact chains from `18` starts give: the figure cell needs at least `9` rounds to `1/2`, the cone's `1 + 8`, at least `11` to `1/4` and at least `19` to `0.01`; the board with `binary` is irreducible under `shift`, the shear `(i + j + 1, j + 1)` and the hyperbolic spread and needs at least `14` rounds to `0.01` under each, while with `primes`, a quadratic rule on four reads, it is reducible under all three. From `16384` sampled difference runs per schedule, at one fixed seed for each side, `0x6d756c746900 + nu`, the same for the three schedules of a side, started at the cell the cone reaches last, the first round at which the sampled largest bit bias `max over p of |Pr(d_r(p) = 1) - 2^(N-1)/(2^N - 1)|` is below `0.05` is `14, 25, 55` for the carpet under `slide`, `8, 13, 23` for the board under `shift`, and `8, 9, 11` for the board under the hyperbolic spread, at `n = 4, 8, 16`, and below `0.2` it is `10, 18, 45`, then `6, 11, 20`, then `6, 8, 9`. These are one sample per side, not a range over seeds; each bit is a test, so they are sampled estimates of a lower bound on the rounds to `d < 0.05`, with a sampling floor about `0.02`, the printed tails running from `0.007` to `0.018`. Generator: `lab/rs/mixing`, verbs `cone` and `multi`.

The local schedules double their depth with the side, as Theorem 8.10 says they must up to the constant; the hyperbolic one adds one or two rounds per doubling, under twice the counting bound of the board, whose void cells are each read by four written cells, so that a support grows at most fivefold a round: at `nu = 6` its cone covers in `10` rounds after the first, against the counting bound `log_5(n^2) > 5.168`. Conjecture 10.5 says this is the truth. The obstruction to a proof is the decorrelation behind the cone: for a local schedule the chain on the plane is a probabilistic cellular automaton driven by the uniform void bits, and the contraction criteria that descend from Dobrushin 1971 need the total influence of the neighbours on a cell to be below `1`, while here a cell's own old difference passes to its image with influence `1`. And ergodicity can fail outright: the abstract of Gacs 2001 recalls a two-dimensional automaton with every local transition of positive probability that keeps a bit forever. The hyperbolic spread leaves the class of cellular automata altogether.

Two more facts bound what the chain says. Three inputs add nothing visible on the cells measured, and the stamps must be uniform.

**Proposition 8.14 (three inputs).** For three distinct inputs the pair of differences `(d_t, e_t)` from the first is, after round `1`, a fixed ordered pair of distinct nonzero differences, every such pair occurring, and from there a doubly stochastic Markov chain `P_3` on the `(2^N - 1)(2^N - 2)` such pairs, `260610` at `3 x 3`. Its first coordinate lumps to the pairwise chain: every eigenvalue of `P` is one of `P_3`, and `sigma_2(P_3^j) >= sigma_2(P^j)` for every `j`.

**Proof.** The argument of Theorem 8.1 gives the kernel `P_3((d, e), (d', e')) = #{y : D_d h(y) = d', D_e h(y) = e'} / 2^N`, and the count of Proposition 8.3 makes it doubly stochastic. Summing over `e'` gives `P(d, d')`, so with `Pi` the matrix sending a pair to its first coordinate, `P_3 Pi = Pi P`. A right eigenvector `v` of `P` gives the eigenvector `Pi v` of `P_3`, and `|Pi v|^2 = (2^N - 2)|v|^2`, so `|P_3^j Pi v| / |Pi v| = |P^j v| / |v|`, with `Pi v` sum-zero when `v` is. □

**Fact 8.15 (design stamps break the chain).** With stamps drawn uniformly from the three neighbourhood designs `{495, 186, 341}` on the cell `0x004a` with the net, `primes` and `shift`, every path enumerated exactly, the law of `d_2` given `d_1` from the void start `e_0` and a uniform input is at distance `0.1667` from the kernel row, which uniform stamps reproduce exactly; and from a uniform input pair, the law of `d_3` given `(d_1, d_2) = (0x084, 0x101)` is at distance `0.5` from the law of `d_3` given `d_2 = 0x101` alone, so no kernel of any kind describes the difference. Generator: `lab/rs/mixing`, verb `stamps`.

The reason is the proof of Theorem 8.1 read backwards: a design stamp leaves `x_t` correlated with the differences that produced it. And a generator whose stamps come from `k` designs has at most `k^r` stamp sequences, so the output pair of two fixed inputs takes at most `k^r` values; the uniform law on the `2^N (2^N - 1)` ordered pairs of distinct grids puts at most `k^r / (2^N (2^N - 1))` on them, so the distance of Proposition 8.2, taken as the worst over input pairs of the distance of `(E(x), E(x'))` from that law, is `d(r) >= 1 - k^r / (2^N (2^N - 1))` whatever the rounds.

## What is in print

The parity of Feistel-type rounds and the generation of alternating groups by round functions are classical in the study of block ciphers. Coppersmith and Grossman 1975 is the source usually cited for the fact that DES-like components can generate the alternating group on the block space; it is paywalled and cited unread, its statement taken from the account of Babinkostova et al. 2012. Even and Goldreich 1983 is cited unread for the same reason, and its two secondary accounts read here disagree in wording: Babinkostova et al. 2012 say that it shows DES-like functions to lie in the alternating group, Aragona, Caranti and Sala 2015 that the group they generate is the alternating group. Wernsdorf proved that the one-round functions of the DES generate the alternating group, and later that the round functions of Rijndael do; both papers are cited unread, by their titles. Nothing in this paper depends on any of the four. For AES-like ciphers Caranti, Dalla Volta and Sala 2009b, read at source in the abstract and Section 4, derive the alternating group from primitivity through the O'Nan-Scott theorem in Li's form for groups with a regular abelian subgroup, excluding the affine case for every hidden sum and the product case from the structure of the cipher; Conjecture 10.3 asks for that step for design schedules, and the remark after Fact 8.9 uses Li's list, as they state it, for 2-transitive rounds. The parity of maps that flip one bit by a function of the others is classical, the setting of Coppersmith and Grossman 1975 as the secondary accounts describe it; Theorem 3.1 proves the parity half from scratch for design rounds, where the Feistel step is a translation on each fibre of the read side, and the count of Corollary 3.2 is elementary; the generation half is not a theorem here in general, and is established cell by cell, by the censuses of Section 7, and on every even torus only up to primitivity, by Theorem 6.7. Babinkostova et al. 2012 record as open whether the sixteen-round DES generates the alternating group; the design family does not touch that question.

The block criterion of Lemma 4.1 is Section 2.1 of Caranti, Dalla Volta and Sala 2009, read at source: a block system of a group containing the translations of a vector space is the coset partition of a subspace. Their Theorem 4.4 proves primitivity for translation-based ciphers whose bricks are weakly `2^r`-uniform and strongly `r`-anti-invariant under a proper mixing layer. Design rounds are not translation-based in their sense, since the mess is not a layer of parallel bricks, so only the subspace fact is used, and Theorem 6.5 is a criterion of a different shape, local and read off second differences. Laws II and III are the design forms of two obstructions that hold for any round function, an invariant space containing all derivatives and a linear structure. Law I and the trap of Theorem 5.1 are the subspace criterion applied to a coordinate subspace, a lemma on top of it; for long-key Feistel networks Aragona, Calderini and Civino 2020, read at source, prove that only linear partitions propagate, and that the Feistel group is primitive exactly when the group of its round function and the translations is. Law V is the product case of Li's list read off the design. Law IV, the count of Theorem 5.2 and the isolation theorem use the geometry of the torus and the design and are not taken from these sources; Theorem 6.5 is new in scope, a local criterion on every torus, and follows Caranti, Dalla Volta and Sala 2009 in method, a block being a subspace closed under derivatives. The five laws as an equivalence are a census of two catalogs, and on every torus Conjecture 10.1.

Jordan's theorem is used as stated in the literature; Lemma 7.1, a long prime cycle in a transitive group, is an elementary consequence, proved on the page. The mean cycle count of an even permutation in Proposition 3.3 follows from the classical generating function of the unsigned Stirling numbers. The Markov property of the difference under independent uniform round keys, Theorem 8.1, is the Markov-cipher property of Lai, Massey and Murphy 1991; it is cited unread, the publisher page being paywalled, and proved from scratch. The distance of Proposition 8.2 is a known measure. It is half the pairwise decorrelation distance of Vaudenay 2003, read at source, in its `l_infinity`-associated matrix norm, the largest over input pairs of the `l_1` distance of the output pair's law from the perfect cipher's; that paper relates it to differential distinguishers and bounds it for Feistel schemes with independent rounds, through the three-round lemma of Luby and Rackoff 1988, cited unread and read there as its Lemma 19. And it measures the almost pairwise independence that Liu, Tessaro and Vaikuntanathan 2021, read at the abstract, prove for substitution-permutation networks and key-alternating ciphers with independent subkeys, a design generator with uniform stamps being a key-alternating cipher. The direction of Theorem 8.4 from the group to the chain is credited to Hornauer, Stephan and Wernsdorf 1994, who treat round functions generating the alternating or the symmetric group; it is cited unread and re-proved here for 2-transitive groups. Theorem 8.5 is the classical probability-one one-round Feistel characteristic, a difference that vanishes on the read half passing untouched, restated for the chain; Theorem 8.6 is the folklore that an affine map has a deterministic difference table; the last rung of the ladder after Theorem 8.4 is the convergence theorem for finite Markov chains; and Theorems 8.10 and 8.11 are the standard light-cone and fan-in depth arguments applied to designs. The remark on hidden sums rests on Li 2003, cited unread and read as Caranti, Dalla Volta and Sala 2009b state it, and on the trapdoor of Calderini, Civino and Sala 2021, read at source. What is specific to designs is their consequences: the distance curves of the alternating cells, exact at `3 x 3` (Fact 8.8) and lower bounds from `18` starts at `4 x 4` (Fact 8.9); `sigma_2(P) = 1` on every alternating `3 x 3` cell (Fact 8.8), so that only a power of the round separates the cells that mix; the absence of a mixing hidden-sum cell from both catalogs; and the exact cone depths of Fact 8.13, whose design reading is that a fractal split buys no diffusion, the carpet under `slide` doubling its depth with the side. The contraction criteria for probabilistic cellular automata that descend from Dobrushin 1971, and the persistence result recalled in the abstract of Gacs 2001, are cited at their abstracts only, for the obstruction of Section 8, and no proof uses them.

What the paper adds, then, relative to the sources it read and to the unread ones as their accounts describe them: a family of reversible rounds built from designs, in which the obstructions to the alternating group can be read off the split, the neighbourhood and the rule; two exhaustive catalogs in which they are the only obstructions for nonlinear rules, an equivalence proved there only; two theorems that hold on every torus, one breaking a split and one certifying a schedule primitive; an alternating group on `2^25` points certified by one cycle; and, for the difference chain, the distance curves and exact cone depths of design rounds, with the classical arguments of Section 8 read on designs. It proves the alternating group on every torus for no family, and no upper bound on the rounds to pairwise independence that holds on every torus.

## Open problems

Seven things are left, the first four about the group and the last three about the mixing; the seventh is the one the paper closes on.

First, the converse as a theorem. On every nonlinear cell of both catalogs, the group is the alternating group exactly when none of the five laws holds (Facts 7.3 to 7.5). That equivalence is a census of the `3 x 3` and `4 x 4` catalogs and is proved nowhere else.

**Conjecture 10.1 (the five laws).** On the `n x n` torus for every `n >= 3`, a single design round whose mess is not affine generates `A_M` with every stamp exactly when none of the five laws of Theorem 4.3 holds.

One direction is Theorem 4.3, on every torus; the evidence for the other is the census of the two catalogs alone, since the `5 x 5` torus has `2^25` grids and as many vectors to close. A proof has two halves: every block of a round whose mess is not affine comes from laws I to IV, and a primitive group generated by the translations and one design round that meets no law V is alternating. For the second half Li's list leaves two cases to exclude, the affine one for every hidden sum and the product one, which law V is exactly, as Caranti, Dalla Volta and Sala 2009b exclude them for AES-like ciphers.

Second, the three checks for pairs. Whether connected reads, spanning second differences and no affine direction force primitivity is open. As context, and not as a result of this paper, the schedule study meets two `4 x 4` singles, `0x0055` with the net, `primes` and `slide`, and `0x0505` with the net, `primes` and `turn`, that pass all three checks, keep the parities of the cosets of a torus subgroup of order `2` and of order `4`, blocks of codimension `8` and `4` with no unit vector, and fail isolation; they are why the conjecture is restricted. On pairs, and on singles whose spread carries the split onto its complement, the catalogs have no such cell.

**Conjecture 10.2 (pairs and onto masks).** For a split and its complement written in turn, or a single split whose spread carries it onto its complement, conditions R, Sp and W force a primitive group with every stamp.

All `6` such `4 x 4` pairs and all `14` such `4 x 4` onto singles are alternating, and no cell of the catalogs contradicts it. By Proposition 6.6 a counterexample pair would keep a parity system between the two sides with no unit vector in either half. The eight `5 x 5` line pairs that meet R, Sp and W without isolation, Theorem 6.8, are the first place to look, by a long prime for the positive side.

Third, primitive is alternating. Every alternating verdict of the paper is certified cell by cell, by a prime cycle: every primitive nonlinear pair of the `3 x 3` and `4 x 4` catalogs is alternating (Fact 7.6), every primitive nonlinear single there is alternating or a product of law V (Facts 7.3 and 7.4), and the one `5 x 5` pair taken to a cycle is alternating (Fact 7.7).

**Conjecture 10.3 (primitive is alternating).** On the `n x n` torus for every `n >= 3`, a schedule of design rounds, each writing two cells or more with a mess that is not affine, whose group with every stamp is primitive and that has no product structure of law V, a coset partition permuted by every spread with every read inside its part, generates `A_M`.

The evidence is the certificates at `3 x 3`, `4 x 4` and `5 x 5` above, the first two exhaustive over the catalogs and the third a single pair; nothing proves it at any size. With Theorem 6.7 it would make the checkerboard and stripes pairs alternating at every even `n >= 6`; the `4 x 4` checkerboard pair is alternating with `binary` and `fibonacci` (Fact 7.6). A proof needs what Caranti, Dalla Volta and Sala 2009b supply for AES-like ciphers: the affine case of Li's list excluded for every hidden sum, and the product case excluded from the structure. Short of that, a prime cycle in the sense of Lemma 7.1 or 7.2 constructed uniformly in `n` would settle each family.

Fourth, isolation on small tori. Isolation never holds for a connected `3 x 3` or `4 x 4` pair, yet `22` of those pairs are alternating (Facts 6.9 and 7.6). A sharper extraction of a unit vector, from read vectors of weight two, may cover them.

Fifth, three inputs.

**Conjecture 10.4 (three inputs at the rate of two).** For every alternating cell, `sigma_2(P_3^j) = sigma_2(P^j)` for every `j`, so that `3`-wise independence costs no more rounds than pairwise independence.

Proposition 8.14 proves the inequality `>=` and that every eigenvalue of `P` is one of `P_3`; on five `3 x 3` cells Lanczos finds no larger singular value, to six digits, and power iteration no larger rate, within `3e-3`. A Lanczos value is a lower estimate, so the equality is measured, not proved.

Sixth, rounds to random. Fact 3.4 says one round is far from a random permutation and Section 8 measures the rounds to pairwise independence; the number of rounds after which the composite's cycle type matches the null of Proposition 3.3, `H_M` cycles, a longest cycle near `0.6243 M` and one fixed point, is open, and so is how the two clocks compare on one schedule.

Seventh, the depth of a stretching spread. Under every local spread the depth is linear in the side (Theorem 8.10), and under any spread at least logarithmic (Theorem 8.11); the hyperbolic spread of Proposition 8.12 is observed to sit near the lower end.

**Conjecture 10.5 (logarithmic depth).** The board split `{i + j even}` with the `binary` Source and the spread `(2i + j + 1, 3i + 2j)` reaches `d(r) < eps` in `O(nu + log 1/eps)` rounds on the torus of side `2^nu`.

The lower side `Omega(nu)` is Theorem 8.11; nothing here measures the upper side: Fact 8.13 gives lower bounds only, and its falsification test, growth faster than linear in `nu` at `n = 4, 8, 16`, did not trigger. The schedule has period one, so its period product is its kernel `P_nu` at side `2^nu`.

The problem the paper closes on: for this hyperbolic board round on the `2^nu x 2^nu` torus, is the number of rounds to `d(r) < eps` of order `Theta(nu + log 1/eps)`? A yes gives `|lambda_2(P_nu)| <= c` for one `c < 1` and every `nu`, an exponential ergodicity of the difference chain uniform in the side, since `2 d(1 + s)` bounds the norm of `P_nu^s` on the sum-zero vectors in `l_1`. The converse is not available: with `|lambda_2|`, or even `sigma_2`, bounded away from `1`, the bound of Proposition 8.3 still costs a number of rounds linear in `N = 4^nu`, and what is needed beyond the uniform rate is decorrelation at every cell, exponentially fast behind the cone and uniformly in `n`. A spread built from the designs themselves that stretches without trapping an orbit in its fill would be the design-native version; every one tried falls to law I. The secondary question is the group: does the same round generate `A_(2^(n^2))` with every stamp at every even `n = 2^nu`? At `n = 4` its chain is irreducible (Fact 8.13), so its group is 2-transitive and, by Li's list as in the remark after Fact 8.9, `A_65536` or, its mess not being affine, affine for a hidden sum; which of the two is not decided here.

## Reproducibility

Every number of the paper is printed by one of three Rust studies, run from the repository root, single-threaded; each prints only, writes nothing, and counts a fault wherever a check fails. The three rebuild the round each from its definitions and share no code.

- `cargo run --release --manifest-path research/random/lab/rs/rounds/Cargo.toml -- all`, about a minute: the checks of Lemma 2.3, Theorem 3.1 and Corollary 3.2 on every state of all `7176` cells, Fact 3.4, and Facts 7.3 to 7.5; the verbs `three` and `four` run one catalog, `pairs` the first half of Fact 7.6, and `null` the check of Proposition 3.3 over every even permutation of up to `8` points.
- `cargo run --release --manifest-path research/random/lab/rs/schedules/Cargo.toml -- <verb>`: `lemma` for Lemma 6.2, `trap` for Theorem 5.2 in about two seconds, `pairs` and `verbose` for Fact 6.9 and the rest of Fact 7.6 in about ten, `singles` for the singles of Facts 6.9 and 7.6 in about `130`, `family` for Theorem 6.7, `onto` for the evidence of Conjecture 10.2, and `five <seconds>` for Theorem 6.8 and Fact 7.7, about `36` seconds to its long prime.
- `cargo run --release --manifest-path research/random/lab/rs/mixing/Cargo.toml -- <verb>`: `check`, `three`, `four`, `triple 30`, `stamps`, `cone` and `multi` for the Facts of Section 8 and the evidence of Conjectures 10.4 and 10.5, each under a minute; `uv run --project . python -I research/random/lab/rs/mixing/dense.py` rebuilds the `3 x 3` chains in numpy from the direct difference tables, an independent route.

The figure recomputes its own object: `research/random/figures/paper-design-rounds.ts` builds the round of the level-2 carpet cell with the stamp `0x0406`, one element of the group chosen for its long prime cycle, decomposes it into cycles, and raises unless the permutation is even and its longest cycle has prime length in `(M/2, M - 3]`, the hypothesis of Lemma 7.1. The two primes of Fact 7.7 are confirmed by PARI's `isprime`.
