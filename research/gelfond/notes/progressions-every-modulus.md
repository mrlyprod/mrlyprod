## Progressions and every modulus

Off the lattice the twist decays; on it the twist is a character of `n mod b - 1`, so the lattice case is the design in progressions. This section proves the Mobius and prime sums of `S_F` in every class mod `N` for every `N` coprime to `b`, at root-log strength, and combines them with the power saving into the law of `s_b(p) mod q` at every `q`.

- **The theorem in progressions. Proved.** Let `F` keep two consecutive digits and carry the shifted-grid certificate of the unconditional dissection of [mobius](../../mobius/notes/mobius.md), `sum_(a < b^i) abs(hat F_i(s + a/b^i)) <= C_F fill^i b^(i alpha_1)` at every `i` and every real `s`, `alpha_1 < 1/5`, and let `N >= 2` with `gcd(N, b) = 1`. Write `M_F(x; N, r)` and `psi_F(x; N, r)` for the sums of `mu(n)` and `Lambda(n)` over the `n` of `S_F` up to `x` with `n = r mod N`, and `kappa_F = (b/phi(b)) #{f in F : gcd(f, b) = 1}/fill` as on [coprime](../../coprime/notes/coprime.md). Then there are `C, c > 0`, effective and depending on `b`, `F` and `N` alone, with `abs(M_F(x; N, r)) <= C A_F(x) exp(-c sqrt(log x))` at every `r`, and `abs(psi_F(x; N, r) - kappa_F A_F(x)/phi(N)) <= C A_F(x) exp(-c sqrt(log x))` at every `r` with `gcd(r, N) = 1`, while at `gcd(r, N) > 1` only powers of the primes of `N` count, at most `omega(N) (log x)^2`. At one missing digit the certificate holds from `base 584` ([mobius](../../mobius/notes/mobius.md)), so the theorem is Proved there at every `N` coprime to `b`, `N = b - 1` among them; the bullets below are the proof.
- **Orthogonality and the blocks.** `M_F(x; N, r) = N^(-1) sum_(a mod N) e(-a r/N) M_F(x; a/N)` with `M_F(x; theta) = sum_(n in S_F, n <= x) mu(n) e(n theta)`. The block split of [mobius](../../mobius/notes/mobius.md) is unchanged, and on a block `e((P y + t) theta) = e(P y theta) e(t theta)`, so its sum is `e(P y theta) y^(-1) sum_(a' mod y) hat F_k(theta + a'/y) S_P(-a'/y)`, by orthogonality on `Z/y` applied to `t -> 1_(D_k)(t) e(t theta)`, whose transform at `a'/y` is `hat F_k(theta + a'/y)`. `S_P` is the untwisted block sum, so the Dirichlet data `l/d`, `h` of `a'/y`, the cut into A, B and C, and the minor-arc bounds on `S_P` are those of the dissection verbatim; only the transform is translated by `theta`.
- **Regions A and B do not change.** Region A reads the certificate at the shift `theta` in place of `0`. The hybrid `l^1` bound reads its top factor on the grid `y theta/V_2 + a'/V_2`, a shifted grid, and its bottom factor on the intervals `theta + J_(l,d)`, still disjoint, through `L^1` norms of `hat F_(i_1)` and its derivative over a period, which a translation does not change. Both bounds hold with the same constants, since the certificate holds at every shift.
- **Region C re-cut: the major arcs move to `l/(N_a b^j)`.** Put `N_a = N/gcd(a, N)`. At a C residue `a'/y = l/d + eta`, `d < Z`, `abs(eta) = h/(d y) < Z/y`, the transform is read at `a/N + l/d + eta`. Write the reduced denominator of `a/N + l/d` as `d''`; then `d'' < N Z`. C1'' is the residues with `d''` carrying a prime outside `b`: there the perturbed Lemma A' of [coprime](../../coprime/notes/coprime.md), as used in region C1 of [mobius](../../mobius/notes/mobius.md), gives `abs(hat F_k(a/N + a'/y)) <= fill^k c'^(floor(2k/(3 m_(d''))))` with `m_(d'') <= log_b(N Z) + 1`, its hypothesis on `eta` unchanged, and the `3 Z^2` count and `abs(S_P) <= y` pay it exactly as C1, with `log_b N` added to `m_d`. C2'' is the rest: `a/N + l/d = l''/d''` with `d''` dividing a power of `b`, so `l/d = l''/d'' - a/N` and `d` divides `d'' N_a`, a divisor of `(N b)^j`. These are the frequencies where the translated transform can keep full mass: `hat F_k` at the `b`-adic fraction `l''/d''`. Partial summation of `e(n eta)` along the block gives `abs(S_P(a'/y)) <= (1 + 2 pi h/d) d max_(u, r') 2 abs(M(u; d, r')) <= 16 Z max_(u <= x, r') abs(M(u; d, r'))`, whether or not `d` divides `y`, and C2'' holds at most `3 Z N'_Z` residues, `N'_Z <= (1 + log_2 Z)^(omega(N b))` the number of `d < Z` dividing a power of `N b`. So the one new input is (M) at the moduli `d` dividing a power of `N b`, the lemma below, and the assembly of [mobius](../../mobius/notes/mobius.md) runs with `c'`, `c_1` and the block depth read at `N b`.
- **The lemma (M_N). Proved.** Let `R = N b`. At every `u <= x`, every `r` and every `d` dividing a power of `R` with `d <= exp(sqrt(log x)/2)`: `abs(M(u; d, r)) <= C_1 x exp(-c_1 sqrt(log x))` and `abs(psi(u; d, r) - 1_(gcd(r,d)=1) u/phi(d)) <= C_2 x exp(-c_2 sqrt(log x))`, with `C_i`, `c_i` effective and depending on `R` alone. Proof. At `u <= x exp(-sqrt(log x))` both are trivial. Otherwise put `g = gcd(r, d)`; every `n = r mod d` has `gcd(n, d) = g`, so `M = 0` unless `g` is squarefree, and then `M(u; d, r) = mu(g) sum_c M(u/g; d_1, c)` over the classes `c mod d_1 = (d/g) s` with `c = r/g mod d/g` and `gcd(c, s) = 1`, `s` the product of the primes of `g` not dividing `d/g`: at most `rad(R)` classes, `d_1 <= d rad(R)` dividing a power of `R`; for `psi` at `g > 1` only powers of the primes of `R` count, at most `omega(R) log u`. Orthogonality bounds a class by the largest `abs(M(v, chi))` over `chi mod d_1`, and `1/L(s, chi) = prod_(p) (1 - chi*(p) p^(-s))^(-1) / L(s, chi*)` over the primes `p` of `d_1` not dividing the conductor `q*` of the primitive `chi*` behind `chi`, so `M(v, chi) = sum_w chi*(w) M(v/w, chi*)` over the `w` built from those primes, at most `(1 + log_2 v)^(omega(R))` of them; the terms with `v/w < sqrt v` are trivial and the rest have `v/w >= x^(1/4)` and `q* <= rad(R) exp(sqrt(log x)/2)`. [Chang and Martin 2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1908.00035), read at source: Lemma 2.1 makes `L(s, chi)` zero-free in `sigma >= 1 - c/log(q (abs(tau) + 4))` for every `chi mod q`, `c` effective, except possibly for one real zero of a single character mod `q`, with `abs(log L) <= log log(q (abs(tau) + 4)) + O(1)` in half that width for the others; Proposition 2.3 makes every `L(s, chi mod q)` zero-free in `sigma >= 1 - c_0/(1 + log^+ abs(tau))`, `c_0 = eta/(q^(1/2) log^2 q)`, with `abs(log L) <= (1/2) log q + 3 log log^+(q tau) + O(1)` for the exceptional one; Lemma 2.4 turns a bound on `abs(log L)` into one on `abs(1/L)`. The exceptional character is real, since a zero of a complex `chi*` is a zero of its conjugate, a second character mod `q*`; a real primitive character has conductor `2^e` times an odd squarefree number with `e in {0, 2, 3}`, so here `q*` divides `8 rad(R)`, a finite family fixed by `R`, and `c_0 >= eta/((8 rad(R))^(1/2) log^2(8 rad(R)))`. Perron's formula at `1 + 1/log v`, height `T = exp(sqrt(log v))`, and the segment moved to `1 - (c/2)/log(q* (T + 4))`, or to `1 - c_0/(1 + log T)` for the finite family, give `M(v, chi*) << v exp(-c_3 sqrt(log v))` with `c_3` depending on `R` alone, since `log(q* (T + 4)) <= 3 sqrt(log v)`; at `q* = 1` the same argument runs on `L(s, chi_0 mod 4) = (1 - 2^(-s)) zeta(s)`, which has no real zero in `(0, 1)` and so no exceptional zero. Summing back over `w`, `c` and `g` gives the bound on `M`. For `psi` the same Perron integral runs on `-L'/L`, bounded by Cauchy's estimate `abs(L'/L) <= max abs(log L)/radius` on discs that fit inside the regions where `log L` is bounded, so the segment sits at `1 - (c/4)/log(q* (T + 4))`, a quarter of Lemma 2.1's width since its bound on `log L` holds in half of it, or at `1 - c_0/(2(1 + log T))`, half of Proposition 2.3's width, for the finite family; Proposition 2.3's region excludes the exceptional zero itself, so no term `v^(beta_1)` appears, `psi(v, chi*) << v exp(-c_3 sqrt(log v))` at `q* > 1`, the pole at `q* = 1` gives `v`, and `psi(v, chi) - psi(v, chi*)` is at most `omega(R) (log v)^2`; orthogonality then gives `psi(v; d_1, c) = v/phi(d_1) + O(v exp(-c_3 sqrt(log v)))`. Siegel's theorem is never used: the exceptional zeros lie on the real primitive characters of conductor dividing `8 rad(N b)`, the family of [mobius](../../mobius/notes/mobius.md) with the primes of `N` added. The Mobius half is the proof of (M) on that page with `rad(base)` read as `rad(N b)` at each of its four uses: the classes `c`, the count of `w`, the conductor bound and the exceptional family; nothing else in it reads `base`. The von Mangoldt half is (M_Lambda) of [coprime](../../coprime/notes/coprime.md) under the same swap, proved there through the exceptional term `u^(beta_1)` and its effective distance from `1`, and here through the region of Proposition 2.3, which excludes it; the two routes agree.
- **The von Mangoldt main term, through a model. Proved.** Put `lambda_R(n) = (R/phi(R)) 1_(gcd(n, R) = 1)`. In each class mod `d` dividing a power of `R`, `lambda_R` sums to `1_(gcd(r,d)=1) u/phi(d) + O(2^(omega(R)) R)`, because `n = r mod d` coprime to `R` is a class mod `d` cut by the primes of `R` outside `d`, and `(R/phi(R)) prod_(p | R, p not dividing d) (1 - 1/p) = d/phi(d)`; so `Lambda - lambda_R` obeys the bound of (M_N) for `psi` in every such class. Its exponential sum on a block is `Lambda`'s, bounded on A and B by [Maynard 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/1510.07711), Lemma 4.2, as on [coprime](../../coprime/notes/coprime.md), less `(R/phi(R)) sum_(e | R) mu(e) sum_(n in block, e | n) e(n theta)`, each inner sum at most `min(y/e + 1, 1/(2 norm(e theta)))`: at `a'/y = l/d + eta`, `norm(e a'/y) >= 1/(2d)` when `d` does not divide `e`, as `e abs(eta) <= R y^(-3/5)/d`, which gives at most `2 d <= 2 y^(3/5)`, and when `d` divides `e`, so `d <= R < Z` and the residue is in B with `h >= Z`, `norm(e a'/y) = e h/(d y)` gives at most `y/h`; both lie below the minor-arc bounds of A and B. C1'' pays `Lambda - lambda_R` through `abs(S_P) <= y (log 2x + R)`. So the dissection gives `sum_(n in S_F, n <= x, n = r mod N) (Lambda(n) - lambda_R(n)) << A_F(x) exp(-c sqrt(log x))`, and at `gcd(r, N) = 1` the model is `(R/phi(R)) #{n in S_F, n <= x : n = r mod N, gcd(n, b) = 1}`, coprimality to `N` being automatic.
- **The local constants split evenly. Proved.** On a block `P y + D_k` with `k >= 1`, `gcd(n, b) = 1` reads the last digit, so with `F*` the digits of `F` coprime to `b`, `#{t in D_k : gcd(t, b) = 1, P y + t = r mod N} = N^(-1) sum_(a mod N) e(-a (r - P y)/N) g_(F*)(a/N) prod_(1 <= j < k) g_F(a b^j/N)`. The term `a = 0` is `#F* fill^(k-1)/N`. At `a != 0`, `a b^j` is nonzero mod `N` because `gcd(b, N) = 1`, so `norm(a b^j/N) >= 1/N` and the pair form of the two-position lemma gives `abs(g_F(a b^j/N)) <= fill rho_N` with `rho_N = 1 - 4 sin^2(pi/(2N))/fill`. Over the at most `fill + 1` blocks per scale the errors sum to `<< (fill rho_N)^L << A_F(x) x^(-log(1/rho_N)/log b)`, and the blocks at scale `0` and the point `x` to `O(b)`. So the count is `(#F*/(fill N)) A_F(x)` up to a power saving, and since `R/phi(R) = (b/phi(b)) (N/phi(N))` the model is `kappa_F A_F(x)/phi(N) + O(A_F(x) x^(-delta_N))`. The design is uniform mod `N` in the limit whatever its digits share with `N`: the consecutive pair puts `1` among the digit differences, the condition of [Saavedra-Araya 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.07418), Theorem A. At `N = b - 1` the rate is exact: `hat F_k(a/(b - 1)) = g_F(a/(b - 1))^k`, since `b^j = 1 mod b - 1`.
- **Out of scope.** `gcd(N, b) > 1`: then `n mod gcd(N, b^j)` is read on the last digits, the major arcs of the translated transform include denominators sharing primes with `b` in both factors, and this section does not carry it.
- **Gelfond at every modulus. Proved.** Let `F` keep two consecutive digits and satisfy (W) in its per-digit form, so that the theorem of The power saving and the theorem above both hold, at one missing digit from `base 92317`. Let `q >= 2`, `g = gcd(q, b - 1)`, `N >= 1` coprime to `b` and `N' = lcm(N, g)`. Then for every `r_1 mod N` and `r_2 mod q`, `sum_(n in S_F, n <= x, n = r_1 mod N, s_b(n) = r_2 mod q) Lambda(n) = (g/q) kappa_F A_F(x)/phi(N') + O(A_F(x) exp(-c sqrt(log x)))` when `r_1` and `r_2` agree mod `gcd(N, g)` and the class mod `N'` they define is prime to `N'`, and `O(A_F(x) exp(-c sqrt(log x)))` otherwise, the implied constant and `c` effective and depending on `b`, `F`, `N` and `q`. At `N = 1`: `s_b(p) mod q` is uniform on the `q phi(g)/g` classes prime to `g`, the classes compatible with `p mod g` since `s_b(p) = p mod g`; at `gcd(q, b - 1) = 1` this is the corollary above. Proof: orthogonality mod `N` and mod `q` writes the sum as `(N q)^(-1) sum_(a mod N) sum_(c mod q) e(-a r_1/N - c r_2/q) sum_(n in S_F, n <= x) Lambda(n) e(a n/N + c s_b(n)/q)`. When `q/g` does not divide `c`, `(b - 1) c/q` is not an integer, since `gcd(q/g, (b - 1)/g) = 1`, so `norm((b - 1) c/q) >= 1/q`, and the theorem of The power saving with the frequency translated by `a/N` gives `O(A_F(x) x^(-delta))`: the two-position lemma bounds `hat w_k` at every frequency, the peel and the hybrid bound hold at every shift, and on a block `e(a (P y + t)/N) = e(a P y/N) e(a t/N)`, so its proof runs with `hat w_k(a/N + a'/y)` and nothing else changes. When `c = (q/g) c'`, `e(c s_b(n)/q) = e(c' n/g)` because `g` divides `b - 1`, and these terms sum to `(g/q) psi_F(x; N', R')` over the class `R'` mod `N'` with `R' = r_1 mod N` and `R' = r_2 mod g`, or to `0` when no such class exists; `N'` is coprime to `b`, and the theorem above gives the main term. The prime count follows by partial summation, as in the corollary: a prime dividing `N'` lies in no unit class mod `N'`, and the prime powers of `S_F` weigh at most `x^(1/2) (log x)^2`.

- **Against the literature. Verified** at source.
