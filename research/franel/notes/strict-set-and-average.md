## The strict set

The strict set gets the same divisor identity and no Mertens face. **`S_F^s(1, Q) = sum_{b in S_F, b <= Q} sum_{d | b} mu(d) sum_{a <= b/d, da in S_F} e(a/(b/d))`. Proved**, by Mobius inversion of the coprimality condition followed by `a -> da`, and it is exact and inert: the inner sum is a digit-restricted exponential sum over an arithmetic progression, the Type II object [mobius](../../mobius/notes/mobius.md) has no bound for. That sum is also not real, reading `1 + e(1/3) = 0.500000000 + 0.866025404 i` already at base 3 `{0,1}` and `Q = 3` and `-1.809016994 + 0.587785252 i` at base 10 without 9 and `Q = 10`, so no Mertens-type sum over `S_F` can equal it. **Refuted**, at once for `M_F(Q)`, for the count-weighted `sum_{b in S_F, b <= Q} mu(b) phi_F(b)` and for the normalised `sum_{b in S_F, b <= Q} mu(b) phi_F(b)/phi(b)`, all three real; the refutation rests on those two witnesses alone, and the observation beside them, that the strict set also fails the pairing `a -> b - a` which makes the denominator set's sum real, is not shown to force a non-real sum. Its modulus rides the node count `card` of the strict set instead, `|S_F^s(1,Q)|/card` reading `0.335693, 0.343837, 0.345905, 0.346338` at `Q = 3^5, 3^7, 3^9, 3^11` and `0.015138, 0.012250, 0.011561` at `Q = 10^2, 10^3, 10^4`, and reaching modulus `374203.231` at `Q = 3^11` against `M_F(3^11) = -10`. **Verified** (`lab/py/restricted-franel`). That is the mass reading of [the meter on a digit design](../../farey/notes/farey.md#the-meter-on-a-digit-design) seen at frequency 1: a set whose frequency-1 sum is proportional to its own count has no cancellation there and does not equidistribute.

## The average over designs

Fix `base` and `F` with `fill = abs F` and `2 <= fill < base`, so `alpha = log fill/log base < 1`. At `level >= 1` put `N = base^level` and read each `n` in `[1, N]` through `n mod N` and its `level` digits `n_0, .., n_(level-1)`. Three families of subsets of `[1, N]` are averaged: the translates `D_s = {n : (n - s) mod N in D}`, `s in [0, N)`, of the level set `D = {u in [0, N) : every digit of u lies in F}`, wrapping at `N`; the digit shifts `S_c = {n : (n_j - c_j) mod base in F for every j}`, `c in (Z/base)^level`; and the digit subsets `{n : n_j in F_j for every j}`, each `F_j` one of the `C(base, fill)` subsets of size `fill`, which depend on `fill` alone and at base 3 with `fill = 2` are the digit shifts. `E` is the uniform average over one family, `M_S(x) = sum_(n <= x, n in S) mu(n)` at `x <= N`, and `level(x)` is the least `level >= 1` with `base^level >= x`, so `x <= N <= base x`. Write `M(x; q, r)` for the Mertens sum over `n = r mod q`, `M_chi(x) = sum_(n <= x) mu(n) chi(n)`, `S_x(t) = sum_(n <= x) mu(n) e(nt)`, `W_x(a) = sum_(n <= x) mu(n) e((a_0 n_0 + .. + a_(level-1) n_(level-1))/base)` for `a in (Z/base)^level`, and `w(a)` for the number of nonzero `a_j`.

**The mean is flat: in each of the three families `E 1_S(n) = (fill/base)^level` at every `n in [1, N]`. Proved.** A translate holds `n` exactly when `s = n - u mod N` for some `u in D`, so `abs D = fill^level` of the `N` translates hold it; a digit shift holds `n` when `c_j in n_j - F` at every `j`, `fill` of `base` choices at each position independently; a digit subset holds `n` when `n_j in F_j`, which `C(base - 1, fill - 1)` of the `C(base, fill)` subsets do, again `fill/base` at each position. **The choice is load-bearing: the translates `D + s` without the wrap, and one digit set at every position, have no flat mean. Proved.** The first hold `n < N` with frequency `#{u in D : u <= n}/N`, `2/9` at `n = 1` and `4/9` at `n = 8` at base 3 `{0,1}` and `level = 2`; the designs `S_F` over the three `F` of size `2` at base 3 hold `n = 1` without leading zeros in two of them and `n = 15 = 120_3` in none.

**The expansion: at every `x <= N`, `E abs(M_S(x))^2 = sum_(n, m <= x) mu(n) mu(m) P(n, m)`, `P(n, m)` the frequency with which a member holds both, and in Fourier form it is `N^(-2) sum_(a mod N) abs(hat F_level(a/N))^2 abs(S_x(a/N))^2` for the translates, `base^(-2 level) sum_a prod_j abs(hat F(a_j/base))^2 abs(W_x(a))^2` for the digit shifts and `(fill/base)^(2 level) sum_a q^(w(a)) abs(W_x(a))^2` for the digit subsets, `q = (base - fill)/(fill (base - 1))`. Proved.** Expand the square and exchange the average with the double sum. For the translates `P(n, m) = r(n - m)/N` with `r(h) = #{(u, v) in D^2 : u - v = h mod N} = N^(-1) sum_(a mod N) abs(hat F_level(a/N))^2 e(-ah/N)`, `hat F_level` being the transform of `D`. For the digit shifts the positions are independent and `P(n, m) = prod_j r_F(n_j - m_j)/base` with `r_F(h) = base^(-1) sum_(a mod base) abs(hat F(a/base))^2 e(-ah/base)`. For the digit subsets `P(n, m) = prod_j p(n_j - m_j)` with `p(0) = fill/base` and `p(h) = fill (fill - 1)/(base (base - 1))` at `h != 0`, so `P = (fill/base)^level lambda^(d(n, m))` with `lambda = (fill - 1)/(base - 1)` and `d` the number of differing digits, and `p(h) = base^(-1) sum_a p'(a) e(-ah/base)` with `p'(0) = fill^2/base` and `p'(a) = fill (base - fill)/(base (base - 1))` at `a != 0`, whose ratio is `q`. Each Fourier form put back in the double sum turns `sum mu(n) mu(m) e(-a(n - m)/N)` into `abs(S_x(a/N))^2`, and its digit analogue into `abs(W_x(a))^2`. Every term is nonnegative, and in each family the weights sum to `P(n, n) = (fill/base)^level`. The frequency `0` carries `(fill/base)^(2 level) M(x)^2`, and since `n_0 = n mod base` the frequencies `a = cN/base` of the translates and `a = (c, 0, .., 0)` of the digit families carry `abs(S_x(c/base))^2` with weight `(fill/base)^(2 level) kappa_c`, where `kappa_c = abs(hat F(c/base))^2/fill^2` for the translates and digit shifts, by `hat F_level(c/base) = hat F(c/base) fill^(level - 1)`, and `kappa_c = q` for the digit subsets. **Verified** at base 3 with `fill = 2`, at `x = 3^level` for every `level <= 8` and at `x = 100, 1000, 5000` inside `level(x) = 5, 7, 8` and at `x = 2000` inside `level = 8`, one above `level(2000) = 7`: the average over the whole family, computed member by member, equals the pair form as an exact rational for the translates of `{0,1}` and of `{0,2}`, for the digit shifts and for the digit subsets enumerated separately, and equals the Fourier form of the digit families summed exactly in `Z[e(1/3)]`; the translates' Fourier form, summed by FFT, agrees to relative `1e-9`; at `x = 3^8` the translates of `{0,1}` read `930830/6561` and the digit shifts `337520/2187`, against `M(3^8) = -13`; and the pair form equals the average in all three families at base 5 `{0,1}` to `level = 4` and at base 4 `{0,2}` at `level = 3`, with no flat-mean count off `2^level` at base 3 to `level = 8` on the translates of `{0,1}`, `{0,2}` and `{1,2}` and on the digit shifts, which are the digit subsets there (`lab/py/design-averages`, verb `check`).

**Square-root cancellation on average forces a zero-free half plane for `zeta`. Let one family be fixed and `0 <= theta < 1`, and suppose that for every `eps > 0` there is `C_eps` with `E abs(M_S(x))^2 <= C_eps x^(2 theta alpha + eps)` at every `x >= 1`, the family taken at `level(x)`. Then `abs(M(x)) <= C_eps^(1/2) base^(1 - alpha) x^(1 - (1 - theta) alpha + eps/2)` at every `x >= 1`, and `zeta(s) != 0` for `Re s > 1 - (1 - theta) alpha`. Proved.** At `theta = 1/2` the hypothesis is square-root cancellation in mean square against the mean mass `E A_S(x) = (fill/base)^level floor(x)`, `A_S(x) = #{n <= x : n in S}`, which lies between `x^alpha/(2 base^(1 - alpha))` and `x^alpha`, and the half plane is `Re s > 1 - alpha/2`, at base 3 with `fill = 2` the half plane `Re s > 1 - log 2/(2 log 3)`, the constant reading `0.684535` to six places (`lab/py/design-averages`, verb `read`); any power saving `theta < 1` in mean square still forces width `(1 - theta) alpha`. By the flat mean `E M_S(x) = (fill/base)^level M(x)`, and `abs(E M_S(x))^2 <= E abs(M_S(x))^2` by Cauchy-Schwarz, the frequency `0` of the expansion; `(fill/base)^level = N^(alpha - 1) >= (base x)^(alpha - 1)`, so `M(x)^2 <= (base x)^(2 - 2 alpha) C_eps x^(2 theta alpha + eps)`. The zeros follow from the Mellin step: if `abs(M_chi(y)) <= C y^sigma` at every `y >= 1` with `sigma < 1`, for a Dirichlet character `chi` (`chi = 1` giving `zeta`), partial summation gives `1/L(s, chi) = s int_1^inf M_chi(y) y^(-s-1) dy` at `Re s > 1`, the integral converges absolutely and locally uniformly on `Re s > sigma` and continues `1/L` to an analytic `G` there, and `L G` is analytic on `Re s > sigma` off `s = 1`, the only possible pole of `L`, and equals `1` on `Re s > 1`, hence on that connected set, so `L(s, chi)` has no zero in it. The passage to the zeros uses every `x`: asked only at `x = base^level`, the same lines give the bound on `M(base^level)` and nothing here reaches the zeros from that sequence, which is why the hypothesis is asked at every `x`, where the mean stays flat on `[1, x]`.

**On the digit subsets, and on the translates and the digit shifts when `hat F(c/base) != 0` at every `c != 0 mod base`, the same hypothesis gives `abs(M(x; base, r)) <<_eps x^(1 - (1 - theta) alpha + eps/2)` for every class `r`, and `L(s, chi) != 0` for `Re s > 1 - (1 - theta) alpha` for every Dirichlet character `chi` mod `base` and every primitive character whose conductor divides `base`. Proved.** Let `kappa = min_c kappa_c`, positive under the condition and at most `1`, since `abs(hat F) <= fill` and `base - fill <= fill (base - 1)`. Dropping every frequency but the `base` frequencies above, `E abs(M_S(x))^2 >= kappa (fill/base)^(2 level) sum_(c mod base) abs(S_x(c/base))^2 = kappa (fill/base)^(2 level) base sum_(r mod base) M(x; base, r)^2` by Parseval mod `base`, and the bound on `(fill/base)^level` above gives the class bound. Then `M_chi(x) = sum_r chi(r) M(x; base, r)` and the Mellin step give the half plane, and a primitive `chi*` of conductor dividing `base` induces `chi` mod `base` with `L(s, chi) = L(s, chi*) prod_(p | base) (1 - chi*(p) p^(-s))`, whose factors do not vanish at `Re s > 0`. At a prime base the condition always holds: `hat F(c/p) = 0` puts a primitive `p`-th root of unity at a zero of `sum_(f in F) X^f`, of degree below `p`, so `1 + X + .. + X^(p-1)` divides it and `F` is every digit. At a composite base it can fail: base 4 `{0,2}` has `hat F(1/4) = hat F(3/4) = 0`, so among the frequencies `c/4` its translates and digit shifts give this argument the twist `c = 2` alone, the classes mod `2`, while the digit subsets at base 4 with `fill = 2` carry `q = 1/3` at every `c`. **The same hypothesis reaches every power of the base: for each fixed `k >= 1` it bounds every class sum `M(x; base^k, r)` by `x^(1 - (1 - theta) alpha + eps/2)` and makes every `L(s, chi)` of modulus `base^k`, and every primitive character whose conductor divides a power of `base`, zero-free in `Re s > 1 - (1 - theta) alpha`, on the digit subsets always, on the digit shifts when `hat F(c/base) != 0` at every `c != 0`, and on the translates at a prime base. Proved.** The frequencies of level at most `k` carry `sum abs(S_x)^2` or `sum abs(W_x)^2` equal to `base^k sum_(r mod base^k) M(x; base^k, r)^2`, as in the (GM) cut below, so it suffices that each of their weights is at least `kappa_k (fill/base)^(2 level)` with `kappa_k > 0` depending on `k` and not on `level` or `x`. On the digit subsets the weight is `q^(w(a)) >= q^k`. On the digit shifts it is `prod_j abs(hat F(a_j/base))^2/fill^2 >= kappa^k` with `kappa = min_c kappa_c`. On the translates the frequency `c/base^k` has weight `fill^(-2k) abs(hat F_k(c/base^k))^2` times `(fill/base)^(2 level)`, a product of `hat F(b/p^m)` and of factors `fill`; at a prime base `p` with `gcd(b, p) = 1` and `m >= 2`, `hat F(b/p^m) = 0` would make the cyclotomic polynomial of order `p^m`, of degree `p^(m-1)(p - 1) >= p`, divide the nonzero `sum_(f in F) X^f` of degree below `p`, which is impossible, and `m = 1` is the case above, so the finitely many weights are positive. The class bound, `M_chi = sum_r chi(r) M(x; base^k, r)`, the Mellin step and the induction from the conductor finish as above. **Verified**: `E abs(M_S(x))^2 >= (4/9)^level M(x)^2` and `E abs(M_S(x))^2 >= (1/4)(4/9)^level 3 sum_(r mod 3) M(x; 3, r)^2` hold as exact rationals in every family at every cell of the check above, `E abs(M_S(x))^2 >= (1/16)(4/9)^level 9 sum_(r mod 9) M(x; 9, r)^2` exactly on the digit families and with `kappa_2 = min_c abs(hat F_2(c/9))^2/16` in floating point on the translates of `{0,1}`, with `sum_c abs(S_x(c/3))^2 = 3 sum_r M(x; 3, r)^2` exact there (`lab/py/design-averages`, verb `check`).

So square-root cancellation reads on three lines, and only the middle one is proved on this page:

- Uniform in the base it implies the Riemann hypothesis, with no converse claimed, the bullet Uniform square-root cancellation over the bases implies RH of [the half interval](../../mobius/notes/half-interval-even-digits.md#the-half-interval).
- On average over a flat family at one base it costs the zero-free half plane `Re s > 1 - alpha/2` for `zeta`, and for every `L(s, chi)` mod the base on the digit subsets and wherever `hat F(c/base) != 0` at every `c != 0`, by the two theorems above.
- On one design it is (SR) at `d = 1`, the square-root conjecture for the meter of [the surrogate and (SR)](threshold-surrogate-repunits.md#the-surrogate-and-sr); what the tree proves there is the saving `exp(-c sqrt(log x))` on the half interval at odd base from `94939` in [the half interval](../../mobius/notes/half-interval-even-digits.md#the-half-interval) and, under GRH, the power saving on the dense columns in [the GRH section](../../mobius/notes/grh-power-saving.md#a-power-saving-under-grh-at-large-base).

**The converse holds against the twists: if `abs(S_x(t)) <= C x^(1/2 + eps)` for every real `t`, the translates have `E abs(M_S(x))^2 <= C^2 x^(alpha + 2 eps)`, and if `abs(W_x(a)) <= C x^(1/2 + eps)` for every `level` and every `a`, so do the digit shifts and the digit subsets. Proved.** The weights sum to `(fill/base)^level = N^(alpha - 1) <= x^(alpha - 1)`. **Under GRH, with step 4 of [the GRH section](../../mobius/notes/grh-power-saving.md#a-power-saving-under-grh-at-large-base) as the one quoted input, the translates have `E abs(M_S(x))^2 <<_eps x^(alpha + 1/2 + eps)`, half a power above the mean mass. Proved**, the same line with `x^(3/4 + eps)` for every `abs(S_x(t))`; the digit families have no such input, `W_x(a)` not being a twist by `e(nt)`.

What a Mertens bound for characters reaches is cut by the level of the frequency: `a` has level at most `k` when `a/N = c/base^k` for the translates and when `a_j = 0` at every `j >= k` for the digit families. Write (GM) for: for every `eps > 0` there is `C_eps` with `abs(M_chi(y)) <= C_eps (q y)^eps y^(1/2)` at every `y >= 1` for every character `chi` of every modulus `q = base^k`, `k >= 1`; at the one modulus `base` it is the Mertens form of GRH mod `base`, and it asks for that form uniformly over the powers; whether GRH gives (GM), through the bound `1/L(s, chi) <<_eps (q (abs t + 2))^eps` at `Re s >= 1/2 + eps` and a truncated Perron integral, is not decided here. **Under (GM), in each family the frequencies of level at most `k` carry at most `2^omega(base) C_eps^2 x^(2 alpha - 1) base^k (base^k x)^(2 eps)`, which is at most `2^omega(base) C_eps^2 x^(alpha + 4 eps)` while `base^k <= x^(1 - alpha)`. Proved.** Each such weight is at most `(fill/base)^(2 level)`, since `abs(hat F) <= fill` and `q <= 1`, and since `S_x(c/base^k)` and `W_x(a)` at level `k` depend on `n` through `n mod base^k` alone, Parseval makes the sum of their squares over those frequencies `base^k sum_(r mod base^k) M(x; base^k, r)^2`. For `q = base^k` that class sum is at most `2^omega(base) max_(chi mod q) max_(z <= x) abs(M_chi(z))^2`: if `g = gcd(r, q)` is not squarefree every `n = r mod q` has `mu(n) = 0`; otherwise `n = g m` with `m = r/g mod q/g` coprime to `q/g`, and `mu(gm) = mu(g) mu(m)` when `gcd(m, g) = 1` and `0` otherwise, so the class sum is `mu(g)` times the sum of `mu(m) 1_(gcd(m, q) = 1)` over `m <= x/g` in the coprime class `r/g mod q/g`, the classes of gcd `g` matching those coprime classes one to one; orthogonality of the characters `psi` mod `q/g` writes the sum of their squares as `phi(q/g)^(-1) sum_psi abs(sum mu(m) 1_(gcd(m, q) = 1) psi(m))^2`, at most the largest `abs(M_chi(x/g))^2` over `chi` mod `q`, each `psi 1_(gcd(m, q) = 1)` being a character mod `q`; and `q` has `2^omega(base)` squarefree divisors. Then `(fill/base)^(2 level) x <= x^(2 alpha - 1)`. So (GM) at the one modulus `base` bounds the frequencies of level at most `1`, the ones that force the half plane for `L(s, chi)` mod `base` above, by `x^(2 alpha - 1 + eps)`, and (GM) over the powers carries the bound to `base^k = x^(1 - alpha)`. There the converse stops: under (GM) mean-square square-root cancellation over the family is equivalent to the same bound on the frequencies of level above `(1 - alpha) log_base x`, which have only the trivial bound here. On the translates they are `S_x(c/base^k)` at `base^k > x^(1 - alpha)` weighted by `abs(hat F_k(c/base^k))^2`, and (GM) bounds their plain sum over `c` by `base^k x^(1 + eps)`, not their sum against weights concentrated where `hat F_k` is large; on the digit families they are the characters touching a digit at a position at least `(1 - alpha) log_base x`. **Verified**: at base 3 with `fill = 2` and `x = 3^level`, the frequencies of level at most `k = floor((1 - alpha) level)` carry a share of the mean square reading `0.270590` at `level = 4` and `0.013826` at `level = 13` on the digit subsets, `0.264550` and `0.015085` on the translates of `{0,1}`, while `E abs(M_S(x))^2/E A_S(x)` reads between `0.465892` and `0.617104` over `level = 6` to `13` in both families, `0.617104` and `0.604427` at `level = 13`, no exponent claimed, and almost all of the mean square sits on the frequencies (GM) does not reach (`lab/py/design-averages`, verb `read`).
