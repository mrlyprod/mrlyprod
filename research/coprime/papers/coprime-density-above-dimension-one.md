Stand at the origin of the square lattice and look out: a point is visible exactly when its coordinates share no prime factor, and `6/Pi^2` of the lattice is visible. Do the same on a fractal built from digits, the Sierpinski gasket of the points of `[0, 2^level)^2` whose binary digit pairs all avoid `(1, 1)`, and the visible share tends to `16/(3 Pi^2)` instead. This paper proves why, for every digital design of dimension above one whose digit differences span a lattice of full rank whose index has no prime outside the base (condition (E)): every prime off the base keeps its classical factor `1 - p^(-dim)`, and the primes of the base are replaced, together and exactly at every finite level, by one rational number `B(F)` read off the digits, which at a composite base need not factor over its primes. The hypothesis `fill > base` is where one series, `sum_m Lambda(m) m^(-log(fill)/log(base))`, converges, and it is not enough alone: the Cantor dust at base 3 has dimension `1.26` and not one visible point. The same sieve, fibred over pairs of coordinates instead of single ones, settles pairwise coprimality on the Menger sponge: its three coordinates are pairwise coprime with limiting probability `(351/400) C_3 = 0.251620868451255...`, `C_3` the constant of the full cube, so drilling the holes costs exactly `12.25` percent of the odds, all of it at the prime 3, where 13 of the sponge's 20 digit vectors keep the prime off every pair of coordinates against 20 of the cube's 27.

## Introduction

![A square grid of 32 by 32 cells holding the level-5 Sierpinski gasket at base 2, the 243 points of [0, 32)^2 whose binary digit pairs all avoid (1, 1), drawn with the origin at the lower left. The 122 points whose two coordinates are coprime, visible from the origin, are yellow; the other 121 are dim. The figure counts both numbers itself, and before drawing it asserts the brackets and the predicted densities of the eight designs of Corollary 6.1 and Theorem 6.2 to the twelve digits printed, the base-6 sample's exact count at levels 1 to 6, the sponge's pairwise census and parity count at levels 1 to 6, its fibre sizes, and the digits of (351/400) C_3.](paper-coprime-density-above-dimension-one)

The gasket's number has a shape, `16/(3 Pi^2) = (2/3) (6/Pi^2) (4/3)`. The middle factor is the classical answer `1/zeta(2)`. The right factor `(1 - 2^(-2))^(-1)` cancels the classical factor at `2`, the base. The left factor is what the digits put in its place: two of the gasket's three digit pairs are not `(0, 0)`, so exactly two thirds of its points have an odd greatest common divisor, at every level `>= 1` and with no error term (Theorem 3.1). That is the pattern in general.

**Theorem 1.1 (coprimality density above dimension one).** Let `(base, dim, F)` be a design that satisfies condition (E) and has `fill > base` (Definitions 2.1 to 2.4). Then `A(level)/fill^level -> delta = B(F) prod_(p not dividing base) (1 - p^(-dim))` as `level -> infinity`. The bracket `B(F) = 0` is allowed, and then the limit is `0`.

The proof, in Section 6, has three parts: an identity at the base, exact at every level (Theorem 3.1); a character contraction that equidistributes `S_level` modulo every `d` prime to the base, the one place condition (E) enters (Lemma 4.1); and a digit-box bound whose Chebyshev sum converges exactly when `fill > base` (Lemmas 5.1 and 5.2). The last is where the dimension lives. Sifting out the primes of a greatest common divisor needs the points of `S_level` divisible by a large `m` to be few, and the box bound gives at most `(base + 1)^dim fill^level m^(-alpha)` of them, `alpha = log(fill)/log(base)`; summed against the von Mangoldt weight it gives `-zeta'(alpha)/zeta(alpha)`, finite exactly when `alpha > 1`. At `alpha <= 1` the series diverges and this route stops; what replaces it at dimension one is the subject of [the coprime note](../notes/the-window-moments-and-rays.md) and of [the pincer paper](lemma-b-pincer.md), which closes the base-3 gasket's case outside one window of prime sizes. Condition (E) cannot be dropped: the Cantor dust `{0, 2}^2` at base 3 has `fill = 4 > 3` and `A(level) = 0` at every level against a predicted `81/(16 Pi^2)`, though the dilation `y -> 2 y` from `{0, 1}^2` returns that constant as the density of the points with greatest common divisor 2 (Theorems 6.2 and 6.3). The theorem makes the densities of the named designs theorems, the gasket's `16/(3 Pi^2)`, the carpet's `189/(32 Pi^2)` and the sponge's `(513/520)/zeta(3)` among them (Corollary 6.1), and, when `B(F) > 0`, at even `dim` and at `dim = 3` it shows that `A(level)` satisfies no linear recurrence with constant rational coefficients (Corollary 6.5).

**Theorem 1.2 (the sponge in pairs).** Let `P_level` be the share of the `20^level` points of the level-`level` Menger sponge whose three coordinates are pairwise coprime (Definition 7.1). Then `P_level -> (13/20) prod_(p != 3) (1 - 3/p^2 + 2/p^3) = (351/400) C_3 = 0.251620868451255...`, where `C_3 = prod_p (1 - 3/p^2 + 2/p^3) = 0.286747428434478...` ([A065473](https://oeis.org/A065473)) is the limit of the same share on the full cube `[0, 3^level)^3` (Corollary 7.6). The holes cost exactly `1 - 351/400 = 49/400 = 12.25` percent of the cube's odds.

In the limit every prime but 3 acts on the sponge as on the cube. At 3 the cube's factor `20/27` becomes `13/20`, because a coordinate is divisible by 3 exactly when its last digit is `0`, and 13 of the sponge's 20 digit vectors have at most one zero. The other primes need a box bound fibred over a pair of coordinates (Lemma 7.3): fibring over one coordinate leaves fibres of 8 digit vectors and the exponent `log_3(20/8) = 0.834 < 1`, which proves nothing, while a pair leaves fibres of 3 and the exponent `log_3(20/3) = 1.7268... > 1`, which closes the tail. The criterion is general: a prime-base design that satisfies condition (E) settles the pairwise question once `fill > base kappa_I` for each pair `I` of coordinates (Theorem 7.2). Levels 1 to 6 sit below the limit, `0.236582625` at level 6 (Fact 8.3), and the parity of the digits is biased, exactly: a coordinate of the sponge is even with probability `(1 + (3/5)^level)/2`, not `1/2` (Theorem 7.7).

The probability throughout is counting on the finite sets `S_level`, level by level, and not Lebesgue measure on the attractor, where a point has no greatest common divisor. Section 2 fixes the objects, Sections 3 to 6 prove Theorem 1.1 and its corollaries, Section 7 proves Theorem 1.2, Section 8 holds the finite checks, and Section 11 says how to rerun every number.

## Definitions

**Definition 2.1 (design, level set, count).** A design is a triple `(base, dim, F)`: an integer `base >= 2`, a dimension `dim >= 2`, and a set `F` of digit vectors, a subset of `{0, ..., base - 1}^dim` with `fill = |F| >= 2`. Its level set is `S_level = {sum_(j < level) base^j f_j : f_0, ..., f_(level-1) in F}`, the sum taken coordinatewise, so `S_0 = {0}`. Base-`base` expansion is unique, so every point of `S_level` has exactly one string of digit vectors and `|S_level| = fill^level`. The count is `A(level) = #{x in S_level : gcd(x_1, ..., x_dim) = 1}`, the points visible from the origin, with `gcd(0, n) = n`; the origin has greatest common divisor `0` and is never counted. The dimension of the design is `log(fill)/log(base)`, the Hausdorff dimension of the attractor of the maps `y -> (y + f)/base`, `f in F`, and it exceeds one exactly when `fill > base`.

**Definition 2.2 (condition (E)).** Let `Diff` be the subgroup of `Z^dim` generated by the differences `F - F`. The design satisfies condition (E) when `Diff` has rank `dim` and every prime dividing its index `m(F) = [Z^dim : Diff]` divides `base`. It is spanning when `m(F) = 1`, the case of (E) at index one.

**Definition 2.3 (the bracket).** For a squarefree `e` let `fill_e` be the number of digit vectors of `F` all of whose coordinates `e` divides, and let `rad(base)` be the product of the distinct primes dividing `base`. The bracket is `B(F) = sum_(e | rad(base)) mu(e) fill_e / fill`.

**Definition 2.4 (the predicted density).** `delta = B(F) prod_(p not dividing base) (1 - p^(-dim)) = (B(F)/zeta(dim)) prod_(p | base) (1 - p^(-dim))^(-1)`, the product over primes and absolutely convergent since `dim >= 2`.

Throughout, `e(y) = exp(2 Pi i y)`, `mu` is the Mobius function, `Lambda` the von Mangoldt function, `ord_d(base)` the multiplicative order of `base` modulo `d`, and `||y||` the distance from `y` to the nearest integer. For `m >= 1` write `T_m(level)` for the number of `x in S_level` with `m | x_i` for every `i`, and `T*_m(level)` for the same count over the nonzero `x`. The star matters: when `0 in F` the origin lies in every `S_level` and every `m` divides it, so a sum of unstarred counts over all `m` diverges for a trivial reason.

## The base is exact

**Theorem 3.1 (the base is exact at every level).** For every design, every squarefree `e | rad(base)` and every `level >= 1`, `T_e(level) = fill_e fill^(level-1)`, and the number of `x in S_level` whose greatest common divisor is prime to `base` is exactly `B(F) fill^level`. Neither (E) nor `fill > base` is needed. If `base = p` is prime, then for `1 <= a <= level` the number of `x in S_level` with `p^a | gcd(x)` is `[0 in F] fill^(level-a)`.

**Proof.** Every prime of `e` divides `base` and `e` is squarefree, so `e | base`. Write a point as `x = f_0 + base y` with `f_0 in F` its last digit vector and `y in S_(level-1)`; the map `(f_0, y) -> x` is a bijection from `F x S_(level-1)` onto `S_level`. Since `e | base y_i` for every `i`, `e` divides every coordinate of `x` exactly when it divides every coordinate of `f_0`, a condition on the last digit vector alone, met by `fill_e` of them, while the other `level - 1` digit vectors are free. So `T_e(level) = fill_e fill^(level-1)` exactly. The greatest common divisor of `x` is prime to `base` when no prime of `base` divides every coordinate, and inclusion and exclusion over the primes of `base` count those points as `sum_(e | rad(base)) mu(e) T_e(level) = fill^(level-1) sum_e mu(e) fill_e = B(F) fill^level`. At a prime base the only digit vector all of whose coordinates `p` divides is `0`, so `p | gcd(x)` forces `f_0 = 0`, which needs `0 in F`, and then `x = p y` with `gcd(x) = p gcd(y)`; iterating `a <= level` times gives the last count. The bound `a <= level` is needed: for `a > level` and `0 in F` the count is `1`, the origin alone. □

**Corollary 3.2 (the bracket is a share of digit vectors).** `B(F)` is the share of the digit vectors `f in F` with `gcd(f_1, ..., f_dim, base) = 1`; so it is a rational number in `[0, 1]`, it is `1 - [0 in F]/fill` at a prime base, and `B(F) = 0` exactly when every digit vector of `F` has all its coordinates divisible by some prime of `base`, which may depend on the vector.

**Proof.** Theorem 3.1 at `level = 1`, where `S_1 = F`. □

At a prime base the bracket only records whether the zero vector is filled. At a composite base the events that the different primes of the base divide the greatest common divisor are correlated through the digit vectors, and the bracket carries the whole correlation.

**Corollary 3.3 (a composite base does not factor).** Let `base = 6`, `dim = 2` and `F = {(0,0), (0,3), (1,1), (1,2), (2,0), (2,1), (4,0), (5,5)}`, the base-6 sample. Then `fill_2 = 3`, `fill_3 = 2`, `fill_6 = 1` and `B(F) = 1 - 3/8 - 2/8 + 1/8 = 1/2`, while the product of the two marginal factors is `(1 - 3/8)(1 - 2/8) = 15/32 = 0.46875`. So exactly half of the `8^level` points of `S_level` have greatest common divisor prime to `6` at every level `>= 1`, `2048` of `4096` at level 4, where independence of the two primes would predict `1920`.

**Proof.** The even digit vectors are `(0,0), (2,0), (4,0)`, those divisible by 3 are `(0,0), (0,3)`, and only `(0,0)` is divisible by 6; apply Theorem 3.1. □

The same base holds brackets that no product of marginals relates: the base-6 pair `{(0,0), (0,1), (1,0), (1,1), (2,3), (3,2), (4,5), (5,4)}` also has `fill = 8`, with `fill_2 = fill_3 = fill_6 = 1` and `B(F) = 7/8`.

## Away from the base

**Lemma 4.1 (character contraction).** Let the design satisfy condition (E). Let `d >= 2` with `gcd(d, base) = 1`, let `t` be a nonzero vector of `(Z/d)^dim`, put `r = ord_d(base)` and `f_l(t) = (1/fill) |sum_(v in F) e(base^l <t, v>/d)|`. Then `prod_(l = 0..r-1) f_l(t) <= c(base, fill) = 1 - (2/fill)(1 - cos(Pi/(2 base))) < 1`. The constant depends on `base` and `fill` alone, not on `d`, `t` or which digit vectors `F` holds.

**Proof.** First, (E) gives a difference the character sees. Since `Diff` has finite index `m(F)`, the quotient `Z^dim/Diff` is a finite abelian group of order `m(F)`, so `m(F) Z^dim` lies in `Diff`. If `<t, v - v'> = 0 mod d` for all `v, v'` in `F`, then `t` vanishes on `Diff`, hence on `m(F) Z^dim`, so `m(F) t = 0` in `(Z/d)^dim`; every prime of `m(F)` divides `base` and `gcd(d, base) = 1`, so `m(F)` is invertible modulo `d` and `t = 0`, contrary to hypothesis. Fix `v, v'` in `F` with `D = <t, v - v'>` nonzero modulo `d`.

Second, the orbit of `D/d` under multiplication by `base` cannot stay near the integers. Put `y_l = ||base^l D/d||`. Multiplication by `base` permutes `Z/d`, so `base^l D mod d` is purely periodic with a period `r'` dividing `r` and never `0`, and `y_l >= 1/d > 0` for every `l`. If `y_l < 1/(2 base)`, then `base^l D/d` lies within `1/(2 base)` of an integer `N`, so `base^(l+1) D/d` lies within `1/2` of `base N` and `y_(l+1) = base y_l`. Were `y_l < 1/(2 base)` at every `l` of one period, going once around would give `y_0 = y_(r') = base^(r') y_0` with `y_0 > 0`, which is impossible. So some `l_0 < r'` has `1/(2 base) <= y_(l_0) <= 1/2`.

Third, one large `y_l` contracts the sum. At `l = l_0` split the character sum into the two terms of `v, v'` and the other `fill - 2`. For unit complex numbers `|e(a) + e(b)| = 2 |cos(Pi (a - b))|`, and here `a - b = base^(l_0) D/d`, at distance `y_(l_0)` from the integers, so `|cos(Pi (a - b))| = cos(Pi y_(l_0)) <= cos(Pi/(2 base))`. Bounding the other terms by `1` each, `|sum_(v in F) e(base^(l_0) <t, v>/d)| <= fill - 2 + 2 cos(Pi/(2 base))`, that is `f_(l_0)(t) <= c(base, fill)`. Every other factor is at most `1`, and `l_0 < r' <= r`. Finally `c(base, fill) < 1` because `cos(Pi/(2 base)) < 1`. □

**Corollary 4.2 (equidistribution).** Let the design satisfy condition (E), let `d >= 2` with `gcd(d, base) = 1` and let `a in (Z/d)^dim`. Then `|#{x in S_level : x = a mod d}/fill^level - 1/d^dim| <= c(base, fill)^floor(level/ord_d(base))`.

**Proof.** A point of `S_level` is `sum_(l < level) base^l f_l` with the `f_l` chosen independently in `F`, so for `t in (Z/d)^dim` the character sum over `S_level` factors as `sum_(x in S_level) e(<t, x>/d) = prod_(l < level) sum_(v in F) e(base^l <t, v>/d)`. By orthogonality of the additive characters of `(Z/d)^dim`, `#{x in S_level : x = a mod d} = d^(-dim) sum_t e(-<t, a>/d) prod_(l < level) sum_(v in F) e(base^l <t, v>/d)`. The term `t = 0` is `fill^level/d^dim`. For `t != 0` the term has modulus `fill^level prod_(l < level) f_l(t)`, and the `level` positions hold `floor(level/r)` disjoint blocks of `r = ord_d(base)` consecutive positions; the block starting at `s` contributes `prod_(l < r) f_l(base^s t)` with `base^s t != 0`, at most `c(base, fill)` by Lemma 4.1, and the other factors are at most `1`. There are `d^dim - 1 < d^dim` nonzero `t`, and dividing by `d^dim` absorbs their number. □

**Corollary 4.3 (the base peel).** Let the design satisfy condition (E) and let `d = e m` be squarefree with `e | rad(base)` and `gcd(m, base) = 1`. Then `T_d(level)/fill^level = (fill_e/fill) m^(-dim) + O(c(base, fill)^floor((level - 1)/ord_m(base)))` with an absolute implied constant, the error term read as `0` when `m = 1`; in particular `T_d(level)/fill^level -> (fill_e/fill) m^(-dim)` as `level -> infinity` at fixed `d`.

**Proof.** Write `x = f_0 + base y` with `f_0 in F` and `y in S_(level-1)`. As in Theorem 3.1, `e` divides every coordinate of `x` exactly when it divides every coordinate of `f_0`. Since `base` is invertible modulo `m`, `m` divides every coordinate of `x` exactly when `y = -base^(-1) f_0 mod m`, a condition on `y` alone. So `T_d(level) = sum_(f_0) #{y in S_(level-1) : y = -base^(-1) f_0 mod m}`, the sum over the `fill_e` digit vectors `f_0` that `e` divides. For `m = 1` each count is `fill^(level-1)`; for `m >= 2` each is `fill^(level-1) (m^(-dim) + O(c^floor((level-1)/ord_m(base))))` by Corollary 4.2. Divide by `fill^level`. □

The coprime note sharpens Lemma 4.1 to one contracting position in every window of `max(1, floor(log_base(d/2)) + 1)` consecutive digits rather than one per period, with a version for a modulus carrying a power of the base; Theorem 1.1 needs only convergence at fixed `d`, and the period form suffices.

## The digit box

Write `alpha = log(fill)/log(base)` for the dimension of the design throughout this section.

**Lemma 5.1 (the digit-box bound).** For every design, every `m >= 2` and every `0 <= h <= level`, `T_m(level) <= fill^(level-h) (base^h/m + 1)^dim`. Consequently `T*_m(level) <= (base + 1)^dim fill^level m^(-alpha)` for every `m >= 2`, and `T*_m(level) = 0` for `m >= base^level`.

**Proof.** Split the digit string at position `h`: every `x in S_level` is uniquely `x = u + base^h w` with `u in S_h` and `w in S_(level-h)`. There are `fill^(level-h)` choices of `w`; fix one. Then `m | x_i` confines `u_i` to one residue class modulo `m`, and `u_i in [0, base^h)` takes at most `floor(base^h/m) + 1 <= base^h/m + 1` values in it; bounding `u` by the whole box gives at most `(base^h/m + 1)^dim` choices, the first bound. For `2 <= m < base^level` take `h = ceil(log_base m)`, so `1 <= h <= level` and `m <= base^h < base m`; then `base^h/m + 1 < base + 1`, and `fill^h = base^(h alpha) >= m^alpha`, so `fill^(level-h) <= fill^level m^(-alpha)`, and `T*_m(level) <= T_m(level)` gives the second bound. For `m >= base^level`, a nonzero `x in S_level` has a coordinate in `(0, base^level)`, which `m` cannot divide, so `T*_m(level) = 0`, and the second bound holds there trivially. □

The bound is the Ahlfors-David regularity of missing-digit sets, recorded as known by Chow, Varju and Yu (2024), used here to close a sieve in `dim` coordinates coupled through `F`.

**Lemma 5.2 (the Chebyshev sum).** If `fill > base`, that is `alpha > 1`, then for every `level >= 1`, `G(level) = sum_(x in S_level, x != 0) log gcd(x) = sum_(m >= 2) Lambda(m) T*_m(level) <= (base + 1)^dim (-zeta'(alpha)/zeta(alpha)) fill^level`.

**Proof.** For a positive integer `g`, `sum_(m | g) Lambda(m) = log g`. Summing over the nonzero `x in S_level` and exchanging the two sums, which is legitimate since every term is nonnegative and only `m < base^level` contribute by Lemma 5.1, gives the equality. Lemma 5.1 then gives `G(level) <= (base + 1)^dim fill^level sum_(m >= 2) Lambda(m) m^(-alpha)`, and `sum_m Lambda(m) m^(-s) = -zeta'(s)/zeta(s)` for real `s > 1`. □

This is the only step that uses `fill > base`, and it uses it sharply: at `alpha = 1` the series `sum_m Lambda(m)/m` diverges and the argument gives nothing.

## The proof, and what it settles

**Proof of Theorem 1.1.** Let `P(z)` be the product of the primes up to `z` and let `A_z(level)` count the `x in S_level` such that no prime `p <= z` divides every coordinate of `x`. By inclusion and exclusion over those primes, `A_z(level) = sum_(d | P(z)) mu(d) T_d(level)`, a finite sum over squarefree `d`. The origin, if it lies in `S_level`, is counted by every `T_d(level)` and contributes `sum_(d | P(z)) mu(d) = 0`, as it should, since every prime divides it.

Step 1, the sifted count converges at fixed `z`. Take `z` at least the largest prime of `base`. Each `d | P(z)` is uniquely `d = e m` with `e | rad(base)` and `m` prime to `base`, and as `d` runs over the divisors of `P(z)`, `e` runs over all divisors of `rad(base)` and `m` over the squarefree products of the primes up to `z` that do not divide `base`, with `mu(d) = mu(e) mu(m)`. Corollary 4.3 gives `T_d(level)/fill^level -> (fill_e/fill) m^(-dim)`, and summing the finitely many terms, `A_z(level)/fill^level -> (sum_(e | rad(base)) mu(e) fill_e/fill) (sum_m mu(m) m^(-dim)) = B(F) prod_(p <= z, p not dividing base) (1 - p^(-dim)) = delta_z`.

Step 2, the sifted count overshoots by little, uniformly in `level`. A point with greatest common divisor `1` is counted by `A_z(level)`. A point counted by `A_z(level)` but not by `A(level)` is nonzero, and its greatest common divisor exceeds `1` with every prime factor above `z`, so its logarithm exceeds `log z`. Hence `0 <= A_z(level) - A(level) <= G(level)/log z <= C fill^level/log z` with `C = (base + 1)^dim (-zeta'(alpha)/zeta(alpha))`, and `alpha = log(fill)/log(base)`, by Lemma 5.2, which is where `fill > base` enters.

Step 3, close. Letting `level -> infinity` at fixed `z`, `delta_z - C/log z <= liminf A(level)/fill^level <= limsup A(level)/fill^level <= delta_z`. As `z -> infinity`, `delta_z -> delta`, the product converging absolutely because `dim >= 2`, and `C/log z -> 0`. So `A(level)/fill^level -> delta`. The two forms of `delta` agree because `prod_p (1 - p^(-dim)) = 1/zeta(dim)`, and when `B(F) = 0` every `delta_z` is `0` and the same sandwich gives the limit `0`. □

Steps 1 and 3 use no hypothesis on `fill`, so `limsup A(level)/fill^level <= delta` holds for every design that satisfies condition (E); the dimension enters only through the lower bound.

Each `fill` exceeds its `base`. The brackets are Corollary 3.2: the zero vector is the one digit vector of the gasket, the carpet and the sponge whose greatest common divisor shares a prime with the base, the or-triangle and the plus have none, the base-6 pair has only `(0,0)`, and the base-6 sample is Corollary 3.3. Then `delta = B(F) zeta(dim)^(-1) prod_(p | base) (1 - p^(-dim))^(-1)` with `zeta(2) = Pi^2/6`: for the gasket `(2/3)(6/Pi^2)(4/3)`, for the carpet `(7/8)(6/Pi^2)(9/8)`, for the sponge `(19/20)(27/26)/zeta(3)`, and for the base-6 sample `(1/2)(6/Pi^2)(4/3)(9/8)`. □

The gasket's sequence `A(level)` is [A396934](https://oeis.org/A396934), `2, 4, 12, 34, 122, 362, 1130, 3406, 10506, 31550, 95260` from level 1, and the sponge's is [A399364](https://oeis.org/A399364), `12, 270, 5916, 123504, 2538447, 51497040` from level 1. The Vicsek plus omits the zero vector, so no point of it has a greatest common divisor divisible by 3 and its density exceeds `6/Pi^2`.

**Theorem 6.2 (`fill > base` alone is not enough).** Let `base = 3`, `dim = 2` and `F = {0, 2}^2`, the Cantor dust. Then `fill = 4 > 3` and the dimension is `log_3 4 = 1.2618...`, but `A(level) = 0` at every `level >= 1`, while `delta = 81/(16 Pi^2) = 0.512938492189... > 0`.

**Proof.** Every digit is even, so every coordinate of every point is even and no greatest common divisor is `1`. Here `Diff = 2 Z^2` has index `4` and `2` does not divide `3`, so condition (E) fails. For the prediction, only `(0,0)` among the four digit vectors has a greatest common divisor sharing a prime with `3`, so `B(F) = 3/4` and `delta = (3/4)(6/Pi^2)(9/8) = 81/(16 Pi^2)`. □

So the dust says that `fill > base` alone fails; it does not say that spanning is needed, and condition (E) is what Lemma 4.1 actually uses. The failure is not a dead end either.

**Theorem 6.3 (the dilation repair).** With `F = {0, 2}^2` at base 3 as in Theorem 6.2, the number of `x in S_level(F)` with `gcd(x) = 2`, divided by `4^level`, tends to `81/(16 Pi^2)`.

**Proof.** Let `G = {0, 1}^2` at base 3. Digitwise, `g -> 2 g` maps `G` onto `F` with no carries, since `2 * 1 < 3`, so `y -> 2 y` is a bijection from `S_level(G)` onto `S_level(F)` with `gcd(2 y) = 2 gcd(y)`; thus `gcd(x) = 2` for `x = 2 y` exactly when `gcd(y) = 1`. The design `G` contains the zero vector and the unit vectors, has `fill = 4 > 3`, and only `(0,0)` among its digit vectors fails Corollary 3.2, so `B(G) = 3/4`, and Theorem 1.1 applies to `G`. □

The dust is the theory read on the sublattice `2 Z^2`: the constant survives, one prime down.

**Theorem 6.4 (the C-finite obstruction).** Let `A(level)` be a sequence of integers and `fill >= 2` an integer with `A(level)/fill^level -> delta` and `delta` irrational. Then `A` satisfies no linear recurrence with constant rational coefficients, of any order.

**Proof.** Suppose it does. Over a splitting field `K`, a subfield of `C`, of the characteristic polynomial, `A(level) = sum_g P_g(level) g^level` for every `level` beyond some point, a finite sum over the distinct nonzero roots `g` with polynomials `P_g` over `K`; a zero root changes finitely many terms only. The representation is unique, since the sequences `level^j g^level` are linearly independent. Put `u(level) = A(level)/fill^level`, which tends to `delta` and is bounded. One averaging fact does all the work: for unimodular `w != w_0`, `(1/N) sum_(n < N) (w conj(w_0))^n -> 0`, so if `s(n) = sum_(w in W) c_w w^n + o(1)` over a finite set `W` of distinct unimodular numbers and `s(n) -> L`, then `c_w = 0` for every `w != 1` and `c_1 = L` (with `c_1 = 0` when `1` is not in `W`).

First, no root outside the circle `|g| = fill` survives and no polynomial on it is nonconstant. Let `R` be the largest `|g|` with `P_g != 0`, there being one since otherwise `A` is eventually `0` and `delta = 0`, and `j` the largest degree of `P_g` over the roots of modulus `R`, and suppose `R > fill`, or `R = fill` and `j >= 1`. Then `u(level)/(level^j (R/fill)^level) = sum c_g (g/R)^level + o(1)`, the sum over the roots of modulus `R` and degree `j` with `c_g` the leading coefficients, not all zero; the left side tends to `0` since `u` is bounded and `level^j (R/fill)^level -> infinity`, so the averaging fact makes every `c_g` zero, a contradiction. So the roots of modulus above `fill` carry nothing, those of modulus `fill` carry constants `c_g`, and those inside contribute `o(1)`.

Second, the circle collapses to the point `fill`. Now `u(level) = sum_(|g| = fill) c_g (g/fill)^level + o(1) -> delta`, so the averaging fact gives `c_g = 0` for `g != fill` and `delta = c_fill`.

Third, Galois. The characteristic polynomial has rational coefficients, so every automorphism `sigma` of `K` over `Q` permutes its roots, and `sigma` fixes the rational sequence `A`. Applying `sigma` to the coefficients of the representation gives another representation of `A`, so by uniqueness `sigma(P_g) = P_(sigma(g))`; as `sigma` fixes the rational root `fill`, it fixes `c_fill`. So `c_fill` is rational, and `delta = c_fill` is not. □

**Corollary 6.5 (census sequences are not C-finite).** Let a design satisfy condition (E) with `fill > base` and `B(F) > 0`. If `dim` is even or `dim = 3`, then `A(level)` satisfies no linear recurrence with constant rational coefficients; in particular [A396934](https://oeis.org/A396934) does not.

**Proof.** By Theorem 1.1, `A(level)/fill^level -> delta = B(F) zeta(dim)^(-1) prod_(p | base) (1 - p^(-dim))^(-1)`, a nonzero rational multiple of `1/zeta(dim)` since `B(F) > 0`. For even `dim`, Euler's evaluation makes `zeta(dim)` a nonzero rational multiple of `Pi^dim`, and `Pi^dim` is irrational because `Pi` is transcendental (Lindemann 1882). For `dim = 3`, `zeta(3)` is irrational (Apery 1979). Apply Theorem 6.4. □

The hypothesis `B(F) > 0` is needed: when `B(F) = 0` the limit is `0`, and a design with `A(level) = 0` satisfies every recurrence. Corollary 6.5 says nothing at odd `dim >= 5`, where the irrationality of `zeta(dim)` is not known, nothing at `fill <= base`, where Theorem 1.1 is not proved, and nothing about recurrences with polynomial coefficients.

## Pairwise coprimality and the sponge

**Definition 7.1 (pairs, fibres, the sponge).** In this section `base` is a prime and `F` is a subset of `{0, ..., base - 1}^3` with `fill = |F| >= 2`, with `S_level` as in Definition 2.1. A prime `p` spoils a point `x` when it divides two of its three coordinates, and `P_level(F)` is the share of the `fill^level` points of `S_level` that no prime spoils, those with `gcd(x_1, x_2) = gcd(x_1, x_3) = gcd(x_2, x_3) = 1` under `gcd(0, n) = n`. The pair bracket `B_2(F)` is the share of the digit vectors of `F` with at most one coordinate `0`. For a set `I` of coordinates, `kappa_I` is the largest number of digit vectors of `F` that agree on the coordinates in `I`, and `alpha_I = log(fill/kappa_I)/log(base)`. The Menger sponge is `base = 3` with `F` the 20 vectors of `{0, 1, 2}^3` having at most one coordinate `1`, and `P_level` is `P_level(F)` for it.

![Three 3 by 3 panels set down the diagonal of a square, one for each value c = 0, 1, 2 of the third digit, the cell in row a and column b of panel c standing for the digit vector (a, b, c). The seven vectors with two or more coordinates equal to 1, which the sponge deletes, are drawn as empty outlines. Of the twenty it keeps, the thirteen with at most one coordinate 0 are green and the seven with two or more zeros are dim: a point whose last digit vector is dim has two coordinates divisible by 3. The figure counts 20 kept and 13 green before drawing.](paper-menger-pairwise-coprimality)

For the sponge `kappa_I = 3` for each pair `I` and `kappa_I = 8` for each single coordinate (Theorem 1.2's proof below), so `alpha_I = log_3(20/3) = 1.7268...` on a pair against `log_3(20/8) = 0.8340...` on one coordinate. The sponge also never counts a point with a zero coordinate: if `x_1 = 0`, pairwise coprimality forces `x_2 = x_3 = 1`, so the last digit vector is `(0, 1, 1)`, which the sponge deletes; the convention `gcd(0, n) = n` is therefore immaterial for it, though `x_1 = 0` holds on the share `(2/5)^level` of its points, eight of its twenty digit vectors having first coordinate `0`.

**Theorem 7.2 (pairwise coprimality above the pair bar).** Let `base` be prime and let `F` satisfy condition (E) with `fill > base kappa_I` for each of the three pairs `I` of coordinates, that is `alpha_I > 1`. Then `P_level(F) -> B_2(F) prod_(p != base) (1 - 3/p^2 + 2/p^3)`. Moreover `B_2(F)` is exactly the share of `S_level` that `base` does not spoil, at every `level >= 1`.

**Lemma 7.3 (the fibred box bound).** Let `I` be a pair of coordinates, `2 <= m <= base^level` and `h = ceil(log_base m)`, so `1 <= h <= level`. Then the number of `x in S_level` with `m | x_i` for both `i in I` is at most `fill^(level-h) kappa_I^h (base^h/m + 1)^2 <= (base + 1)^2 fill^level m^(-alpha_I)`.

**Proof.** Split each coordinate at the `h`-th digit, `x_i = u_i + base^h w_i` with `u_i in [0, base^h)`. Choose the top `level - h` digit vectors first, in `fill^(level-h)` ways; they fix every `w_i`. For `i in I`, `m | x_i` confines `u_i` to one residue class modulo `m`, which meets `[0, base^h)` in at most `base^h/m + 1` integers, so the pair `(u_i)_(i in I)` takes at most `(base^h/m + 1)^2` values; no coprimality of `m` and `base` is needed here. Fix such a pair. By unique expansion it fixes the coordinates in `I` of each of the low `h` digit vectors, and each of these is then one of at most `kappa_I` vectors of `F`, so at most `kappa_I^h` low strings remain. Multiplying gives the first bound. For the second, `m <= base^h < base m` gives `base^h/m + 1 < base + 1`, and `fill^(level-h) kappa_I^h = fill^level (kappa_I/fill)^h = fill^level base^(-h alpha_I) <= fill^level m^(-alpha_I)`, using `base^h >= m` and `alpha_I > 0`. □

**Lemma 7.4 (equidistribution with the last digit).** Let `F` satisfy condition (E), let `M >= 2` with `gcd(M, base) = 1`, and put `phi_M(t) = (1/fill) sum_(v in F) e(<t, v>/M)` for `t in (Z/M)^3`. Then `theta_M = max_(t != 0) |phi_M(t)| < 1`, and for `x` uniform on `S_level` with last digit vector `v_0`, every `w in F`, every `r in (Z/M)^3` and every `level >= 1`, `|Prob(v_0 = w, x = r mod M) - 1/(fill M^3)| <= theta_M^(level-1)/fill`.

**Proof.** `|phi_M(t)| = 1` only if all the unit numbers `e(<t, v>/M)` coincide, that is only if `t` vanishes on `F - F`, and the first step of Lemma 4.1 then forces `t = 0`; the maximum over the finitely many nonzero `t` is below `1`. Given `v_0 = w`, an event of probability `1/fill`, `x = w + base y` with `y` uniform on `S_(level-1)`, and for `t != 0`, `E e(<t, y>/M) = prod_(j < level-1) phi_M(base^j t)` with `base^j t != 0`, so its modulus is at most `theta_M^(level-1)`. Fourier inversion on `(Z/M)^3` gives `|Prob(y = s mod M) - M^(-3)| <= ((M^3 - 1)/M^3) theta_M^(level-1)` for every `s`, and `s -> w + base s` is a bijection of `(Z/M)^3`. Multiply by `1/fill`. □

**Lemma 7.5 (the uniform tail).** Let `alpha = min_I alpha_I > 1`, the minimum over the three pairs, and for an integer `z >= 2` let `E_level(z)` be the share of `S_level` spoiled by some prime `p > z`. Then `E_level(z) <= 3 (base + 1)^2 z^(1-alpha)/(alpha - 1) + 3 (max_I kappa_I/fill)^level`.

**Proof.** First the points with `x_i = x_j = 0` for some pair `I = {i, j}`: each of their digit vectors has `0` on both coordinates of `I`, so there are at most `kappa_I^level` of them for each pair, the second term. Any other spoiled point has a prime `p > z` dividing `x_i` and `x_j` for some pair, not both zero, so `p <= max(x_i, x_j) < base^level` and Lemma 7.3 applies with `m = p`. A union bound over the primes and the pairs bounds the share of these points by `sum_I (base + 1)^2 sum_(z < p < base^level) p^(-alpha_I) <= 3 (base + 1)^2 sum_(n > z) n^(-alpha)`, and `sum_(n > z) n^(-alpha) <= integral_z^infinity t^(-alpha) dt = z^(1-alpha)/(alpha - 1)` for integer `z`. □

**Proof of Theorem 7.2.** Fix an integer `z >= max(base, 3)` and let `M` be the product of the primes `p <= z` other than `base`, so `M >= 2` and `gcd(M, base) = 1`; the bound `z >= base` puts the prime `base` among those sifted. Let `A_level(z)` be the share of `S_level` spoiled by no prime `p <= z`. A point counted by `P_level(F)` is counted by `A_level(z)`, and one counted by `A_level(z)` but not by `P_level(F)` is spoiled by a prime above `z`, so `A_level(z) - E_level(z) <= P_level(F) <= A_level(z)`.

Step 1, the event lives at `(base, M)`. The prime `base` spoils `x` exactly when two coordinates of `x` are `0` modulo `base`; since every higher digit carries a factor `base`, `x = v_0 mod base`, and a digit is `0` modulo `base` exactly when it is `0`, so this reads the last digit vector alone: `base` spoils `x` exactly when `v_0` has two or more zero coordinates. A prime `p | M` spoils `x` according to `x mod M` alone. So the event counted by `A_level(z)` is a union of pairs `(w, r)` in `F x (Z/M)^3`.

Step 2, its limit. Summing Lemma 7.4 over the at most `fill M^3` pairs, `|A_level(z) - B_2(F) #R/M^3| <= M^3 theta_M^(level-1) -> 0`, where `R` is the set of `r in (Z/M)^3` with at most one `r_i = 0 mod p` for each prime `p | M`. By the Chinese remainder theorem `#R/M^3` is a product over `p | M`, and at each `p` inclusion and exclusion over the three events that coordinates `i` and `j` both vanish modulo `p`, each of density `p^(-2)`, with every pairwise and triple intersection the event that all three vanish, of density `p^(-3)`, gives the union the density `3 p^(-2) - 3 p^(-3) + p^(-3) = 3/p^2 - 2/p^3`. So `A_level(z) -> B_2(F) prod_(p <= z, p != base) (1 - 3/p^2 + 2/p^3) = Q(z)`.

Step 3, the order of limits. Letting `level -> infinity` at fixed `z` with Lemma 7.5, whose second term vanishes because `max_I kappa_I < fill`, gives `Q(z) - 3 (base + 1)^2 z^(1-alpha)/(alpha - 1) <= liminf P_level(F) <= limsup P_level(F) <= Q(z)`. As `z -> infinity` the subtracted term vanishes because `alpha > 1`, and `Q(z)` converges because `sum_p 3/p^2 < infinity`. The last sentence of the theorem is Step 1: the last digit vector is uniform on `F` and the others are free. □

**Proof of Theorem 1.2.** The sponge contains the zero vector and the three unit vectors, so it is spanning, hence satisfies (E). Fix a pair `I` and a target `w` for its two coordinates: if `w` has no `1`, the third coordinate is free in `{0, 1, 2}`; if `w` has one `1`, the third lies in `{0, 2}`; and `w = (1, 1)` is never attained. So `kappa_I = 3` and `fill = 20 > 9 = base kappa_I`. On one coordinate the fibre over `0` or `2` is `{0, 1, 2}^2` without `(1, 1)`, of size 8, and over `1` it is `{0, 2}^2`, so `kappa = 8` there. Of the twenty vectors, seven have two or more zero coordinates, the zero vector and the six with one nonzero coordinate, so `B_2 = 13/20`, and Theorem 7.2 gives the limit `(13/20) prod_(p != 3) (1 - 3/p^2 + 2/p^3)`. The factor of `C_3` at `3` is `1 - 3/9 + 2/27 = 20/27`, so the limit is `(13/20)(27/20) C_3 = (351/400) C_3`, and `1 - 351/400 = 49/400`. □

**Corollary 7.6 (the full cube).** The share of the points of `[0, 3^level)^3` whose coordinates are pairwise coprime tends to `C_3 = (20/27) prod_(p != 3) (1 - 3/p^2 + 2/p^3)`.

**Proof.** The full design `{0, 1, 2}^3` at base 3 has `S_level = [0, 3^level)^3`, contains the zero and unit vectors, and has `kappa_I = 3` on every pair, so `27 > 9`; its pair bracket counts the 8 vectors of `{1, 2}^3` and the 12 with exactly one zero, `B_2 = 20/27`. Apply Theorem 7.2. □

So the comparison in Theorem 1.2 is of two instances of one theorem, and the factors of the two products agree at every prime except 3.

**Theorem 7.7 (the bias at two).** For every `level >= 0`, the share of the sponge's points with at most one even coordinate is exactly `beta_level = 1/2 - (3/4)(3/5)^level + (1/4)(-1/5)^level`, and a single coordinate is even with probability exactly `(1 + (3/5)^level)/2`. So `beta_level` rises to `1/2`, the factor of the limit at `p = 2`, geometrically with ratio `3/5`.

**Proof.** Since `3 = 1 mod 2`, the parity vector of `x` is `s = sum_(j < level) pi(v_j)` in `(Z/2)^3`, where `pi(v)` reduces `v` modulo 2; for `v in {0, 1, 2}^3` it marks the coordinates equal to `1`. So `pi(v) = 0` for the eight vectors with coordinates in `{0, 2}`, and `pi(v) = e_i` for the four vectors whose `i`-th coordinate is `1`, for each `i`; the `pi(v_j)` are independent with these weights `8, 4, 4, 4` out of `20`. For `t in {0, 1}^3` of weight `w`, `E (-1)^(<t, pi(v)>) = (8 + 4 sum_i (-1)^(t_i))/20 = (5 - 2w)/5`, so `E (-1)^(<t, s>) = lambda_w^level` with `lambda_0 = 1`, `lambda_1 = 3/5`, `lambda_2 = 1/5`, `lambda_3 = -1/5`. A coordinate is even when `s_i = 0`, and `t = e_i` gives `Prob(x_i even) = (1 + (3/5)^level)/2`. At most one coordinate is even exactly when `s` has weight at least 2. By Fourier inversion that probability is `(1/8) sum_t g(t) lambda_(w(t))^level` with `g(t) = sum_(weight(s) >= 2) (-1)^(<t, s>) = 8 [t = 0] - (4 - 2 w(t))`, since the vectors of weight at most one contribute `1 + sum_i (-1)^(t_i) = 4 - 2 w(t)`; so `g = 4, -2, 0, 2` at `w = 0, 1, 2, 3`, with multiplicities `1, 3, 3, 1`, and `beta_level = (1/8)(4 - 6 (3/5)^level + 2 (-1/5)^level)`. □

The prime 2 spoils `x` exactly when two coordinates are even, so `beta_level` is exactly the share of `S_level` that 2 does not spoil, and its limit `1/2 = 1 - 3/4 + 2/8` is the factor at 2 of Theorem 1.2's product.

The fibring is what carries the theorem. A box bound fibred over one coordinate, counting the points with `m | x_i` for a single `i`, leaves fibres of 8 digit vectors and the exponent `log_3(20/8) = 0.834 < 1`, so its tail series diverges and the large primes are not controlled; a pair leaves fibres of 3 and `1.7268 > 1`. The criterion `fill > base kappa_I` names exactly the box bound fibred over a proper set of coordinates.

## What is in print

The density `1/zeta(dim)` of the visible points of the full lattice `Z^dim` is classical. The one-dimensional theory of integers with restricted digits studies their distribution in residue classes to moduli that grow with the number of digits: Erdos, Mauduit and Sarkozy (1998), Konyagin (2001), and Maynard (2019) on the way to primes with a missing digit. A uniformity of that kind for vector digit sets, at dimension one, is what the coprime note calls Lemma B, and the route of Theorem 1.1 avoids needing it at the price of `fill > base`. The digit-box bound of Lemma 5.1 is the Ahlfors-David regularity of missing-digit sets, recorded as known by Chow, Varju and Yu (2024); the same paper, read at source, records in its Remark 6.1 that every base-3 missing-digit measure on two digits has Fourier `l^1` dimension below `1/2`, which the coprime note reads as closing the componentwise route at dimension one. The fibred bound of Lemma 7.3 is the same counting with the low digits confined to a fibre. The C-finite obstruction uses only the exponential-polynomial form of a linear recurrence and a Galois argument, with the transcendence of `Pi` (Lindemann 1882) and the irrationality of `zeta(3)` (Apery 1979) as inputs, both taken from their standard statements. `C_3` is the strongly carefree constant of [A065473](https://oeis.org/A065473); Corollary 7.6 derives its reading as the pairwise coprimality of the cube `[0, 3^level)^3` from Theorem 7.2 and cites nothing.

## Open problems

The theorems above close every design of dimension above one that satisfies condition (E), and the sponge's pairs. Two conjectures and three questions remain, the first conjecture being the standing problem of the coprime note.

**Conjecture 10.1 (dimension one).** Every design that satisfies condition (E) with `fill = base` has `A(level)/fill^level -> delta`. By the remark after the proof of Theorem 1.1 the upper bound holds already, so the conjecture is the lower bound, which follows from the coprime note's Lemma B: `lim_z limsup_level fill^(-level) sum_(p > z, p not dividing base) T*_p(level) = 0`. At base 3 in dimension 2 the note reduces Lemma B on all 36 lines of Fact 8.2 with `fill = base`, the 32 spanning and the 4 of index 3, to the equidistribution of the base-3 gasket's points in shifted residue classes, so that equidistribution would settle all 36. For Lemma G, the gasket case of Lemma B, [the pincer paper](lemma-b-pincer.md) proves the estimate at every prime exponent `log_3 p/level` in `(0.6402122, 1]` and, quoting the coprime note's moment ladder, in `(0, 0.4475978]` (its Corollary 1.3), so only the window `(0.4475978, 0.6402122]` remains; by the note, the ladder holds for every shifted class while the upper edge reaches only the lines whose class is zero, and a proof of the conjecture at base 3 has to close that window. The evidence beyond it is Fact 8.2: the 32 spanning lines sit within `0.06` of their predicted densities at level 11. The conjecture dies on one line whose `liminf` falls below `delta`; nothing is known here below dimension one, at `fill < base`, where the box bound loses even more.

**Conjecture 10.2 (the sponge's pairs approach at rate `3/5`).** There is a constant `c > 0` with `(5/3)^level (delta_M - P_level) -> c`, `delta_M = (351/400) C_3`, so the approach from below is geometric with the ratio `3/5` of the prime 2. The evidence: the factor at 2 has exactly that rate (Theorem 7.7); replacing it by its level-`level` value predicts shares below the true ones at levels 5 and 6, so it alone accounts for the whole deficit there and overshoots it, the other primes pulling the other way (Fact 8.3); the deficit ratios `0.404, 0.712, 0.690, 0.523, 0.575` straddle `3/5`; and the character rate at every prime `p <= 19` other than 2 and 3 is below `3/5` (Fact 8.4). It is in doubt if a larger prime has a character rate above `3/5`; the tail bound of Lemma 7.5 decays in `z` and not in `level`, so it cannot see an error term at all, and a proof needs a tail bound decaying in both; six levels cannot separate `3/5` from a nearby ratio; and `(5/3)^level (delta_M - P_level)` could oscillate in a band without converging.

The questions. First, Theorem 1.1 is qualitative, while the gaps of Fact 8.1 shrink geometrically for the carpet and the sponge and change sign for the Vicsek plus; what sets the rate, and is it a property of the digits alone? Second, Corollary 6.5 stops at odd `dim >= 5`, where it waits on the irrationality of `zeta(dim)`. Third, nothing here excludes a recurrence with polynomial coefficients: is `A(level)` holonomic for some design of dimension above one?

## Reproducibility

The numbers of Section 8 are printed by three crates and asserted by one figure, each run from the repository root; the theorems and their proofs print no number beyond the arithmetic written out in them. `cargo run --release --manifest-path research/coprime/lab/rs/dimension-one-ladder/Cargo.toml -- terms carpet 12`, and the same with `terms vicsek 12` and `terms menger 10`, each in about a second once built, print the live terms, ratios, gaps and gap ratios of Fact 8.1 against the stored ladders, then the stored levels past the live ones to levels 20, 20 and 18 with their gaps, and the brute-force agreement. `cargo run --release --manifest-path research/bijection/lab/rs/oeis-terms/Cargo.toml`, in about forty seconds on eight cores, prints the gasket's terms to level 20 by the row walk with its two cross-checks and the gap `-3.6e-6`. `cargo run --release --manifest-path research/coprime/lab/rs/design-census/Cargo.toml`, in about half a minute, prints the family table, the split by `fill` against `base` and the per-line rows of the sponge and the two base-6 designs of Facts 8.1 and 8.2. The figure is `figures/paper-coprime-density-above-dimension-one.ts`, drawn in the browser in about a quarter of a second cold and milliseconds warm: before drawing the gasket it recomputes the bracket of each of the eight designs of Corollary 6.1 and Theorem 6.2 both as a share of digit vectors and by Mobius inversion and asserts each predicted density to the twelve digits printed, from `zeta(2)` and `zeta(3)` of `@mrlyprod/mrly`; enumerates the base-6 sample at levels 1 to 6 and asserts that exactly half the points have greatest common divisor prime to 6; counts the sponge's pairwise coprime points and its points with at most one even coordinate at levels 1 to 6 against Fact 8.3 and Theorem 7.7 on the integer, with the deficits, their ratios and the two parity predictions; asserts the fibre sizes, the exponents `log_3(20/3)`, `log_3(20/8)` and `log_3 4` to the digits printed, the counts 13 of 20 and 20 of 27, the prefix of `351/400` of the thirty digits of A065473, and the seven character rates of Fact 8.4. The second figure, `figures/paper-menger-pairwise-coprimality.ts`, counts the sponge's 20 digit vectors and the 13 with at most one zero.
