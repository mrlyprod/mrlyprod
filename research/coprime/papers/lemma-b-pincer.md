Take the base-3 Sierpinski gasket at level `n`, the `3^n` lattice points whose base-3 digit pairs all lie in `F = {(0,0), (1,0), (0,1)}`, and ask how often a point's two coordinates are coprime. Above dimension one the answer is a theorem; at dimension one, where the gasket lives, one estimate is missing, and it is missing only for primes of one size. Measure a prime against the level by its exponent `beta = log_3 p / n`. This paper proves the missing estimate, Lemma G, at every `beta > 1/(2 - log_3 phi) = 0.6402121937...`, `phi` the golden ratio ([A001622](https://oeis.org/A001622)), with no hypothesis and no computation in the proof; with the moment ladder of [the coprime note](../notes/the-window-moments-and-rays.md), which closes every `beta <= 0.4475978`, Lemma G can fail only for `beta in (0.4475978, 0.6402122]`. The upper edge comes from one combinatorial injection, the burst certificate, which bounds the growth `rho(a, b)` of every one of the infinitely many ray automata of the gasket by `phi`. Two impossibility results follow: keeping the exact Fibonacci product in place of `phi` cannot move the edge, and an average of the radii cannot stand in for their maximum. An exact census of the 18056 rays of height at most `243` up to the swap finds the mass rate of every ray off the shifts at most the supergolden ratio `1.4655712...`, attained on exactly six of them; the edge a uniform form of that bound would give, `0.605303`, is open (Conjecture 8.2).

## Introduction

Above dimension one the coprime density of a digital design is a routine sieve, and the reason it is routine is exactly the reason it stops at a line. A prime `p` in the greatest common divisor confines a point to `p Z^2`, whose share of a design of dimension `D` is of order `p^(-D)`; summing over the large primes costs `sum_(p > z) p^(-D)`, small for large `z` when `D > 1` and divergent for every `z` when `D = 1`. The gasket is the canonical design of dimension one, and what blocks the sieve there is one estimate: the points whose coordinates a large prime divides must be few, summed over all large primes at once. For a general design that estimate is Lemma B of the coprime note. Only its gasket case is at stake here, and it gets its own name, Lemma G (Definition 2.6).

The estimate is easy for small `p` and for enormous `p` and hard in between, and the middle is named by the exponent `beta`. From below, the Fourier moment ladder of the coprime note closes every `beta <= 0.447597813453`; it shares no object with this paper and is quoted as Lemma 2.7, not reproduced. From above, an automaton argument with no Fourier analysis in it closes every `beta > 1/(2 - log_3 phi)`. Every nonzero point of the gasket off the axes is uniquely `g (a, b)` with `(a, b)` coprime, a ray, and a large prime in the greatest common divisor forces a short ray, so the top range is a count of the multiples of short rays inside the gasket. That count is run by a finite automaton on the base-3 digits of `g`, whose state is the pair of carries of `g a` and `g b`, and the whole top range hinges on one bound for its spectral radius `rho(a, b)`. There are infinitely many rays and the automaton of `(a, b)` has `ab` states, so no finite computation can supply it.

**Theorem 1.1 (the burst certificate).** Let `(a, b)` be a ray of class div or opp with parameter `k` (Definition 2.4). Then at every state `s` and every `w >= 1`,

```
P_w(s) <= D_k(w) = prod_(r = 1..k) F(m_r + 2) ,     m_r = #{i in {1, ..., w} : i = r mod k} ,
```

with `F(1) = F(2) = 1` the Fibonacci numbers ([A000045](https://oeis.org/A000045)), and so `P_w(s) <= phi^(w+k)`. For class eq, `P_w(s) <= 1`. Hence `rho(a, b) <= phi` for every ray, at every 3-adic depth, with equality on the shift rays `(1, 3^k)` and `(3^k, 1)`, `k >= 1` (Lemma 3.5).

Each carry state admits `2`, `1` or `0` digits, read off a single residue. When `3^k` divides the coordinate divisible by `3` (class div) or the coordinate sum (class opp), the type of a state is fixed `k` steps in advance, and the two children of a branching state are forced into different types exactly `k` steps downstream: a free choice today costs a forced move on day `k`. Recording the free choices injects the admissible paths of length `w` into the subsets of `{1, ..., w}` with no two elements at distance exactly `k`, and those are counted by a product of Fibonacci numbers.

**Theorem 1.2 (the upper edge).** For `0 < c < 1` let `N_top(n, c)` count the nonzero points of `G_n` whose two coordinates share a prime factor `p > 3^((1-c) n)`, and put `psi(c) = 2c + (1 - c) log_3 phi`. Then at every `n >= 1`

```
N_top(n, c) <= 2^(n+1) + 49 * 3^(n psi(c)) .
```

Since `psi(c) < 1` exactly when `c < c* = (1 - log_3 phi)/(2 - log_3 phi) = 0.3597878...`, Lemma G holds on `(beta', 1]` for every `beta' > 1 - c* = 1/(2 - log_3 phi) = 0.6402121937...`, with no hypothesis.

**Corollary 1.3 (the pincer).** Lemma G holds on `(0, 0.4475978]` and on `(0.6402122, 1]`, so it holds if and only if it holds on the window `(0.4475978, 0.6402122]`. The upper edge is Theorem 1.2 and is proved here in full; the lower edge is the moment ladder of the coprime note, quoted as Lemma 2.7. Both edges are unconditional, and both are rounded toward the window: `0.4475978` is the tenth rung `0.447597813454...` truncated down and `0.6402122` is `0.6402121937...` rounded up, so the window is never printed smaller than it is proved.

**Theorem 1.4 (closure one: the exact Fibonacci product does not help).** For `0 < c < 1` let

```
B_n(c) = 2^(n+1) + sum_(0 <= j < cn) sum_(r in octave j) b_r(n - j) ,     b_r(w) = D_k(w) on a ray of class div or opp with parameter k ,  b_r(w) = 1 on class eq ,
```

the bound of the proof of Theorem 1.2 with the exact per-ray bound of Theorem 1.1 kept in place of `phi^(w+k)` and the true set of rays of each octave in place of the census. Then `N_top(n, c) <= B_n(c)`, and there is `n_0` such that `B_n(c) >= 3^(n psi(c) - 4)` at every `n >= n_0`. So `B_n(c) = o(3^n)` fails at `c = c*` and at every `c > c*`, and the threshold this chain of inequalities yields is exactly `1/(2 - log_3 phi)`. This is a statement about the relaxation and not a lower bound on the true mass of the rays.

**Theorem 1.5 (closure two: averaging the radii does not help).** Let `nu` be a probability measure on rays with `nu({r_0}) = q > 0` at a ray `r_0` with `rho(r_0) = phi`, as every shift ray is. Then at every `w >= 1`

```
int rho^w d nu >= max( (int rho d nu)^w , q phi^w ) ,
```

and `(int rho^w d nu)^(1/w) -> phi` as `w -> infinity`. Moreover the inverse-square weight is no such measure: over the rays of height at most `H >= 2`, `sum 1/(a^2 + b^2) > (log H)/4`, so it has infinite mass and every mean taken with it is a statement about a height cutoff and not about rays.

Mass is what the octave sum of Theorem 1.2 adds, and the mass of a ray grows at most like `rho^w` (Lemma 2.3), so an average of `rho` in place of its supremum asserts the reverse of the inequality above; Remark 5.1 shows the substitution at work and why its output is no bound. The edge `0.6402121937...` is not known to be sharp, and the census of Fact 6.1 suggests how far it may sit from the truth. The shift rays grow exactly like `phi`, and over the 18056 rays of height at most `243` up to the swap the mass rate of every ray off the shifts is at most the supergolden ratio `1.4655712318768`, the real root of `x^3 = x^2 + 1` ([A092526](https://oeis.org/A092526)), attained on exactly six rays, `(1, 12)`, `(3, 10)`, `(4, 9)`, `(1, 90)`, `(9, 82)` and `(10, 81)`, the three ways of splitting `{1, 3, 9}` and of splitting `{1, 9, 81}` between the two coordinates. A uniform form of that bound would move the edge to `0.605303` (Conjecture 8.2). The census also refutes one reading of smaller scans: the mass rates are not all roots of `x^k = x^(k-1) + 1` or of `x^k = x + 1`, the first exception sitting at `(25, 57)`, of height `57`.

Two ingredients are classical and are labelled so: `D_k(w)` factors into Fibonacci numbers because it counts the independent sets of disjoint paths (Lemma 3.4), and `rho(A) <= (max_s (A^w 1)_s)^(1/w)` for a nonnegative matrix is the row-norm bound (Lemma 3.1). The contribution is the delayed split, Lemma 3.3, and the injection it powers, the octave census that turns it into an exponent, and the two closures.

## Definitions

**Definition 2.1 (the gasket).** For `n >= 0` let `G_n = {sum_(i < n) d_i 3^i : d_i in F}`, a subset of `Z^2`, with `F = {(0,0), (1,0), (0,1)}`. Then `|G_n| = 3^n`, the base-3 digit expansion of a point of `G_n` is unique, and both coordinates of a point are at most `(3^n - 1)/2`. A point is a fibre point when one of its coordinates is `0`; there are `2^(n+1) - 2` nonzero fibre points, since a point with second coordinate `0` has every digit in `{(0,0), (1,0)}`. `F` is symmetric under the swap of the coordinates, and so is `G_n`.

**Definition 2.2 (rays and their automata).** A ray is an ordered pair `(a, b)` of coprime positive integers. Its height is `max(a, b)`, and it lies in octave `j >= 0` when `3^j <= max(a, b) < 3^(j+1)`. Its mass at level `n` is `M_n(a, b) = #{g >= 1 : g (a, b) in G_n}`, and `M_n(a, b) = M_n(b, a)` by the swap. The ray automaton of `(a, b)` has the states `(c_1, c_2)` with `0 <= c_1 < a` and `0 <= c_2 < b`. A digit `d in {0, 1, 2}` is admissible at `(c_1, c_2)` when `((a d + c_1) mod 3, (b d + c_2) mod 3)` lies in `F`, and it moves the state to `(floor((a d + c_1)/3), floor((b d + c_2)/3))`, again a state since `floor((3a - 1)/3) < a`. Let `A` be the `0/1` transition matrix, `rho(a, b) = rho(A)` its spectral radius, and `P_w(s) = (A^w 1)_s` the number of admissible digit paths of length `w` from the state `s`. The automaton of `(b, a)` is that of `(a, b)` with the two carries swapped, so it has the same spectral radius and the same path counts.

**Lemma 2.3 (mass under the automaton).** For a ray of octave `j` and every `n > j`, `M_n(a, b) <= P_(n-j)(0)`, where `0` is the state `(0, 0)`.

**Proof.** If `g (a, b) in G_n` with `g >= 1`, then `g max(a, b) <= (3^n - 1)/2 < 3^n` and `max(a, b) >= 3^j` give `g < 3^(n-j)`, so `g = sum_(i < w) d_i 3^i` with `w = n - j` digits. Multiplying `g` by `a` and by `b` digit by digit from the least significant end, the two carries after digit `i` form a state `(c_1, c_2)`, and the `i`-th base-3 digits of `g a` and `g b` are `(a d_i + c_1) mod 3` and `(b d_i + c_2) mod 3`; they form the `i`-th digit pair of `g (a, b)`, which lies in `F`. So the digits of `g` spell an admissible path of length `w` from `0`, and distinct `g` spell distinct paths. □

**Definition 2.4 (class and parameter).** A ray `(a, b)` is of exactly one class:

- eq: `3` divides neither coordinate and `a = b mod 3`;
- opp: `3` divides neither coordinate and `a != b mod 3`, so `3 | a + b`; its parameter is `k = v_3(a + b) >= 1`;
- div: `3` divides exactly one coordinate; its parameter `k >= 1` is the 3-adic valuation of that coordinate.

Coprimality forbids `3` dividing both coordinates, so the classes exhaust the rays, and the swap preserves class and parameter. The branching type is `B = 0` for div and `B = 1` for opp. The shift rays are `(1, 3^k)` and `(3^k, 1)` for `k >= 1`, of class div with parameter `k`; `(1, 1)` is of class eq and is no shift.

**Definition 2.5 (distance-free subsets).** For `k, w >= 1`, `D_k(w)` is the number of subsets of `{1, ..., w}` in which no two elements differ by exactly `k`.

**Definition 2.6 (Lemma G and its exponent ranges).** For a prime `p` let `T_p(n)` count the nonzero points `x` of `G_n` with `p | gcd(x_1, x_2)`, the mass one prime carries in the greatest common divisor; the coprime note writes it `T*_p(level)`. The origin is left out because it lies in every class of every modulus. Lemma G is the statement

```
lim_(z -> infinity) limsup_(n -> infinity) 3^(-n) sum_(p > z) T_p(n) = 0 ,
```

and Lemma G holds on a range `I` in `(0, 1]` when the same limit, taken over the primes `p > z` with `log_3 p / n in I`, is `0`. A nonzero point of `G_n` has greatest common divisor below `3^n`, so only primes `p < 3^n` occur, every exponent lies in `(0, 1)`, and every sum is finite. Lemma B of the coprime note asserts the same vanishing for a general digital design, with its own point set and normalisation; Lemma G is its gasket case and is all that is meant below.

**Lemma 2.7 (the moment ladder, quoted).** Put `kappa = 3 - log_3 5`, let `lambda_10` be the largest real root of `x^4 - 7833 x^3 + 7916949 x^2 - 850684437 x + 13054946580`, and put `kappa_10 = 10 - log_3 lambda_10`, `Lambda_10 = 2 - kappa + 2 kappa_10` and `beta_0 = kappa_10 / Lambda_10`, the tenth rung. For every `eta in (0, beta_0)`, `z >= 5` and `n >= 1`,

```
3^(-n) sum_(z < p <= 3^((beta_0 - eta) n)) T_p(n) <= 2/z + 35 z^(1 - kappa) + 40 * 3^(-(kappa - 1) n/8) + 6 * 3^(-(Lambda_10/10) eta n) .
```

This is the moment ladder of the coprime note, section The window at dimension one, at moment order `10`: exact moment identities for the character sums of the gasket, a Holder bound over three blocks of digits whose block of order `10` is supplied by an energy cap on an exact carry matrix, and a dyadic summation over the primes. By Fact 6.2, `beta_0 > 0.447597813453`, so `eta = beta_0 - 0.447597813453` is positive, and since `kappa > 1` the right side tends to `2/z + 35 z^(1 - kappa)` as `n -> infinity` and then to `0` as `z -> infinity`: Lemma G holds on `(0, 0.447597813453]`, and so on `(0, 0.4475978]`. The note also proves the peak wall `beta_0^(2K) < 2/(3 + log_3 5) < 0.447931` for the rung of every moment order `2K`, so no moment order reaches `1/2`; it is used below only as a signpost.

**Remark 2.8 (scope).** Every statement here is about `G_n`. The coprime note reduces Lemma B on every dimension-one line to counts of gasket points in a shifted residue class; the ray count of Section 4 is a count at the zero class, so nothing here is claimed about the shifted classes and the reduction is not used.

## The burst certificate

**Lemma 3.1 (the window lemma; classical).** For a nonnegative square matrix `A` and every `w >= 1`, `rho(A) <= (max_s (A^w 1)_s)^(1/w)`.

**Proof.** `rho(A)^w = rho(A^w) <= ||A^w||_inf`, since the spectral radius is at most any induced norm, and for a nonnegative matrix the induced `inf`-norm is the largest row sum, `max_s (A^w 1)_s`. Take `w`-th roots. □

In the classes div and opp write the coordinate divisible by `3` first, which the swap allows, so that `3 | a` in class div. The type of a state `(c_1, c_2)` is `X mod 3`, where `X = c_1` in class div and `X = c_1 + c_2` in class opp.

**Lemma 3.2 (branching types).** Let `(c_1, c_2)` be a state of the automaton of a ray `(a, b)`. (i) Class div: the first output residue is `(a d + c_1) mod 3 = c_1 mod 3`, the type, whatever the digit; at type `2` no digit is admissible, at type `1` exactly one, and at type `0` exactly two, distinct mod `3`. (ii) Class opp: the two output residues sum to `(c_1 + c_2) mod 3`, the type, whatever the digit; at type `2` no digit is admissible, at type `0` exactly one, and at type `1` exactly two, distinct mod `3`. (iii) Class eq: at most one digit is admissible. So in the classes div and opp a state branches exactly at the type `B` and is dead exactly at the type `2`.

**Proof.** (i) `a d = 0 mod 3`, so the first residue `t = c_1 mod 3` is free of `d`. No element of `F` has first coordinate `2`, so `t = 2` kills the state. At `t = 1` the only element of `F` with first coordinate `1` is `(1, 0)`, so `d` must solve `b d = -c_2 mod 3`, and since `3` does not divide `b` exactly one digit does. At `t = 0` the elements `(0, 0)` and `(0, 1)` ask `b d = -c_2` and `b d = 1 - c_2 mod 3`, each solved by exactly one digit, and the two digits differ. (ii) The residues sum to `(a + b) d + c_1 + c_2 = c_1 + c_2 mod 3`, free of `d`. The elements of `F` have coordinate sums `0, 1, 1`, so type `2` kills the state. At type `0` only `(0, 0)` is possible: `a d = -c_1 mod 3` has exactly one solution since `3` does not divide `a`, and then the second residue is `0` automatically. At type `1` the elements `(1, 0)` and `(0, 1)` ask `a d = 1 - c_1` and `a d = -c_1 mod 3`, one digit each, and the two differ. (iii) The residues differ by `(a - b) d + c_1 - c_2 = c_1 - c_2 mod 3`, free of `d`. The elements of `F` have coordinate differences `0, 1, -1`, distinct mod `3`, so at most one element of `F` is reachable, and it fixes the first residue; since `3` does not divide `a`, `d -> (a d + c_1) mod 3` is a bijection and at most one digit qualifies. □

**Lemma 3.3 (the delayed split).** Let `(a, b)` be of class div or opp with parameter `k`, and put `u = a/3^k` in class div and `u = (a + b)/3^k` in class opp, so that `3` does not divide `u`. Let `X_i` be the value of `X` at step `i` of an admissible path with digits `d_i`. (i) For `1 <= j < k`, if the path is admissible through step `i + j - 1`, the type at step `i + j` is `floor(X_i/3^j) mod 3`, a function of the state at step `i` alone. (ii) If the path is admissible through step `i + k - 1`, the type at step `i + k` is `tau(i, d_i) = (u d_i + floor(X_i/3^k)) mod 3`, a function of the state at step `i` and the digit `d_i`; at a branching state the two admissible digits give distinct values of `tau`.

**Proof.** First, at every admissible step `X_(i+1) = 3^(k-1) u d_i + floor(X_i/3)`. In class div this is the move `c_1 -> floor((3^k u d + c_1)/3)` itself. In class opp, an admissible step with outputs `(r_1, r_2)` in `F` moves `c_1 -> (a d + c_1 - r_1)/3` and `c_2 -> (b d + c_2 - r_2)/3`, so `X_(i+1) = 3^(k-1) u d_i + (X_i - r_1 - r_2)/3`; and `r_1 + r_2` lies in `{0, 1}` and is `X_i mod 3` by Lemma 3.2(ii), so `(X_i - r_1 - r_2)/3 = floor(X_i/3)`. Next, for `0 <= j <= k`, if the path is admissible through step `i + j - 1`,

```
X_(i+j) = u sum_(m < j) 3^(k-j+m) d_(i+m) + floor(X_i/3^j) .
```

This is trivial at `j = 0`. If it holds at some `j < k`, every exponent `k - j + m` is at least `1`, so the sum is divisible by `3` and `floor(X_(i+j)/3) = u sum_(m < j) 3^(k-j-1+m) d_(i+m) + floor(X_i/3^(j+1))`; adding `3^(k-1) u d_(i+j)`, the term `m = j` since `k - 1 = k - (j+1) + j`, gives the claim at `j + 1`. Reduce mod `3`. For `j < k` every exponent is at least `1`, so the type at step `i + j` is `floor(X_i/3^j) mod 3`, which is (i). At `j = k` the exponents are `m = 0, ..., k-1` and only `m = 0` survives, so the type at step `i + k` is `(u d_i + floor(X_i/3^k)) mod 3`. At a branching state the two admissible digits are distinct mod `3` by Lemma 3.2, and `3` does not divide `u`, so `u d_i` separates them, which is (ii). □

**Lemma 3.4 (distance-free subsets; classical).** `D_k(w) = prod_(r = 1..k) F(m_r + 2)` with `m_r = #{i in {1, ..., w} : i = r mod k}`, and `phi^w <= D_k(w) <= phi^(w+k)`.

**Proof.** Split `{1, ..., w}` into its `k` residue chains mod `k`. Two elements differ by exactly `k` if and only if they are consecutive in one chain, so an admissible subset is exactly a choice of an independent set in each of `k` disjoint paths, of `m_1, ..., m_k` vertices with `m_1 + ... + m_k = w`, and a path on `m` vertices has `F(m + 2)` independent sets, by the recursion on its last vertex. For the upper bound, `F(m + 2) <= phi^(m+1)` holds at `m = 0, 1` and propagates, `F(m + 2) = F(m + 1) + F(m) <= phi^m + phi^(m-1) = phi^(m+1)`; the product over the chains is at most `phi^(w+k)`. For the lower bound, `F(m + 2) >= phi^m` holds at `m = 0, 1` and propagates the same way, `F(m + 1) + F(m) >= phi^(m-1) + phi^(m-2) = phi^m`; the product is at least `phi^w`. □

**Proof of Theorem 1.1.** Class eq is Lemma 3.2(iii): at most one admissible digit at every state gives `P_w(s) <= 1`, and Lemma 3.1 gives `rho <= 1`. Let `(a, b)` be of class div or opp with parameter `k` and branching type `B`. Call a state blocked when one of the values `floor(X/3^j) mod 3`, `1 <= j < k`, is `2`; by Lemma 3.3(i) and Lemma 3.2 no admissible path through a blocked state at step `i` survives to step `i + k`. At a branching state that is not blocked, Lemma 3.3(ii) gives its two admissible digits distinct values of `tau`, so at most one of them gives the type `B`; designate the admissible digit with `tau != B`, the smaller one when both qualify. At a blocked branching state designate the smaller digit. The designation is a function of the state alone. Fix a start state `s` and `w >= 1`, and send an admissible path `(d_0, ..., d_(w-1))` from `s` to

```
S = {i + 1 : 0 <= i < w, the state at step i branches, d_i is designated} .
```

The map is injective. Two distinct admissible paths from `s` agree up to a first index `i` and differ there; both digits being admissible at the same state, that state branches, both paths see the same designation, and exactly one of the two digits is designated, so `i + 1` lies in exactly one of the two images. The image avoids the distance `k`. Suppose `i + 1` lies in `S` and `i + 1 + k <= w`. Then step `i + k` is a step of the path, which is admissible through it, so the state at step `i` is not blocked, `d_i` was designated by `tau`, and the type at step `i + k` is `tau(i, d_i) != B` by Lemma 3.3(ii). By Lemma 3.2 only states of type `B` branch, so `i + 1 + k` is not in `S`. Hence `P_w(s) <= D_k(w)`, and `D_k(w) <= phi^(w+k)` by Lemma 3.4. Lemma 3.1 at every `w` gives `rho(a, b) <= phi^((w+k)/w)`, which tends to `phi`. Equality on the shifts is Lemma 3.5. □

**Lemma 3.5 (the shift rays are exactly golden).** Let `k >= 1`. On the ray `(3^k, 1)`, `P_w(0) = D_k(w)` at every `w >= 1`; `M_n(3^k, 1) = M_n(1, 3^k) = D_k(n - k) - 1` at every `n > k`; and `rho(3^k, 1) = rho(1, 3^k) = phi`. For instance `P_36(0) = D_2(36) = F(20)^2 = 6765^2 = 45765225` on `(9, 1)` and `P_36(0) = D_3(36) = F(14)^3 = 377^3 = 53582633` on `(27, 1)`.

**Proof.** Since `b = 1` the second carry is always `0`, so a state is one integer `c` with `0 <= c < 3^k`. On the digit `d` the output residues are `c mod 3` and `d mod 3`, and the move is `c -> 3^(k-1) d + floor(c/3)`. By Lemma 3.2(i), at `c = 0 mod 3` the admissible digits are `0` and `1`, at `c = 1 mod 3` only `0`, and `c = 2 mod 3` is dead. From `c_0 = 0`, induction on `i` gives `c_i = sum_(j = 1..min(i, k)) 3^(k-j) d_(i-j)`: every term with `j < k` is divisible by `3` and the term `j = k`, present when `i >= k`, is `d_(i-k) < 3`, so `floor(c_i/3) = sum_(j = 1..min(i, k-1)) 3^(k-1-j) d_(i-j)`, and adding `3^(k-1) d_i` reindexes to the claim at `i + 1`. Reducing mod `3`, `c_i = 0` for `i < k` and `c_i = d_(i-k) mod 3` for `i >= k`. Every admissible digit is `0` or `1`, so no state on a path from `0` is dead, and an admissible path of length `w` from `0` is exactly a word in `{0, 1}^w` in which `d_i = 1` forces `d_(i+k) = 0`; sending it to `{i + 1 : d_i = 1}` is a bijection onto the subsets counted by `D_k(w)`. For the mass, the digit pair of `(3^k g, g)` at position `i` is `(d_(i-k), d_i)` with `d_i` the digits of `g` and `d_(i-k) = 0` at `i < k`; it lies in `F` exactly when both are `0` or `1` and not both `1`, and `3^k g < 3^n` asks `d_i = 0` at `i >= n - k`, so the `g >= 0` with `g (3^k, 1)` in `G_n` are the binary words of length `n - k` with no two ones at distance `k`, `D_k(n - k)` of them, and the swap gives `(1, 3^k)`. Finally `P_w(0) = D_k(w) >= phi^w` by Lemma 3.4, `P_w(0) <= ||A^w||_inf` and `rho(A) = lim_w ||A^w||_inf^(1/w)` by Gelfand's formula, so `rho >= phi`, and Theorem 1.1 gives `rho <= phi`. The two instances are Lemma 3.4 at `m_1 = m_2 = 18` and at `m_1 = m_2 = m_3 = 12`, with `F(20) = 6765` and `F(14) = 377` from [A000045](https://oeis.org/A000045). □

## The upper edge

**Lemma 4.1 (the octave census).** Fix an octave `j >= 0` and a parameter `k >= 1`. Of the rays of octave `j`, fewer than `18 * 3^(2j-k)` are of class div with parameter `k`, at most `9 * 3^(2j-k)` are of class opp with parameter `k`, and fewer than `9 * 3^(2j)` are of class eq. At every `j >= 7`, at least `3^(2j-2)` are of class div with parameter `1`.

**Proof.** Both coordinates of a ray of octave `j` lie in `[1, 3^(j+1))`. Class div with the first coordinate divisible by `3`: that coordinate is a multiple of `3^k` in the range, fewer than `3^(j+1-k)` choices, and the other has fewer than `3^(j+1)`; so fewer than `9 * 3^(2j-k)` such rays, and as many again with the second coordinate divisible by `3`. Class opp: `a` has fewer than `3^(j+1)` choices; `3^k | a + b` and `2 <= a + b < 2 * 3^(j+1) < 3^(j+2)` force `k <= j + 1`, and then `b` lies in one residue class mod `3^k` inside `[1, 3^(j+1))`, at most `3^(j+1-k)` choices. Class eq: fewer than `3^(j+1)` choices of each coordinate.

For the lower bound put `N = 3^j` and count the pairs `(3a', b)` with `a'` in `[N/3, N)`, `3` not dividing `a'`, `b` in `[1, 3N)`, `3` not dividing `b`, and `gcd(a', b) = 1`. Each is a ray, since `3` does not divide `b`, of class div with parameter `1` and height in `[N, 3N)`, so of octave `j`, and distinct pairs are distinct rays. There are exactly `4N/9` admissible `a'` and `2N` admissible `b`, so `8N^2/9` pairs before the coprimality condition. Discarding, for each prime `p != 3` with `p < N`, the pairs with `p | a'` and `p | b`, at most `(2N/(3p) + 1)(3N/p) = 2N^2/p^2 + 3N/p` of them, leaves a lower bound. Since `sum_(p != 3) p^(-2) <= 1/4 + sum_(m odd, m >= 5) 1/((m-1)(m+1)) = 1/4 + 1/8 = 3/8` and `sum_(p < N) 1/p <= 1 + log N`, the count is at least `(8/9 - 3/4) N^2 - 3N (1 + log N) = (5/36) N^2 - 3N (1 + log N)`, which is at least `N^2/9 = 3^(2j-2)` once `N >= 108 (1 + log N)`, true from `N = 3^7` on. □

**Proof of Theorem 1.2.** Let `x` be a nonzero point of `G_n` with a prime `p > 3^((1-c) n)` dividing both coordinates. If `x` is a fibre point it is one of the `2^(n+1) - 2` of Definition 2.1. Otherwise `x = g (a, b)` with `(a, b)` a ray and `g = gcd(x_1, x_2)`, so `g >= p > 3^((1-c) n)`; both coordinates are below `3^n`, so `max(a, b) < 3^n / g < 3^(cn)` and the octave `j` of the ray satisfies `j < cn`. Hence

```
N_top(n, c) <= 2^(n+1) + sum_(0 <= j < cn) sum_(r in octave j) M_n(r) .
```

By Lemma 2.3, `M_n(r) <= P_(n-j)(0)`, and by Theorem 1.1, with `w = n - j`, a ray of class eq contributes at most `1` and a ray of class div or opp with parameter `k` at most `D_k(w) <= phi^(w+k)`; this already gives `N_top(n, c) <= B_n(c)`, the first claim of Theorem 1.4. By Lemma 4.1 the sum over octave `j` is at most

```
9 * 9^j + sum_(k >= 1) (18 + 9) 3^(2j-k) phi^(w+k) = 9 * 9^j + 27 * 9^j phi^(n-j) sum_(k >= 1) (phi/3)^k ,
```

and the series converges, the rarity `3^(-k)` of the parameter beating the growth `phi^k` of the mass: it sums to `phi/(3 - phi) = 1.17082039...`, so the octave sum is at most `9 * 9^j + 31.613 * 9^j phi^(n-j)`. With `J` the largest octave below `cn`, `sum_(j <= J) 9^j <= (9/8) 9^(cn)` and

```
sum_(j <= J) 9^j phi^(n-j) = phi^n sum_(j <= J) (9/phi)^j <= phi^n (9/phi)^(cn) / (1 - phi/9) <= 1.2192 * 3^(n psi(c)) ,
```

since `9^(cn) phi^((1-c) n) = 3^(n (2c + (1-c) log_3 phi))`. As `2c <= psi(c)`, `N_top(n, c) <= 2^(n+1) + (10.125 + 38.55) 3^(n psi(c)) <= 2^(n+1) + 49 * 3^(n psi(c))`, each constant rounded up. With `L = log_3 phi`, `psi(c) < 1` reads `c (2 - L) < 1 - L`, that is `c < c* = (1 - L)/(2 - L)`, and `1 - c* = 1/(2 - L)`. So at every `beta' > 1/(2 - L)`, `N_top(n, 1 - beta') = o(3^n)`, since `2^(n+1) = o(3^n)` too.

Now Lemma G on `(beta', 1]`. A prime `p` with `log_3 p / n` in `(beta', 1]` has `p > 3^(beta' n)`, so every point counted by `T_p(n)` is counted by `N_top(n, 1 - beta')`. A point is counted for at most `1/beta'` such primes, since their product divides its greatest common divisor, which is below `3^n`. So

```
sum_(log_3 p / n in (beta', 1]) T_p(n) <= N_top(n, 1 - beta') / beta' = o(3^n) ,
```

and the limit of Definition 2.6 over this range is `0` at every `z`. □

**Proof of Corollary 1.3.** Lemma 2.7 gives Lemma G on `(0, 0.4475978]`. Theorem 1.2 at `beta' = 0.6402122`, which exceeds `1/(2 - log_3 phi) = 0.6402121937...`, gives it on `(0.6402122, 1]`. Every prime that occurs has its exponent in `(0, 1)`, so the sum of Definition 2.6 splits over the three ranges; each part is nonincreasing in `z`, the two outer parts vanish in the double limit, and the limsup of a sum is at most the sum of the limsups and at least the limsup of any one part, so the whole vanishes if and only if the part over the window does. □

## Two closed doors

**Proof of Theorem 1.4.** The bound `N_top(n, c) <= B_n(c)` was shown in the proof of Theorem 1.2. Every term of `B_n(c)` is nonnegative, so `B_n(c)` is at least the part from the top octave `j = ceil(cn) - 1`, the largest integer below `cn`, and from its rays of class div with parameter `1`, each of which carries `b_r(n - j) = D_1(n - j) = F(n - j + 2) >= phi^(n-j)` by Lemma 3.4. Once `n` is large enough that `j >= 7`, Lemma 4.1 gives at least `3^(2j-2)` such rays, and since `cn - 1 <= j < cn`,

```
B_n(c) >= 3^(2j-2) phi^(n-j) >= 3^(2cn-4) phi^((1-c) n) = 3^(n psi(c) - 4) .
```

At `c >= c*`, `psi(c) >= 1` and `B_n(c) >= 3^(n-4)`, which is not `o(3^n)`. Solving `psi(c) = 1` gives `c = c*` and the threshold `beta = 1 - c* = 1/(2 - log_3 phi)`, the one Theorem 1.2 already reaches, so the exact Fibonacci product changes constants and nothing else. The true mass of the rays of depth one may be far smaller; what is proved is that this chain of inequalities cannot see it. □

**Proof of Theorem 1.5.** The first inequality is Jensen's for the convex function `t -> t^w` on `[0, infinity)`, and the second is `int rho^w d nu >= nu({r_0}) rho(r_0)^w = q phi^w`. Taking `w`-th roots, `(int rho^w d nu)^(1/w) >= q^(1/w) phi`, which tends to `phi`, and `(int rho^w d nu)^(1/w) <= phi` because `rho <= phi` at every ray by Theorem 1.1; the shift rays have `rho = phi` by Lemma 3.5. For the last clause let `H >= 2`. Every pair `(x, y)` with `1 <= x, y <= H` is uniquely `g (a, b)` with `(a, b)` a ray of height at most `H/g`, so

```
sum_(1 <= x, y <= H) 1/(x^2 + y^2) = sum_(rays of height <= H) (1/(a^2 + b^2)) sum_(g <= H/max(a, b)) 1/g^2 < 2 sum_(rays of height <= H) 1/(a^2 + b^2) ,
```

as `sum_g 1/g^2 < 2`. The `2m - 1 >= m` pairs with `max(x, y) = m` have `x^2 + y^2 <= 2m^2` and contribute at least `1/(2m)` to the left side, which is therefore at least `(1/2) sum_(m <= H) 1/m > (log H)/2`. So the inverse-square sum over the rays of height at most `H` exceeds `(log H)/4`, and it is unbounded. □

**Remark 5.1 (the substitution).** The formula `1/(2 - log_3 lambda)` turns a bound `M_n(r) <= C lambda^(n-j)` holding uniformly over the rays of every octave `j` into an edge, exactly as the proof of Theorem 1.2 turns `phi` into `0.6402121937...`. Fed an averaged radius instead, it returns a number that is no coprimality bound and may never be quoted as one, and Theorem 1.5 is the proof that the substitution is invalid: the octave sum adds masses, and Jensen points the wrong way. The census of Fact 6.1 shows how low such a number can fall. Of the 18056 rays of the box of Fact 6.1, 17815 have mass rate `1`, so the median mass rate there is `1`, and the formula at `lambda = 1` returns `1/2`, which is no theorem but the per-ray wall of the coprime note, the edge that no per-ray bound can pass, there being of order `3^(2cn)` rays below the height `3^(cn)`. Nor are the shifts removable because they have density zero: a family of density zero can still be exponentially numerous and dominate once multiplied by `phi^w`. What makes them harmless in Theorem 1.2 is that there are at most two of them in each octave, and even so the proof does not remove them, it absorbs them.

## The numbers

The census reads the mass of a ray through a second automaton, on the digits of the point rather than of the multiplier. For a ray `(a, b)` its states are integers, it starts at `0`, and a digit pair `(x_i, y_i)` of `F` moves the state `c` to `(c + b x_i - a y_i)/3` when that is an integer. A word of `n` digit pairs spells a point `(x, y)` of `G_n` and ends at `(b x - a y)/3^n`, and when `b x = a y` every partial sum `sum_(l <= i) (b x_l - a y_l) 3^l` is divisible by `3^(i+1)`; so the closed walks of length `n` at `0` are exactly the points of `G_n` on the ray together with the origin, `M_n(a, b) + 1` of them. On the strongly connected component of `0`, which carries the loop of the pair `(0, 0)` at `0`, the automaton is primitive, so the mass rate `mu(a, b) = lim_n (M_n(a, b) + 1)^(1/n)` exists and is the Perron root of that component. By Lemma 2.3 and Gelfand's formula `mu(a, b) <= rho(a, b)`, so `mu <= phi` at every ray by Theorem 1.1, and `mu = phi` on the shifts by Lemma 3.5, since `phi^(n-k) <= D_k(n - k) <= phi^n`.

**Fact 6.1 (the mass-rate census to height 243).** On the box `1 <= a <= b <= 243`, which by the swap holds every ray of height at most `243` in one orientation, there are 18056 rays. On 17815 of them the component of `0` is the state `0` alone, so `M_n = 0` at every level and `mu = 1`; among them are the 8995 with `3` dividing neither coordinate and, writing `q = 3^k q_1` for the coordinate divisible by `3` and `p` for the other, 4534 more failing `q_1 = p mod 3`. The other 241 have components of 2 to 37 states, each with its exact integer characteristic polynomial, and `mu` is its largest real root, read on a Perron bracket of width at most `1.0e-13` and matched to its minimal polynomial; they take 68 distinct values. The five shift rays of the box, `(1, 3^k)` with `k <= 5`, sit at `phi`, and no other ray lies within `0.02` of `phi`. No ray off the shifts has `mu` above the supergolden ratio `1.4655712318768`; exactly six attain it, `(1, 12)`, `(3, 10)`, `(4, 9)`, `(1, 90)`, `(9, 82)` and `(10, 81)`, each with `x^3 - x^2 - 1` dividing its characteristic polynomial, and no other ray lies within `0.02` of it, the next value down being `1.4092352349583` at `(13, 108)` and `(4, 117)`, the root of `x^10 - x^9 - 2x^5 + x^4 - x^3 + 1`. Of the 241, 167 have `mu` equal to `theta_k`, the root of `x^k = x^(k-1) + 1`, by minimal polynomial, with `k` in `2..17` or `25`, and 74 have `mu` a root of neither `x^k = x^(k-1) + 1` nor `x^k = x + 1` at any `k`; the least of them by height is `(25, 57)`, with `mu = 1.3471897983562` the root of `x^10 - x^9 - x^3 - 2x^2 + 1` on 12 states. So the reading of the mass rates as trinomial roots, true on every ray of height at most `56`, ends at height `57`. The reading concerns `mu` and not the radius `rho` of Definition 2.2, which can exceed `mu` on a component of the multiplier automaton away from `0`. Generator: `lab/rs/dimension-one-ladder`, job `radii 243`, and its `radii.py`.

**Fact 6.2 (the tenth rung).** Write `E_10(G_a)` for the number of 10-tuples `(x_1, ..., x_5, y_1, ..., y_5)` of points of `G_a` with `x_1 + ... + x_5 = y_1 + ... + y_5`. Carrying the digit sums through base `3` turns it into a walk count on the carry box `{-2, ..., 2}^2`, an exact 25-by-25 integer matrix `M_10` with `E_10(G_a) = (M_10^a)_((0,0),(0,0))`, whose values at `a = 0..5` are `1, 4653, 28967859, 190911254427, 1270015973323281, 8461182216374750493`, those at `a = 1..3` matched by direct convolution of the digit set. Its characteristic polynomial factors as `x^6 (x - 120)(x^2 - 450x + 12231)(x^3 - 2190x^2 + 282096x - 5186835)^2 (x^3 - 990x^2 + 116154x - 2569725)^2 q(x)`, `q` the quartic of Lemma 2.7, whose largest root `lambda_10 = 6664.113662506...` is the Perron root of the strongly connected component of the zero state. A Sturm count on `q` in exact rational arithmetic finds no root above `66641136626/10^7` and exactly one in the bracket of width `10^(-7)` below it, so `lambda_10 < 6664.1136626`, `kappa_10 > 1.985805792698` and `beta_0 > 0.447597813453`, every digit truncated down and never rounded up; the eighth rung, from `lambda_8 = 456 + 3 sqrt(11017)`, is `0.446717310462`, and the peak wall is `2/(3 + log_3 5) = 0.447930987882`. ## What is in print

The one-dimensional theory of integers with missing digits is deep, and the natural way to use it here, one coordinate at a time, is closed by a published remark. Chow, Varju and Yu (2024), Remark 6.1, read at source, record that every base-3 missing-digit measure on two digits has Fourier `l^1` dimension below `1/2`; the coprime note draws the consequence that no componentwise transfer from one dimension reaches the window, so a Fourier attack must use the cancellation of the joint two-variable mask. The ray machine of Sections 3 and 4 uses nothing from that literature. Its two classical ingredients are textbook: the independent sets of a path are counted by the Fibonacci numbers ([A000045](https://oeis.org/A000045)), and Lemma 3.1 is the row-norm case of Gelfand's formula. The moment ladder behind the lower edge is the coprime note's, quoted and not reproduced.

## Open problems

Three statements would move the window, and none of them is close. Two walls frame them, both proved in the coprime note: no moment order of the ladder passes the peak wall `0.447931`, and no bound on the rays one at a time passes `1/2`, there being of order `3^(2cn)` rays below the height `3^(cn)`. So a closed window needs the upper edge brought down, never the lower edge pushed up, and below `1/2` it needs more than a per-ray bound.

**Conjecture 8.1 (strictness and the supergolden supremum).** Off the shift rays, `mu(a, b) <= 1.4655712318768...`, the real root of `x^3 = x^2 + 1` ([A092526](https://oeis.org/A092526)), with equality exactly on the three splits of `{1, 3^j, 9^j}` between the coordinates, `(1, 3^j + 9^j)`, `(3^j, 1 + 9^j)` and `(1 + 3^j, 9^j)`, `j >= 1`, and their swaps; in particular `mu < phi` off the shifts. The evidence is Fact 6.1, whose six attainers are exactly the splits at `j = 1, 2`; the six splits at `j = 3, 4` have `3^j` states and `x^3 - x^2 - 1` dividing their characteristic polynomials, with the Perron root at the supergolden ratio to `6e-15` (`lab/rs/dimension-one-ladder`, job `ray`). The coprime note proves the renewal lemma behind the census: `mu(a, b)` is the positive root of `1 = sum_j f_j x^(-j)` over the first returns of the direction automaton, so `mu >= theta_(l_0)` with `l_0` the length of the shortest first return other than the loop, and `l_0 = 3` off the shifts `(1, 9)` and `(9, 1)` happens exactly at `(1, 12)`, `(3, 10)`, `(4, 9)` and their swaps. That is a lower bound, and the conjecture asks for the upper one. A mechanism is visible in Lemma 3.3: the deep carry `floor(X/3^k)` is a function of the state, and the shifts are the rays on which it always sits where an adversary would put it. The conjecture dies if one ray off the shifts exceeds the supergolden ratio or a family of them of growing height has `mu -> phi`; a finite box decides neither, and the 74 rays of Fact 6.1 outside every trinomial family show that the mass rates follow no one-parameter pattern.

**Conjecture 8.2 (Statement (A), the averaged bound that would work).** There are `C > 0`, a polynomial `poly` and `lambda < phi` such that at every level `n` and every octave `j`, the masses of the rays of octave `j` other than the shifts add to at most `C poly(n) 3^(2j) lambda^(n-j)`. Under it Lemma G holds on `(beta', 1]` at every `beta' > 1/(2 - log_3 lambda)`, by the proof of Theorem 1.2: the two shift rays of octave `j` carry `D_j(n - j) - 1 < phi^n` each by Lemma 3.5, so all of them together carry less than `2n phi^n = o(3^n)`, and the rest of the octave sum becomes at most `C' poly(n) 3^(n (2c + (1-c) log_3 lambda))`, which is `o(3^n)` exactly when `c < (1 - log_3 lambda)/(2 - log_3 lambda)`. At the supergolden `lambda` of Conjecture 8.1 the edge would be `1/(2 - log_3 1.4655712...) = 0.6053028664...` and the window `(0.4475978, 0.605303]`. Conjecture 8.1 alone does not give this, since a bound on each `mu` carries a constant that depends on the ray, whose automaton grows with its height; Statement (A) is exactly the uniformity it lacks. It is weaker than a uniform per-ray bound `M_n(r) <= C lambda^(n-j)` off the shifts and far stronger than any average of the radii, which Theorem 1.5 shows is no substitute for their maximum, and its tail form asks that the rays of octave `j` with `mu >= t` number at most `3^(2j - I(t) j + o(j))`, followed by an optimisation over `t`. Two routes are named and neither is complete: a large sieve on a bounded local deficit observable, Fourier-expanded over the residue modulus of the ray, which meets three obstructions, state spaces that vary with no common separated family of frequencies, the Cauchy-Schwarz loss of the square root of the ray count in passing from an `L^2` average to the `L^1` mass of an octave, and an unweighted octave count that the measure of the sieve must match; and a finite-state fractional-moment operator. One route is ruled out: Turan power sums detect extremal eigenvalues and bound maxima from below, while this needs an upper bound for a positive sum over many nonnegative matrices of growing dimension.

**Conjecture 8.3 (Conjecture Z, the second moment).** Let `E(n) = sum_r M_n(r)^2` over the rays, the second moment of the ray masses, written `E` as in the coprime note to keep it apart from the occupied-ray count of the gasket. Then `E(n) = 2 * 3^n + o(3^n)`. The note's Theorem R turns `E(n) <= C 3^(gamma n)` into Lemma G above `gamma/2` by Cauchy-Schwarz against the rays that qualify, so under Conjecture Z the window shrinks to `(0.4475978, 1/2]` and the per-ray wall becomes the binding one. The note proves `E(n) = T(n) + S(n) + R(n)` exactly, with the diagonal `T(n) = 3^n - 2^(n+1) + 1` and the family of powers of `3`, `S(n) = 3^n - 4 * 2^n + 2n + 3`, in closed form, so the constant `2` is exact before any measurement and the conjecture is the single statement `R(n) = o(3^n)`. The residual is measured to `n = 17`: `R(n)/3^n` peaks at `0.8401158` at `n = 8` and falls at every level to `0.2737709`, and the level ratio `R(17)/R(16) = 2.5073119` sits below `phi^2 = 2.6180339` (`lab/py/gasket-witness-weights`). The shift rays paired with themselves are closed at less than `2.803 phi^(2n)` in the note, and the rest of `R` is the whole obstruction; a decaying ratio is evidence and not an estimate. Under Conjecture Z together with the occupancy Conjecture O of the note, its Theorem R+ closes the window outright.

## Reproducibility

The numbers of Section 6 and of the Open problems are printed by three generators, each run from the repository root, and the constants of Section 1 by the figure. `cargo run --release --manifest-path research/coprime/lab/rs/dimension-one-ladder/Cargo.toml -- radii 243`, in about two seconds on eight cores, prints Fact 6.1: the 18056 rays and their classes, every occupied ray with its component, its mass rate inside a Perron bracket, its first returns and its exact characteristic polynomial, the shift rays and the six attainers; `uv run python research/coprime/lab/rs/dimension-one-ladder/radii.py` on that output, in about seven seconds, factors the polynomials in exact arithmetic and prints the 68 distinct mass rates, their minimal polynomials and the 74 rays outside both trinomial families, and the job `ray 1 756` prints one ray, the splits of Conjecture 8.1 among them. `uv run python research/coprime/lab/rs/dimension-one-ladder/ladder.py`, in under a second, prints Fact 6.2 and the rungs of Lemma 2.7. `uv run --with numpy python research/coprime/lab/py/gasket-witness-weights/witness.py`, in about three minutes, prints the residual of Conjecture 8.3. The figure is `figures/paper-lemma-b-pincer.ts`, drawn in the browser in milliseconds: it reads `phi` and the supergolden ratio as the Perron roots of the digit-memory rules of `@mrlyprod/mrly` that forbid two ones at distance `1` and two ones within distance `2`, counts the words of length `36` of the rules that forbid two ones at distance exactly `2` and `3` and asserts `D_2(36) = 45765225 = F(20)^2` and `D_3(36) = 53582633 = F(14)^3` against the Fibonacci numbers of the same library, recomputes the tenth rung from the quartic of Lemma 2.7, and asserts the edges and constants of Section 1 to the digits printed: `0.6402121937`, `0.3597878062`, `0.6053028664`, `0.4479309878`, `0.4475978134` and the octave constant `48.67 < 49` of Theorem 1.2. The theorems and their proofs print no other number beyond the arithmetic written out in them.
