### Gaussian primes on a design

- **The setup. Proved.** Read base `b >= 2` on both coordinates of `z = z_1 + i z_2` in `Z[i]`: the digit vectors `(z_(1,j), z_(2,j))` lie in `{0..b-1}^2`, the numbers `z_(1,j) + i z_(2,j)` form a complete residue system mod `b` in `Z[i]`, so this is base `b` on `Z[i]` with the square digit set, which represents the quadrant of nonnegative coordinates, and `F = {0..b-1}^2` less one vector `a`, `fill = b^2 - 1`, is the Gaussian design missing one digit vector, `S_k` its `fill^k` points in the box `[0, b^k)^2`. The Morton code above maps `S_k` onto the integers missing the one digit `a_1 + b a_2` at base `b^2`, but it is not multiplicative, so Gaussian primes on `S_k` are not rational primes on its image. The transform is a product on `T^2`, `hat 1_(S_k)(t) = Prod_(j<k) hat F(b^j t)` with `hat F(t) = D_b(t_1) D_b(t_2) - e(<a, t>)`, and with `K(u) = sin(b pi u)/sin(pi u)` and `m = a - ((b-1)/2, (b-1)/2)` it reads `|hat F(t)| = |K(t_1) K(t_2) - e(<m, t>)|`; a sign change of one coordinate of `m` or the swap carries this modulus to the modulus at the reflected or swapped `t`, and reflection and swap carry the grid to itself, so every unshifted grid sum and every supremum or infimum over shifts depends on `a` only through the orbit of `m` under sign changes and swap, `C(floor((b+1)/2) + 1, 2)` orbits at base `b`. At the grid `D_b(r_1/b) D_b(r_2/b)` is `b^2` at `r = 0` and `0` elsewhere, so the one-digit sum at shift `0`, `Sum_(r in (Z/b)^2) |hat F(r/b)|/fill`, is exactly `2` at every vector, as over `Z`. The closed form matches the digit sum at every vector of bases 3, 4, 5 ([lab/py/gaussian-digits](../lab/py/gaussian-digits/), verb `census`).
- **The block-sum reduction. Proved.** With `y = b^k`, a block `P y + S_k` for `P` in `Z[i]` and `Lambda` the von Mangoldt function of `Z[i]`, the block sum is `Sum_(v in S_k) Lambda(P y + v) = y^(-2) Sum_(r in (Z/y)^2) hat 1_(S_k)(r/y) S_P(-r/y)` with `S_P(t) = Sum_(z in P y + [0, y)^2) Lambda(z) e(<z, t>)`, since `P y` lies in `y Z[i]`. On grid points where `|S_P| <= C y^(2 - 2 delta)` the contribution is at most `C C_F fill^k y^(2 (alpha_2 - delta))` once the shifted-grid certificate `Sum_(r in (Z/y)^2) |hat 1_(S_k)(s + r/y)| <= C_F fill^k y^(2 alpha_2)` holds at every shift `s`, so the two-dimensional exponent `alpha_2`, read in units of the `y^2` lattice points of a block, must beat the minor-arc saving `delta`, as `alpha_1 < 1/5` does over `Z` against Maynard's Lemma 4.2.
- **What sums over `Z[i]` give, read at source. Verified** at source. [Baier 2017](https://arxiv.org/abs/1706.06453), read in its sections 3.4 and 3.8, bounds a linear sum over a disc sector of `Z[i]` by slicing it into rows, `Sum_(N(m) <= Y_B, sector) e(Im(m kappa)) << Y_B^(1/2) min(||Im kappa||^(-1), Y_B^(1/2))^(1/2) min(||Re kappa||^(-1), Y_B^(1/2))^(1/2)`, its (38), an upper bound that saves the square root of the range at a generic frequency and no more; the sum of its two minimum factors over `N(n) <= Z_B` at a Hurwitz denominator `q` is `G_c(Y_B, Z_B) << (1 + Z_B/N(q))(Y_B^(1/2) + N(q)) (log 2 Y_B)^2`, its (46), and the sum of linear sums is `Y_B^(1/2) G_c`, so a Type I sum over divisors of norm up to `D_0` on a region of norm range `X` costs `(X D_0)^(1/2)`; it states Harman's sieve over `Z[i]` as its Theorem 9, the proof referred to the classical case, and its Gaussian analogue of Vaughan's `p^(-1/4)` is `|p|^(-1/12 + eps)` on the distance of `p c` to `Z[i]` in the sup norm, its Theorem 4. [Pratt 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/1806.02699), read in its Theorems 1.1 and 1.2 in arXiv v1, counts primes `m^2 + l^2` with `l` missing one decimal digit, or up to three in Theorem 1.2, and `m` free, both with `l` free of prime factors up to a parameter `P` and an ineffective constant; neither paper restricts the digit vector of a Gaussian integer.
- **The box factors Type I. Proved.** For a square block `R = I_1 x I_2` and `d` in `Z[i]`, `1_(d | z) = N(d)^(-1) Sum_(kappa in (conj d)^(-1) Z[i]/Z[i]) e(<z, kappa>)`, the dual of `d Z[i]` under `<z, kappa> = Re(z conj kappa)` being `(conj d)^(-1) Z[i]`, so every Type I inner sum is an exact average of products of two one-dimensional geometric sums, `Sum_(z in R, d | z) e(<z, t>) = N(d)^(-1) Sum_kappa G_(I_1)(t_1 + kappa_1) G_(I_2)(t_2 + kappa_2)` with `G_I(u) = Sum_(n in I) e(n u)`; a disc or a sector has no such factorisation, and it is the slicing of those regions that costs the square root above.
- **The minor-arc lemma over `Z[i]`. Proved.** Let `X >= 3`, `L = log X`, `R = I_1 x I_2` a box of Gaussian integers with `N(z) <= X` on it, `xi` in `C`, and `q != 0` and `lambda` coprime Gaussian integers with `|xi - lambda/q| <= 4/N(q)`. Then `S(xi) = Sum_(z in R, z != 0) Lambda(z) e(Re(z xi)) << (X^(7/8) + X N(q)^(-1/2) + X^(11/16) N(q)^(1/2) + X^(1/2) N(q)) L^4` with an absolute effective constant, `Lambda`, `mu` and the divisor count `tau` read on the ideal `(z)`; as `xi` runs over `C/Z[i]`, `z -> e(Re(z xi))` runs over the additive characters of `Z[i]`, the grid point `r/y` of the block-sum reduction being `xi = (r_1 - i r_2)/y`. The four bullets below are the proof; it uses `|Sum_(n in J) e(n u)| <= min(|J|, 1/(2||u||))` on an interval `J` and elementary lattice-point counts, and the factorisation of Type I is not needed at this exponent.
- **Units and Vaughan's identity. Proved.** `D = {u_1 + u_2 i : u_1 > 0, u_2 >= 0}` holds one generator of each nonzero ideal, so for ideal functions `f_1, f_2` the sum `Sum_(z in R) (f_1 * f_2)(z) e(Re(z xi))` is `Sum_(d in D) f_1(d) Sum_(w : d w in R) f_2(w) e(Re(d w xi))`, each `z` and each ideal divisor of it giving one `d` and one `w = z/d`, units absorbed in `w`. On ideals `Lambda = Lambda_(<=U) + mu_(<=V) * ell - mu_(<=V) * Lambda_(<=U) * 1 + mu_(>V) * Lambda_(>U) * 1` with `ell(z) = log N(z)`, `1` the constant function, `iota` the unit of convolution and the subscripts restricting the norm, since the last three terms sum to `mu * Lambda_(>U) * 1 = Lambda_(>U)` by `Lambda * 1 = ell` and `mu * 1 = iota`. At `U = V = X^(1/4)` this splits `S` into `S_0 << U L`; `S_1`, a Type I sum over `d in D`, `N(d) <= V`, with weights `log N(w)`; `S_2`, a Type I sum over `d in D`, `N(d) <= U V`, with coefficients `|c_d| <= Sum_(varpi | d) Lambda(varpi) <= L`; and `S_3 = Sum_(varpi in D, N(varpi) > U) Lambda(varpi) Sum_(n : varpi n in R) g(n) e(Re(varpi n xi))` with `g(n) = Sum_(d | n, d in D, N(d) > V) mu(d)`, which vanishes at `N(n) <= V` and is at most `tau(n)`.
- **Spacing. Proved.** If `d != d'` and `|d - d'| <= sqrt(2) |q|/16`, then `(d - d') lambda/q` lies at distance at least `1/|q|` from `Z[i]`, since `q` does not divide `d - d'`, and `|(d - d')(xi - lambda/q)| <= sqrt(2)/(4|q|)`, so `d xi` and `d' xi` are at least `1/(2|q|)` apart on `C/Z[i]`; the same holds for `d xi` against `0` when `0 < |d| <= sqrt(2)|q|/16`. Points `s_0`-separated on the torus, here `s_0 = 1/(2|q|)`, number at most `(2 sqrt(2) r_0/s_0 + 1)^2` within sup distance `r_0 < 1/2` of a point, by disjoint discs, so over such a set and for any `A > 0`, `Sum min(A, 1/||p||_inf) <= 6 A + C N(q)` and, with no point within `1/(4|q|)` of `0`, `Sum 1/||p||_inf <= C N(q)`, `||.||_inf` the sup distance to `Z[i]` and `C` absolute. Dirichlet's pigeonhole on `q xi`, `q` in `{0..M-1}^2`, gives `q` with `|Re q|, |Im q| < M` and `|q xi - lambda| <= sqrt(2)/(M - 1)`, and after removing a common factor `|xi - lambda/q| <= 4/N(q)` because `|q| < sqrt(2) M <= 2 sqrt(2)(M - 1)`.
- **Type I. Proved.** Let `Y_R <= 2 X^(1/2)` be the longer side of `R` and `Omega_d = {w : d w in R}`. Every horizontal and vertical line meets `Omega_d` in one interval, and `d^(-1) R` has width at most `sqrt(2) Y_R/|d|`, so there are at most `sqrt(2) Y_R/|d| + 1` rows, each of at most that length, and on a row the phase is `w_1 Re(d xi)` up to a constant; every partial row sum is therefore at most `min(sqrt(2) Y_R/|d| + 1, 1/(2||Re(d xi)||))`, and the same holds for columns with `Im(d xi)`. Summing rows or columns, `|Sum_(w in Omega_d) e(Re(d w xi))| <= Theta_d` with `Theta_d = 9 (Y_R/|d|) min(Y_R/|d|, 1/||d xi||_inf)` at `|d| <= Y_R` and `Theta_d = 6` beyond. The weight `log N(w) <= L` is monotone on each half of a row, so Abel summation along the row, which reads only partial row sums, gives `|Sum_(w in Omega_d) log N(w) e(Re(d w xi))| <= 4 L Theta_d`. Put `T(D_0) = Sum_(d in D, N(d) <= D_0) Theta_d`. Cut `D` into squares of side `|q|/16`: on the square at the origin no `d xi` comes within `1/(4|q|)` of `0` and the square pays `C Y_R N(q)`; a square at distance `rho >= n |q|/16` pays `(Y_R/rho)(6 Y_R/rho + C N(q))`, and at most `2n + 1` squares sit at index `n`. Summing, `T(D_0) << X L/N(q) + X^(1/2) N(q) + (X D_0)^(1/2) + D_0`; hence `S_1 << L T(X^(1/4))` and `S_2 << L T(X^(1/2))`, both `<< L^2 (X/N(q) + X^(1/2) N(q) + X^(3/4))`. Type I carries `X L/N(q)`, and the term `X N(q)^(-1/2)` of the lemma comes from Type II alone.
- **Type II. Proved.** Split `S_3` at `N(varpi)` in `[E, 2E)`, `U <= E < X/V`, at most `2 L` pieces, and write a piece as `B = Sum_v Sum_u alpha_v beta_u 1_(v u in R) e(Re(v u xi))`, where `u` is the variable of larger norm range `B_0 = max(2E, X/E)`, `v` the other, of range `A_0 = min(2E, X/E) >= X^(1/4)`, and the coefficients are `Lambda(varpi)` and `g(n)` in that order or the reverse; Cauchy-Schwarz over `u` gives `|B|^2 <= ||beta||^2 Sum_(v, v') |alpha_v alpha_v'| |Sum_(u in Omega(v, v')) e(Re((v - v') u xi))|`, where `Omega(v, v')` is `{N(u) in its range}` cut by `v^(-1) R` and `v'^(-1) R` and by `D` when `u = varpi`, so every line meets it in at most two intervals and the slicing gives `<< B_0^(1/2) min(B_0^(1/2), 1/||(v - v') xi||_inf)`. The differences `h = v - v'` have `|h| <= 2 A_0^(1/2)`, the squares of side `|q|/16` covering them number `<< A_0/N(q) + 1`, and each pays `6 B_0^(1/2) + C N(q)`. With `Sum Lambda(varpi)^2 << E L^2` and `Sum_(N(n) <= x_0) tau(n)^2 <= 9 x_0 (Sum_(N I <= x_0) 1/N I)^3 << x_0 (log x_0)^3` over nonzero ideals `I`, the latter from `#{n : J | n, N(n) <= x_0} <= 9 x_0/N J` for an ideal `J` of norm at most `x_0` and `#{(I_1, I_2) : lcm(I_1, I_2) = J} <= tau_3(J)`, the number of ordered factorisations of `J` into three ideals, a piece is `<< X L^(5/2) (N(q)^(-1/2) + B_0^(-1/4) + (B_0/X)^(1/2) + B_0^(1/4) N(q)^(1/2) X^(-1/2))`, and `X^(1/2) <= B_0 <= 2 X^(3/4)` gives `S_3 << L^(7/2) (X^(7/8) + X N(q)^(-1/2) + X^(11/16) N(q)^(1/2))`. The sliced inner sum saves only `B_0^(-1/4)`, which is why the balanced split caps the exponent at `1/8`.
- **Region A over `Z[i]`. Proved.** In the block-sum reduction let the blocks have side `y = b^k` inside `[0, x)^2`, with `x/y` bounded (over `Z`, `x/y < b^(K_0)` with `K_0` of order `sqrt(log x)`), take `X = 2 x^2 >= 256`, and fix at each grid point one Dirichlet approximation at `M = floor(X^(3/16)/sqrt 2) >= 2`, so every grid point has one `q`, with `N(q) < 2 M^2 <= X^(3/8)`. On the residues with `N(q) >= X^(1/4)` all four terms of the lemma are at most `X^(7/8)`, so they contribute at most `y^(-2) C_F fill^k y^(2 alpha_2) X^(7/8) L^4 << C_F fill^k y^(2 alpha_2 - 1/4) (x/y)^(7/4) L^4` to a block sum, a power saving in `y` at bounded `x/y` exactly when `alpha_2 < 1/8`: the gate `1/8` of this page is the one the lemma pays, with the square chain wall `5054` and the window certificate at base `2000` behind it.
- **The band the lemma leaves. Proved.** Read at a grid point `xi = r/y` of the block-sum reduction, `r` in `Z[i]` mod `y`, with `X = 2 x^2` and `x/y` bounded as in region A, and with `lambda/q` its fixed first approximation, below `N(q) = X^(1/4)` the bound of the lemma saves only `N(q)^(1/2)`. A second approximation at height `h_r = |r q - lambda y| >= 1` exists as over `Z`: Dirichlet at `M' = max(ceil(2 sqrt(2) y/h_r), ceil(2 sqrt(2) |q|)) + 1` gives `lambda'/q' != lambda/q` with `y/(2h_r) <= |q'| << y/h_r`, from `1/(|q| |q'|) <= |lambda/q - lambda'/q'|` and `|q| h_r <= 4 y`; but there the lemma reads `X h_r/y + X^(11/16) y/h_r + X^(1/2) y^2/h_r^2`, nontrivial only for `h_r` of order `X^(1/4)` and beyond, and even with the square at the origin of Type I refined to `(X N(q))^(1/2)` the sliced Type II term `X^(11/16) y/h_r` keeps `h_r` below about `X^(3/16)` open, where the one-dimensional lemma pays `(X q)^(1/2)`. So the residues with small `N(q)` and `h_r` between `exp(C_0 sqrt(log x))`, the height below which the `Z` dissection hands a residue to its major arcs, and that height get no saving from the bound at either approximation, while the hybrid mass of such a class grows with `h_r`; over `Z` the same band is closed by the term `x h_r^(-1/2)` of the second approximation, and here it is open. This reads the bound, not the sum.
- **The count lemma. Proved.** Let `q != 0` and `lambda` be coprime Gaussian integers with `|xi - lambda/q| <= 4/N(q)`, and write `||w||` for the Euclidean distance from `w` to `Z[i]`. Then for every `H > 0` and `eta > 0`, `#{h in Z[i] : N(h) <= H, ||h xi|| <= eta} <= 2^18 (H/N(q) + 1)(N(q) eta^2 + 1)`. Proof: cut the plane into half-open squares of side `s_q = |q|/16` on the grid `s_q Z^2`; at most `(2 sqrt(H)/s_q + 2)^2 <= 2048 (H/N(q) + 1)` of them meet the disc `|h| <= sqrt(H)`. Two distinct `h, h'` in one square have `0 < |h - h'| < sqrt(2) |q|/16`, so by the spacing bullet `h xi` and `h' xi` are at least `s_0 = 1/(2|q|)` apart on `C/Z[i]`; discs of radius `s_0/2` about the points with `||h xi|| <= eta` are disjoint and lie in the disc of radius `eta + s_0/2` about `0`, which embeds in the torus when `eta + s_0/2 <= 1/2`, so a square holds at most `(4 |q| eta + 1)^2 <= 32 (N(q) eta^2 + 1)` of them; otherwise `eta >= 1/4`, and the area of the torus gives at most `16 N(q)/pi <= 82 N(q) eta^2`. Multiplying, `2048 * 82 < 2^18`. By brute force at `H = 10^6`, over all `3141549` Gaussian integers of the disc at 184 frequencies within `4/N(q)` of sampled coprime `lambda/q` with `N(q)` from `2` to `441650` and eight `eta` each, the count reads at most `18.6834` times `(H/N(q) + 1)(N(q) eta^2 + 1)`, at `N(q) = 194625` and `eta = 10^(-3)`, and the cell `q = 9 + 4i`, `lambda = 53 + 77i`, `xi = lambda/q`, `H = 1940`, `eta^2 = 5/97` reads `10.8651`: readings, not the supremum ([lab/py/gaussian-digits](../lab/py/gaussian-digits/), verb `hcount`).
- **The bilinear bound at Vaughan's strength. Proved.** With `q`, `lambda`, `xi` as in the count lemma, `U_0, V_0 >= 1`, and any complex `alpha_v` supported on `N(v) < 2 V_0` and `beta_u` on `N(u) < 2 U_0`, the form `B = Sum_v Sum_u alpha_v beta_u e(Re(v u xi))` satisfies `|B| <= 2 * 10^4 ||alpha||_2 ||beta||_2 (U_0 + V_0 + N(q) + U_0 V_0/N(q))^(1/2)`, the two-dimensional form of Vaughan's `(M N)^(1/2) (M + N + q + M N/q)^(1/2)`, with no logarithm. Proof: `Phi(v) = e^pi exp(-pi N(v)/(2 V_0))` is at least `1` on `N(v) < 2 V_0`, so Cauchy-Schwarz over `v` gives `|B|^2 <= ||alpha||^2 Sum_(u, u') beta_u conj(beta_u') K_Phi(u - u')` with `K_Phi(h) = Sum_v Phi(v) e(Re(v h xi))`. Poisson summation on `Z[i]` for the Gaussian, `Sum_v exp(-pi N(v)/T_0) e(<v, theta>) = T_0 Sum_(nu in Z[i]) exp(-pi T_0 |nu - theta|^2)`, read with `e(Re(v h xi)) = e(<v, conj(h xi)>)` and `T_0 = 2 V_0`, makes `K_Phi(h)` positive and a product of two one-dimensional sums; `Sum_(m_1 in Z) exp(-2 pi V_0 (t - m_1)^2) <= 2.01 exp(-2 pi V_0 ||t||^2)` at `V_0 >= 1`, since the other terms sit at distances `j - ||t||` and `j + ||t||`, `j >= 1`, and `(j - ||t||)^2 >= ||t||^2 + j^2 - j`, so `0 < K_Phi(h) <= 188 V_0 exp(-2 pi V_0 ||h xi||^2)`. With `|beta_u beta_u'| <= (|beta_u|^2 + |beta_u'|^2)/2` and `K_Phi(h) = K_Phi(-h)`, `|B|^2 <= ||alpha||^2 ||beta||^2 max_u Sum_(u') K_Phi(u - u')`; here `N(u - u') < 8 U_0`, and the count lemma summed over the shells `j <= V_0 ||h xi||^2 < j + 1`, each weighted `exp(-2 pi j)`, gives `Sum_(N(h) < 8 U_0) exp(-2 pi V_0 ||h xi||^2) <= 2^21 * 1.004 (U_0/N(q) + 1)(N(q)/V_0 + 1)`, by `Sum_j (j + 1) exp(-2 pi j) < 1.004`; and `188 * 2^21 * 1.004 < 4 * 10^8`. The Gaussian majorant is what the sliced Type II above lacks: its inner sum over a region cut by the box pays `B_0^(1/2)/||h xi||_inf` where the Gaussian pays `exp(-2 pi V_0 ||h xi||^2)`. On the annuli `N(v) in [V_0, 2 V_0)`, `N(u) in [U_0, 2 U_0)`, with `alpha` the Cauchy-Schwarz extremal for `beta` random unimodular or `Lambda`, at five shapes `(U_0, V_0)` from `(400, 400)` to `(300, 6000)` and `(6000, 300)` and ten bands of `N(q)` from `2` to `4 U_0 V_0`, the ratio of `|B|` to `||alpha|| ||beta|| (U_0 + V_0 + N(q) + U_0 V_0/N(q))^(1/2)` reads at most `1.4444`, a reading below the operator norm; the operator norm itself, the largest singular value of `e(Re(v u xi))` on the discs of the statement, reads `6.1940` times the bracket at `U_0 = V_0 = 200`, `q = 1`, `xi = 0`, the all-ones matrix, where it tends to `2 pi`, and `6.1640` at `U_0 = V_0 = 100`, `q = 1 + i`, `xi = 1/(1 + i)` (verb `bilinear`).
- **The minor-arc lemma on a smooth annular weight. Proved.** Let `X >= 3`, `L = log X`, `W` smooth on `C` and supported in `X^(1/2)/e <= |z| <= X^(1/2)` with `|partial^gamma W| <= X^(-|gamma|/2)` for every multi-index `|gamma| <= 10`, and `q`, `lambda`, `xi` as in the count lemma. Then `S_W(xi) = Sum_z Lambda(z) W(z) e(Re(z xi)) << (X^(4/5) + X N(q)^(-1/2) + X^(1/2) N(q)^(1/2)) L^(7/2)` with an absolute constant, which at `X^(2/5) <= N(q) <= X^(3/5)` is `X^(4/5) L^(7/2)`, the gate `1/5`. Vaughan's identity of the units bullet at `U = V = X^(2/5)` splits `S_W` as before, with `W(z)` in place of `1_R(z)`, and `S_0 = 0` since `W` vanishes at `N(z) < X/e^2`, which exceeds `U = X^(2/5)` once `X > e^(10/3)`, smaller `X` being absorbed in the constant. The two bullets below are the proof; it uses no slicing and no factorisation, only Poisson summation on `Z[i]`, the count lemma and the bilinear bound.
- **Type I by Poisson summation. Proved.** For `d != 0`, `zeta_d = conj(d xi)` and a weight `W_1` with the support of `W` and `|partial^gamma W_1| <= C_1 X^(-|gamma|/2)` at `|gamma| <= 10`, Poisson summation gives `Sum_(w in Z[i]) W_1(d w) e(Re(d w xi)) = N(d)^(-1) Sum_(nu in Z[i]) hat W_1((nu - zeta_d)/conj(d))` with `hat W_1(phi) = Int W_1(z) e(-<z, phi>) dz`, and ten integrations by parts along `phi` give `|hat W_1(phi)| << C_1 X min(1, (X^(1/2) |phi|)^(-10))`; the lattice point nearest `zeta_d` is at distance `||d xi||` and every other one at least `1 - 1/sqrt 2` away, so the inner sum is `<< C_1 (X/N(d)) (min(1, (X^(1/2) ||d xi||/|d|)^(-10)) + (|d|/X^(1/2))^10)`. Put `T_W(D_0) = Sum_(d in D, N(d) <= D_0) (X/N(d)) min(1, (X^(1/2) ||d xi||/|d|)^(-10))`. On a shell `R_d <= |d| < 2R_d` the weight is at most `2^(-10(j-1))` on the `d` with `||d xi|| > 2^j R_d/X^(1/2)`, and the count lemma at `eta = 2^(j+1) R_d/X^(1/2)` gives the shell `<< (X/R_d^2)(R_d^2/N(q) + 1)(N(q) R_d^2/X + 1) << X/N(q) + R_d^2 + N(q)` once `R_d >= |q|/48`.
