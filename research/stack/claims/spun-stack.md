- 2026-10-30 [Proved] The mean-square upper half of the error of the node count: for every imaginary quadratic field `K`, with `E(x) = sum_{N(a) <= x} Phi(a) - (rho_K/(2 zeta_K(2))) x^2`, `int_N^(2N) E(x)^2 dx <= C_K N^(7/2)` for all `N >= 1` and `sum_{N <= n < 2N} E(n)^2 = O(N^(7/2))`, so the root mean square of `E` over `[N, 2N)` is `O(N^(5/4))`, the order the Verified row reads, with no `eps`; four steps. (1) The layers are exact: `E(x) = sum_b mu_K(b) E_1(x/N(b))` over all ideals `b`, with `E_1(y) = T(y) - (rho_K/2) y^2` and `T(y) = 0` below 1, so the terms with `N(b) > x` are the main terms alone and no tail is left, absolutely convergent since `abs(E_1(y)) <= (1 + 2 rho_K) y^2` on `(0, 2)`. (2) The circle input: each ideal class counts the lattice points of the ellipse `f <= t`, a convex domain about the origin with `C^infinity` boundary and non-vanishing curvature, for which Iosevich, Sawyer and Seeger's mean-square theorem (Theorem 1.2.1 at `h = R`, read in arXiv:math/0205127) gives `int_Y^(2Y) P_f(tau)^2 dtau <= C Y^(3/2)` for `Y >= 1`, hence `int_Y^(2Y) P^2 <= C_P Y^(3/2)` for the ideal-count error `P` summed over the classes; Cauchy-Schwarz then gives `abs(int_0^y P) <= C y^(5/4)` with no bound on the integral term of its own, so `E_1(y) = y P(y) - int_0^y P` has `int_Y^(2Y) E_1^2 = O(Y^(7/2))`. (3) Minkowski over the layers: the `L^2([N, 2N])` norm of `x -> E_1(x/m)` is `m^(1/2)` times that of `E_1` on `[N/m, 2N/m]`, which for `m <= N` is at most `C m^(-5/4) N^(7/4)`, summing over the ideals to `C zeta_K(5/4) N^(7/4)`, while the layers with `m > N` cost `O(N^(3/2))`. (4) On `[n, n + 1)` the real and the integer `E` differ by `O(n)`, which costs `O(N^3)`. The data sit under it: `rms(E)/N^(5/4)` is at most `0.2750` (Gaussian) and `0.2535` (Eisenstein) over the windows `2^12 .. 2^22`, and the exact layer identity of step (1) holds within `0.01` at `n = 100, 250, 400` in both fields. The lower half and the pointwise bound `E(N) = O(N^(5/4 + eps))` are not claimed. Witness: ../lab/py/totient-constant README section THE MEAN SQUARE, ../refs.md, ../lab/rs/node-error `main`, `layers_sum_to_the_error`.
- 2026-10-30 [Conjecture] The lower half of the mean-square order exactly `N^(5/4)` of the error of the node count: `E` is not `O(N^(5/4 - eps))` for any `eps > 0`, and `(1/N) int_N^(2N) E^2` is at least a positive multiple of `N^(5/2)`; the Proved row above makes `int_1^infty abs(E(x))^2 x^(-2 sigma - 1) dx` converge for every `sigma > 5/4`, so the lower half is divergence below `5/4`, and it needs the other layers not to cancel the `b = 1` term (the pointwise bound `E(N) = O(N^(5/4 + eps))` is as hard as the circle problem at that term and is not claimed); the route for the lower half is Mellin-Plancherel, `int_1^infty abs(E(x))^2 x^(-2 sigma - 1) dx = (1/(2 pi)) int abs(F(s)/s - c/(s - 2))^2 dt` on `Re s = sigma > 1` whenever the left side is finite, with `F = zeta_K(s-1)/zeta_K(s)`, whose integrand at `sigma = 1 + delta` is at least half of `zeta_K(1 + delta)^-2 abs(zeta_K(delta + i t))^2/abs(s)^2` minus the integrable `abs(c/(s - 2))^2`, of size `t^(-4 delta) abs(zeta_K(1 - delta + i t))^2` by the functional equation, so the integral diverges for `delta < 1/4` once the mean square of `zeta_K` on `Re s = 1 - delta` is bounded below (a mean-value theorem for the Dedekind zeta, unread), giving `E = Omega(N^(5/4 - eps))`; over the integers the same integrand is `t^(-1 - 2 delta)`, convergent for every `delta > 0`, which is why the totient error there is `N log N` and this one is not.
