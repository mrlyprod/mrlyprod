## The design horocycle

Objects. Fix a base `base >= 3` and a digit set `F` inside `{0..base-1}` with `fill = |F| >= 2`, and write `S_F` for the positive integers whose digits all lie in `F`, `A_F(x)` for its count up to `x`, `alpha = log fill/log base`, and `M_F(x) = sum_(n in S_F, n <= x) mu(n)` for the design's Mobius meter, whose exponent `theta(F)` against `A_F` is the open question of [the Mobius page](../../mobius/notes/mobius.md), `theta(F) = 1/2` being the square-root conjecture there. The design Ford circles are the Ford circles over the reduced `a/c` with `c in S_F`, and `m_F(Q) = sum_(c in S_F, c <= Q) phi(c)` is the number of them on one period that the horocycle at height `Q^(-2)` meets, by the threshold of [apollonian](../../apollonian/notes/apollonian.md), `## The horocycle`. The design Mobius series is `Z_F(s) = sum_(n in S_F) mu(n) n^(-s)`, absolutely convergent on `Re s > alpha`. The sieve weight is `a_F = (mu 1_(S_F)) * 1`, so `a_F(g) = sum_(e | g, e in S_F) mu(e)`, and the design novelty is `nu_F = (mu 1_(S_F)) * id`, so `nu_F(c) = sum_(e | c, e in S_F) mu(e) c/e`, with Dirichlet series `zeta(s - 1) Z_F(s)`. In the words of the stack: the plain stack visits the node `a/b` at every layer `kb`, the novelty counts the first visit `k = 1` alone, and the design novelty counts every visit with the `k`-th weighted `a_F(k)`, layer `c` carrying `sum_(b | c) phi(b) a_F(c/b) = (phi * a_F)(c) = nu_F(c)`. At the full digit set `a_F(k) = [k = 1]`, `nu_F = phi` and `Z_F = 1/zeta`, and everything below specialises to the section above. For a window `f` supported on `[1, 2]`, with `F(s) = int f(u) u^(s-1) du`, the smoothed design novelty is `S_(F,f)(y) = sum_c nu_F(c) f(cy)` and the design meter is `E_F(y) = y^2 S_(F,f)(y) - F(2) Z_F(2)`.

No orbit. **No semigroup of `SL(2, Z)` containing `T^m` for some `m != 0`, `T = [[1, 1], [0, 1]]`, and one element `gamma = [[a, b], [c, d]]` with `c != 0` has every lower-left entry in `S_F`, `-S_F` or `0` at a digit set with `fill < base`. Proved**: `gamma T^(mk) gamma` lies in the semigroup for every `k >= 0`, with lower-left entry `c(a + d) + c^2 m k`, whose absolute values meet `[1, x]` in at least `x/(c^2 |m|) - |a + d|/|c| - 1` points, while `A_F(x) <= fill + ... + fill^L <= 2 fill x^alpha` for `x` of `L` digits, `alpha < 1`. So the design cusps contain the orbit of `infinity` under no semigroup that contains a translation and moves `infinity`; an unfolding of the closed horocycle of length `|m|` uses exactly such a translation, the integral being one over `<T^m> \ R`. The restricted series `psi^F f(z) = sum f(Im gamma z)` over the cosets with bottom row `(c, d)`, `c in S_F`, is a function on the cylinder `Gamma_infinity \ H` and not on the modular surface. On the cylinder it is still exact, and it carries both of the design's counts. **Below the support of `f`, `int_0^1 psi^F f(x + ih) dx = h sum_(c in S_F) phi(c) g(c h^(1/2))`, the smoothed `m_F`, and `int_0^1 psi^F f(x + ih) e(x) dx = h sum_(c in S_F) mu(c) g(c h^(1/2)) + O(h^(3/2))`, a smoothed `M_F` whose leading term is `Z_F(1) F(-1/2) h^(1/2)` at `fill < base`, since `g(t) ~ F(-1/2)/t` at `0`, `M_F` sitting in the remainder, with `g` the transform of [apollonian](../../apollonian/notes/apollonian.md), `## The horocycle`. Proved**: the first is that section's unfolding with the `c`-sum restricted; for the second the reduced residues `d mod c` contribute `sum_d e(-d/c) = mu(c)`, the Ramanujan sum at `1`, times `h int f(1/(c^2 h (1 + t^2))) e(h t) dt`, and on the support `|h t| <= h^(1/2)/c`, so replacing `e(h t)` by `1` moves the term by at most `4 pi^2 ||f||_infinity h^(3/2) c^(-3)`. So the design Ford circles hold `m_F` on the zeroth harmonic of the closed horocycle and `M_F` on the first, and neither harmonic lives on the surface.

The design Eisenstein series. Move the design from the orbit onto the gcd. For `f` smooth with compact support in `(0, infinity)` put `Theta_F f(z) = sum a_F(gcd(c, d)) f(Im z/|cz + d|^2)`, the sum over the integer pairs with `c >= 1`, or `c = 0` and `d >= 1`. **`Theta_F f = sum_(g >= 1) a_F(g) psi_(f_g)` with `f_g(u) = f(u/g^2)` and `psi_(f_g)` the incomplete Eisenstein series of `f_g`, the sum finite at every `z`, so `Theta_F f` is invariant under `SL(2, Z)`, a smooth function on the modular surface with integral `zeta(2) Z_F(2) F(-1)` over it and mean `(3/pi) zeta(2) Z_F(2) F(-1) = (pi/2) Z_F(2) F(-1)`, the surface having area `pi/3`; and its integral over the closed horocycle at a height `h` below the support of `f` is `int_0^1 Theta_F f(x + ih) dx = h sum_(c >= 1) nu_F(c) g(c h^(1/2))`. Proved**: a pair is `g` times a coprime pair, the coprime pairs run once over `Gamma_infinity \ SL(2, Z)`, and `f(Im z/(g^2 |c'z + d'|^2)) = f_g(Im z/|c'z + d'|^2)`; a term is nonzero only when `|cz + d|^2 <= Im z/min supp f`, finitely many pairs at each `z`; the surface integral is `sum_g a_F(g) int f_g(u) u^(-2) du`, since `psi_f` integrates to `F(-1)` by unfolding, with `sum_g a_F(g) g^(-2) = zeta(2) Z_F(2)`, absolutely convergent since `|a_F(g)| <= d(g)`; the `c = 0` pairs vanish below the support; and for `c >= 1` the weight depends on `d mod c` alone, each class contributing `h g(c h^(1/2))` exactly as in [apollonian](../../apollonian/notes/apollonian.md), `## The horocycle`, while `sum_(d mod c) a_F(gcd(c, d)) = sum_(e | c, e in S_F) mu(e) #{d mod c : e | d} = nu_F(c)`. The design reaches the surface through the weight on the multiplicity of a lattice point and never through the orbit, and at the full digit set `Theta_F f = psi_f`. (**Verified**, `lab/py/design-horocycle`, verb `bridge`: integrating `Theta_F f` over the horocycle pair by pair, every lattice pair weighted by `a_F` of its gcd, against `h sum nu_F(c) g(c h^(1/2))` at `h = 4^(-k)`, `k = 2..6`, for the `C^infinity` bump, the worst gap is `7.5e-16` at base 3 `{0,1}` and `4.4e-16` at base 10 missing `7`, over `1808` and `1298` weighted pairs, `3544` and `2564` arcs, at `k = 6`, the two routes sharing one quadrature rule so that the agreement tests the gcd weight and the sum over `d mod c`; `Theta_F f` summed directly at 5 seeded points and 4 images of each under `SL(2, Z)` moves by at most `6.1e-13`; and the sieve for `nu_F` matches its divisor definition at every `c <= 300` on both designs.)

The design meter. **Let `f` be a `C^infinity` window on `[1, 2]`. (a) `E_F(y) = y^2 sum_(d in S_F) mu(d) R_f(dy)` with `R_f(u) = sum_(e >= 1) e f(eu) - F(2) u^(-2)`, which is `O(u^k)` for every `k` as `u -> 0` and equals `-F(2) u^(-2)` for `u > 2`. (b) If `M_F(x) = O(x^b)` with `0 <= b < 2`, then `E_F(y) = O(y^(2 - b))`; so the square-root conjecture `theta(F) = 1/2` gives `E_F(y) = O(y^(2 - alpha/2 - eps))` for every `eps > 0`, which against the design's own mass reads `|E_F(y)|/y^2 = O(A_F(1/y)^(1/2 + eps))`, where the trivial bound is `A_F(1/y)`. (c) If `E_F(y) = O(y^(2 - b - eps))` for every `C^infinity` window and every `eps > 0`, with `b >= 0`, then `Z_F` continues holomorphically to `Re s > b`. (d) If `Z_F` continues holomorphically to `Re s > b` with polynomial growth uniform on each closed strip `b + eps <= Re s <= 3`, then `E_F(y) = O(y^(2 - b - eps))` for every `C^infinity` window. Proved.** (a): exchange the finite sums, `sum_c nu_F(c) f(cy) = sum_(d in S_F) mu(d) sum_e e f(dey)`, and by Poisson `sum_e e f(eu) = u^(-2) sum_k hat(vf)(k/u)`, with `hat(vf)` the Fourier transform of `v f(v)`, whose terms at `k != 0` fall faster than any power of `u`; the constant term is `F(2) u^(-2)`, and `sum_d mu(d) F(2) (dy)^(-2) = F(2) y^(-2) Z_F(2)`. (b): partial summation, `sum_d mu(d) R_f(dy) = -int_1^infinity M_F(x) y R_f'(xy) dx`, at most a constant times `y^(-b) int_0^infinity u^b |R_f'(u)| du`, finite for `b < 2`; and `A_F(x) >= fill^(L-1) - 1` at `L` digits gives `x^alpha << A_F(x)`. (c): the backward half of the section above, word for word with `zeta(s - 1)/zeta(s)` replaced by `zeta(s - 1) Z_F(s)`: `int_0^2 S_(F,f)(y) y^(s-1) dy = F(s) zeta(s - 1) Z_F(s)` for `Re s > 2`, the left side equals `F(2) Z_F(2) 2^(s-2)/(s - 2) + int_0^2 E_F(y) y^(s-3) dy`, holomorphic on `Re s > b + eps`; on `b < Re s <= alpha < 1` the factor `zeta(s - 1)` has no zero, `Re(s - 1)` lying in `(-1, 0)`, and the windows `u^k f(u)` with Weierstrass leave no point where every `F(s + k)` vanishes. (d): Mellin inversion against the novelty series `zeta(s - 1) Z_F(s)`, moved to `Re s = b + eps` past the pole at `s = 2`, the transform of a `C^infinity` window outrunning every polynomial. At the full digit set (a) to (c) are the section above, and there the circle closes: holomorphy of `1/zeta` on `Re s > 1/2` gives the Mertens bound back through the bound on `1/zeta` that the Euler product supplies. `Z_F` has no Euler product, `mu 1_(S_F)` not being multiplicative: in base 3 `{0,1}`, `10 = 101_3` and `13 = 111_3` are squarefree, coprime and in `S_F` while `130 = 11211_3` is not, so `mu 1_(S_F)(130) = 0` against `mu(10) mu(13) = -1`. So the design meter and the square-root conjecture are proved in one direction only: `theta(F) = 1/2` gives the meter its rate `2 - alpha/2`, the rate gives `Z_F` holomorphic on `Re s > alpha/2`, and the distance back is the growth of `Z_F` on vertical lines.

The scattering coefficient. Write `C_F(h)` for the horocycle integral of `Theta_F f` at height `h < 1`, for `f` on `[1, 2]`. **`int_0^1 C_F(h) h^(s-2) dh = phi(s) zeta(2s) Z_F(2s) F(-s)` on `Re s > 1`, with `phi(s) = xi(2s - 1)/xi(2s)` the scattering coefficient of the modular surface, so the design's scattering coefficient is `phi_F(s) = phi(s) zeta(2s) Z_F(2s)`, the surface's own multiplied by `sum_g a_F(g) g^(-2s)`. At the full digit set `zeta(2s) Z_F(2s) = 1` and `phi_F = phi`. The sampled horocycle. A design owns one orbit of its own: the maps `z -> (z + d)/base`, `d in F`, an affine semigroup of `GL(2, Q)` that carries the horocycle at height `h` to the one at `h/base` and samples it along the design's Cantor set, so that the closed horocycle is averaged against the self-similar measure `mu_F` of `F` in place of `dx`. That orbit puts the digits on the position and not on the cusp index. **For `f` smooth with compact support in `(0, infinity)` and `h` below its support, `int psi_f(x + ih) d mu_F(x) = sum_(c >= 1) sum_(m in Z) c_c(m) hat G_c(m) conj(hat mu_F(m))`, with `c_c(m)` the Ramanujan sum, `hat G_c(m) = int_R f(h/(c^2 (u^2 + h^2))) e(-mu) du` and `hat mu_F(m) = int e(-mx) d mu_F(x)`; the frequency-`m` term has, at `m != 0`, `c`-series `sum_c c_c(m) c^(-s) = sigma_(1-s)(m)/zeta(s)`; and `hat mu_F(base m) = hat mu_F(m)` at every integer `m`. Proved**: group the cosets by `c` and the reduced residues `d mod c` as in the unfolding, expand the `1`-periodic `G_c(u) = sum_k f(h/(c^2 ((u + k)^2 + h^2)))` in its Fourier series, rapidly convergent since `G_c` is smooth, with finitely many `c` contributing, and sum `e(m d/c)` over the reduced residues; `c_c(m) = sum_(e | (c, m)) mu(c/e) e` gives the series; and `hat mu_F(t) = prod_(j >= 1) (1/fill) sum_(d in F) e(-d t/base^j)` gives `hat mu_F(base t) = ((1/fill) sum_(d in F) e(-d t)) hat mu_F(t)`, whose first factor is `1` at an integer. The frequency `0` is the plain horocycle integral; every other frequency weighs `1/zeta` by `sigma_(1-s)(m)` and by the design's coefficient, which repeats along `m, base m, base^2 m, ...` without decaying, the non-Rajchman property, and no term of the expansion carries `Z_F`. For this target the route is closed: `M_F` needs `mu` on the digit integers, which on the modular surface are cusp indices, and only a weight on the cusp index, the gcd weight `a_F` above, puts them there. Whether the sampled horocycle equidistributes as `h -> 0` is not decided here.

## The local moire limit

Objects. Fix a code of `dim 2` and write `t(a, b)` for its ink at the corner `(a, b)`, so the level-1 design at side `N` inks the cell with digits `(i, j)` exactly when `t(i mod 2, j mod 2) = 1`; code 7 voids a cell exactly when both digits are odd. Lay the design at side `N` and the design at side `N + h` on the same unit square and keep a point exactly when one of the two inks it: that is the overlay `X_N` under the difference filter. Write `p(y) = floor(y) mod 2`, so the first digit of the point `(u, v)` at side `N` has parity `p(Nu)`, and `Lambda(s) = 1 - 2 dist(s, 2Z)`, the triangle wave of period 2, which is the correlation of the square wave `(-1)^p(y)` with its own shift by `s`. The local mean is the box mean `avg_Q X_N` over a box `Q = I x J`. The lag torus is `(R/2Z)^2`: near `(u, v)` the layer at side `N + h` is the layer at side `N` shifted by `(hu, hv)` cells, since `(N + h)u - Nu = hu`. Let `w_0, w_1, w_2, w_12` be the Walsh coefficients of `(-1)^t(a, b)` against `1`, `(-1)^a`, `(-1)^b` and `(-1)^(a + b)`, so `w_0 = 1 - 2 fill` with `fill` the inked fraction of the corners and the four squares sum to `1`.

The law on the lag torus. For every code and every integer gap `h >= 1` the overlay converges locally to `Phi(hu, hv)`, with

```
Phi(s, t) = (1 - w_0^2 - w_1^2 Lambda(s) - w_2^2 Lambda(t) - w_12^2 Lambda(s) Lambda(t)) / 2
```

a function on the lag torus, which the unit square covers exactly once at `h = 2`. **Proved**, the mode and rate below. A box mean of a product picture is the product of its 1D means, and the overlay is a fixed Boolean function of the parities `A = p(Nx)` and `B = p((N + h)x)` on each axis, so `avg_Q X_N` is a bilinear form in the joint law of `(A, B)` on `I` and on `J`. Local correlation, global independence. The two statements are different moments of one function. So the limit carries exactly the independent global mean, `int int Phi(hu, hv) = (1 - w_0^2)/2 = 2 fill (1 - fill)`, for every code and every gap. **Proved**, and **Verified** in exact rationals for all 16 codes at gaps 1 to 8 (`check_walsh`). Level 2. The level-2 design at side `N` has `N^2` cells per axis and inks a cell exactly when both its top digit pair and its second digit pair are inked; take code 7 and gap 2. On one axis write `A, B` for the top parities and `C, D` for the second ones at sides `N` and `M = N + 2`. With `f = {Nx}` the place inside a top cell, `C = p(Nf)` and `D = p(Nf + lambda)` with `lambda = 4f + 4x + epsilon floor(f + 2x)` modulo 2 and `epsilon = N mod 2`, exactly. **Proved**, from `floor(N^2 x) = N floor(Nx) + floor(Nf)` and `Mx = Nx + 2x`. The second lag is not slow: it winds twice round the lag circle inside every top cell, locked to the top phase. The two-scale law. Let `P_u` be the law of `(A, B, C, D)` when the top phase `Nx mod 2` is uniform on `[0, 2)`, the second phase `Nf mod 2` is uniform and independent of it, and `x = u` is frozen in the slow terms, and let `H2(u, v)` be the overlay's mean under `P_u` on one axis and `P_v` on the other. Then

```
H2(u, v) = 5/8 - alpha(u) alpha(v) - gamma(u) gamma(v) - 2 beta(u) beta(v)
alpha = |1 - 2u|/2,   beta = (|1 - 2u| + w)/8,   gamma = 1/4 at even N,   gamma = (1 + 2w)/4 at odd N
```

with `w(u) = -2u(1 - 4u)` on `[0, 1/4]`, `w(u) = (4u - 1)(1 - 2u)` on `[1/4, 1/2]` and `w(1 - u) = w(u)`. The ink at side `N` is `I = (1 - A_x A_y)(1 - C_x C_y)` and `I'` is the same in `B, D`, so `H2 = E I + E I' - 2 E[I I']`, and expanding `E[I I']` over the four factors leaves a signed sum of products of one-axis chances that a set of parities are all odd. Under `P_u` the second phase is uniform given the top one, and the shift of the top phase by one cell flips `A` and `B` and fixes `lambda`, so each such chance is a constant or a multiple of `alpha = P(A = B = 1)`, `gamma = P(C = D = 1)` or `beta = P(A = B = C = D = 1)`, and the sum is `H2` above, with `alpha = (1 + Lambda(2u))/4`, `gamma = (1 + E Lambda(lambda))/4` and `beta = alpha/4 + E[A = B] Lambda(lambda)/8`. Write `T(y) = int_0^y Lambda`, odd, of period 2, with `T(1 + y) = -T(y)` and `T(1 - y) = T(y)`. On `[0, 1/2]` the parities `A` and `B` agree exactly when `floor(f + 2u) = 0`, that is `f < 1 - 2u`, so `E[A = B] Lambda(lambda) = int_0^(1 - 2u) Lambda(4f + 4u) df = (T(4 - 4u) - T(4u))/4 = -T(4u)/2 = w`; where they disagree the floor is `1` and `int_(1 - 2u)^1 Lambda(4f + 4u + epsilon) df = (T(4u + epsilon) - T(epsilon - 4u))/4`, which is `T(4u)/2 = -w` at even `N` and `-T(4u)/2 = w` at odd `N`; on `[1/2, 1]` the floor takes the values `1` and `2` and the same two integrals give the mirror image. Hence `E Lambda(lambda)` is `0` at even `N` and `2w` at odd `N`, and `w` is the second-level correlation left on the part of a top cell where the two top parities agree, a part that cuts the double winding at a fraction of a turn. **Proved.**

The rate at level 2 is the rate of level 1 taken twice. Fix one axis, an interval `I` and `N >= 24`. First freeze the second lag.
