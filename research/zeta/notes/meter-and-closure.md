## THE METER

- The design's Mobius meter does oscillate at the zeta ordinates, and not at the design's own pole lattice. Read `M_F(x)/x^(alpha/2)` uniformly in `log x`, Hann-windowed, against a local-median floor and a null of rigid shifts of each candidate list: at base 10 with the digit `9` missing all six strongest peaks sit within one bin of a nontrivial zeta zero, offsets `0.068` to `0.216`, with the full-set control at the same depth reading ten of ten, offsets `0.018` to `0.196`. Thirteen zeta ordinates are reachable in the band `4 < gamma < 60`, so a peak lands within one bin of one by chance with probability `0.159` and six of six is `P = 1.6e-5`. The pole lattice `2 pi j / log base` scores `-0.592`, `-0.640`, `-0.734` at base 3 `{0,1}`, base 3 `{0,2}` and base 5 `{0,1}`, below its own null, while the counting function over the identical elements scores `3.602`, `3.764` and `3.973`: the pipeline would have seen a lattice and there is none. **Verified** ([lab/py/design-meter](../../mobius/lab/py/design-meter/), verb `spectrum`, and the echo demo).
- The echo is a mean field and not a signature, and its size is a theorem. `M_F(x) = sum_(n <= x) mu(n) A_F(n)/n + R_F(x)` defines `R_F` at every base and digit set, and partial summation gives `sum_(n <= x) mu(n) A_F(n)/n = A_F(x) H(x) - sum_(m in S_F, m <= x) H(m-1)` with `H(y) = sum_(n <= y) mu(n)/n`, which is `O(y^(-1/2 + eps))` under RH; so the echo is `O(x^(alpha - 1/2 + eps))` when `alpha > 1/2`, while for `alpha < 1/2` the second sum converges absolutely and the echo tends to a nonzero constant, base 16 `{0,1}` reading `-0.0937, -0.1330, -0.1242, -0.1051, -0.1099` at `10^3` to `10^7` against `x^(alpha - 1/2)` falling `0.1778` to `0.0178`, and base 10 `{0,1}` reading `-0.0500` at `10^7` against `0.0405`. Against the square-root bar `x^(alpha/2)` the echo dies at `x^(-min(alpha, 1 - alpha)/2)`, equal to `1` only at `alpha = 1`. **Proved** ([lab/py/design-meter](../../mobius/lab/py/design-meter/), [mobius](../../mobius/notes/mobius.md)).
- The split separates the two by hand and the frequencies follow the echo. At base 10 missing `9` the echo carries six of six top peaks at zeta zeros and the residual none of the two it has, the echo being `0.1342` of the meter in root mean square against `0.6476`, and the echo's share of the meter falls `0.356028, 0.242495, 0.207229` there and `0.208549, 0.099001, 0.047902` at base 3 `{0,1}`, share over prediction reading `1.0000, 0.7387, 0.6846` and `1.0000, 0.9219, 0.8663`, each design decaying at least as fast as its own rate. So the zeta zeros neither obstruct nor help the design's square-root shape, which is a statement about `R_F` alone. **Proved** ([lab/py/design-meter](../../mobius/lab/py/design-meter/), verb `spectrum`).
- There is no family law behind the frequencies, and the obvious one is dead. The frequency set is not a function of `(q, alpha)`: base 3 `{0,1}` and base 9 `{0,1,2,3}` share `alpha = 0.630930`, element count `1048575` and log range to within `1.4%`, and their meters split ten peaks against none, where support-matched random-sign meters reach `0` to `4` peaks on the first support and `0` to `2` on the second over eight draws each, so the ten sit above their own null and the none does not; base 9 `{0,1,2,3}` and base 9 `{0,1,3,4}` share base and `alpha` and split the same way, the second being base 3 `{0,1}` element for element since its digits are the base-3 pairs `00, 01, 10, 11`. The scaled pair base 3 `{0,1}` and `{0,2}` shares eight of ten peaks, so the scaling transfer carries into the spectrum where no `(q, alpha)` law does. **Refuted** ([lab/py/design-meter](../../mobius/lab/py/design-meter/), verb `family`, [mobius](../../mobius/notes/mobius.md)).
- The arcs are not a frequency family the meter carries. The primitive quadratic Dirichlet `L`-zeros of conductor `3`, `4` or `5`, the quadratic conductor each base carries, separate from their shift null at no design over the eleven censused: the largest score is `0.372` against a null of `0.341` at base 4 `{0,1,2}` and the widest gap over a null is `0.371` against `0.290` at base 3 `{1,2}`, while the zeta ordinates on the same meters reach `1.130` against `0.392` at base 10 missing `9` and `0.742` against `0.291` at base 3 `{0,1}`, so the pipeline would have seen an arc family and there is none. The wider prediction, over arcs of denominator `base^j`, is untestable by this spectrum rather than false, the generator carrying `L`-zeros at the three quadratic conductors alone, and it is not claimed here. **Refuted** ([lab/py/design-meter](../../mobius/lab/py/design-meter/), verb `spectrum`).

## THE CLOSURE PROBLEM

- The other classical face of the hypothesis is a closure problem, and it restricts to a design by restricting an index set. In `H = L^2(0, infinity)` let `rho_n(t) = {1/(nt)}` and `chi = 1_(0,1]`; [Baez-Duarte 2003](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0202141) Theorem 1.1 says the Riemann hypothesis holds iff `chi` lies in the closure of the span of `rho_n`, `n = 1, 2, 3, ...`, the natural Beurling functions, and the proof of the hard half runs on the approximants `sum_(a <= n) mu(a) a^(-eps) rho_a`, supported on the squarefree integers, where the natural approximation `sum_(a <= n) mu(a) rho_a` itself diverges in `H`. The Mellin transform `int_0^infinity rho_n(t) t^(s-1) dt = -n^(-s) zeta(s)/s` on `0 < Re s < 1` turns the span into `zeta(s)/s` times the Dirichlet polynomials, so the distance `d_N^2 = inf over c of norm(chi - sum_(n <= N) c_n rho_n)^2`, over real `c`, which is also the infimum over complex `c` since `chi` and every `rho_n` are real, is `inf over P of (1/2 pi) int abs(1 + zeta P)^2 dt/abs(s)^2` on `Re s = 1/2`. [Burnol 2002](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0103058) Theorem 1.3 gives `liminf D(lambda)^2 log(1/lambda) >= sum_rho m_rho^2/abs(rho)^2` for the distance `D(lambda)` over the continuous family `{theta/t}`, `lambda <= theta <= 1`, the sum over the zeros on the line with their multiplicities `m_rho`, which under the hypothesis with simple zeros is `C = 2 + gamma - log(4 pi) = 0.046191`; the integer truncation `n <= N` sits inside the family at `lambda = 1/N`, so `d_N >= D(1/N)` and `liminf d_N^2 log N >= C` with it; no upper bound on `d_N` is known, `d_N -> 0` being the hypothesis itself. Remark 1.1 of [Burnol 2002 estimate](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0202166) writes the bound as `C/sqrt(log N)` on `d_N` with `C >= 2 + gamma - log(4 pi)`, which squares consistently only with `C >= sqrt(2 + gamma - log 4 pi)`; the squared reading is Theorem 1.3 and the one printed here. The design object is the same infimum with `n` confined to `S_F`, `d_N^2(F) = inf over c of norm(chi - sum_(n in S_F, n <= N) c_n rho_n)^2`, at once `>= d_N^2` since the span is a subspace. **Verified** against the sources in [REFS](../refs.md).
- The Gram matrix is explicit and the system is mild. For coprime `h, k` the inner product `<rho_h, rho_k>` is Vasyunin's formula as [Bettin and Conrey 2013](https://arxiv.org/abs/1111.0931) print it, `(log 2 pi - gamma)/2 (1/h + 1/k) + ((k - h)/(2hk)) log(h/k) - (pi/(2hk)) (V(h/k) + V(k/h))` with the cotangent sum `V(h/k) = sum_(m=1)^(k-1) {mh/k} cot(pi m/k)`, and the dilation `rho_(gh)(t) = rho_h(gt)` gives `<rho_(gh), rho_(gk)> = <rho_h, rho_k>/g`; the target is `<chi, rho_n> = (log n + 1 - gamma)/n` and `d_N^2 = 1 - b^T G^(-1) b`. Vasyunin's own paper is not read here, so the formula is checked against exact quadrature instead, `<rho_h, rho_k> = (1/(hk)) int_0^1 {hw}{kw} zeta(2, w) dw` with the Hurwitz zeta carrying the periodic tail, at eleven pairs from `(1,1)` to `(40,81)` and `(6,10)` to `1e-57`, and the whole pipeline against the quadrature of the residual itself at `S = {1,2,3}`, `{1,3}`, `{1,2,3,4}` and `{1,3,4}`, reading `0.0938372, 0.2328324, 0.0651289, 0.1256225` on both paths to forty digits. The 1-norm condition number reads `6.6e2, 9.6e3, 1.4e5, 1.9e6, 2.3e7` on the full set at `N = 9, 27, 81, 243, 729` and `1.7e1` to `4.1e6` on the design at `N = 3` to `3^7`, so 60 digits carry every printed digit and the 90-digit rerun agrees on all of them. **Verified** ([lab/py/nyman-beurling](../lab/py/nyman-beurling/), verbs `gram` and `table`).
- The table separates the two at once. Distances round up, floors truncate, everything else prints to nearest. On the full set `d_N^2` reads `0.024015, 0.015314, 0.010949, 0.008254, 0.006738` at `N = 3^2..3^6` against Burnol's `C/log N = 0.021023, 0.014015, 0.010511, 0.008409, 0.007008`, and `d_N^2 log N` reads `0.052766, 0.050472, 0.048113, 0.045335, 0.044412`, falling through `C = 0.046191` between `N = 81` and `N = 243`; the theorem says `liminf d_N^2 log N >= C` and nothing more, so the product sits below the constant at `N = 243` and `N = 729` and climbs back to at least `C` in the limit inferior, and no source prints where. On base 3 `{0,1}`, with `2, 4, 8, 16, 32, 64, 128` elements at `N = 3, 3^2, ..., 3^7`, `d_N^2(F)` reads `0.232833, 0.116043, 0.106008, 0.104951, 0.104689, 0.104610, 0.104579`, a plateau at `0.1046` while the full set at the same `N` is fifteen times smaller and still falling, and `d_N^2(F) log N` reads `0.2558` at `N = 3` and `0.8042` at `N = 3^7`. The constant is read from the zeros as its own control, `2 sum_(0 < gamma < 1000) 1/(1/4 + gamma^2)` over 649 zeros plus the smooth tail giving `0.0461922` against `0.0461914`. **Verified** ([lab/py/nyman-beurling](../lab/py/nyman-beurling/), verbs `table` and `zeros`).
- The plateau is a theorem, and the obstruction is local. Read the approximant at `u = 1/t`: `f(u) = sum_(n in S) c_n {u/n} = sigma u - sum_(n in S) c_n floor(u/n)` with `sigma = sum c_n/n`, so on `[k, k+1)` it is the line `sigma u - A_k` with `A_k = sum_(m <= k) a(m)` and `a = c * 1` the Dirichlet convolution of `c`, extended by zero off `S`, with the constant `1`; the target is `0` on `(0,1)` and `1` on `[1, infinity)`, and the norm is `int abs(.)^2 du/u^2`. Restrict the norm to `u < K`: there, for `n >= K`, every `{u/n}` is `u/n`, so the restricted residual depends on `sigma` and on the `c_n` with `n in S`, `n < K`, alone, and `d_N^2(S) >= delta_K(S) = inf over sigma, c of sigma^2 + sum_(k=1)^(K-1) int_k^(k+1) (sigma u - 1 - A_k)^2 du/u^2` for every `N`, a finite least squares in `1 + card(S cap [1, K))` real unknowns. It vanishes iff `sigma = 0` and `A_k = -1` for `1 <= k < K`, that is `a(1) = -1` and `a(k) = 0` for `2 <= k < K`, and Mobius inversion on `[1, K)` then forces `c_k = -mu(k)` for every `k < K`; so `delta_K(S) = 0` iff every squarefree `k < K` lies in `S`, and `delta_K(S) > 0` as soon as `K` passes `q_0(S)`, the least squarefree integer outside `S`. The floor is nondecreasing in `K`, and the full set has `delta_K = 0` at every `K`, witnessed by `c = -mu` and `sigma = 0`, the sign being the one Baez-Duarte's approximants carry, which converge to `-chi`. **Proved** ([lab/py/nyman-beurling](../lab/py/nyman-beurling/), verb `floor`).
- Every proper design has a `q_0`, so its distance never falls to zero, and the closure problem sees exactly the squarefree numbers. `S_F` counts `x^alpha` elements below `x` with `alpha < 1` while the squarefree integers have density `6/pi^2`, so some squarefree integer is missing and `liminf d_N^2(F) >= delta_(q_0 + 1)(S_F) > 0`, unconditionally. In the other direction, if `S` contains every squarefree integer then Baez-Duarte's approximants lie in the span over `S`, so under the hypothesis `chi` is in its closure; and any `S` whose closure holds `chi` puts `chi` in the closure of the natural Beurling functions, which is the hypothesis by the easy half of Nyman-Beurling. So for a set `S` of positive integers, `chi` lies in the closure of the span of `rho_n`, `n in S`, iff `S` contains every squarefree integer and the Riemann hypothesis holds. That is the Euler wall in closure form: the criterion needs the support of `mu`, and a design misses it at `q_0`, the first squarefree integer with a forbidden digit, which is `1` when `1` is not a digit, `2` when `1` is a digit and `2` is not, `19` at base 10 missing `9` and `38` at base 10 missing `8`, base 2 being the full set. **Proved** ([Baez-Duarte 2003](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0202141), [lab/py/nyman-beurling](../lab/py/nyman-beurling/), verb `floor`).
- The floor and the table bracket the limit. At base 3 `{0,1}` the floor reads `0.070873` at `K = 3`, from `u < 3` alone, where the two lines `sigma u - c_1 - 1` and `sigma u - 2 c_1 - 1` cannot both vanish, and rises to `0.098689` at `K = 40`, `0.100877` at `K = 60` and `0.101926` at `K = 100`, its largest steps at `K = 3, 6, 8, 12`, one past the missing squarefree `2, 5, 7, 11`; against `d_2187^2(F) = 0.104579` that puts `lim d_N^2(F)` in `[0.101926, 0.104579]`, the lower end a truncated floor, the upper end the last computed distance and an upper bound at every larger `N`. On the full set the same least squares returns `abs(delta_K) < 1e-75` at `K = 2..12`. At `K = 40` the other designs read `0.304563` at base 3 `{0,2}`, `0.217197` at base 5 `{0,1}`, `0.164256` at base 4 `{0,1}`, `0.064881` at base 3 `{1,2}`, `0.010772` at base 4 `{1,2,3}`, `0.002263` at base 10 missing `9` and `0.000287` at base 10 missing `8`, the last two small because their `q_0` is `19` and `38` and the weight `du/u^2` has little mass left there. **Verified** ([lab/py/nyman-beurling](../lab/py/nyman-beurling/), verb `floor`).
- The `L^2(0,1)` variant, the same functions with the tail cut off, says the same. Dropping `int_1^infinity` removes `1/(mn)` from every Gram entry and the `sigma^2` term from the floor; the design reads `0.158347, 0.097858, 0.091353, 0.090586, 0.090398, 0.090341, 0.090319` at `N = 3` to `3^7` against the floor `0.085724` at `K = 40`, the full set `0.023064, 0.014950, 0.010763, 0.008148, 0.006668` at `N = 3^2..3^6`, and the vanishing criterion of the floor is the same, since a line `sigma u - A_k - 1` vanishing on one interval already forces `sigma = 0`. **Verified** ([lab/py/nyman-beurling](../lab/py/nyman-beurling/), verbs `table` and `floor`).
- What the floor does not see is any zero. `delta_K` is zero on the squarefree numbers at every `K`, so it carries nothing about `zeta`; the hypothesis lives in the tail `u -> infinity`, and a design is refused before the tail begins. So `d_N^2(F)` does not fall like `1/log N` beside the full set, the design carries no part of the criterion, and the meter question of [mobius](../../mobius/notes/mobius.md) is not a closure problem on `S_F`. **Refuted** ([lab/py/nyman-beurling](../lab/py/nyman-beurling/), verbs `table` and `floor`).
