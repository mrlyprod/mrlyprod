Write the positive integers in a base and keep those whose digits all lie in a fixed set `F`: a digit design `S_F`, with `x^(alpha + o(1))` members up to `x`, `alpha = log(fill)/log(base)`, `fill` the number of digits kept. Its Dirichlet series `zeta_F(s) = sum_(n in S_F) n^(-s)` converges absolutely on `Re s > alpha` and is Riemann's zeta at the full digit set. This paper asks what plays the part of the Mobius function for it. Once the digit `1` is kept, the indicator of the design has a unique Dirichlet inverse `nu_F`, and `nu_F` is `mu` only at the full set, since the indicator is multiplicative only there; at every other digit set that keeps `1` a coprime pair is written down whose product leaves the design or enters it. Every zero `rho` of `zeta_F` with `Re rho > alpha` forces the abscissa of convergence of `N_F(s) = sum_n nu_F(n) n^(-s)` to be at least `Re rho`, and every real `sigma > alpha` with `zeta_F(sigma) < 2` bounds its abscissa of absolute convergence by `sigma`, with an explicit constant, so the abscissa of the design's own Mobius series lies between the real part of any zero right of the abscissa and the point where `zeta_F` crosses `2`. The argument principle finds zeros right of the abscissa at every proper design of a twenty-four design sweep and at none of its three full sets; read through the transport, the partial sums of `nu_F` are not `O(A_F(x))` at the nineteen of them that keep the digit `1`, and at three of those, base 10 missing `9` among them, not even `O(x)`, with a fourth over that line by `1.1e-5` on a census to height `4`. Those zeros are resolved numerically with propagated truncation bounds and are not certified in ball arithmetic, so the anti-cancellation is a Fact on its domain, while every theorem has its proof on the page.

Write every whole number in base `3` and keep the ones whose digits are only `0` and `1`: `1, 3, 4, 9, 10, 12, 13, 27, ...`, `2^level - 1` of them below `3^level`. Their Dirichlet series `zeta_F(s)` converges absolutely on `Re s > alpha = log 2/log 3 = 0.6309...`, and the upper left panel of the figure is the strip just right of that line, from height `0` to `60`. The dots on its left edge are the poles that the continuation of `zeta_F` carries on the line, one every `2 pi/log 3 = 5.7192` in height. Inside the strip, where `zeta_F` is still a convergent sum of positive terms, the argument principle finds three zeros below height `60`: the one near height `28.6` is drawn inside the box that holds it, the other two as the height bands they were counted in. The lower right panel is the same strip for the integers, right of `Re s = 1`, with the one pole of [Riemann's zeta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riemann_zeta_function) at `s = 1` and nothing else: the Euler product forbids a zero there.

For the integers the Mobius function inverts zeta, `zeta(s) sum_n mu(n) n^(-s) = 1`. For a design the question is what weight inverts `zeta_F`. As soon as `1` is a digit, the indicator `1_(S_F)` takes the value `1` at `1` and so has a unique Dirichlet inverse `nu_F`, built by one recursion, with `zeta_F(s) N_F(s) = 1` wherever both series converge absolutely (Theorem 4.1). At the full set it is `mu`. Everywhere else it is not `mu`, and it is not `mu` cut down to the design either: the indicator is multiplicative only at the full set (Theorem 3.1), and an inverse pair `zeta_F M_F = 1`, with `M_F(s) = sum_(n in S_F) mu(n) n^(-s)`, would force every power of every prime of the design into the design (Theorem 3.3), which fails at every proper set read (Fact 3.4).

The Euler product is also what keeps `zeta` free of zeros on `Re s > 1`, where it converges absolutely. A design has no Euler product, and its zeta does vanish inside its own half plane of absolute convergence (Section 5). Each such zero is transported to the inverse: if `1 in F` and `zeta_F(rho) = 0` with `Re rho > alpha`, then `sigma_c(N_F) >= Re rho`, so `sum_(n <= x) nu_F(n)` is `O(x^theta)` for no `theta < Re rho` (Theorem 4.3), and since `A_F(x) = O(x^(alpha + eps))` the design's own Mobius sums are not `O(A_F(x))` (Corollary 4.4): along a sequence they outrun the number of terms being summed. The positivity of the coefficients caps the abscissa from the other side: every real `sigma > alpha` with `zeta_F(sigma) < 2` has `sum_n |nu_F(n)| n^(-sigma) <= 1/(2 - zeta_F(sigma))` (Theorem 4.6). So `sigma_c(N_F)` lies between the real part of any zero right of the abscissa and the point where `zeta_F` crosses `2` (Corollary 4.7).

The zeros come from computation. At base 3 `{0,1}` a winding box holds one zero at `Re s in [0.72074, 0.72084]`, near height `28.606`, and at base 10 missing `9` one at `Re s in [1.00150, 1.00168]`, right of `Re s = 1` (Fact 5.4). So the abscissa of `N_F` lies in `[0.72074, 1.17903]` at base 3 `{0,1}`, against `alpha = 0.6309`, and in `[1.00150, 1.69934]` at base 10 missing `9`, the right ends being the roots of `zeta_F = 2` rounded up, where the partial sums of `nu_F` are not even `O(x)` (Fact 5.5). Over twenty-four designs every proper one carries a zero right of its abscissa and the three full sets carry none (Fact 5.2); nineteen of the proper ones keep the digit `1`, and at three of those the partial sums of `nu_F` are not `O(x)`, at a fourth only by a margin of `1.1e-5`. These are readings of a numerical argument principle with propagated truncation bounds, resolved and not certified in ball arithmetic, so they are Facts; the transport and the ceiling are theorems with nothing numerical in them.

What is classical and what is cited. The abscissa is Kohler and Spilker (2009), Satz 2, read through its restatement as Theorem 2 of Allouche, Shallit and Stipulanti (2025), and is proved again in Lemma 2.2. The continuation of `zeta_F` to the whole plane, with simple poles all among `s_(m,j) = alpha - m + 2 pi i j/log(base)`, is Proposition 4.1 of Burnol (2026), on the automatic mechanism of Allouche, Mendes France and Peyriere (2000); only its first step is used here, Lemma 2.3. The Dirichlet inverse of a function that is `1` at `1` is the standard construction. That `mu` is not `k`-automatic for any `k`, so that the automatic machinery continues no Mobius-weighted design series, is Theorem 2.3 of Coons (2010). Burnol's paper, the fullest treatment of this zeta among the sources read here, carries no Mobius or Mertens sum. The transport is one-sided: nothing here proves `sigma_c(N_F) <= sup Re rho`, and the bracket of Corollary 4.7 is the only upper bound given.

Section 5 prints the zeros and what the transport reads off them. Section 6 shows that gluing `M_F` to `zeta_F` gains nothing. ## Definitions

**Definition 2.1 (design).** Fix an integer `base >= 2` and a set `F` of digits inside `{0, ..., base-1}`, other than `{0}`, with `fill = |F| >= 1`. The design `S_F` is the set of positive integers whose every base-`base` digit lies in `F`, `A_F(x) = #{n in S_F : n <= x}` is its counting function, `alpha = log(fill)/log(base)` is its dimension, and `a_min` is the least nonzero digit of `F`, which is the least element of `S_F`, since an element of two digits or more is at least `base`. For `level >= 1`, `D_level` is the set of the `fill^level` integers `0 <= u < base^level` whose `level` padded digits all lie in `F`. The full set is `F = {0, ..., base-1}`, where `S_F` is every positive integer and `alpha = 1`; every other `F` is proper. With `e(t) = exp(2 pi i t)`, the digit transform is `hat F(t) = sum_(d in F) e(d t)` and its level form is `hat F_level(t) = prod_(i < level) hat F(base^i t)`.

**Lemma 2.2 (count and abscissa).** (i) `A_F(x) <= 2 fill x^alpha` for every `x >= 1` when `fill >= 2`, and `A_F(x) <= 1 + log(x)/log(base)` when `fill = 1`. (ii) The design zeta `zeta_F(s) = sum_(n in S_F) n^(-s)` converges absolutely exactly on `Re s > alpha`, and `zeta_F(sigma) -> +infinity` as `sigma -> alpha+` along the reals.

**Proof.** An element of `S_F` with exactly `L` digits lies in `[base^(L-1), base^L)`, and there are at most `fill^L` of them; there are at least `fill^(L-1)`, since a nonzero digit of `F` can lead and the other `L - 1` digits are free. (i) Let `base^(L-1) <= x < base^L`. If `fill = 1` there is one element of each length, so `A_F(x) <= L <= 1 + log(x)/log(base)`. If `fill >= 2`, `A_F(x) <= sum_(k <= L) fill^k < fill^(L+1)/(fill - 1) <= 2 fill^L = 2 fill base^((L-1) alpha) <= 2 fill x^alpha`. (ii) At real `sigma`, `sum_(n in S_F) n^(-sigma)` lies between `sum_(L >= 1) fill^(L-1) base^(-L sigma) = fill^(-1) sum_L (fill base^(-sigma))^L` and `sum_(L >= 1) fill^L base^(-(L-1) sigma) = base^sigma sum_L (fill base^(-sigma))^L`, two geometric series of ratio `fill base^(-sigma)`, which is below `1` exactly when `sigma > alpha`. So the series converges absolutely exactly on `Re s > alpha`, and the lower bound tends to `+infinity` as `sigma -> alpha+` by monotone convergence. □

**Lemma 2.3 (the digit recursion).** Put `E_1(s) = sum_(a in F, a != 0) a^(-s)` and `gamma_l = sum_(a in F) a^l` for `l >= 1`. On `Re s > alpha`,

```
(1 - fill base^(-s)) zeta_F(s)  =  E_1(s) + sum_(l >= 1) binom(-s, l) base^(-s-l) gamma_l zeta_F(s+l) .
```

The right side converges uniformly on compact subsets of `Re s > alpha - 1`, so the cofactor `Z(s) = zeta_F(s)(1 - fill base^(-s))` continues analytically to `Re s > alpha - 1`, and there `zeta_F = Z/(1 - fill base^(-s))` is meromorphic with poles only among the points `s_(0,j) = alpha + 2 pi i j/log(base)`, `j` an integer.

**Proof.** By uniqueness of the base expansion, `S_F` is the disjoint union of the one-digit elements `F` less `{0}` and the set of `base m + a` with `m in S_F` and `a in F`: an element of two digits or more is `base m + a` with `m` its leading digits and `a` its last one, and conversely. For `Re s = sigma > alpha`, `(base m + a)^(-s) = (base m)^(-s) sum_(l >= 0) binom(-s, l) (a/(base m))^l`, since `0 <= a/(base m) <= 1 - 1/base`. The triple sum over `m`, `a` and `l` converges absolutely, because `sum_l |binom(-s, l)| t^l <= sum_l binom(|s| + l - 1, l) t^l = (1 - t)^(-|s|)` bounds it by `fill base^(|s| - sigma) zeta_F(sigma)`. Summing in `m` and `a` first, the term `l = 0` is `fill base^(-s) zeta_F(s)` and the term of index `l >= 1` is `binom(-s, l) base^(-s-l) gamma_l zeta_F(s+l)`, which is the identity. For the continuation fix `delta > 0` and a compact set `K` in `Re s >= alpha - 1 + delta`. For `l >= 1` and `s` in `K`, `zeta_F(s+l)` is an absolutely convergent series bounded by `zeta_F(alpha + delta)`, `gamma_l <= fill (base - 1)^l` and `|binom(-s, l)| <= binom(R + l - 1, l)` with `R = max_K |s|`, so the `l`-th term is at most `fill zeta_F(alpha + delta) base^(1 - alpha) binom(R + l - 1, l) (1 - 1/base)^l`, whose sum over `l` is finite. Each term is analytic, so the series is analytic on `Re s > alpha - 1` and continues `(1 - fill base^(-s)) zeta_F(s)` there. The factor `1 - fill base^(-s)` vanishes exactly where `base^s = fill`, at the points `s_(0,j)`. □

This is the first step of Burnol's Proposition 4.1, which iterates it to continue `zeta_F` to the whole plane with every pole simple and among `s_(m,j) = alpha - m + 2 pi i j/log(base)`, `m >= 0`, and proves the pole at `alpha` has positive residue. Nothing below uses more than the strip `Re s > alpha - 1`, and the transport uses none of it: it lives in `Re s > alpha`, where `zeta_F` is its own convergent series.

**Definition 2.4 (the inverse and the restricted series).** When `1 in F`, `nu_F` is the Dirichlet inverse of the indicator `1_(S_F)` of Theorem 4.1, and `N_F(s) = sum_(n >= 1) nu_F(n) n^(-s)`, with `sigma_c(N_F)` and `sigma_a(N_F)` its abscissas of convergence and of absolute convergence. Separately, for every `F`, `M_F(s) = sum_(n in S_F) mu(n) n^(-s)`, with `mu` the [Mobius function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M%C3%B6bius_function): the classical arithmetic read only on the design, dominated termwise by `zeta_F` and so absolutely convergent on `Re s > alpha`. The two are different objects and this paper keeps them apart: `M_F` is `mu` restricted to the design, `nu_F` is the design's own Mobius function.

## The wall

**Theorem 3.1 (the wall).** The indicator `1_(S_F)` is multiplicative if and only if `F` is the full set.

**Proof.** At the full set the indicator is `1` at every positive integer, which is multiplicative. Let `F` be proper and `f = 1_(S_F)`. A multiplicative function has `f(1) = 1`, so if `1` is not in `F` there is nothing to prove; let `1 in F`. Either some digit `c >= 2` is missing, or `0` is the only missing digit.

Some `c >= 2` is missing: take the least, so `1, ..., c-1` lie in `F` and `c` does not. Let `R_k = (base^k - 1)/(base - 1)`, the number written with `k` ones, so `R_c` and `R_(c+1)` lie in `S_F`, and `gcd(R_c, R_(c+1)) = R_(gcd(c, c+1)) = R_1 = 1`. The product `R_c R_(c+1) = sum_(i < c) sum_(j <= c) base^(i+j)` has at the power `base^m` the count of pairs with `i + j = m`, which is `min(m+1, c, 2c-m)` for `0 <= m <= 2c-1`. That count is at most `c <= base - 1`, so no carry occurs and the digits of the product are exactly the values `1, 2, ..., c`. Since `c` is not in `F`, `f(R_c R_(c+1)) = 0` while `f(R_c) f(R_(c+1)) = 1`.

Only `0` is missing, so `F = {1, ..., base-1}`. If `base` is odd, then `base >= 3` and `(base^2 + 1)/2 = ((base-1)/2) base + (base+1)/2` has the two digits `(base-1)/2` and `(base+1)/2`, both in `F`; it is odd, since `base^2 = 1 mod 8`, so it is coprime to `2`, which also lies in `F`, while the product `base^2 + 1` has the digits `1, 0, 1` and leaves `S_F`. If `base` is even, `base^2 - 1` and `base^2 + 1` are odd and differ by `2`, hence coprime; `base^2 - 1` has the two digits `base - 1` and lies in `S_F`, `base^2 + 1` has a digit `0` and does not, while the product `base^4 - 1` has four digits `base - 1` and lies in `S_F`, so `f(mn) = 1` against `f(m) f(n) = 0`. At `base = 2` this is `F = {1}` with the pair `(3, 5)` and the product `15 = 1111` in base `2`. □

**Fact 3.2 (the wall, swept).** Over all `8177` nonempty digit sets with `2 <= base <= 12`, the eleven sets `{0}` that Definition 2.1 excludes among them, `4083` leave out the digit `1` and fail at `f(1) = 1`, `11` are full, and at each of the other `4083` the pair constructed in the proof of Theorem 3.1 is asserted coprime and breaking, `4072` by the repunits, `5` by the odd-base pair and `6` by the even-base pair. An independent search over products up to `4000` finds a least witness at every one of the `4083`; the hardest is base 12, `F = {1}`, where the least pair is `(5, 377)`, product `1885`, against the constructed `(13, 157)`. Generator: `zeta/lab/py/mrly-euler`, verb `wall`.

**Theorem 3.3 (what an inverse pair would force).** Let `F` be proper and write `c_F(n) = sum_(d e = n, d, e in S_F) mu(e)` for the coefficients of the Dirichlet series `zeta_F M_F`. Then `c_F(1) = 0` when `1` is not in `F`, and `c_F(p^j) = -1` when `p` is a prime in `S_F` and `j` is the least exponent with `p^j` outside `S_F`. Consequently, if `zeta_F M_F = 1`, then `1 in F`, every power of every prime in `S_F` lies in `S_F`, and the least element `g > 1` of `S_F` has `mu(g) = -1`, so it is squarefree with an odd number of prime factors, and has every power `g^j` in `S_F`. At the full set `zeta_F M_F = zeta (1/zeta) = 1`.

**Proof.** If `1` is not in `F`, the only factorisation of `1` is `1 * 1` and `1` is not in `S_F`, so `c_F(1) = 0`. Let `p` be a prime in `S_F` and `j >= 1`. A factorisation `p^j = d e` with `mu(e) != 0` has `e` in `{1, p}`, so `c_F(p^j) = 1_(S_F)(1) 1_(S_F)(p^j) - 1_(S_F)(p^(j-1))`. At the least `j` with `p^j` outside `S_F`, where `j >= 2` and `p^(j-1)` lies in `S_F`, this is `-1`. Now suppose `zeta_F M_F = 1`, so `c_F(1) = 1` and `c_F(n) = 0` for every `n > 1`. Then `1 in F`, and no prime of `S_F` has a power outside it, for the least such power would carry the coefficient `-1`. Let `g` be the least element of `S_F` above `1`. The factorisations of `g` with both factors in `S_F` are `g * 1` and `1 * g`, since a factor strictly between `1` and `g` is not in `S_F`; so `0 = c_F(g) = 1 + mu(g)` and `mu(g) = -1`. For `j >= 2`, a factorisation `g^j = d e` with `e` in `S_F` and `mu(e) != 0` has `e` squarefree, hence dividing `g`, which is squarefree, and so `e = 1` or `e >= g`, that is `e = g`; hence `c_F(g^j) = 1_(S_F)(g^j) - 1_(S_F)(g^(j-1))`, and induction on `j` puts every `g^j` in `S_F`. At the full set `zeta_F = zeta` and `M_F` is the Mobius series `1/zeta`. □

The statement is an implication and not a universal: it does not say that `zeta_F M_F = 1` fails at every proper `F`. Its conclusion asks a thin set to hold every power of every prime it holds, which the next reading breaks at once. The least element `g` need not be prime: at base `31` with `F = {0, 1, 30}` it is `30`.

**Fact 3.4 (where the pair breaks).** Over `257` digit sets, every `F` containing `1` at `2 <= base <= 8`, base 10 missing `9`, base 10 missing `0` and the full base 10 set, the coefficients of `zeta_F M_F` up to `n = 4000` vanish beyond `n = 1` at the `8` full sets and at none of the `249` proper ones, where the least `n > 1` with `c_F(n) != 0` is at most `50`. It is `n = 4` with `c_F(4) = 1` at base 3 `{0,1}`, `n = 6` with `c_F(6) = 2` at base 5 `{0,1}`, `n = 9` with `c_F(9) = -1` at base 10 missing `9`, the clause on `p = 3` of Theorem 3.3, and `n = 10` with `c_F(10) = -2` at base 10 missing `0`. Generator: `zeta/lab/py/mrly-euler`, verb `pair`.

## The inverse, the transport and the ceiling

**Theorem 4.1 (the inverse).** Let `1 in F`. Then `1_(S_F)` has a unique Dirichlet inverse `nu_F`, given by `nu_F(1) = 1` and `nu_F(n) = -sum_(d | n, d > 1, d in S_F) nu_F(n/d)` for `n > 1`, and `zeta_F(s) N_F(s) = 1` at every `s` where both series converge absolutely. The support of `nu_F` lies inside the multiplicative semigroup generated by `S_F`, and at base 3 `{0,1}` it is neither that semigroup nor `S_F`. Finally `nu_F = mu` if and only if `F` is the full set, and `nu_F = mu 1_(S_F)` if and only if `zeta_F M_F = 1`.

**Proof.** The defining condition `sum_(d | n, d in S_F) nu_F(n/d) = [n = 1]` has the term `nu_F(n)` at `d = 1`, since `1 in S_F`, and every other term at a smaller argument; solved for that term it is the stated recursion, which fixes `nu_F` uniquely by induction on `n`. Where `zeta_F` and `N_F` converge absolutely, their product is the absolutely convergent Dirichlet series of the convolution `1_(S_F) * nu_F`, which is `[n = 1]`, so the product is `1`. For the support, induct on `n`: if `nu_F(n) != 0` with `n > 1`, some `d > 1` in `S_F` dividing `n` has `nu_F(n/d) != 0`, so `n/d` lies in the semigroup and so does `n = d (n/d)`. At base 3 `{0,1}`, where `S_F = {1, 3, 4, 9, 10, 12, 13, ...}`, the recursion gives `nu_F(3) = -1`, `nu_F(4) = -1`, `nu_F(9) = -(nu_F(3) + nu_F(1)) = 0` and, since the only divisor of `16` above `1` in `S_F` is `4`, `nu_F(16) = -nu_F(4) = 1`: so `9` lies in `S_F` and in the semigroup and outside the support, and `16 = 4 * 4`, written `121` in base 3, lies in the support and outside `S_F`. At the full set the recursion is `nu_F(n) = -sum_(d | n, d > 1) nu_F(n/d)`, which is the recursion of `mu`. Conversely, if `nu_F = mu` then `1_(S_F)` is the inverse of `mu`, which is the constant `1` by uniqueness, so `S_F` holds every positive integer, among them every nonzero digit and the number `base`, written `10`, and `F` is full. Last, `M_F` has the coefficients `mu 1_(S_F)`, so `zeta_F M_F = 1` as Dirichlet series exactly when `mu 1_(S_F)` is an inverse of `1_(S_F)`, that is when it is `nu_F`. □

The construction is classical. What the theorem records is where `nu_F` lives. Four sets are in play, the design, the semigroup it generates, the Beurling integers on the primes of the design of Theorem 7.3, and the support of `nu_F`, and at base 3 `{0,1}` the support is neither of the first two.

**Lemma 4.2 (partial sums and the abscissa).** Let `a` be an arithmetic function with partial sums `A(x) = sum_(n <= x) a(n)`. (i) If `A(x) = O(x^theta)` with `theta >= 0`, then `sum_n a(n) n^(-s)` converges on `Re s > theta`. (ii) If `sum_n a(n) n^(-sigma_0)` converges at a real `sigma_0 > 0`, then `A(x) = o(x^(sigma_0))`.

**Proof.** Both parts are partial summation. (i) For `Re s = sigma > theta`, `sum_(n <= x) a(n) n^(-s) = A(x) x^(-s) + s int_1^x A(u) u^(-s-1) du`. The first term is `O(x^(theta - sigma))`, which tends to `0`, and the integrand is `O(u^(theta - sigma - 1))`, which is integrable on `[1, infinity)`, so the partial sums converge. (ii) Let `B(u) = sum_(n <= u) a(n) n^(-sigma_0)`, which tends to a limit `B`, and write `B(u) = B + r(u)` with `r(u) -> 0`. Then `A(x) = B(x) x^(sigma_0) - sigma_0 int_1^x B(u) u^(sigma_0 - 1) du = B + r(x) x^(sigma_0) - sigma_0 int_1^x r(u) u^(sigma_0 - 1) du`, and both remaining terms are `o(x^(sigma_0))`. □

**Theorem 4.3 (the transport).** Let `1 in F` and let `rho` be a zero of `zeta_F` with `Re rho > alpha`. Then `sigma_c(N_F) >= Re rho`, and `sum_(n <= x) nu_F(n)` is `O(x^theta)` for no `theta < Re rho`.

**Proof.** Put `sigma_0 = sigma_c(N_F)` and suppose `sigma_0 < Re rho`. Then `sigma_0 < +infinity`, so `N_F` converges absolutely on a right half plane, since `sigma_a <= sigma_c + 1` for every Dirichlet series, and on `U = {s : Re s > max(sigma_0, alpha)}` both `N_F` and `zeta_F` are given by convergent Dirichlet series and are holomorphic. On the nonempty open half plane where both converge absolutely, `zeta_F N_F = 1` by Theorem 4.1. The half plane `U` is connected, so `zeta_F N_F = 1` on all of `U` by the identity theorem, and `zeta_F` has no zero in `U`. But `Re rho > alpha` and `Re rho > sigma_0` put `rho` in `U`, a contradiction; so `sigma_c(N_F) >= Re rho`. If `sum_(n <= x) nu_F(n) = O(x^theta)` with `theta < Re rho`, raise `theta` to a value in `(0, Re rho)`, which keeps the bound true since `Re rho > alpha >= 0`; Lemma 4.2(i) then gives `sigma_c(N_F) <= theta < Re rho`, against what was just proved. □

The proof is the identity theorem on a connected half plane and nothing more. It is strictly one-sided: it bounds `sigma_c(N_F)` from below with no error term and no hypothesis beyond `1 in F`, and the sentence that the rightmost zero is the abscissa is not a statement of this paper.

**Corollary 4.4 (the mass is outrun).** Under the hypotheses of Theorem 4.3, `sum_(n <= x) nu_F(n)` is `O(A_F(x) x^delta)` for no `delta < Re rho - alpha`. In particular it is not `O(A_F(x))`, and so not `O(A_F(x)^(1/2 + eps))` for any `eps <= 1/2`.

**Proof.** Let `delta < Re rho - alpha` and pick `theta` with `alpha + delta < theta < Re rho`. By Lemma 2.2(i), `A_F(x) x^delta` is at most `2 fill x^(alpha + delta)` when `fill >= 2`, and at most `(1 + log(x)/log(base)) x^delta` when `fill = 1`, where `alpha = 0`; either way it is `O(x^theta)`. A bound `O(A_F(x) x^delta)` would therefore be a bound `O(x^theta)`, which Theorem 4.3 forbids. Taking `delta = 0` gives the second statement, and the third follows from it, since `A_F(x)^(1/2 + eps) <= A_F(x)` once `A_F(x) >= 1` and `eps <= 1/2`. □

So a zero right of the abscissa refutes every square-root-shaped bound for the design's own Mobius function, and the digit `1` is the hypothesis that bites: a design that omits it can carry such a zero and have no `nu_F` at all. The square-root conjecture on a design therefore cannot be carried by `nu_F`; it lives on `mu` restricted to `S_F`, the meter `sum_(n in S_F, n <= x) mu(n)` of [the Mobius page](../../mobius/notes/mobius.md). The transport needs an inverse, and wherever `zeta_F M_F != 1`, at every proper set of Fact 3.4, `M_F` is not one, so no zero of `zeta_F` reaches that meter by this route.

**Theorem 4.5 (the zero-free half plane).** If a real `sigma > alpha` has `a_min^sigma zeta_F(sigma) < 2`, then `zeta_F` has no zero in `Re s >= sigma`.

**Proof.** Every element of `S_F` other than `a_min` exceeds it, and the coefficients are nonnegative. For `Re s = sigma' >= sigma`, `|a_min^s zeta_F(s) - 1| = |sum_(n in S_F, n > a_min) (n/a_min)^(-s)| <= sum_(n in S_F, n > a_min) (n/a_min)^(-sigma) = a_min^sigma zeta_F(sigma) - 1 < 1`, using `(n/a_min)^(-sigma') <= (n/a_min)^(-sigma)`. So `a_min^s zeta_F(s) != 0`. □

The hypothesis `sigma > alpha` is load bearing, and the test is one real evaluation. Absolute convergence alone is no zero-free region for a series of positive terms: `1 + 2^(-s)` converges absolutely everywhere and vanishes at `(2m+1) pi i/log 2`. What Section 5 shows is that a design zeta, an infinite series with a finite abscissa, vanishes there too.

**Theorem 4.6 (the ceiling).** Let `1 in F` and let a real `sigma > alpha` have `zeta_F(sigma) < 2`. Then `sum_(n >= 1) |nu_F(n)| n^(-sigma) <= 1/(2 - zeta_F(sigma))`, so `sigma_a(N_F) <= sigma`, `N_F = 1/zeta_F` on `Re s >= sigma`, and `|sum_(n <= x) nu_F(n)| <= x^sigma/(2 - zeta_F(sigma))` for every `x >= 1`.

**Proof.** Write `1_(S_F) = delta_1 + g` with `delta_1 = [n = 1]` and `g` the indicator of `S_F` less `{1}`. The `k`-fold convolution `g^(*k)` vanishes below `2^k`, so `nu_F = sum_(k >= 0) (-1)^k g^(*k)` holds pointwise as a finite sum at every `n`, and it is an inverse of `delta_1 + g`, hence the inverse. So `|nu_F(n)| <= h(n) = sum_(k >= 0) g^(*k)(n)`, the number of ordered factorisations of `n` into elements of `S_F` above `1`. The Dirichlet series of `g` at `sigma` is `zeta_F(sigma) - 1 < 1`, so `sum_n h(n) n^(-sigma) = sum_(k >= 0) (zeta_F(sigma) - 1)^k = 1/(2 - zeta_F(sigma))`, by Tonelli. That bounds `sum_n |nu_F(n)| n^(-sigma)`, so `N_F` converges absolutely on `Re s >= sigma`, where `N_F = 1/zeta_F` by Theorem 4.1. Finally `sum_(n <= x) |nu_F(n)| <= x^sigma sum_(n <= x) |nu_F(n)| n^(-sigma)`. □

With `a_min = 1` the theorem contains Theorem 4.5, since `zeta_F N_F = 1` on `Re s >= sigma` leaves `zeta_F` no zero there. The majorant `h` is the ordered factorisation count, and its own abscissa is exactly the root of `zeta_F(sigma) = 2`; what `nu_F` saves on `h` by cancellation is what the bracket below leaves open.

**Corollary 4.7 (the bracket).** Let `1 in F`. Then `Re rho <= sigma_c(N_F) <= sigma_a(N_F) <= sigma` for every zero `rho` of `zeta_F` with `Re rho > alpha` and every real `sigma > alpha` with `zeta_F(sigma) < 2`.

**Proof.** Theorem 4.3, Theorem 4.6, and `sigma_c <= sigma_a` for every Dirichlet series. □

On the real axis `zeta_F` falls strictly from `+infinity` at `alpha` (Lemma 2.2) to `1` at `+infinity`, so it crosses `2` at exactly one point, Theorem 4.6 holds at every `sigma` above it, and the bracket is `[sup Re rho, that point]`, the supremum over the zeros right of the abscissa, which is finite by Theorem 4.5. Both ends are read in Section 5, the right end at the crossing itself and not at the grid point `sigma_1` of Fact 5.1.

## The glue that gains nothing

Where `zeta_F M_F != 1`, at every proper set read in Fact 3.4, the remainder `D_F = zeta_F M_F - 1` is the price of using `M_F` as if it were the inverse, and the question is whether it is cheap enough that `M_F = (1 + D_F)/zeta_F` buys anything.

**Theorem 6.1 (the defect).** Write `zeta_F M_F = 1 + D_F`, with `c_F(n)` the coefficients of `zeta_F M_F` as in Theorem 3.3 and `P(x) = sum_(n <= x) c_F(n)`. Then `sigma_a(D_F) <= alpha`. If moreover `fill >= 2` and `sigma_c(D_F) < alpha`, then `P(x)/x^alpha -> 0` as `x -> infinity` and `M_F(sigma) -> 0` as `sigma -> alpha+` along the reals.

**Proof.** `|c_F(n)|` is at most the number of factorisations `n = d e` with `d, e` in `S_F`, the `n`-th coefficient of `zeta_F^2`, which converges absolutely on `Re s > alpha` as a product of two absolutely convergent series; so `sigma_a(D_F) <= alpha`. Now let `fill >= 2`, so `alpha > 0`, and `sigma_c(D_F) < alpha`. The coefficients of `D_F` are `c_F(n) - [n = 1]`, so Lemma 4.2(ii) at a real `sigma_0` with `max(sigma_c(D_F), 0) < sigma_0 < alpha` gives `P(x) - 1 = o(x^(sigma_0))`, hence `P(x)/x^alpha -> 0`. And `D_F` is holomorphic on `Re s > sigma_c(D_F)`, a half plane containing `alpha`, so `D_F(sigma)` stays bounded as `sigma -> alpha+`, while `zeta_F(sigma) -> +infinity` by Lemma 2.2; so `M_F(sigma) = (1 + D_F(sigma))/zeta_F(sigma) -> 0`, the product identity holding at every `sigma > alpha`, where all three series converge absolutely. □

There is no circularity: `M_F` is dominated termwise by `zeta_F` and converges absolutely at every `sigma > alpha` with no hypothesis on the size of `sum_(n in S_F, n <= x) mu(n)`.

**Fact 6.2 (the defect is not cheap).** Read at the four phases `log_base x = level`, `level + 1/4`, `level + 1/2`, `level + 3/4`, the ratio `P(x)/x^alpha` is `0.493767`, `0.699235`, `0.758519`, `0.587055` at base 3 `{0,1}`, `level = 17`; `0.596436`, `1.047015`, `0.843833`, `0.709576` at base 4 `{0,1}`, `level = 13`; and `0.611328`, `1.048657`, `0.860060`, `0.723221` at base 5 `{0,1}`, `level = 11`, the generator printing every level from `1`; on the full sets `P(x) = 1` at every `x`. Four phases are read because `P(x)/x^alpha` is log-periodic, and a sample at `x = base^level` alone aliases every Fourier mode onto one number. At base 10 missing `9` the phase `x = 10^level` reads `-0.011742` at `level = 6`, falling in size, against `0.033309`, `0.058519` and `0.073298` at the other three, and that design is not read. So on the three designs read, nothing in the window points to `P(x) = o(x^alpha)`, which by Theorem 6.1 is what an abscissa of `D_F` below `alpha` would force: the numerator of `M_F = (1 + D_F)/zeta_F` sits at the design's own abscissa, and the glue moves nothing. A finite window proves no abscissa, and `sigma_c(D_F) = alpha` is left open.

## Three products in place of the Euler product

**Theorem 7.1 (the position product).** For every `level >= 1` and every integer `n`, `int_0^1 hat F_level(t) e(-n t) dt = 1_(D_level)(n)`. Hence for every arithmetic function `a` and every `s` with `sum_n |a(n)| n^(-Re s) < infinity`, with `A(s, t) = sum_(n >= 1) a(n) e(-n t) n^(-s)`,

```
sum_(n in D_level, n >= 1) a(n) n^(-s)  =  int_0^1 hat F_level(t) A(s, t) dt .
```

**Proof.** Expanding the product over positions, `hat F_level(t) = sum e((sum_(i < level) d_i base^i) t)` over the strings `(d_0, ..., d_(level-1))` in `F^level`, so the integral counts the strings with `sum_i d_i base^i = n`. Since every digit is below `base`, that is the number of base-`base` expansions of `n` with `level` padded digits in `F`, which is `1` on `D_level` and `0` off it. For the second identity `|hat F_level| <= fill^level` and `sum_n |a(n)| n^(-Re s)` converges, so the series `A(s, t)` converges uniformly in `t` and may be integrated term by term against `hat F_level`, and the first identity keeps exactly the `n >= 1` in `D_level`. □

Taking `a = 1` makes `A(s, t)` the periodic zeta function `F(-t, s)` of DLMF 25.13.1, and `a = mu` the Lerch-Mobius series: `zeta_F` and `M_F` are two pairings of one product that sees only the digit set against one kernel that sees only the arithmetic, and the set enters through its positions and never through the primes. This is the identity the pair route of [the Mobius page](../../mobius/notes/mobius.md) spends. It is checked against the direct sum to `1.95e-16` and `2.04e-16` at base 10 missing `9`, `level = 3` and `4`, `s = 3.3`, and to `2.9e-16` at base 3 `{0,1}`, `level = 3`, `4` and `5`. Generator: `zeta/lab/py/mrly-euler`, verb `position`.

**Theorem 7.2 (the word product).** For `|u| < 1/fill`, `1/(1 - fill u) = prod_(L >= 1) (1 - u^L)^(-c(L))` with `c(L) = (1/L) sum_(d | L) mu(d) fill^(L/d)`, the number of Lyndon words of length `L` over `fill` letters. At `u = base^(-s)` the left side is the norm zeta `sum_w N(w)^(-s)` of the free monoid on `F` with `N(w) = base^(|w|)`, on `Re s > alpha`. It has no zero, its poles are the points `s_(0,j)`, and the inverse of the all-ones function under concatenation is `1` at the empty word, `-1` at each letter and `0` elsewhere, so its sum over the words of norm at most `x` is `1 - fill` for every `x >= base`.

**Proof.** Every word of length `n` is a power `v^(n/L)` of a unique primitive word `v` whose length `L` divides `n`, so `fill^n = sum_(L | n) p(L)` with `p(L)` the number of primitive words of length `L`, and Mobius inversion gives `p(L) = sum_(d | L) mu(d) fill^(L/d) = L c(L)`. The `L` rotations of a primitive word are distinct and exactly one of them is least in lexicographic order, the Lyndon word of the class, so `c(L)` counts Lyndon words and is a nonnegative integer. For `|u| < 1/fill`, `log(1/(1 - fill u)) = sum_(n >= 1) fill^n u^n/n`, while `sum_L c(L) log(1/(1 - u^L)) = sum_L c(L) sum_(m >= 1) u^(Lm)/m = sum_(n >= 1) (u^n/n) sum_(L | n) L c(L) = sum_n fill^n u^n/n`, every series converging absolutely since `c(L) <= fill^L/L`. Exponentiating gives the product. At `u = base^(-s)` there are `fill^n` words of norm `base^n`, which gives the norm zeta, whose only singularities are where `fill base^(-s) = 1`, the points `s_(0,j)`, and which never vanishes. The inverse of the all-ones function under `(f * g)(w) = sum_(xy = w) f(x) g(y)` is the coefficient sequence of `1 - fill u`, the value `1` at the empty word and `-1` at each letter: at a nonempty word `w` the sum is `1 - 1 = 0`, from `x = w` and from `y` its last letter. □

So the design carries an exact Euler product, over the Lyndon words in its digit alphabet and in the norm `base^(|w|)`, which at `z = base^(-s)` is the Artin-Mazur zeta `exp(sum_(n >= 1) fill^n z^n/n)` of the full shift on `fill` symbols, `fill^n` being its number of points of period `n`. It has no zero and its Mobius function lives on the letters, so it carries no Riemann-hypothesis content: all of that sits in the cofactor `Z(s) = zeta_F(s)(1 - fill base^(-s))` of Lemma 2.3, which is what Section 5 censuses. The expansion is checked through `u^16` at `fill = 2, 3, 4, 9, 10` in exact integers, and `c(L)` at `fill = 2` reads `2, 1, 2, 3, 6, 9, 18, 30, 56, 99` for `L = 1` to `10`, [A001037](https://oeis.org/A001037). Generator: `zeta/lab/py/mrly-euler`, verb `word`.

**Theorem 7.3 (the Beurling system at a digit gcd).** Let `a = gcd(F) > 1`. Then every element of `S_F` is a multiple of `a`. If `a` is a prime lying in `F`, the only prime in `S_F` is `a`, the Beurling integers of the design, the products of the primes lying in `S_F`, are the powers of `a`, and their Mertens function `M_B(x)`, the sum of `mu` over them up to `x`, is `0` for every `x >= a`. Otherwise `S_F` holds no prime, the Beurling integers are `{1}` and `M_B` is identically `1`.

**Proof.** Every digit of every element of `S_F` is a multiple of `a`, hence so is the element. A prime in `S_F` is then a multiple of `a`, so it is `a`, and `a` must be prime; and `a <= max F <= base - 1` is a one-digit number, which lies in `S_F` exactly when it lies in `F`. When `a` is a prime in `F`, the Beurling integers are the powers `a^j` and `M_B(x) = mu(1) + mu(a) = 0` for `x >= a`. Otherwise there is no prime, the only Beurling integer is the empty product `1`, and `M_B = 1`. □

So the Beurling route is blind to every scaled column, base 10 on `{0, 4, 8}` and on `{0, 4, 6}` for instance, while Theorem 5.7 reads those columns exactly. A Beurling system need not satisfy a Riemann hypothesis at all: Beurling (1937) introduced generalised prime systems, and Diamond, Montgomery and Vorhauer (2006), read here in its abstract only, construct one whose integer count is regular while its zeta violates the analogue of the Riemann hypothesis; so the census below is a measurement on a set that is not `S_F`, and not evidence for a conjecture.

**Fact 7.4 (the Beurling census).** Base 3 `{0,2}` has the single prime `2` and `M_B(x) = 0` for `x >= 2`. Base 3 `{0,1}`, read to the cut `x = 920483 = 3^12.5` rounded, has `525` primes and `2198` Beurling integers there, running maximum `max |M_B| = 98` and `log(98)/log(920483) = 0.3339` against `alpha/2 = 0.3155`. Base 10 missing `9`, read to the cut `x = 10^6`, has `35139` primes and `488864` Beurling integers there against `x^alpha = 531441`, `M_B(10^6) = 1860` with running maximum `1866`, and `log(max)/log x` reads `0.4203`, `0.4882`, `0.5452` at `x = 10^4`, `10^5`, `10^6` against `alpha/2 = 0.4771`, where the full base 10 set, as control, reads `0.4084`, `0.4241`, `0.4276` against its own `alpha/2 = 0.5` and reproduces `M(10^4) = -23`, `M(10^5) = -48`, `M(10^6) = 212`, [A084237](https://oeis.org/A084237). There is cancellation on the primes of base 10 missing `9`, `0.5452` against the trivial `alpha = 0.9542`, and it sits above `alpha/2`. Generator: `zeta/lab/py/mrly-euler`, verb `beurling`.

## Open problems

Six things are open. First, the converse of the transport: whether `sigma_c(N_F)` equals the supremum of `Re rho` over the zeros right of the abscissa. Corollary 4.7 brackets it between that supremum and the root of `zeta_F(sigma) = 2`, `[0.72074, 1.17903]` at base 3 `{0,1}` on the zeros found so far, and the readings of Fact 5.6 inside the bracket prove nothing; the absence of zeros alone places no abscissa, and the obvious route needs a growth bound for `1/zeta_F` on vertical lines as well. Second, a certificate: right of the abscissa `|zeta_F'(s)| <= -zeta_F'(Re s)`, one real evaluation, so a winding box sampled finely against that bound and evaluated in ball arithmetic would make Fact 5.4 a theorem, and with it Corollary 4.4 an unconditional statement at a named design; neither is done here. Third, whether every proper design has a zero right of its abscissa: every proper design of the sweep does (Fact 5.2), and no argument is known, while the locus itself obeys none of the laws in `alpha` and `fill/base` that the census of [the zeta page](../notes/zeta.md) tried. Fourth, whether `zeta_F M_F = 1` fails at every proper digit set: Theorem 3.3 asks an escapee to hold every power of every prime it holds, and every proper set read fails by `n = 50` (Fact 3.4), but the implication is not a universal. Fifth, `sigma_c(D_F) = alpha`, read on three designs in Fact 6.2 and not proved. Sixth, the conjecture the apparatus was built to test, the square-root bound `sum_(n in S_F, n <= x) mu(n) = O(A_F(x)^(1/2 + eps))` of [the Mobius page](../../mobius/notes/mobius.md): Corollary 4.4 rules out `nu_F` as its carrier and says nothing about `mu` restricted to `S_F`; it is a conjecture.
