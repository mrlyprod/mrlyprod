## The question mark

The memory dial above and the radix dial below each turn one slot. This section reads two classical objects in the three-slot language: the Stern-Brocot tree, a base-2 design whose cell carries a `2 x 2` matrix product instead of a bit, and Minkowski's `?`, which keeps the address word, swaps the continued fraction place slot for the dyadic one, and turns the reals whose partial quotients are restricted to a finite or cofinite alphabet into run-length rules of the memory dial. The mathematics of the second part is classical; the reading, the dimension lemma on the run-length rules and the table are the ink. Every number is `lab/py/question-mark`.

### Stern-Brocot is a design with a rank-2 carry

Write `s` for the Stern diatomic sequence [A002487](https://oeis.org/A002487), `s(0) = 0`, `s(1) = 1`, `s(2n) = s(n)`, `s(2n+1) = s(n) + s(n+1)`, and `v(n) = (s(n), s(n+1))` for the row of two consecutive values. Reading `n` in base 2, `v(2n + b) = v(n) M_b` with `M_0 = [[1,1],[0,1]]` and `M_1 = [[1,0],[1,1]]`, so `v(n) = (0, 1) M_(d_1) M_(d_2) ... M_(d_L)` over the digits of `n` most significant first, a leading zero changing nothing since `(0,1) M_0 = (0,1)`. **Proved.** `v(n) M_0 = (s(n), s(n) + s(n+1)) = (s(2n), s(2n+1))` and `v(n) M_1 = (s(n) + s(n+1), s(n+1)) = (s(2n+1), s(2n+2))`, the two lines of the recursion. (**Verified** with `0` mismatches on both forms below `2^16` and against the first `92` terms of the OEIS entry, `lab/py/question-mark`.) That is a linear representation, the matrix form of a 2-regular sequence in the sense of [Allouche and Shallit 1992](https://doi.org/10.1016/0304-3975(92)90001-v). The Calkin-Wilf row at depth `d` is `s(n)/s(n+1)` for `2^d <= n < 2^(d+1)`, and the Stern-Brocot row is the same list under bit reversal of the position (**Verified**, `0` mismatches through depth `12`, `lab/py/question-mark`), so the tree at depth `d` is the level-`d` cells of a `dim 1`, base `2` design carrying at cell `n` the vector `v(n)` in place of a bit.

Where that sits on the dial. A width-`k` rule carries a state of `k - 1` digits, finitely many, and its transition appends a digit and tests a window; the Collatz carry of `### The carry of a Collatz step` carries two bits and reads the word once. Here the state is a vector in `Z^2`, unbounded, the transition is a matrix product, and the output is a weight and not a yes or no: a carry of rank `2` with infinitely many states. What it breaks is digit independence. `M_0 M_1 = [[2,1],[1,1]]` and `M_1 M_0 = [[1,1],[1,2]]`, so the value of a word depends on the order of its digits: `s(5) = 3` and `s(6) = 2` on the words `101` and `110`, the least such pair when a word is the digits of `n` without a leading zero, and `s(3) = 2` against `s(5) = 3` on `011` and `101` when the words are the `2^L` cells of a level, leading zeros included. (**Proved** by the two products; **Verified**, `lab/py/question-mark`.) A level of a design is the Kronecker power of its rule, and a Kronecker power `f(d_1) f(d_2) ... f(d_L)` is invariant under every permutation of the digits, so from `L = 3` on no level of `s` is a Kronecker power of any one-digit table; at `L = 2` the cells read `0, 1, 1, 2`, which no `f(d_1) f(d_2)` fits either, `f(0)^2 = 0` and `f(0) f(1) = 1` being incompatible. (**Proved.**)

### The question mark swaps the place slot

Minkowski's `?` sends the Farey subtree of `[0,1]` to the dyadic addresses: with the row at depth `d` the `2^d` fractions inserted as mediants at that depth, sorted, node `i` goes to `(2i + 1)/2^(d+1)`, the centre of cell `i` at level `d + 1`. (**Verified** with `0` mismatches through depth `12` on `8193` nodes, the mediant recursion `?((p+p')/(q+q')) = (?(p/q) + ?(p'/q'))/2` run exactly, `lab/py/question-mark`.) In three-slot terms `?` keeps the address word and swaps the place slot: one word `d_1 d_2 ... d_d` names the Stern-Brocot node on one side and the dyadic cell on the other. Its closed form is Denjoy's, `?([0; a_1, a_2, ...]) = 2^(1 - a_1) - 2^(1 - a_1 - a_2) + 2^(1 - a_1 - a_2 - a_3) - ...`, read in the restatement of [Boca and Linden 2018](https://arxiv.org/abs/1705.01238), and that sum is the binary word `0.0^(a_1 - 1) 1^(a_2) 0^(a_3) 1^(a_4) ...`: the partial quotients are the run lengths. Two consequences are exact. **Proved.** `?(1/(a + x)) = 2^(-a)(2 - ?(x))`: the quotient `a` prepends `0^(a-1) 1` and complements what follows, so the continued fraction branch `x -> 1/(a + x)` becomes the similitude `y -> 2^(-a)(2 - y)` of ratio `2^(-a)`, a dyadic place map of `a` digits with a twist. **Proved.** `?` conjugates the Farey map `F(x) = x/(1-x)` on `[0, 1/2]`, `(1-x)/x` on `[1/2, 1]` to the tent map `T(y) = 2y`, `2 - 2y`: at `a_1 >= 2` both sides drop one leading zero, at `a_1 = 1` both complement. (Both **Verified** exactly on the same `8193` nodes, the branch law at `a = 1..5`, `lab/py/question-mark`.) None of this is new; the reading is.

Let `A` be a set of positive integers and `E_A` the reals in `(0,1)` whose partial quotients all lie in `A`. Then `?(E_A)` is the set of binary words whose runs, after the first, all have length in `A`, and that is a rule of the memory dial whenever `A` is finite or cofinite. At finite `A` with `m = max A` it is the width-`(m+1)` rule forbidding the windows `0^(m+1)` and `1^(m+1)`, and `1 0^j 1` and `0 1^j 0` for the `j < m` outside `A`; at `A = N \ F` with `f = max F` it is the width-`(f+2)` rule forbidding `1 0^j 1` and `0 1^j 0` for `j` in `F`. At `A = {1, ..., m}` the rule forbids the two runs `0^(m+1)` and `1^(m+1)` and nothing else, and at `m = 2` that is code `126`, the rule `### The memory meter` prints beside code `14`. Call these the **run-length rules**. On the accept slot `?` recodes the full shift over the alphabet `A` into a run-length rule over `{0,1}`, and on the place slot it turns `x -> 1/(a + x)` into `y -> 2^(-a)(2 - y)`, a design-to-design map slot by slot. The run-length rules number `2^(k-1) - 1 + 2^(k-2)` at width `k`, `2`, `5`, `11` at widths `2`, `3`, `4`, on `11` distinct alphabets. The identification is exact up to the first run. **Proved.** Every word of `?(E_A)` is accepted, and an accepted word lies outside `?(E_A)` exactly when its leading run refuses the alphabet: a leading `0^j` with `j + 1` outside `A`, or a leading `1^j` with `1` or `j` outside `A`. At `A = {1, ..., m}` the surplus is exactly the cylinder `[0^m]`, which by the branch law at `a = m + 1` is `2^(-m-1)(2 - ?(E_A))`, an affine copy of ratio `2^(-m-1)`. (**Verified** at level `14` on all `18` codes with `0` accepted words missing: at code `126` the surplus is the `233` words opening on `00` among `1220` accepted, the `987` others the prefixes of `?(E_A)`; at code `219`, `A = N \ {1}`, it is the `610` words opening on `1`, `lab/py/question-mark`.) So the accepted set is `?(E_A)` together with affine images of it, one per refused leading run, finitely many at finite `A` and countably many at cofinite `A`, and every dimension below is shared.

**Proved, `rho` of a run-length rule.** Write `C(x) = sum_(a in A) x^a`, and `B(x) = x + ... + x^m` at finite `A`, `B(x) = x/(1-x)` at cofinite `A`. An accepted word is a single run, or a first run, inner runs in `A` and a last run, so `sum_L N_W(L) x^L = 2 (B + B^2/(1 - C))`, whose least positive singularity is the root `x_0` in `(0,1)` of `C(x_0) = 1`, `B` being finite there; by `### The transfer matrix` `rho = 1/x_0`, the largest real root of the polynomial `P_A` cleared from `sum_(a in A) rho^(-a) = 1`, which is `x^m - sum_(a in A) x^(m-a)` at finite `A` and `x^(f+1) - 2x^f + (x-1) sum_(j in F) x^(f-j)` at cofinite `A`. (**Verified** on all `18` codes: `P_A` divides the exact characteristic polynomial of the transfer matrix and `rho` is its largest root, `lab/py/question-mark`.) `A = {1,2}` and `A = N \ {1}` are both the golden `x^2 - x - 1`, `{1,3}` and `N \ {1,2}` both the supergolden `x^3 - x^2 - 1`, `{2,3}` the plastic `x^3 - x - 1` and `{1,2,3}` the tribonacci `x^3 - x^2 - x - 1`: the four constants of `### The famous constants are one notch in` return as alphabets of partial quotients, at width `4` for the last three.

**Proved, the Hausdorff dimension of a run-length rule is `log_2 rho`.** Upward is a cover: the accepted set lies in `N_W(L)` cells of length `2^(-L)` at every `L`, so `dim_H <= log_2 rho` for every rule of the dial. Downward, at finite `A`, `?(E_A)` is compact and is the union of its images under the similitudes `y -> 2^(-a)(2 - y)`, `a in A`, so it is their attractor; the images of `(0,1)` are the disjoint intervals `(2^(-a), 2^(1-a))`, so the open set condition holds and by [Hutchinson 1981](https://doi.org/10.1512/iumj.1981.30.30055), 5.3(1), `dim_H ?(E_A)` is the `D` with `sum_(a in A) 2^(-aD) = 1`, which is `log_2 rho` by the polynomial above. At cofinite `A` the truncations `A_n = A intersect [1, n]` put `?(E_(A_n))` inside `?(E_A)` with `rho(A_n)` increasing to `rho(A)`, `C_n` increasing to `C`. That is the dimension identity of `### The transfer matrix`, proved at every `dim` by citation in `### Every rule is a graph-directed continued fraction set` and proved here by Hutchinson's route on every run-length rule at every width, `18` of them in the census at widths `2` to `4`.

Two dimensions, one word count. The continued fraction side has its own Hausdorff dimension `dim_H E_A`, the zero of the pressure `s -> log lambda(s)` with `lambda(s)` the leading eigenvalue of `L_(A,s) f(x) = sum_(a in A) (a + x)^(-2s) f(1/(a + x))` on `[0,1]`, Lemma 1 of [Jenkinson and Pollicott 2018](https://arxiv.org/abs/1611.09276) at finite `A`, read at source; its Theorem 1 gives `dim_H E_2 = 0.53128050627720514162446864736847178549305910901839...` and its Remark 3 restates the estimate `0.5312805062772051416` of Hensley 1996, not read here. `?` sends `E_2` to a set of dimension `log_2 phi = 0.694241913630`. Word counts and Perron roots travel with the address word; metric data do not, `?` being Holder of order `log 2 / (2 log phi) = 0.720210` and of no better order, Salem's theorem read in the restatement of [Boca and Linden 2018](https://arxiv.org/abs/1705.01238). **Proved.** A Holder map of order `alpha` raises no Hausdorff dimension by more than the factor `1/alpha`, a cover of `E` by sets of diameter `d_i` going to a cover by sets of diameter at most `c d_i^alpha`, so `dim_H E_A >= alpha log_2 rho` on every run-length rule, and at `A = {1,2}` the floor is `alpha log_2 phi = 1/2` exactly. (**Verified** on all `18` rows, `lab/py/question-mark`.)

### What has no clean alphabet

Every run-length rule is fixed by the digit flip and by reversal, a run constraint reading both symbols and both directions alike, and the codes fixed by both number `4`, `8`, `64` at widths `2`, `3`, `4`, so `2`, `3`, `53` symmetric codes carry no alphabet. (**Verified**, `lab/py/question-mark`.) At widths `2` and `3` the orphans are the dead code `0` and codes `9`, `129`, `165`, which allow only the constant words or those and the alternating words `(01)^n`: their preimages under `?` are finite sets, `rho = 1`, and the only finite `E_A` are the one-letter points. At width `4`, `19` of the `53` carry `rho > 1`, the least being code `11892`, which forbids `0000`, `1111`, `0001`, `1000`, `0111`, `1110`, `0011`, `1100`: runs of length `1` or `2` with two consecutive `2`s forbidden, the alphabet `{1,2}` under a pair constraint, at the supergolden `rho = 1.465571231876`. On the continued fraction side that is `{x : a_i in {1,2}, (a_i, a_(i+1)) != (2,2)}`, a Markov set and not an `E_A`, whose pressure zero on the matrix transfer operator `### Every rule is a graph-directed continued fraction set` builds is `0.416817764433`. The codes not fixed by the flip put one alphabet on the odd quotients and another on the even. In general a forbidden window `x^(r_1) y^(r_2) ... ` reads as a bounded-window constraint on consecutive partial quotients, the outer runs as lower bounds and the inner runs exact, with the parity of the position tracked, so every `dim 1` rule of the census is a graph-directed continued fraction set, and the table above is the `18` rows where that constraint is one alphabet on every position.
