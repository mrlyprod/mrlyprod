### Every rule is a graph-directed continued fraction set

This subsection closes two threads: the dimension of the accepted set of any rule with a cycle, by citation at every `dim` and by a second citation at `dim 1`, and the continued fraction side of the orphans, by the graph-directed form the last subsection named and did not build. Every number is `lab/py/question-mark`, verb `graph`.

**Proved by [Mauldin and Williams 1988](https://doi.org/10.2307/2000940), the Hausdorff dimension of the accepted set of a width-`k` rule with `rho >= 1` is `log_2 rho`, at every `dim`.** Read a width-`1` rule as the width-`2` rule allowing the windows `(d, d')` with both digits in `W`, the same accepted set and the same `rho`, so that `k >= 2` and a state is a nonempty word. Let `X_W` be the infinite words all of whose windows lie in `W`, `K_W` the accepted set, the intersection over `L` of the union of the closed cells of the accepted words of length `L`, and `pi(w) = sum_j d_j 2^(-j)` in `[0,1]^dim`. `K_W = pi(X_W)`: a point of `K_W` lies at every `L` in the closed cell of an accepted word, those words form a finitely branching infinite tree under prefix, Konig's lemma gives an infinite branch, accepted since each of its windows is a window of a prefix, and its cells shrink to the point. Prune the dead states, those from which no cycle is reachable: they are closed under successors and carry no cycle, so the transfer matrix is block triangular with a nilpotent dead block, the live block has the same `rho`, and `rho >= 1` says the live block is nonempty. For a live state `u` let `F_u` be the set of `pi(t)` over the infinite walks `t` from `u`, nonempty and compact; then `F_u` is the union over the edges `u -> v` of `(d + F_v)/2`, `d` the digit vector the edge appends, and `K_W` is the union over `u` of `pi(u) + 2^(1-k) F_u`, a finite union of similar copies of the `F_u`. Seed one unit cube per live state, `J_u = c_u + [0,1]^dim` with the `c_u` spaced apart, and put on each edge `u -> v` the similarity `T_(uv)(x) = c_u + (x - c_v + d)/2` of ratio `1/2`: the seeds are nonoverlapping compact sets with interior, every live state has an edge out, each ordered pair carries at most one edge since `v` is `u` shifted with `d` appended, `T_(uv)(J_v) = c_u + (d + [0,1]^dim)/2` lies in `J_u`, and for fixed `u` the images over distinct `d` are among the `2^dim` subcubes of `J_u`, nonoverlapping. That is the geometric graph directed construction of Mauldin and Williams as [Abram and Lagarias 2012](https://arxiv.org/abs/1210.2478), Section 2.1, restate it, Definition 2.1 and Propositions 2.2 to 2.4 attributed to Theorems 1, 3, 4 and 5 with page numbers, read at source, Mauldin and Williams's own text being reached here only through restatements; the nonoverlapping images are also the open set condition in the graph directed wording of [Edgar and Golds 1998](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/9806039), Definition 3.11, read at source, with `U_u` the interior of `J_u`, a citation that carries no part of the claim. Its construction object is the list `c_u + F_u`, the unique nonempty compact invariant list. Two hypotheses of the restated definition need a word. It asks the underlying undirected graph to be connected, which the live graph may fail, code `9` at `(1,2)` being two loops with no edge between them: apply the theorem to each undirected component, whose objects sit apart, `K_W` being the finite union of their similar copies, its dimension the largest over the components and `rho` the largest of their Perron roots, so the value does not move; [Baker, Dajani and Jiang 2014](https://arxiv.org/abs/1406.3263), Section 5.1, restate the definition without that condition, read at source, so whether it is Mauldin and Williams's own stays unread. And a loop, which the same restatement prints as allowed in its Definition 2.1 and reads as forbidden in its commentary, is removed by splitting its state into two copies joined both ways with the same exits, which changes neither the tails nor the growth rate of the walk count. The ratio matrix at exponent `beta` is `2^(-beta) A` on the live block, `A` the transfer matrix, so on a strongly connected component `H` with a cycle its spectral radius is `2^(-beta) rho_H`; Theorem 4 puts the dimension of the construction object at the largest `beta_H` with `2^(-beta_H) rho_H = 1` over the components with a cycle, a live state off every cycle being a trivial component with nothing to solve, which is `log_2 max_H rho_H = log_2 rho`, the radius of a block triangular nonnegative matrix being the largest over its diagonal blocks; Theorem 3 as restated, on the whole live matrix, gives the same `beta`. A finite union of similar copies has the dimension of its largest piece, so `dim_H K_W >= log_2 rho`. The citation carries only that lower bound: the closed cells of level `L` meeting `K_W` number between the accepted words of length `L` that extend to an infinite accepted word and `3^dim` times them, a point lying in at most `3^dim` closed cells of a level, and those words grow like `rho^L` as the `dim 1` paragraph below counts them, so `dim_B K_W = log_2 rho` in house at every `dim` and `dim_H <= log_2 rho` is free. That is the dimension identity of `### The transfer matrix` at every `dim` and every width on the rules with a cycle; at `rho = 0` the accepted set is empty. At `k = 1`, `dim 2`, all four digits, the construction is the square and reads `log_2 4 = 2`: the total dimension in `[0,1]^dim`, not one per axis.

**Proved, every `dim 1` rule is a finite graph-directed continued fraction set.** Fix a width-`k` rule and, for a state `u` of `### The transfer matrix`, a word of `k - 1` digits with last digit `u'`, let `E_u` be the set of `x = [0; a_1, a_2, ...]` such that feeding from `u` first `a_1` copies of `b = 1 - u'`, then `a_2` copies of `1 - b`, and so on, closes only allowed windows. The first run decides everything: `E_u` is the union over the edges `u -a-> v` of `1/(a + E_v)`, the edge existing exactly when the `a` copies of `b` close allowed windows and `v` being the state they leave. For `a >= k` the state left is `b^(k-1)` and the windows closed are those closed at `a = k`, so the labels from `u` to `b^(k-1)` are a subset of `{1, ..., k-1}` alone or together with every `a >= k`: a graph on `2^(k-1)` states, each edge labelled by a finite or cofinite set of quotients, each label the branch `x -> 1/(a + x)`, distinct labels sending `(0,1)` to the disjoint intervals `(1/(a+1), 1/a)`. ### The pressure zero is the dimension
