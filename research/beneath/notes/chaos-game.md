## The chaos game

[The chaos game](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chaos_game) draws the Sierpinski triangle with a pencil: start anywhere, pick a corner at random, jump halfway to it, mark the point, repeat. This chapter asks why it works for every design on this tree, and finds one mechanism behind all of it: the game writes the design's digits backwards in time.

A design here is what [core](../../core/notes/core.md) builds: a base `b >= 2`, a dimension `dim`, a digit set `F` inside `{0, ..., b-1}^dim`, and its Kronecker power. At level `k` the design is the `card F^k` cells `C(u) = int(u) b^(-k) + [0, b^(-k))^dim`, one per word `u = u_1 ... u_k` over `F`, with `int(u) = sum_(j=1..k) u_j b^(k-j)`; distinct words give distinct cells, base-`b` representation being unique on each coordinate. Its limit set is `K_F = {sum_(j>=1) d_j b^(-j) : d_j in F}`. The chaos game of the design has one map per digit,

```
phi_a(x) = (x + a) / b,    a in F,
```

and plays `x_n = phi_(a_n)(x_(n-1))` from a start `x_0` in the half-open cube `[0,1)^dim`, the draw `a_n` taken from `F` at random. The half-open cube is invariant, `phi_a` carrying it into `[a/b, (a+1)/b)`, so every `x_n` stays in it.

### The game is a shift register

**Proved.** For every start and every draw sequence,

```
x_n = sum_(j=1..n) a_(n+1-j) b^(-j) + b^(-n) x_0,
```

and for `n >= k` the point `x_n` lies in the level-`k` cell `C(a_n a_(n-1) ... a_(n-k+1))`. The first line is induction on `n`: `phi_a` divides the sum by `b` and adds `a/b`, which is the new first term. For the second, `phi_(u_1) phi_(u_2) ... phi_(u_k)(y) = (int(u) + y) b^(-k)` by the same induction, and `x_n = phi_(a_n) ... phi_(a_(n-k+1))(x_(n-k))` with `x_(n-k)` in `[0,1)^dim`, so `x_n` lies in the cell of the word `a_n ... a_(n-k+1)`. (**Verified**, `lab/py/chaos-game` verb `register`, `241768` exact checks at `(b, dim) = (2,1), (2,2), (3,2), (2,3)`, four starts, two of them off every attractor, `0` mismatches.)

In words: each draw is pushed on as the new leading digit and every older digit moves one place finer. The digit string of `x_n` reads `a_n a_(n-1) ... a_1` and then the digits of `x_0`, so the game is a [shift register](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shift_register) whose cells are the design's cells. Picture the level-2 square: the newest corner picks the quadrant, the corner before it picks the sub-quadrant inside it, and everything older only moves the point inside a sub-quadrant too small to see. For `n < k` the cell of `x_n` is the word `a_n ... a_1` followed by the first `k - n` digits of `x_0`, so the start is remembered for exactly `k - 1` steps at level `k` and never after.

That reading is not new where the game draws sequences. The chaos game representation of DNA puts the four bases on the corners of a square and jumps halfway, and [Chan and Corless 2020](../refs.md), read at source, trace a point back through its quadrant and sub-quadrants to recover the bases before it; the picture's missing `CG` sub-squares are the human genome's dinucleotide deficit. What this chapter adds is the reading as a law for every design, with each slot of [beneath](beneath.md) a knob of the game.

### Exact forgetting

**Law 1, Proved.** Let the draws be independent with law `p` on `F`, every `p(a) > 0`. For every start `x_0` in `[0,1)^dim`, every level `k` and every `n >= k`,

```
P(x_n in C(u)) = p(u_1) p(u_2) ... p(u_k)    for every word u in F^k,
```

so with uniform draws the level-`k` cell of `x_n` is exactly uniform over the `card F^k` cells of the level-`k` design. The proof is the shift register: the cell is the word `a_n ... a_(n-k+1)`, a function of `k` independent draws and of nothing else, and its law is the product of theirs. The equality is exact at every `n >= k`; nothing is asymptotic.

**Proved, the mixing time is exactly `k`.** For `n < k` the cell ends in the fixed `k - n` digits of `x_0`, so its law sits on at most `card F^n` cells. With uniform draws its total variation distance from the uniform law is therefore at least `1 - card F^(n-k) >= 1 - 1/card F`, and it is `1` when a digit of `x_0` lies outside `F`. Weighted draws give at least `1 - (max p)^(k-n) > 0` when `card F >= 2`, the product law putting at most that mass on the cells ending in `x_0`'s digits. So for every `eps < 1 - 1/card F` the uniform game reaches distance `eps` at level `k` in exactly `k` steps from every start, the worst start included. (**Verified**, verb `forget`: `27` designs, every non-empty digit set at `(b, dim) = (2,1), (2,2), (3,1)`, the base-3 carpet and the octahedron flake `bang dim 3, code 126`, uniform and weighted draws, four starts; the law summed over every draw word in exact arithmetic. At `n >= k`, `1328` of `1328` cases are exactly the product law; at `n < k`, `872` of `872` sit at positive distance and `436` of `436` uniform cases sit at distance at least `1 - card F^(n-k)`.)

The process of cells is the walk that shifts a word of length `k` and appends a fresh letter, the random walk on the [de Bruijn graph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/De_Bruijn_graph) of order `k` over `F`, and its exact stationarity after `k` steps is the one-line property proved above; no source is cited for it and none is needed.

**Proved, the point forgets geometrically and the cell forgets exactly.** Write `phi_w = phi_(a_n) ... phi_(a_1)` for the maps of one draw sequence. `K_F` is the union of the `phi_a(K_F)`, so `phi_w` maps `K_F` into itself; take `z` in `K_F` nearest `x_0`, `K_F` being compact, and `|phi_w(x_0) - phi_w(z)| = b^(-n) |x_0 - z|` gives

```
dist(x_n, K_F) <= b^(-n) dist(x_0, K_F)
```

for every draw sequence, not almost surely. With weights, let `mu_p` be the law of `sum_j d_j b^(-j)` with independent digits of law `p`, the self-similar measure of [weights](../../weights/notes/weights.md); prepending an independent digit preserves it, so `mu_p` is stationary for the game. Coupling `x_0` with an independent `z_0` of law `mu_p` and driving both by the same draws gives `W_1(law(x_n), mu_p) <= b^(-n) integral |x_0 - z| d mu_p(z)` in the [Wasserstein distance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wasserstein_metric). The point converges at rate `b^(-n)`; its cell is exact from step `k` on.

The long-run statement is older and stronger in one direction. [Elton 1987](../refs.md) proves, for finitely many maps that contract on average, that time averages along the game converge almost surely to a constant independent of the start (its abstract read; the full text is a scan not read here), and [Barnsley and Vince 2010](../refs.md), read at source, prove that the tail of a random orbit converges to the attractor almost surely, for any process that gives every map probability at least a fixed `p > 0` whatever the history. Law 1 is the finite-time, exact version that only the grid allows: one integer ratio makes the cells a partition, and the shift register makes the partition visible.

### Perfect painting

Randomness is only one way to choose the draws. Write `m = card F`. A **linear [de Bruijn sequence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/De_Bruijn_sequence)** of order `k` over `F` is a word `a_1 ... a_N` with `N = m^k + k - 1` in which every word of length `k` over `F` occurs exactly once as a window `a_i ... a_(i+k-1)`.

**Law 2, Proved.** Played as the draw schedule, a linear de Bruijn sequence of order `k` puts the points `x_k, ..., x_N` one in each of the `m^k` level-`k` cells, and no schedule does it in fewer draws, counting only the points `x_n` with `n >= k`. The cell of `x_n` for `n >= k` is the reversed window ending at `n`, the windows ending at `n = k, ..., N` are the `m^k` distinct words, and reversal is a bijection of words. For the bound: the cells of `x_1, ..., x_(k-1)` still carry digits of the start, so only the `N - k + 1` points from `x_k` on have cells the draws alone decide, one cell each, and covering `m^k` cells needs `N - k + 1 >= m^k`. Equality forces every window to be new, which is the definition of a de Bruijn sequence, so the perfect schedules of length `m^k + k - 1` are exactly the linear de Bruijn sequences. Played cyclically, a de Bruijn cycle of length `m^k` paints every level-`k` cell exactly once per lap, forever. (**Verified**, verb `paint`: `54` of `54` (design, `k`) cases, every design of `forget` with `m >= 2`, paint every cell exactly once on the first lap and again on the second.)

**Verified, the number of perfect paintings.** The cyclic schedules that paint every level-`k` cell once per lap are the Eulerian circuits of the de Bruijn graph of order `k - 1`, and a brute-force count of those circuits gives `1, 1, 2, 16` at `m = 2`, `k = 1..4`, `2, 24` at `m = 3`, `k = 1, 2` and `6, 20736` at `m = 4`, `k = 1, 2`, each equal to the classical count `(m!)^(m^(k-1)) / m^k` of de Bruijn cycles (verb `paint`). So the four corners of the square have `20736` perfect cyclic paintings at level 2, each `16` draws a lap and `17` from a cold start.

The idea that de Bruijn words drive the game is not this tree's. A talk abstract of [Mendivil 2011](../refs.md) says de Bruijn sequences arise naturally in modifying the chaos game to render an attractor, and [Caban and Strobin 2026](../refs.md), read at source, study de Bruijn drivers of the deterministic chaos game for any contractive iterated function system: a noncyclic de Bruijn sequence of order `m` over `K` maps has length exactly `K^m + m - 1`, which they call an optimal packing, and an infinite de Bruijn driver brings its orbit within `L^m (diam A + d(x_0, A))` of every point of the attractor `A` in at most `K^m + m - 1` draws for `K >= 3`, with `L` the largest Lipschitz constant. Law 2 is that statement read on the grid, where an `epsilon`-cover becomes an exact cell count and the bound becomes an equality.

### The random painting time

The random game paints too, only slower. Let `T` be the number of uniform draws until every level-`k` cell has held some `x_n` with `n >= k`, that is, until every word of length `k` over `F` has occurred as a window of the draws. At `k = 1` the windows are the draws themselves, independent, and `T` is the [coupon collector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coupon_collector%27s_problem)'s time, `E[T] = m H(m)` with `H` the harmonic number. At `k >= 2` the windows overlap, and the coupon collector's `(k - 1) + m^k H(m^k)` is no longer the answer.

**Proved, the painting time is an alternating sum over restricted games.** For a set `W` of windows of length `k` over `F`, let `N_W(t)` count the words of length `t` whose windows all lie in `W`, the count of [the memory dial](memory-dial.md), and `Z_W(x) = sum_(t>=0) N_W(t) x^t` its counting series. Then

```
E[T] = sum over non-empty M inside F^k of (-1)^(card M + 1) Z_(F^k minus M)(1/m).
```

`E[T] = sum_(t>=0) P(T > t)`, and `T > t` says some word is missing from the windows of `a_1 ... a_t`. By inclusion-exclusion over the set `M` of missing words, and since the draws avoid every word of `M` exactly when all their windows lie in `F^k minus M`, `P(T > t) = sum_M (-1)^(card M + 1) N_(F^k minus M)(t) m^(-t)`. Each series converges at `1/m`: a word avoiding `M` has its `floor(t/k)` disjoint windows outside `M`, so `N_W(t) m^(-t) <= (1 - m^(-k))^(floor(t/k))`. On the dial's transfer matrix `A_W`, `Z_W(1/m) = (k - 1) + m^(-(k-1)) 1^T (I - A_W/m)^(-1) 1`, so each term is a finite rational computation. At `k = 1` the matrix is `[m - card M]`, `Z = m / card M`, and the sum is `m sum_j (-1)^(j+1) C(m, j)/j = m H(m)`, the coupon collector again.

So the speed of the free game is priced by every restricted game at once: forbidding the set `M` of windows is a rule of the memory dial, and its counting series at `1/m` is the expected time a free game spends before it first plays a window of `M`.

That is a statement about the law and not about the mean, and nothing on this page extends it to `E[T]`.

### Restricted games are the memory dial

The Wikipedia article on the game shows variants on the square: "the currently chosen vertex cannot be the same as the previously chosen vertex", "cannot be 1 place away (anti-clockwise)", "cannot be 2 places away", and "cannot neighbor the previously chosen vertex if the two previously chosen vertices are the same". Each is a rule on consecutive draws, and each draws a fractal where the free game on the square draws a filled square. The shift register says exactly which fractal.

A **restricted game** of memory `k - 1` is a set `W` of allowed windows of `k` consecutive draws, in time order; the next draw is taken among the letters that keep the newest window in `W`, uniformly unless weights are given. Its **move graph** has the words of `k - 1` letters as states and one edge per window of `W`, and the rule is **irreducible** when that graph is strongly connected on the states it uses. Write `W^rev` for the windows of `W` read backwards.

**Proved, the restricted game draws the dial's set of the reversed rule.** The digits of `x_n` are the draws newest first, so the leading `n` digits of `x_n` form a word whose every window lies in `W^rev`: the words a restricted game can write are exactly the words that the width-`k` rule `W^rev` of [the memory dial](memory-dial.md) accepts. For an irreducible `W`, almost surely the set of limit points of `x_0, x_1, x_2, ...` is the dial's accepted set

```
K(W^rev) = { sum_j d_j b^(-j) : every window of d_1 d_2 d_3 ... lies in W^rev }.
```

Every limit point lies in it: `x_n` lies in the closed cell of an accepted word of length `n`, an irreducible rule extends every accepted word forever, so that cell meets `K(W^rev)` and `dist(x_n, K(W^rev)) <= sqrt(dim) b^(-n)`. Every point of it is a limit point: the draws are an irreducible finite Markov chain on the states, which almost surely visits every state infinitely often and so, by the strong Markov property, plays every finite path of the move graph infinitely often, and the path that writes the first `L` digits of a point of `K(W^rev)` puts `x_n` in that point's level-`L` cell. The dimension of that set is the dial's: `dim_H K(W^rev) = log rho(W) / log b`, **Proved by [Mauldin and Williams 1988](../refs.md)** as [the memory dial](memory-dial.md) states it, the open set condition holding with the open unit cube because distinct digits put its images in disjoint open subcubes; `W` and `W^rev` share `rho`, their transfer matrices being transposes.

**Proved, every restricted game falls into an irreducible one.** For a reducible `W` the draws are absorbed almost surely into a closed communicating class of the move graph or stop at a state with no allowed successor, and once absorbed into a class `C` the game is the irreducible game of the windows inside `C`; its limit set is that game's set. So the irreducible rules are the whole census of what restricted games draw.

Barnsley and Vince's theorem does not reach these games: its hypothesis gives every map probability at least `p > 0` whatever the history, and a restricted game gives a forbidden map probability `0`. The proof above replaces it by the move graph.

**Proved, a restricted game draws a plain design's cells only when its rule is a product.** At level `k` the cells an irreducible restricted game visits are the accepted words of length `k`, which are the windows of `W^rev` themselves; they are the level-`k` cells of a design `G` exactly when `W = G^k`. The coupling theorem of [the memory dial](memory-dial.md) prices the alternative. Measured in base `b`, the window budget of a rule with `w = card W` windows is `log_b(w) / k`, the dimension a design with `w^(1/k)` digits would have, and

```
kappa_b(W) = log_b(w) / k - log_b rho(W) >= 0,  with equality iff W = G^k,
```

the dial's `kappa` scaled from base 2 to base `b`, since `kappa = 0` iff product holds over any finite alphabet. A non-product therefore always draws a set of dimension strictly below its window budget, by at least `log_b(w^2 / (w^2 - 1)) / (2k)`, the dial's gap. The three classic one-step games on the square all allow `12` of the `16` ordered pairs: the budget is `log_2(12)/2 = 1.792481`, the dimension is `log_2 3 = 1.584963`, and the coupling `0.207519` is spent on memory.

**Proved, exact forgetting is nilpotence and not independence.** Fix the first draw `a_1` and let `P` be the move matrix of an irreducible one-step game, `pi` its stationary law. The level-`k` cell of `x_n` is the window `a_(n-k+1) ... a_n`, whose law is the stationary window law exactly when `a_(n-k+1)` has law `pi`, that is when `e_(a_1) P^(n-k) = pi`. This holds for every first draw iff `P^(n-k) = 1 pi^T`, and some power of `P` equals `1 pi^T` iff every eigenvalue of `P` other than the simple eigenvalue `1` is `0`: write `P = 1 pi^T + Q` with `Q 1 = 0` and `pi^T Q = 0`, so `P^m = 1 pi^T + Q^m`, and `Q` carries the spectrum of `P` with the `1` replaced by `0`, so it is nilpotent iff the rest of the spectrum is `0`. The rank-one index `nu`, the least such `m`, is `1` on a product, where the next draw ignores the last, and the cell law of any irreducible game is exact from `n = k + nu` on and not before. A game whose matrix has another non-zero eigenvalue never forgets exactly and converges at the rate of its second eigenvalue: the no-repeat game has `P = (J - I)/3` with eigenvalues `1` and `-1/3`, so its cell law closes on the stationary one like `3^(-n)` and never reaches it.

### The census of the square

Take the four corners of the base-2 square, corner `c = x + 2y` as in [beneath](beneath.md), and every one-step rule, a set `W` of ordered pairs (previous, next): `2^16 = 65536` rules, coded by bit `4 previous + next`. The square's `8` symmetries act on both corners of a pair at once.

**Verified.** The rules fall into `8548` classes, by orbit walk and by Burnside's average, the count the memory dial prints at `(dim, k) = (2, 2)` under the diagonal `B_2` alone, there reached by a different generator. `3421` classes are irreducible, using `1, 2, 3, 4` corners in `1, 6, 76, 3338` classes, and `5` of them are products `G x G`: one corner, an edge pair, a diagonal pair, three corners, all four. The spectral radius takes `185` distinct values over all classes and `184` over the irreducible ones, the missing value being `rho = 0`, and `185` is the dial's count of distinct minimal polynomials of `rho` at `(2, 2)`; the characteristic polynomials number `333` in both. `rho` is computed here from the squarefree part of the exact characteristic polynomial, since a numerical eigenvalue of a defective matrix splits a repeated root. (Verb `census`.)

**Verified.** Exact forgetting is rare: the rank-one index is `1` on the `5` products, `2` on `16` classes, `3` on `18` and infinite on the other `3382`, and the cell law is exactly stationary from `n = k + nu` and not before in all `68` (class, `k`) cases with finite `nu` at `k = 1, 2`, from every first draw, while `80` of `80` sampled cases with no finite `nu` stay at positive distance through `n = k + 5` (verb `census`, exact enumeration of the draws). The smallest named non-product that forgets exactly is the shift rule below.

**Proved by exact finite computation, on the square.** No irreducible non-product one-step game on the square draws the limit set of a design. Two compact sets are equal only if they meet the same closed cells at every level, and the `3416` irreducible non-product classes are separated from all `15` non-empty design sets `K_G` by the closed cells they meet, the least separating level being `1, 2, 3, 4` in `33, 2059, 1289, 35` classes. The closed cells of a graph-directed set at level `L` are exact: a word `v` reaches the neighbouring cell `int(v) + e`, `e` in `{-1, 0, 1}^2`, iff some infinite continuation has every coordinate-`i` digit `0` where `e_i = -1` and `1` where `e_i = +1`, the only way a point of `v`'s cell can sit on its boundary; that is a finite graph question. The rule is checked against the cells of exact eventually periodic points on `510` (set, level) pairs with `0` mismatches (verb `census`). The language version of the previous section holds at every base and memory; the point-set version is proved here on the square only, and in general it is a **Conjecture**.

The three classic restrictions draw sets of the gasket's dimension, `log_2 3`, and none of them is a gasket: they are irreducible non-products, separated from every design by the census, and their row sums say why the dimension still comes out at `log_2 3`. Each forbids one successor of every corner, so every corner has `3` successors, the move matrix has constant row sums `3` and `rho = 3`: three choices a step, as in the gasket, but which three depends on the last draw. The no-repeat rule is the memory dial's no-repeat rule at alphabet `4` and width `2`, code `31710` there too, where its `kappa = 0.207519` is already printed.

The "not a neighbour" game is the one famous rule that falls apart. From a corner it may stay or jump to the opposite corner, so its move graph splits into the two diagonal pairs, each a product `G x G`; the game falls into one of them and draws one diagonal of the square, a plain design, as the absorption theorem says it must, while the dial's set of the whole rule is both diagonals at once. The shift rule asks the next corner's row bit to equal the previous corner's column bit, `y_next = x_prev`: reversed into digits it says `y_j = x_(j+1)`, so the `y` digits are the `x` digits shifted by one place and the set is the graph of `y = 2x mod 1`, two segments of dimension `1`; its move matrix is the de Bruijn matrix of order two in disguise and forgets exactly after two draws.

**Proved.** The fourth Wikipedia game, "cannot neighbor the previously chosen vertex if the two previously chosen vertices are the same", has memory `2`, a width-3 rule with `56` of the `64` windows, and `rho = 2 + sqrt 2`, dimension `log_2(2 + sqrt 2) = 1.771553`, `kappa = 0.164232`. Every state is a pair of corners, either a repeat or not, and the successors of a state split by type the same way for every state of a type: a repeat `aa` may go on to `a` (a repeat) or the opposite corner (no repeat), a non-repeat `ab` may go on to `b` (a repeat) or to any of the other three (no repeat). That partition is equitable with quotient `[[1, 1], [1, 3]]`, whose characteristic polynomial is `x^2 - 4x + 2`; its positive Perron vector lifts to a positive eigenvector of the `16 x 16` transfer matrix with eigenvalue `2 + sqrt 2`, which is therefore the spectral radius, a nonnegative matrix with a positive eigenvector having it at `rho` by Collatz-Wielandt ([Seneta 2006](../refs.md)). (**Verified**, verb `census`, the full matrix and the quotient both read `3.414213562`.)

### Across the families

Every dial of [beneath](beneath.md) is a knob of the game, and the families of the tree are games with more knobs turned. One bullet a family, each saying whether a game draws it and which law survives.

- **The accept slot is the draw process. Proved.** Today's full shift is independent draws; the memory dial is a restricted game, `### Restricted games are the memory dial`.
- **The Kronecker level is the memory. Proved.** The level-`k` cell is the last `k` draws, so the level of a design is how many draws the picture remembers. A Kronecker product `g (x) h` of tiles at bases `b_1` and `b_2`, `g` outermost, is two draws, `h` first and `g` last: at base `b_1 b_2` the digit `a = a_g b_2 + a_h` gives `(x + a)/(b_1 b_2) = phi_(a_g)(phi_(a_h)(x))`, the newest draw outermost.
- **Weights are the odds. Proved.** Draw probabilities `p` paint the self-similar measure `mu_p` of [weights](../../weights/notes/weights.md), with the level-`k` cell law exactly `p^k` from step `k` on, law 1.
- **Independent coordinates are independent games. Proved.** When `F = F_1 x F_2` splits across two blocks of coordinates, `phi_(a_1, a_2)(x_1, x_2) = (phi_(a_1)(x_1), phi_(a_2)(x_2))` and a uniform draw from `F` is a pair of independent uniform draws, so the game is two games, one per block, run in lockstep.
- **The radix dial is the contraction, the twist a turn in each map. Proved, from the definitions.** The radix dial's place map `phi_d(z) = (u_d z + d)/beta` is a chaos-game map: the base `beta` contracts by `|beta|^(-1)` and turns by `arg beta`, the unit `u_d` turns the copy before it is placed. The shift register survives as the radix dial's closed form, `x_n = sum_(j=1..n) (prod_(i<j) u_(a_(n+1-i))) a_(n+1-j) beta^(-j) + (prod_(i<=n) u_(a_(n+1-i))) beta^(-n) x_0`: the address of the last `k` draws is still the word `a_n ... a_(n-k+1)` and its law is still exactly `p^k`. What the twist can break is the picture, not the law: the radix dial's witness, `Z[i]` at base 2 with digits `{0, 1}` and twists `1, -1`, lands two words of length two on one point, so two cells of the address law are one cell of the drawing.
- **Magic words are non-autonomous games. Proved; Verified.** A [magic](../../magic/notes/magic.md) word `c_1 c_2 ...` takes a different digit set `F_(c_j)` at each scale, first letter outermost; it is a non-autonomous iterated function system, as that page says. Played with a schedule `s_1 s_2 ...`, step `n` drawing from `F_(s_n)`, the level-`k` cell of `x_n` is exactly uniform over the cells of the magic design of the reversed window `s_n s_(n-1) ... s_(n-k+1)` for every `n >= k`: exact forgetting holds level by level, and the design it holds for moves with `n`. So one orbit paints a magic word's rotations in turn and never one non-constant word: its level-2 cells stay in one magic design for all `n` only if the schedule is constant, when the letters are distinct digit sets, the first digit of a cell naming the letter `s_n`. A magic word of length `L` is drawn exactly by a game played in the order `c_L, ..., c_1`, one point per run, an ensemble and not an orbit. De Bruijn painting becomes an [Eulerian circuit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eulerian_path) on states (phase, last `k - 1` draws) for a periodic word of period `p`: the out-degree at phase `r` is `card F_(c_r)` and the in-degree is `card F_(c_(r-k))`, the alphabet of the draw that leaves the window, and the graph is strongly connected, so a lap that paints every cell of every phase's design exactly once exists iff `card F_(c_r) = card F_(c_(r-k))` for every phase `r`, indices modulo `p`. So it exists iff the letter sizes repeat with period `gcd(k, p)` around the word, always when `p` divides `k`. (**Verified**, verb `families`: at the gasket and the diagonal, sizes `3, 2`, the law is exact in `27` of `27` cases and off in `18` of `18` early ones; perfect laps of `12` and `72` draws at `k = 2, 4` and none at `k = 1, 3`; at sizes `3, 3, 2` a lap of `54` at `k = 3` and none at `k = 1, 2, 4`.)
- **Mosaics are games on a graph. Proved by [Mauldin and Williams 1988](../refs.md); Verified on one example.** Iterating a mosaic, one mask per palette slot, gives each slot `t` a mask whose filled sites `a` name a slot `M_t(a)`. The game keeps the current slot `v` and draws an edge into it, a pair `(u, a)` with `M_u(a) = v`, moving `x -> (x + a)/b` and the slot to `u`: if `x` lies in the slot-`v` set then `phi_a(x)` lies in the slot-`u` set, which is the union of those images. That is the graph-directed chaos game; the slot-`t` set is the construction object of Mauldin and Williams, and on a strongly connected slot graph its Hausdorff dimension is `log rho(S) / log b`, `S` the substitution matrix counting the sites of each mask by slot, the open set condition holding with the open unit cube since one mask's sites are distinct, and a slot reached from two sites of one mask handled by passing to the graph whose vertices are the edges, whose adjacency `In Out` shares its non-zero spectrum with `Out In = S`. [magic](../../magic/notes/magic.md) records that no generator on the tree computed a mosaic's dimension; `lab/py/chaos-game` now does it for one: masks `A: 0 -> A, 1 -> B, 2 -> A` and `B: 0 -> B, 3 -> A` at base 2, `S = [[2, 1], [1, 1]]`, `rho = phi^2 = 2.618034`, dimension `2 log_2 phi = 1.388484`, filled cells `3, 8, 21, 55, 144, 377, 987, 2584` by substitution and by `e_A S^L 1` alike, and the graph game visits all `377` level-6 cells of slot `A` and nothing else in `200000` draws (verb `families`).
- **The `hexagon` topic and the Eisenstein designs.** Two different objects answer to the word. [The hexagon page](../../hexagon/notes/hexagon.md) is the moire of diagonal cuts of solid cubes at every odd side `n`, a statistic between layers and not a design; each layer is the cut of a single-level parity tile at side `n`, a finite set of cells with no recursion, and a pair of layers carries two sides `n` and `n'`, two ratios, which no game of one integer ratio holds, so the moire sits outside this chapter. The Eisenstein designs live in the radix dial. **Proved.** At a rational integer base `m` in `Z[omega]` with digits in one translate of the box `{x + y omega : 0 <= x, y < m}`, lattice coordinates conjugate the game to a square game at base `m` by one invertible affine map, so laws 1 to 3 hold word for word, and the open set condition holds with the open fundamental parallelogram, whose images under distinct box digits are disjoint open sub-parallelograms. The radix dial's gasket `(Z[omega], 2, 7)` has digits `0, 1, 1 + omega` under its canonical residues, least norm with ties broken by argument, all in the box at `m = 2`, so its Hausdorff dimension equals its similarity dimension `log 3 / log 2` by [Hutchinson 1981](../refs.md) 5.3(1); the radix dial carries that equality as a Conjecture for every named object, and this settles it for the gasket alone. At a non-real base, `1 + i`, `2 + omega`, `3 + omega`, the cells are the images `phi_w(T)` of the tile `T` of the full residue system: the address law holds as it always does, and the cells partition the plane up to boundaries only where `T` is a tile, which the radix dial carries from [Lagarias and Wang 1997](../refs.md) in a restatement; nothing is added here.
- **Slices and cuts are games on carry states. Proved; Verified at the smallest case.** Cut a design's set `K` in the plane by the line `x + y = h`. Each digit `d` carries the weight `w(d) = d_x + d_y`, and `K cap {x + y = h}` is the union over `d` of `phi_d(K cap {x + y = 2h - w(d)})`: the slice at height `h` is built from slices at the heights `2h - w(d)`, the carry states. At a rational `h` the reachable heights in `[min w, max w]` are finitely many, their denominators never growing, so the slice is a graph-directed set and a game on the carry states draws it, drawing an edge into the current height exactly as a mosaic does. When the weights of `F` take only two adjacent values there is no carry: the state is just the position in `h`'s binary digits, and the slice is the magic word those digits spell, which is the schedule theorem of [cuts](../../cuts/notes/cuts.md) for `bang dim 3, code 126`, weights `1` and `2`. The smallest carrying case is the gasket turned so that its weights are `0, 1, 2`. At `h = 2/3` the gasket `bang dim 2, code 7`, corners `00, 10, 01` with weights `0, 1, 1`, has carry states `1/3, 2/3`, matrix `[[0, 1], [2, 0]]`, `rho = sqrt 2` and a slice of dimension `1/2`, the density of `1`s in `0.101010...`; the same gasket as `code 11`, corners `00, 10, 11` with weights `0, 1, 2`, has states `1/3, 2/3, 4/3, 5/3` joined in a path, `rho` the golden ratio, and a slice of dimension `log_2 phi = 0.694242`, its level-`L` cells meeting the line counting `2, 3, 5, 8, ..., 377` at `L = 1..12`, the Fibonacci numbers, by integer enumeration and by the matrix alike. The dimension is Mauldin and Williams on a strongly connected graph, the open set condition holding with the open unit square at every state since the edges out of a height carry distinct digits, and the double edge of `code 7` handled by the same passage to the edge graph; the carry-state game visits all `8` and all `21` level-6 cylinders and nothing else (verb `families`). The solid cube of [slices](../../slices/notes/slices.md) is the full design and its diagonal cuts are polygons, the same construction with every corner kept. At an irrational height the carry states never repeat, and the slice is a non-autonomous graph-directed set that no finite game draws: a sum over carries with no last carry.

### The one-line law, and where it breaks

**The law.** The chaos game writes a design backwards: each draw becomes the new leading digit, so the last `k` draws are the level-`k` cell. Every slot and dial is then one knob of the game: accept is what may be drawn after what, place is the maps, weights are the odds, the Kronecker level is the memory, a magic word is a schedule, a mosaic or a cut is a game on a graph of states, and glue is the one slot the game never sees, since it writes addresses and glue decides which addresses land on one point.

How far it is true:

- **On addresses, always. Proved.** For any finite family of maps, the word of the last `k` draws is the address of the cylinder `phi_(a_n) ... phi_(a_(n-k+1))(X)` holding `x_n`, `X` any set the maps keep, and its law is exactly the law of `k` draws; this is the whole of law 1 stripped of geometry.
- **On cells, where the cylinders partition. Proved.** With one integer ratio and distinct digits the cylinders are the grid cells and the law reads as positions, laws 1 to 3 in full. With twists or overlaps the address law holds and the picture glues cells together, the radix dial's twisted witness.
- **On counts, where there is one integer ratio. Proved.** The closed forms of the tree come from the grid: `card F^k` cells at level `k`, `N_W(L)` cells for a rule. A game with unequal ratios, the Moran construction, or with Mobius maps, the Farey maps of [the question mark](question-mark.md) and the continued fraction maps of [rules as continued fraction sets](graph-directed.md), keeps the address law with cylinders of unequal sizes, so uniform over cells becomes the product law over cylinders and the dimension leaves the transfer matrix for a pressure equation.

**Proved, the place slot is where the measures stop decaying.** A game with one integer ratio paints a measure whose Fourier transform does not decay along the powers of the base. For `mu_p` and an integer vector `m`, `mu_p` is the law of `sum_j d_j b^(-j)`, so its Fourier transform at `xi` is `prod_j phi_p(xi b^(-j))` with `phi_p(eta) = sum_a p(a) e^(-2 pi i eta . a)`, and at `xi = b^n m` the first `n` factors are `phi_p(integer vector) = 1`, giving `hat mu_p(b^n m) = hat mu_p(m)` for every `n >= 0`. Unless every `hat mu_p(m)`, `m != 0`, is zero, which on the torus says `mu_p` is Lebesgue measure, that is the full digit set with uniform draws, the measure is not [Rajchman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rajchman_measure). This is the place slot, one integer ratio, read through the game, and the memory dial does not touch it: a restricted game started in its stationary law paints the law of a shift-invariant digit process, and the same computation gives `hat nu(b^n m) = hat nu(m)`, the tail of the digits from place `n + 1` on having the law of the whole.

What the game does not draw, on this tree:

- **The Collatz carry** of [the memory dial](memory-dial.md) is a transducer, a map from words to words, not a set; only its zero-carry sets land on the dial, and those are restricted games.
- **The Mobius meter and the memory zeta** of [the memory meter](memory-meter.md) are functions on the integers, not compact sets, and the Mobius function is beyond every finite memory by the automaticity theorem the dial cites.
- **The hexagon moire** is a statistic between layers at different odd sides, two integer ratios at once, outside every game of one ratio.
- **A cut at an irrational height** has infinitely many carry states and is a non-autonomous set that no finite game draws; a magic word that is not constant is drawn by an ensemble of games, one point per run, and by no single orbit.

Each of these breaks the law at a named place: the carry is a function and not a set, the moire mixes two ratios, and the irrational cut and the magic word lose the autonomy of the draws. Inside the place slot, every design, every irreducible rule of the memory dial, every mosaic on a strongly connected slot graph and every rational cut on strongly connected carry states is the limit set of a finite chaos game, almost surely and from every start.

- The chaos game and its convergence: [Barnsley and Demko 1985](../refs.md), iterated function systems and the global construction of fractals, which Barnsley and Vince list among the earlier convergence results that need the maps contractive or contractive on average (behind the publisher, unread); [Elton 1987](../refs.md) proves the ergodic theorem for the game (abstract read); [Barnsley and Vince 2010](../refs.md), read at source, prove convergence to the attractor under weak conditions on the draws, and their hypothesis excludes restricted games.
- Graph-directed sets and their dimension: [Mauldin and Williams 1988](../refs.md), read at source on [the memory dial](memory-dial.md), carry every dimension of a restricted, mosaic or carry-state game here.
- The shift-register reading in sequence pictures: the chaos game representation of DNA ([Jeffrey 1990](../refs.md), abstract read) and its quadrant tracing in [Chan and Corless 2020](../refs.md), read at source.
- De Bruijn drivers: [Mendivil 2011](../refs.md), a talk abstract read at source, and [Caban and Strobin 2026](../refs.md), read at source, building on [Lesniak, Snigireva and Strobin 2022](../refs.md), known here only through that restatement.
- Cover times of all words of length `k`: [Mori 1990](../refs.md), through the restatement of [Abraham, Brockman, Sapp and Godbole 2009](../refs.md), read at source.
- The restricted games of the square are the examples of the [Wikipedia article](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chaos_game), read at source; no source read here computes their dimensions, and no claim is made that none exists.
