## The object

- A recipe is one line of text, and the line is the key: `carpet.3.2 x3 island mask:net.5.1 birth:carpet_fills_zeros survive:carpet_voids x:256 cap:151` names a seed design with its side and level, a tiling, a mode, a mask, a birth side, a survive side, the generation budget `X` and, for an island, the cap. A wallpaper drops the cap and tiles once.
- The name regenerates the run bit for bit: the engine parses it, builds the seed, the mask and the two count lists, and runs; nothing is picked by hand and nothing is stored that the name does not fix. A recipe that could not be regenerated from its name would not be a row.
- The seeds are the two-dimensional designs of the tree: the five plane classes, written as codes `code1 code3 code6 code7 code15` (the dust, the H-tree, the star, the carpet and the full square, the least code of each class of the two-dimensional codes in base 2), and the ten catalog designs by name, the five classics carpet, net, htree, vtree, void and their five antis point, dust, hline, vline, star. Every shape is built at sides 3, 5 and 7 and levels 1 and 2, the board side being `side^level`, so a seed is a square of side 3, 5, 7, 9, 25 or 49.
- Equal designs run once: at each side and level the grammar builds every shape and keeps the first of each equal board, so `code7` and carpet, or `code1` and dust, are one seed. The grammar's `count` verb prints 66 seeds over the three sides and two levels.
- A rule is a pair of count lists read at the mask's budget, the number of offsets in the mask. The grammar draws both lists from one of the 23 sources of mrlylife: the six number sequences evens, odds, primes, binary, fibonacci and grid squares, one fixed random source `random_0`, and the sixteen design sequences, the fills and the voids of the eight families carpet, net, tree, void, point, dust, line and star, each the counts of its family's designs level by level.
- The four rolls decide whether the counts 0 and 1 stay in the list when the sequence holds them: `{source}`, `{source}_zeros`, `{source}_ones`, `{source}_zeros_ones`. With the rolls off evens begins 2, 4, 6 and odds 3, 5, 7; with the zeros roll evens begins at 0, which puts a zero in birth.
- The rules of the grammar are the 23 sources by the four rolls on both sides, the classic `B3/S23`, and the eight family pairs by the four rolls, the family's fills for birth and its voids for survive: 92 plus 1 plus 32, which `count` prints as 125 rules.
- A mask is a design popped at its centre, the offsets of its live cells from the centre being the neighbourhood: the same shapes as the seeds at sides 3, 5, 7 and 9 and levels 1 and 2, so a mask has side 3, 5, 7, 9, 25, 49 or 81 and reach, the largest offset, half that side rounded down. Equal masks run once: a popped board that equals an earlier one in the walk runs under the earlier name, and an empty popped board is no mask, so the kinds hold 7, 10, 11, 11, 11, 11, 10, 11 masks at 3.1, 3.2, 5.1, 5.2, 7.1, 7.2, 9.1, 9.2, counted over the mask names of the cache lines, and `count` prints 82 design masks.
- Two more masks copy the seed: `copy` is the tiled board popped at its centre, `copyinv` its inverse popped, each taken only when it differs from every design mask and is not empty. The copy masks add 38500 recipes, by `count`.
- Why 2744500: for each of the 66 seeds the grammar walks three islands, tilings 1, 3 and 5, and one wallpaper, and in each of those four slots every mask, the 82 design masks plus the slot's copies, against every one of the 125 rules. `count --x 256 --cap 151` prints island x1 680125, island x3 692125, island x5 692125 and wallpaper 680125, together 2744500 recipes. The tilings 3 and 5 are larger because a tiled board has copy masks that the untiled board has not.
- The order of the walk is the order of the catalogue: seed by seed, slot by slot, mask by mask, rule by rule. An index names a recipe as surely as its line, and the chunks below are slices of this index.

## The two modes

- Every run is on the infinite plane in one of two modes, and the plane is exact in both: no frame of the catalogue is a finite approximation of an infinite one.
- Island: the seed, tiled 1, 3 or 5 times, sits alone on an empty plane. The engine keeps only the live box, the smallest rectangle holding the live cells, and grows it by the mask's reach at every step, so the walls of the computation move back as the pattern grows and the frame is the infinite plane as long as nothing could have been counted that lies outside the box.
- The cap is the one wall that does not move: a square canvas of side `cap`, or the board's side when the board is larger, centred on the seed. The run stops as a wall stop at the first generation at which a live cell lies within the mask's reach of that canvas's edge, because the next step would need cells the engine has not computed. Every frame before the stop is the infinite plane; the frame at the stop is still the plane, since the test is made before the step that would break it; no frame past it exists in the catalogue.
- The cap is tested before the first step as well: a tiled board whose live cells already lie within reach of the wall stops at generation 0 and the catalogue holds the seed and nothing else. The census below counts these.
- The run also stops at `X` generations, as a limit, and at a repeated frame, as dead, still, loop or mover, with the period and, for a mover, the displacement and the generation by which the mover would have reached the wall. Dead, still, loop, mover, wall and limit are the six stop kinds; they are readings of the run, never filters on it.
- Wallpaper: the seed board is the torus. A torus of side `n` wrapped is exactly the plane tiled by the seed with period `n` in both directions, so the wallpaper run is the infinite plane filled with the seed, with no margin and no wall, and it runs for `X` generations or to a repeated frame. The tilings 1, 3 and 5 of one seed are the same plane under wallpaper, so a seed has one wallpaper recipe and three island recipes. A mask wider than the torus counts every wrapped site, which is what the tiled plane does.
- A zero in birth makes the empty plane fill in one step. The engine runs such a rule as a rule pair on a flipping background, as the classic engines do: a background bit says whether the plane is empty or full away from the pattern, the stored cells are the differences from that background, a count on a full background is the budget minus the stored count, and the next background is full after an empty one when 0 is in birth, and full after a full one when the full count is in survive. The `flips` and `background` fields of a cache line say that this happened.
- Dead on a flipping background means that the plane became uniform, all empty or all full, and the population curve counts the cells that differ from the background, so a plane that is full except for a hole has the population of the hole. A period on a flipping background is a period of the pattern and the background together, since the repeated-frame test keys the frame with its background bit. Counted over the cache lines, 638744 recipes of the catalogue ran on a flipping background.
- The readings of a run are the stop and its generation, the settled generation, the population curve and the box curve, the box growth slope on a log-log fit, the entropy and the churn of the last step, the symmetry of the last frame, the Kronecker cuts of the last frame with the tilings they prove, the visited count and the peak of the heatmap, and in the full line the last frame as hex rows with its origin and the heatmap's median level set with its cuts. A reading is never a filter: a recipe is catalogued whatever happens.

## The census as run

- One standard budget for every recipe: `X = 256` generations and `cap = 151`. The pair was fixed from the test timings; the two numbers live in every name, so a catalogue at another budget is another catalogue, not an update of this one.
- The `big` verb ran the whole grammar in the grammar's order in 55 chunks of 50000 recipes on 4 threads, launching no chunk past its wall-clock budget and letting the running chunk finish, and printed the cut: none. Its print, recorded in the claims file, is 919 s wall and 3663 thread-seconds. The catalogue has no cut, and nothing below is read on one.
- The cache is one JSON line a recipe under the data directory of the crate, in 55 chunk files named by their index range. Counting the lines of the chunk files gives 2744500, and their sizes add to 944266606 bytes, which is 344 bytes a recipe; the `laws` verb reads them all and reports 2744500 rows read of 2744500 recipes, last index 2744499, last recipe `vline.7.2 x1 wallpaper mask:copyinv birth:star_fills_zeros_ones survive:star_voids_zeros_ones x:256`.
- A chunk is resumable: a line is keyed by the name, a rerun of a chunk skips every cached recipe, and a chunk that stops early leaves what it wrote. The crate reads 944 MB of lines for every law below in well under a minute.
- A cache line is sparse: the stop, generation, period, displacement and wall generation when any, the settled generation, the growth slope, entropy, churn and symmetry, the Kronecker cuts and tilings when any, the heat visited count and peak, the flips and background when set, and the population and box curves sampled as the first 16 values, every 16th after, the last, the maximum and its generation. The last frame and the heat level set are not in the line; `run NAME --full` prints them for any recipe.
- The four classes of the catalogue, by `laws`: island x1 680125 of 680125 complete, island x3 692125 of 692125, island x5 692125 of 692125, wallpaper 680125 of 680125; by seed side and level 3.1 456125, 3.2 458500, 5.1 456125, 5.2 458750, 7.1 456250, 7.2 458750, each complete.
- The stop tally, in the order dead, still, loop, mover, wall, limit, by `laws`:
  - island x1: 151610, 13735, 13142, 0, 501020, 618.
  - island x3: 111087, 10306, 10344, 0, 559509, 879.
  - island x5: 98866, 9247, 9049, 0, 573926, 1037.
  - wallpaper: 422694, 47824, 139376, 0, 0, 70231.
- Read across: the islands end mostly at the wall, 1634455 walls of 2064375 islands, and the dead share falls with the tiling, since a bigger seed has more to lose before it is gone; the wallpaper, which has no wall, ends dead in 422694 of 680125 runs, periodic, still or loop, in 187200, and past `X` in 70231. No run of either mode is a mover.
- The island limits, the 2534 runs that reach `X` short of the wall, are the slow growers: their box growth slope has median 0.064 and 235 of them have a slope of at least 0.5, by `laws`.
- The wall stops at generation 0: counted over the cache lines, 242625 of the 1634455 wall stops have generation 0, all of them in the four classes of a level-2 seed tiled 3 or 5 times: 13750 of the 115375 recipes of side 5 level 2 at tiling 3, 32875 of 115375 at tiling 5, 86750 of 115375 of side 7 level 2 at tiling 3 and 109250 of 115375 at tiling 5. The geometry says why: a board of side 147 under a cap of 151 leaves 2 cells to the wall, so every mask of reach 3 or more, side 7 and up, is within reach before the first step; a board of side 245 is past the cap, the canvas is the board, and every live cell on the board's edge is within reach of the wall; a board of 125 leaves 13 cells, within reach of the masks of side 29 and up; a board of 75 leaves 38, within reach of the side-81 mask alone. These runs hold the seed frame and no other, they count as wall stops in the tally and in the wall law's fractions, and they never count as light-speed stops, since their generation is 0.

## The laws

- Each law below is read on the complete catalogue, cut none, by the `laws` verb, with the one exception of the budget law, which is read off the recipes alone and needs no run.

### The wall law

- The fraction of islands that reach the cap wall rises with the mask's side and with its level, by `laws`, the mask kind being the side and level of the mask's name: 0.487 at mask 3.1, which sits within 0.02 of 1/2, 0.624 at 5.1, 0.727 at 7.1, 0.799 at 9.1; 0.737 at 3.2, 0.906 at 5.2, 0.952 at 7.2, 0.967 at 9.2; 0.940 for copy and 0.948 for copyinv. Level 2 lies above level 1 at every side, and the level-2 masks of side 7 and 9, which are 49 and 81 wide, reach the wall in nineteen runs of twenty.
- The counts behind the fractions, by `laws`: 84437 walls of 173250 islands at 3.1, 182426 of 247500 at 3.2, 169863 of 272250 at 5.1, 246652 of 272250 at 5.2, 197907 of 272250 at 7.1, 259293 of 272250 at 7.2, 197639 of 247500 at 9.1, 263297 of 272250 at 9.2, 15282 of 16250 for copy, 17659 of 18625 for copyinv. The kinds 3.1, 3.2 and 9.1 hold fewer islands than the others because they hold fewer masks, 7, 10 and 10 against 11, every mask kind meeting the same 24750 islands a mask.
- The light-speed bound of an island is the number of generations a signal moving one reach a step needs to cross from the board to the wall's reach band: the cells from the board to the wall plus one, less the reach, over the reach, rounded up, and at least 1. The median wall generation over that bound is 1.00 for every mask kind, by `laws`: a typical wall stop is a pattern growing at the speed of light under its mask.
- The share of wall stops at exactly the bound, by `laws`: 0.702, 0.560, 0.440, 0.467 for sides 3, 5, 7, 9 at level 1; 0.497, which sits within 0.02 of 1/2, 0.604, 0.685, 0.750 at level 2; 0.522 for copy and 0.572 for copyinv. The counts are 59270, 95045, 87051, 92304 at level 1, 90700, 148939, 177520, 197351 at level 2, 7974 and 10107 for the copies. At level 1 the share falls with the side and at level 2 it rises, so the two levels cross: a small dense mask spreads its pattern at light speed more often than a large one, and a large sparse one more often than a small one.
- No mover reaches the wall by its displacement, since there is no mover: the mover count with a wall generation is 0 for every mask kind.

### The budget law

- The budget of a recipe is the number of offsets in its mask, and a drawn count list is cut at the budget; a side whose list is empty at the budget can never fire, so a recipe's fate is partly fixed by its name before any run.
- The grammar's masks take 141 distinct budgets from 2 to 60024, by `laws`; the smallest is the two-cell mask of a popped three-cell line, the largest the copy of the full square tiled five times at side 49, the 245-board popped, `245^2 - 1`.
- The first count of each source with the rolls off, by `laws`: 2 for evens, primes, binary, fibonacci and `random_0`; 3 for odds, tree voids and line fills; 4 for carpet voids, net voids, void voids, point fills, dust fills and star fills; 5 for net fills, void fills, dust voids and star voids; 6 for tree fills and line voids; 8 for carpet fills and point voids; 9 for grid squares.
- So the budgets of the grammar on which a source has no count at all are 0 for the sources whose first count is 2, 1 at first count 3 or 4, 2 at 5 or 6, 3 at 8 and 4 at 9, by `laws`: the budget 2 is the only one below 3, the budgets 2 and 4 the only ones below 5, 2, 4 and 6 the only ones below 8, and 2, 4, 6 and 8 the only ones below 9.
- All 125 rules have both sides live on some budget and none has an empty birth side on every budget, by `laws`: no rule of the grammar is dead by its name alone, and the budget law cuts recipes, not rules.
- Within the budget 8 of the popped three-by-three square, the counts a source leaves are few: evens 2, 4, 6, 8; odds 3, 5, 7; primes 2, 3, 5, 7; binary 2, 4, 8; fibonacci 2, 3, 5, 8; grid squares none; and every design sequence leaves one count, 3 to 8, or none, by `laws`. This is the mini lesson of the retired atlas restated on the grammar: a design sequence read at a small budget is a one-count rule, and one-count rules on a small mask mostly die.

### Island against wallpaper

- The same seed, mask and rule run as an island and as wallpaper end in the same stop kind in 147088 of 680125 pairs at tiling 1, a fraction of 0.216, in 111861 of 676500 at tiling 3, 0.165, and in 100379 of 676500 at tiling 5, 0.148, by `laws`; the copy masks are compared at tiling 1 only, since the copy of a tiled board is not the copy the wallpaper took.
- None of the three fractions sits within 0.02 of 0, 1/2 or 1: a seed alone on the plane and the same seed filling the plane are different objects under most recipes, and the agreement falls as the island grows, since a larger island reaches the wall more often while the wallpaper has no wall to reach.
- The disagreement is the wall's: an island's commonest stop is the wall and a wallpaper's is death, and the two can only agree on dead, still and loop. The fraction says how often a seed's fate is decided before the pattern notices whether it has neighbours.

### The odd-rule law

- A rule whose birth and survive lists are both the odd counts is linear over `GF(2)`: the next cell is the neighbour count mod 2, so the next frame is `x_{t+1} = A x_t` with `A` the sum of the shifts by the mask offsets, the centre being popped. On a torus of odd side `n` every shift has order `n`, and the Frobenius map of characteristic 2 gives `A^{2^k}` as the sum of the shifts by `2^k` times each offset; with `k = ord_n(2)`, the order of 2 modulo `n`, the multiplier `2^k` is 1 modulo `n` and `A^{2^k} = A`. Then `x_{2^k} = A^{2^k} x_0 = A x_0 = x_1`, and more generally `x_{t + 2^k - 1} = x_t` for every `t` at least 1, so from generation 1 the run is periodic with a period dividing `2^k - 1`. The grammar's linear rules are `odds_ones` and `odds_zeros_ones`, which draw the same counts, since 0 is not odd.
- The tori of the grammar have sides 3, 5, 7, 9, 25 and 49, with `k = 2, 4, 3, 6, 20, 21`, so the periods divide 3, 15, 7, 63, 1048575 and 2097151, by `laws`; the last two bounds lie past `X = 256`, so the catalogue can only read those runs as limits when their period is long.
- The periods the catalogue shows under the linear rules on wallpaper, by `laws`: period 1 only on the 3-torus, 1804 runs; periods 1 and 3 on the 5-torus, 1356 and 448 runs; 1 and 7 on the 7-torus, 548 and 1256; 1 and 7 on the 9-torus, 576 and 1246; 1 and 31 on the 25-torus, 420 and 168, with 1236 runs past `X`; 1 on the 49-torus, 384, with 1440 past `X`. Every period divides its bound.
- The periods are shorter than the bounds: the bound is the order of `A` on the whole space at worst, and a seed lies in a small invariant subspace. The 9-torus shows 7, the bound of the 7-torus, not 63, and the 25-torus shows 31, which is `2^5 - 1`, not `2^20 - 1`: the seeds of the grammar are designs, and a design's orbit under `A` closes early.
- All 33030 islands under a linear rule reach the wall, by `laws`: on the plane a linear rule copies its seed to the offsets scaled by `2^j` at generation `2^j`, so the pattern grows without bound at light speed and the wall is certain.
- The affine rule `evens_zeros` on both sides, even counts with zero included, sets the next cell to `1 + count mod 2`, which is `x_{t+1} = A x_t + 1` with the same `A`. The catalogue shows the same periods for it, by `laws`: 1 only on the 3-torus, 902 runs; 1 and 3 on the 5-torus, 678 and 224; 1 and 7 on the 7-torus, 274 and 628, and on the 9-torus, 288 and 623; 1 and 31 on the 25-torus, 210 and 84, with 618 past `X`; 1 on the 49-torus, 192, with 720 past `X`. Each divides `2^k - 1`. The Frobenius argument above does not cover the constant term, and the claims file holds this as a Conjecture, not a theorem.

### Zero movers

- No recipe of the grammar is a mover: the mover count is 0 in every class of the stop tally, by `laws`. No design seed, tiled 1, 3 or 5 times, under any of the 125 rules and any of the 84 masks, births a pattern that translates.
- The engine does detect movers: the crate's hand checks run the glider under `B3/S23` on the Moore mask as an island and read a mover of period 4 with displacement one cell down and one right, with the generation by which it reaches the wall. The mover stop is an island reading: on wallpaper a translated frame has another hash and is read as a loop when the frame itself returns, so the zero in the wallpaper column is by construction and the zero in the island columns is the fact.
- The reading: every seed of the grammar is a design, and every design and every mask of the grammar is symmetric under the two axis reflections through its centre; a tiled seed keeps them, and a rule that counts commutes with them, so every island frame keeps the two reflections about one fixed centre. A frame with both reflections cannot translate: if the frame at `t + p` were the frame at `t` shifted by `d`, it would be symmetric about the old centre and about the centre shifted by `d`, and a non-empty finite pattern symmetric about two distinct parallel axes would be invariant under the translation by twice their distance across them. So the zero is forced on this grammar and says nothing past it: a mover under one of its rules needs a seed outside it, as the glider shows.
- A mover probe is a door: the same engine on asymmetric seeds, a random soup under each rule, or the classic Life patterns under the design masks, would say whether the grammar's rules can carry a mover at all.

## The population sequences

- Every run has a population curve, and a curve is an integer sequence. The catalogue holds the curves of 680125 wallpaper runs; restricted to the plane-class seeds, with the flipping backgrounds left out, since the population of a pattern against a flipping background is not the population of a plane, they give 17576 distinct sequences, by `laws`.
- The material filter, by `laws`: a sequence is OEIS material when it has 12 terms or more, is non-decreasing, takes 8 distinct values or more, has no period of 4 or less, and is fitted by no polynomial of degree 1 or 2. The filter leaves 11 of the 17576, and `laws` lists ten, with their first 12 terms and their windows looked up in the OEIS dump.

- Every one of the ten is a wallpaper run of a level-2 plane class on a torus of side 25 or 49, under a rule that keeps a cell at counts 1, 2, 4 and 8 or at the even counts, with the three-cell line or the dust as the mask. What the sequences show is a torus filling: the pattern spreads from the seed in a front of constant width, the population rises by a constant step for each generation the front advances, and the step changes when the front meets itself around the torus. The second sequence is the squares 16, 20, 24, 28, 29, 30, 31, 32, 33, 34, 35, 36 squared, a square front growing by four a side and then by one; the sixth rises by 192 and then by 9 towards the 2401 cells of the 49-torus.

No 12-term run of any of the ten sits in the dump.
- The reading is honest: plane-class torus fills turn arithmetic after a few terms, an arithmetic tail is in the dump under its common difference and offset or is not worth a record, and a sequence whose only content is the side of a torus and the width of a front is not a sequence of the plane. Nothing here reaches the OEIS bar, and nothing is handed on. The population curves of the islands, which have no torus to fill, end at the wall before 12 terms under most masks, and the island limits are the slow growers whose curves are polynomial fits; neither was filtered.

## The library

- The `library` verb writes the library extract: one shelf cell per stop kind by class by seed family, the first 8 recipes of each cell in grammar order with their readings and no image. It prints 207 shelf cells and 1648 rows, 721011 bytes, and the count agrees with the size of the file it writes.
- The families are the fifteen shape tokens of the grammar, the five codes and the ten names, the classes are island x1, x3, x5 and wallpaper, and the stop kinds are the six; a cell is empty when no recipe of its family and class ends that way, so the mover cells and the wallpaper wall cells are empty, and 207 of the possible cells hold a row.
- A row holds the recipe name, which is the key, the stop and its generation, the period, the settled generation, the growth, entropy, churn and symmetry, the heat visited count and peak, and the sampled population and box curves; the app draws each item live from its recipe and the extract carries no picture.
- The Life app of the site runs the same engine through wasm: its Island and Wallpaper modes take every knob of the grammar, the seed shape, side, level and tiling, the mask as a design of side 3 to 9 at level 1 or 2 or a copy of the seed, the zero and one rolls, the cap and the limit, and its Encyclopedia shelves hold this extract, every item drawn live from its recipe name.

## What the catalogue does not contain

- No seed that is not a design: no random soup, no classic Life pattern, no hand-drawn seed; the glider, the blinker and the R-pentomino run only in the crate's tests. The one random thing in the grammar is the `random_0` source, a fixed count sequence, not a seed.
- No tiling past 5 and no even tiling; no seed side past 7 and no level past 2, so no board past 245.
- No mask past the side-81 design or past the 245-cell copy; no mask of level 3; no mask that is not a popped design or a popped copy, so no Moore neighbourhood except where a design is one, no von Neumann neighbourhood, no asymmetric mask.
- No `X` past 256 and no cap past 151: a run that would have settled at generation 300 is a limit, and an island that would have reached the wall at a cap of 1000 is a wall stop at 151 or a limit. The odd-rule periods on the 25- and 49-torus are read as limits for this reason.
- No rule outside the grammar: the 23 sources by four rolls, the eight pairs by four rolls and `B3/S23`; no exhaustive sweep of birth and survive lists, no rule with birth drawn from one source and survive from another outside the pairs, no non-totalistic rule.
- No frame but the last: the population and box curves are sampled in the cache line, the heat grid is kept as its visited count and peak, and the last frame and the heat level set are printed only by `run NAME --full`, never stored. The catalogue is keys and readings; a picture is regenerated from its key.
- No third dimension, no base past 2, no boundary other than the two modes: the padded finite torus of the retired atlas is not a mode of the catalogue.
- No mover, as a fact of the grammar and not as a limitation of the engine; the mover probe above is where the limitation would show.

## How to regenerate

- Every verb runs from the crate's manifest, `cargo run --release --manifest-path research/automata/lab/rs/encyclopedia/Cargo.toml -- <verb>`, and every number of this note comes from one of them on the complete cache.
- `count --x 256 --cap 151` prints the grammar's counts: 66 seeds, 82 design masks, 125 rules, 38500 copy-mask recipes, the four classes and the 2744500 total.
- `list FROM TO` prints the names of a slice of the walk; `run NAME` prints one cache line and `run NAME --full` the full line with the last frame and the heat level set.
- `chunk FROM TO --x 256 --cap 151 --threads 4` runs a slice into the cache and skips what is cached; `big --x 256 --cap 151 --threads 4 --minutes 20 --chunk 50000 --floor 15` runs the whole grammar in chunks and prints the cut.

## The retired atlas

- The atlas was the first census of mrlylife over presets picked by hand, and its geometry was a padded finite torus, the seed centred in a wrapped canvas: neither the infinite plane with the seed alone nor the plane tiled by the seed, a third thing. The encyclopedia replaced it with the two exact modes and a grammar that nothing picks by hand; the readings of the atlas were rewritten as code, and its mini preset lives on as a control. The `mini` verb replays it in a test-only torus configuration, the seed centred in a wrapped canvas of 27 for 64 generations, 1272 runs ending dead, still, loop, timeout: the six number rules 99, 18, 65, 136 against the atlas's 106, 24, 56, 132, moved because the `random_0` source changed on both engines alike; the nine design rules of the atlas 448, 2, 0, 27, equal to the atlas; all twenty design sequences 843, 4, 0, 54; `B3/S23` 31, 17, 4, 1, equal to the atlas. The sublattice lemma kept its proof and took the new engine as witness; the lobe law and the seed-comb law are lines under Lost. Its story is section 14 of [Life, the siblings and the atlas](life-and-the-atlas.md#14-the-atlas).
