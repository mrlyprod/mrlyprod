A third of the triangle's spectrum sits on a single number

Build the graph on the filled cells of a design, take its normalised Laplacian I - D^-1/2 A D^-1/2, and diagonalise. On the Sierpinski triangle the spectrum is extraordinarily degenerate: the eigenvalue 1 alone carries a third of it, and a cascade of families sits beneath, the first at 1 -/+ sqrt(30)/6. The same operator on the diagonal section of a cube answers a different question - the low end of its integrated density of states has a slope, and twice that slope is the random-walk spectral dimension d_s. One staircase, two readings: the jumps are the degeneracy, the low corner is the dimension.

The graph, the Laplacian, the eigenvalues, the clustering and the fit all come out of the crates: a dense real symmetric eigensolver written from scratch - Householder tridiagonalisation then implicit QL with Wilkinson shifts - returns the spectrum ascending, the clusters split it at consecutive gaps above 1e-9, and the low-window least squares in log-log returns an intercept and a slope the page only draws. The slice reading is taken on the giant piece; the whole section's piece count is reported beside it. Anything past the export's node cap is refused rather than served slowly.
